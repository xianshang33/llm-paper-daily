## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Knowledge Graphs (KGs) provide structured, verifiable factual grounding for Large Language Models (LLMs), while LLMs assist in KG construction and completion. Existing LLM-driven KG construction methods predominantly use stateless batch processing pipelines.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current methods suffer from three structural deficiencies: 1) Loss of long-range semantic relations across text chunks due to independent processing; 2) Entity ambiguity due to lack of global context for disambiguation; 3) Lack of interpretability in the construction process, making it hard to trace knowledge sources. These issues undermine KG quality, retrieval precision, and trust in high-stakes domains.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: RAGA (Reading And Graph-building Agent), an LLM-based autonomous framework fusing KG construction with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).
- **Challenge 1: Cross-chunk semantic fragmentation and entity ambiguity**. The difficulty lies in maintaining global state to capture long-distance dependencies and unify entity identities in traditional batch processing. RAGA addresses this by embedding a "Read-Search-Verify-Construct" cognitive constraint into a ReAct tool loop, using an atomic toolset for full lifecycle CRUD operations to enable dynamic, stateful incremental construction.
- **Challenge 2: Black-box construction and lack of traceability**. The challenge is ensuring the credibility and auditability of generated knowledge. RAGA introduces an evidence-anchored verification mechanism that links every knowledge entry to its source text for auditable provenance. Additionally, a KG-vector synchronization mechanism enables hybrid symbolic-vector retrieval, enhancing precision and transparency.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: RAGA features an atomic toolset supporting KG CRUD operations and integrates cognitive constraints into a ReAct agent loop. It combines the strengths of symbolic KG retrieval and vector retrieval.
- **Evaluation Results**: Preliminary experiments on a subset of the QASPER scientific QA dataset show that RAGA's fusion retrieval outperforms zero-shot baselines.
- **Comparison**: Compared to fixed-pipeline methods like GraphRAG and LightRAG, RAGA demonstrates measurable gains in both answer quality and evidence quality, highlighting the advantages of KG integration for factual accuracy and explainability.

## 4. Conclusion
RAGA addresses the limitations of traditional LLM-driven KG construction—specifically cross-chunk semantic loss, entity ambiguity, and lack of interpretability—by introducing an autonomous Agent mechanism. Its core "Read-Search-Verify-Construct" loop and evidence-anchored mechanism not only improve KG construction quality but also enhance the reliability of RAG systems through hybrid retrieval, offering a reference paradigm for trustworthy AI applications in high-stakes domains.
