## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is widely used to mitigate hallucinations and knowledge obsolescence in medical question answering. However, existing RAG systems predominantly employ a single-round, static retrieval paradigm, which misaligns with the multi-stage nature of clinical reasoning.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: This compressed workflow induces two structural deficiencies: question-to-query translation often lacks clinically grounded semantic interpretation, and retrieval lacks iterative sufficiency feedback, making it difficult to form reliable evidence chains. The root cause is overloading heterogeneous tasks (interpretation, exploration, adjudication) into a single reasoning chain, leading to high cognitive load and interdependence that prevents timely adjustment when evidence is insufficient or conflicting.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SEMA-RAG**: A Self-Evolving Multi-Agent RAG framework for medical question answering that reconstructs the workflow via task decoupling and dynamic multi-round exploration.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of clinically grounded semantic interpretation in query translation**. The difficulty lies in articulating implicit constraints explicitly. SEMA-RAG addresses this by assigning an Interpreter Agent to handle clinical schema interpretation, transforming patient narratives into clinically meaningful searchable queries.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of sufficiency assessment and iterative feedback in retrieval**. The difficulty is that single-round retrieval cannot handle evidence gaps or converge reliably. SEMA-RAG introduces an Explorer Agent for sufficiency-driven self-evolving retrieval through multi-round interactions to fill evidence gaps, and an Arbiter Agent for evidence adjudication and answer selection.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated across five benchmarks and five LLM backbones.
- **Results vs. Baselines**: SEMA-RAG consistently outperforms the strongest baselines. On average, it improves accuracy by +6.46 percentage points per backbone. It demonstrates significant performance gains on datasets such as BioASQ, PubMedQA, MedMCQA, and MedQA-USMLE, proving its effectiveness in complex medical reasoning tasks.

## 4. Conclusion
SEMA-RAG mimics the multi-stage reasoning process of clinicians by restructuring traditional single-round static RAG into a multi-round dynamic framework collaborated by three specialist agents (Interpreter, Explorer, Arbiter). This approach effectively addresses the structural deficiencies of traditional RAG in clinical semantic understanding and evidence sufficiency feedback, significantly enhancing the accuracy and reliability of medical question answering.
