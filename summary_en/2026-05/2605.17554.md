## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Frontier Deep Research Agents (DRAs) are being rapidly deployed in enterprise workflows to plan research, synthesize across documents, and produce structured deliverables. However, evaluation methods are lagging behind deployment.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current benchmarks primarily measure factual recall, single-hop QA, or generic agentic skills, failing to capture the multi-document, decision-grade work DRAs are designed for. Even recent professional-domain benchmarks frame evaluation as QA rather than the production of structured, decision-ready deliverables.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a benchmark for expert consulting work**: The benchmark features 42 SME-authored prompts simulating a management consultant's typical week, evaluated using a dual-layer approach of deterministic verifiers and expert rubrics.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of rigorous evaluation for 'decision-grade' structured deliverables**. The difficulty lies in traditional benchmarks focusing only on answer correctness, ignoring completeness, structural compliance, and logical rigor. The paper addresses this by introducing the 'Verifier-Rubric Score (VRS)', combining deterministic ground-truth verifiers (avg 13.8 checks per task) with a five-criterion SME rubric (0-3 scale) to jointly assess quality and task completion.
- **Challenge 2: Susceptibility to surface-level pattern matching over deep logical verification (Cognitive Traps)**. The difficulty is that agents may generate plausible-looking content with subtle factual or logical errors. The paper embeds 'cognitive traps' in most prompts to penalize agents that match surface patterns without thorough verification, better reflecting reliability in high-stakes decision scenarios.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated three frontier agents: Claude Opus 4.6 (with web search), OpenAI o3-deep-research, and Google Gemini 3.1 Pro deep-research. Each agent responded to 42 prompts, generating 126 responses.
- **Metrics**: Used a joint threshold for acceptance (Rubric Mean >= 2.5 AND Verifier Rate >= 80%).
- **Key Results**:
    - Acceptance rates were uniformly low: Gemini 21.4%, o3 9.5%, Claude 9.5%.
    - Mean VRS scores aligned with existing rubric-based benchmarks (top score 62.6 vs. APEX-v1 64.2, ProfBench 65.9), validating the rubric construct.
    - Acceptance rates were lower than APEX-Agents' Pass@1 band for dedicated DR agents (12.3%-22.7%), with a floor 3 points lower due to stricter conjunctive grading and trap design.
- **Agent-Specific Failures**:
    - **Claude**: Most reliable in producing deliverables (4.5x higher rate on file-required tasks) but had the highest fabrication signature.
    - **o3**: Cleanest average reasoning but frequently dropped required sections and propagated arithmetic errors.
    - **Gemini**: Bimodal performance, exhibiting the highest acceptance rate alongside the most zero-scored rubric cells.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper identifies a critical gap in DRA evaluation: the lack of rigorous assessment for multi-document, decision-grade structured deliverables. By introducing a new benchmark with cognitive traps, deterministic verifiers, and expert rubrics, it reveals the limitations of current frontier agents in high-stakes consulting tasks. Results show significant gaps in meeting strict joint quality and accuracy standards, with distinct failure modes for different agents, emphasizing the need for more rigorous, workflow-aligned evaluations before deploying DRAs in critical enterprise decisions.
