## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: LLM agents exhibit a consistent "over-calling" tendency, invoking tools even when unnecessary. On the When2Call benchmark, while call accuracy is high, no-call accuracy is significantly lower, resulting in an overall accuracy of only 55%-70%.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Previous studies largely treat over-calling as an empirical phenomenon or address it via prompt engineering, lacking a mechanistic explanation. Traditional activation-based accounts assume decisions depend solely on relative activation strengths, failing to explain why models favor calling even at activation parity, thus making fundamental correction difficult.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed the Intrinsic Bias Hypothesis (IBH)**: Posits that the call/no-call decision mapping contains an activation-independent "call offset," causing the model to favor calling even when activations are balanced.
- **Challenge 1: How to diagnose and quantify this intrinsic bias from black-box models?**
  - **Difficulty**: High-dimensional and entangled internal representations make it hard to isolate feature directions specifically responsible for the "call" vs. "no-call" decision.
  - **Solution**: Used Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) to recover behavior-aligned feature bases, reducing high-dimensional activations to a signed activation margin to directly estimate the offset.
- **Challenge 2: How to correct the bias without compromising normal calling capabilities?**
  - **Difficulty**: Simply suppressing calls could negatively impact scenarios where tool use is genuinely required, leading to a drop in call accuracy.
  - **Solution**: Introduced Adaptive Margin-Calibrated Steering (AMCS), a closed-form counter-bias shift along SAE decoder directions. This method cancels the diagnosed offset, mitigating false calls with negligible loss in call accuracy.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation**: Experiments were conducted on six models from three families (Qwen3.5, Gemma-3, Ministral-3). SAE-extracted features validated IBH, showing that decision neutrality requires no_call activation to outweigh call activation.
- **Results & Comparison**: Applying AMCS significantly mitigated over-calling across all models and improved overall accuracy. Compared to baselines, AMCS substantially increased no-call accuracy while maintaining high call accuracy, demonstrating its effectiveness in causal correction.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper reframes over-calling from an empirical observation into a mechanistically diagnosable and causally correctable object. By combining SAEs for mechanistic diagnosis and AMCS for intervention, the study not only explains the inherent calling bias in LLM agents but also provides an efficient, low-loss correction method, offering new insights and techniques for enhancing the reliability of LLM agent tool use.
