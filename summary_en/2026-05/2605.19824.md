## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Autonomous Vehicles (AVs) are advancing from L4 to L5 autonomy, requiring sophisticated behavior planning. Recent efforts leverage Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) powered by Large Language Models (LLMs) and Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) to enhance perception, decision-making, and control.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current agentic planners treat time as a secondary property, lacking temporal grounding. This leads to reactions based only on instantaneous stimuli, failing to maintain causal continuity across consecutive actions. Consequently, decisions may appear locally coherent but become inconsistent over time, undermining safety and long-horizon interpretability.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A temporal extension to the "Prompt-to-Pavement" formulation introducing multi-frame contextual reasoning, along with the first empirical benchmark for temporal scene-to-plan reasoning.
- **Challenge 1: How to maintain contextual awareness of prior states in multi-agent interactions without explicit memory mechanisms?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional approaches rely on external memory modules or complex sequence modeling, adding complexity and potential error points.
  - **Solution**: The paper defines planner-executor interactions as "temporally conditioned message exchanges," embedding temporal information directly into inter-agent communication via prompting, allowing the LMM swarm to retain context implicitly.
- **Challenge 2: How to evaluate logical coherence of plans over time, beyond mere semantic similarity?**
  - **Difficulty**: Standard NLP metrics (e.g., BLEU, ROUGE) measure surface-level semantic similarity and fail to capture the underlying causal logic and temporal consistency of action sequences.
  - **Solution**: Introduced a novel evaluation metric specifically designed to measure logical continuity between successive plans, capturing temporal-causal coherence.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Investigated three planner architectures with progressively increasing temporal integration: static, one-step temporal, and multi-frame temporal. Evaluated on curated subsets of the BDD-X dataset.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - **Quantitative**: Temporal conditioning reshaped the reasoning style but yielded no statistically significant improvements in standard NLP-based correctness metrics.
  - **Qualitative**: Analysis revealed predictive hazard reasoning, stable corrective behavior, and strategic divergence in the Sentinel agent.
- **Comparison**: Compared against baselines without temporal grounding, the proposed method showed advantages in long-horizon logical consistency and safety-related reasoning (e.g., hazard prediction), despite the lack of improvement in traditional metrics, highlighting the limits of prompt-based temporal grounding.

## 4. Conclusion
This work explores whether temporal conditioning in inter-agent communication preserves or enhances coherence. It finds that while standard NLP metrics do not reflect significant improvements, qualitative analysis demonstrates the importance of temporal grounding for predictive safety and stable behavior. The study clarifies the limitations of prompt-based temporal grounding and establishes a benchmark for future research in temporal scene-to-plan reasoning.
