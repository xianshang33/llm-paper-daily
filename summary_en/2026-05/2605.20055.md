## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Explicit software architecture models are crucial for complex systems, but in ROS 2-based robotic systems, structural decomposition and integration semantics are implicitly encoded across distributed artifacts (source code, launch files), making hierarchical architecture recovery difficult.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current approaches primarily focus on node-level entities and communication wiring, offering limited support for recovering hierarchical structural decomposition across multiple abstraction levels and handling complex integration semantics.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: An agent-based multi-level approach for hierarchical structural architecture reconstruction using an enhanced LLM-assisted pipeline.
- **Challenge 1**: LLM-generated architecture synthesis often suffers from inconsistency and lack of controllability. **Solution**: The paper introduces refined prompting strategies to improve the consistency and controllability of the architecture synthesis process.
- **Challenge 2**: Recovering hierarchical structures across abstraction levels is complex due to implicit semantics like namespace propagation and runtime instantiation. **Solution**: A staged recovery strategy based on multi-level intermediate architectural representations, incorporating atomic ROS node lists and launch file dependencies, enables structurally constrained reconstruction.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation**: The approach was evaluated on a real-world automated product disassembly system using cooperative robotic arms, which features significantly higher integration complexity than previous case studies.
- **Results & Comparison**: Compared to the authors' previous work, the new method demonstrates improved structural consistency, scalability, and robustness. While effective, the study also highlights remaining challenges in handling dynamic integration semantics in large-scale ROS 2 systems.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the lack of explicit architecture models in ROS 2 systems by proposing an enhanced LLM-assisted recovery pipeline. By optimizing prompting and employing a staged, multi-level recovery strategy, the method successfully reconstructs hierarchical structures across abstraction levels. Evaluations on a complex real-world system confirm its effectiveness in improving recovery quality and handling complexity, advancing automated architecture documentation for distributed cyber-physical systems.
