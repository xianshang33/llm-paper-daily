## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: LLM agents perform well in complex interactive environments requiring long-horizon decision-making but traditionally cannot learn on the fly at test time. Self-evolving agents address this by accumulating memory and reflection across episodes without model-weight updates.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current self-evolving agents suffer from "exploration collapse." As memory grows, behavior concentrates around familiar high-reward routines, reducing the discovery of better alternatives. Since the strategy space for LLMs is implicit and unbounded, agents lack a natural view of untried choices, making it difficult to actively discover new exploration directions.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: APEX (Autonomous Policy EXploration), a framework that builds and maintains an explicit strategy space via a "strategy map"—a directed acyclic graph of milestones with prerequisite dependency edges.
- **Challenge 1: How to explicitly represent and expand the unknown strategy space?**
  - **Difficulty**: The action space is vast and unstructured; agents struggle to perceive untried strategies and easily get stuck in local optima.
  - **Solution**: Introduces "Fork Discovery" to expand the map with evidence-grounded unexplored directions, systematically recording tried and potential paths to make the implicit space explicit.
- **Challenge 2: How to balance exploration and exploitation during planning?**
  - **Difficulty**: With historical memory, agents tend to repeat high-reward old strategies, ignoring unverified new paths that might yield higher rewards.
  - **Solution**: Designs a "Policy Selection" module that dynamically balances exploiting known high-reward strategies and exploring under-explored nodes in the strategy map, ensuring sustained strategic diversity.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on nine Jericho text-adventure games and WebArena, a realistic web interaction benchmark.
- **Results**: APEX outperforms all baselines (including static models and self-evolving baselines like Reflexion). In maze experiments, while Reflexion achieved higher average scores by sticking to a narrow corridor (max score 40), APEX maintained broad coverage and consistently reached high-reward areas (max score 80, avg score 54.0).
- **Ablation Studies**: Extensive ablations validate the contribution of each component (Fork Discovery, Policy Selection, Strategy Map) and demonstrate robustness across diverse settings.

## 4. Conclusion
APEX effectively addresses exploration collapse in self-evolving LLM agents by constructing an explicit strategy map. By combining evidence-based fork discovery and policy selection that balances exploration and exploitation, it enables agents to continuously discover superior strategies and maintain high performance in complex long-horizon tasks without updating model weights. The method demonstrates significant advantages and robustness in both text adventure and web interaction tasks.
