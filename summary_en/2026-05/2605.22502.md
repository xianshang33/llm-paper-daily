## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Current agent development relies heavily on external orchestration frameworks (e.g., LangGraph, CrewAI), which manage workflows by injecting instructions and routing decisions at every turn. Recent work shows that for procedural tasks, simply placing the workflow in a frontier model's system prompt (In-context) yields superior results. However, this approach consumes context windows, requires expensive frontier models for every conversation, and exposes proprietary logic to third-party providers.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: While "compiling" workflows into the weights of small fine-tuned models (termed "Subterranean Agents") theoretically resolves these issues—and prior works like SimpleTOD and FireAct have demonstrated feasibility—developer adoption remains low due to three perceived barriers: concerns over quality degradation, inflexible/long retraining cycles, and unclear cost benefits.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A systematic empirical framework evaluating the compilation of agent workflows into small LLM weights, specifically addressing and debunking misconceptions regarding quality, cost, and flexibility.
- **Challenge 1: Quality Gap**. The concern that small compiled models cannot match the complex reasoning and naturalness of frontier models using In-context learning. **Solution**: Empirical results across travel booking, Zoom support, and insurance claims show that an 8B compiled model achieves 87–98% of the quality of frontier In-context baselines. Furthermore, compiled models exhibit significantly lower failure rates than external orchestrators (e.g., 5.5% vs. 24% in travel scenarios).
- **Challenge 2: Flexibility and Deployment Cycle**. The misconception that modifying workflows requires prohibitively long model retraining. **Solution**: The paper demonstrates that the re-compilation cycle takes only 30–50 minutes on production hardware, fitting within standard CI/CD deployment cycles rather than representing a paradigmatic bottleneck.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Datasets**: Evaluated on three real-world scenarios: Travel Booking (14 nodes), Zoom Support (14 nodes with product-specific knowledge), and Insurance Claims (55 nodes, 6 decision hubs).
- **Quality Comparison**:
    - In controlled same-model (3B) comparisons, the compiled model outperformed the base model with explicit orchestration on 4 out of 5 quality metrics (p < 0.001).
    - Scaling to 8B, compiled models matched the performance of LangGraph orchestrators built on frontier models ~70x larger, achieving 87–98% of In-context quality.
    - Compiled models had lower failure rates: 5.5% vs. 24% (Travel) and 9% vs. 17% (Insurance).
- **Cost Comparison**:
    - Compiled models are 128–462× cheaper per conversation than the In-context baseline. This combines a ~65× reduction in per-token cost (via self-hosting) and a 2–7× reduction in token volume (as the workflow is no longer in the prompt).
    - Cost advantages grow with procedure complexity since the compiled model's prompt size remains constant.
- **Latency**: Local inference reduced latency by 2.8× in the insurance scenario.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper empirically dismantles three major misconceptions hindering the adoption of compiled agents. It establishes that compiling procedural knowledge into model weights is the natural solution for persistent structural knowledge: persistent structure belongs in the weights, while transient state belongs in the prompt. This approach offers near-frontier quality at two orders of magnitude lower cost, with agile engineering iteration capabilities, providing strong evidence for replacing expensive and fragile external orchestration frameworks.
