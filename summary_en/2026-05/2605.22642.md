## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Spreadsheet systems (e.g., Microsoft Excel) are central to modern data-centric workflows. As AI agents become more capable, building agents to automate spreadsheet tasks is a promising research direction.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Most current spreadsheet agents rely on specialized prompting over general-purpose LLMs. While effective for simple operations, they struggle to manage the complex, multi-step workflows typical of real-world applications, failing to handle long-horizon dependencies and intricate tool usage.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Spreadsheet-RL**, a reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning framework designed to train specialized spreadsheet agents within a realistic Microsoft Excel environment.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of scalable, high-quality training data for complex tasks.**
  - **Difficulty**: Real-world spreadsheet tasks involve multi-step operations and domain-specific knowledge, making manual annotation costly and insufficiently diverse.
  - **Solution**: Developed an automated pipeline to scalably collect paired start-goal spreadsheets from online forums and compiled them into the new Domain-Spreadsheet benchmark dataset, covering areas like finance and supply chain management.
- **Challenge 2: Inadequate environments for multi-turn RL and precise tool interaction.**
  - **Difficulty**: General code execution environments do not fully expose Excel functionalities and lack optimized tool-routing mechanisms, hindering the agent's ability to correctly select and use tools in complex multi-turn interactions.
  - **Solution**: Created Spreadsheet Gym, which exposes extensive Excel functionality via a Python sandbox and incorporates a comprehensive tool set with carefully designed tool-routing rules, specifically tailored for multi-turn RL training.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Spreadsheet-RL includes a data collection pipeline, the Spreadsheet Gym training environment, and an RL fine-tuning process. Code and data are open-sourced.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - On the general SpreadsheetBench, Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507's Pass@1 improved from 12.0% to 23.4%.
  - On the curated Domain-Spreadsheet dataset, Pass@1 increased from 8.4% to 17.2%.
- **Comparison with Related Work**:
  - Compared to baseline models relying solely on prompting (e.g., GPT-4o, OpenAI o3), the Spreadsheet-RL fine-tuned smaller model (Qwen3-4B) demonstrated superior competitiveness and generalization on specific tasks.
  - Experiments showed a stepwise performance improvement by sequentially introducing native interaction harnesses, full tool access, and finally RL training, highlighting the critical role of RL fine-tuning in handling complex multi-step workflows.

## 4. Conclusion
Spreadsheet-RL significantly enhances LLM agent performance on real-world spreadsheet tasks by introducing RL fine-tuning and a specialized training environment. It addresses the limitations of existing methods in handling complex workflows and fosters future research through open-sourced resources. The results demonstrate substantial improvements in both general and domain-specific tasks, indicating strong potential for office automation and advanced LLM-based data interface interactions.
