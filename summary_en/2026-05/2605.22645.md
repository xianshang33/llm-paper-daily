## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Text-to-image (T2I) systems increasingly rely on upstream prompters (humans or Multimodal Large Language Models, MLLMs) to translate user intent into detailed prompts. As generation quality improves, prompting proficiency has become a critical bottleneck.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current benchmarks fix prompts and only evaluate T2I models, completely ignoring the measurement of upstream prompting proficiency. Research on human prompting is limited to fragmented qualitative user studies, lacking a standardized benchmark, which misleads research on prompt optimizers.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed AtelierEval**: The first unified benchmark quantifying prompting proficiency across 360 expert-crafted tasks. Grounded in a cognitive view, it spans three task categories using a taxonomy of real-world challenges, with dual interfaces for both humans and MLLMs.
- **Challenge 1: How to achieve scalable and reliable subjective and objective evaluation of prompt-image pairs?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional evaluation relies on expensive, non-scalable human annotation and struggles to balance subjective aesthetics with objective constraint consistency.
  - **Solution**: Proposed AtelierJudge, a skill-based, memory-augmented agentic evaluator. It generates subjective and objective scores, achieving a Spearman correlation of 0.81 with human experts, enabling efficient and reliable automated evaluation.
- **Challenge 2: How to comprehensively diagnose the effectiveness of different prompting strategies (e.g., mimicry vs. planning) in real-world scenarios?**
  - **Difficulty**: Lack of a unified framework to compare different MLLMs and humans across diverse T2I backends makes it hard to reveal the underlying mechanisms of prompt engineering.
  - **Solution**: Through extensive experiments benchmarking 8 MLLMs and 48 human users across 4 T2I backends, AtelierEval serves as a robust diagnostic tool, revealing the superiority of mimicry over planning and advocating for image-augmented future prompters.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: AtelierEval comprises 360 tasks across three main categories. AtelierJudge, the core evaluation component, uses memory augmentation to enhance consistency.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - AtelierJudge achieved 95.5% accuracy on objective constraints.
  - Achieved a Spearman correlation of 0.81 with human expert scores, demonstrating high reliability.
  - Benchmarks revealed specific patterns in MLLM prompting, showing that "mimicry" is more effective than "planning".
- **Comparison**: Unlike existing benchmarks that only assess generative models (e.g., Bakr et al., 2023; Zhang & Tang, 2024), AtelierEval fills the gap in upstream prompter evaluation, offering a more systematic and quantitative diagnostic capability than fragmented qualitative studies.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper introduces AtelierEval, the first unified benchmark for human and MLLM prompting proficiency, addressing the neglect of upstream prompt construction in existing research. By proposing the AtelierJudge agentic evaluator, it achieves highly correlated, scalable automated evaluation. Large-scale experiments not only validate the benchmark's diagnostic value but also provide insights that mimicry outperforms planning in current MLLM prompting strategies, pointing the way forward for image-augmented prompter research.
