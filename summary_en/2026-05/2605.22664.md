## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: LLM agents are increasingly expected to execute end-to-end workflows, generating complete artifacts from high-level instructions. In finance, core workflows like financial modeling, forecasting, and scenario analysis are predominantly conducted via spreadsheets. Frontier AI labs have developed agents capable of constructing entire spreadsheets from scratch.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current spreadsheet benchmarks focus on atomic tasks such as question-answering or single-formula edits, failing to measure the advanced capability of building complete, professional-grade financial spreadsheets. While benchmarks like GDPval touch on financial tasks, they rely on opaque internal expert annotations, lacking transparency for the community.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed WorkstreamBench**: The first transparent benchmark for evaluating LLM agents on end-to-end spreadsheet construction tasks in the critical finance domain, focusing on economically significant workflows like modeling and scenario analysis.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of Multidimensional Quality Standards**. The difficulty lies in the fact that financial deliverables are reviewed by multiple stakeholders, requiring quality metrics beyond correctness, such as readability and ease of modification. The paper addresses this by developing an evaluation taxonomy comprising three dimensions: Accuracy, Formula, and Format, each with fine-grained criteria reflecting professional standards.
- **Challenge 2: Quantifying Performance Degradation in Complex Workflows**. The challenge is that real-world financial workflows involve complex chained calculations, and existing evaluations fail to capture agent performance shifts as difficulty increases. The paper addresses this by designing end-to-end tasks of varying difficulties, systematically evaluating agent performance beyond simple chained calculations, and revealing limitations in handling high-complexity tasks.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Evaluation Results**: The Claude family of models leads the benchmark and produces the most professional-looking outputs in qualitative reviews. However, even the strongest agents frequently fall short of professional finance standards, with performance degrading sharply as task difficulty exceeds a few chained calculations.
- **Comparison Conclusion**: Unlike existing benchmarks that test single-cell edits or simple formulas, WorkstreamBench reveals that current agents cannot yet reliably produce professional-quality spreadsheets at the complexity level demanded by real-world workflows. This indicates a significant gap between current technology and enterprise-grade financial automation needs.

#### Summary
This paper introduces WorkstreamBench, filling the gap in evaluating end-to-end financial spreadsheet construction. By introducing a multidimensional evaluation system covering accuracy, formula, and format, the study finds that while leading models like Claude perform relatively well, current LLM agents remain insufficient for complex financial modeling tasks, particularly regarding scalability and professionalism.
