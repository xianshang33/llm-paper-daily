## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: LLM-based multi-agent systems are becoming dominant for complex decision-making and long-horizon tasks but remain fragile in practical deployments.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current failure repair methods (post-hoc and online) predominantly attribute failures to execution-level faults (e.g., invalid tool outputs). They fail to address "epistemic miscalibration in planning," where plans are executed correctly but fail because the plan itself is infeasible due to a mismatch between goals and required actions. This miscalibration is latent (plans appear self-consistent) and dynamic (new information obscures past signals), making it invisible to traditional execution-error monitoring.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: The Epistemic Planning Calibration Agentic Workflow (EPC-AW), which assesses whether plans remain supported under varying information conditions rather than directly verifying feasibility.
- **Challenge 1: Latency of Epistemic Miscalibration**. The difficulty lies in the fact that generated plans can be self-consistent and executable without observable errors, making direct detection hard. EPC-AW addresses this via "Information-consistency-based Plan Selection," selecting plans whose evaluations are stable across agents, thereby filtering out plans with unwarranted confidence.
- **Challenge 2: Dynamics and Recurrence of Miscalibration**. The difficulty is that new information alters feasibility assessments, potentially obscuring past miscalibration signals and causing them to recur. EPC-AW solves this through "Consistency-guided Epistemic State Refinement," leveraging past discrepancies to adaptively guide future planning and calibrate epistemic states over time.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Results**: Experiments demonstrate that EPC-AW improves system-level success rates by an average of 9.75%.
- **Comparison**: Unlike related works (e.g., Liang et al., 2025; Ma et al., 2025) that focus on correcting execution errors or monitoring error accumulation, EPC-AW specifically targets the planning stage's cognitive misjudgments. It enhances robustness by calibrating the agent's assessment of its own knowledge limits, addressing failures where correct execution leads to unsolved tasks.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper identifies and addresses "epistemic miscalibration in planning" in LLM multi-agent systems, where agents overestimate the feasibility of their plans. By introducing EPC-AW, which employs information-consistency-based selection and consistency-guided state refinement, the authors effectively mitigate the latent and dynamic nature of these failures. The work shifts the focus from merely correcting execution errors to calibrating cognitive uncertainty during planning, significantly improving system reliability.
