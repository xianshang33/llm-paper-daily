## 1. What the paper did
- **Background**: Developing constitutive models that accurately capture material deformation under load traditionally requires years of specialized expertise in continuum mechanics, machine learning, and scientific programming. Large Language Models (LLMs) have recently shown potential to lower this barrier by generating constitutive models on demand.
- **Why existing work fails**: Existing single-agent LLM pipelines lack systematic checks to ensure that the generated models respect fundamental physical laws, resulting in models that may be physically invalid or unreliable.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a**: First multi-agent LLM-driven approach for constitutive model generation, featuring a "Creator" agent and an "Inspector" agent.
- **Challenge 1**: How to ensure generated constitutive models strictly adhere to physical constraints (e.g., thermodynamic consistency, symmetry)?
  - **Difficulty & Solution**: It is difficult for a single LLM to balance data fitting quality with complex physical rule verification. The method introduces an Inspector agent that critically audits proposals from the Creator against nine physical constraints, returning them for refinement if violations are detected, thereby enforcing physical validity.
- **Challenge 2**: How to maintain prediction accuracy and generalization capability while ensuring physical validity?
  - **Difficulty & Solution**: Strict physical constraints might limit model flexibility and reduce fitting performance. By separating generation (Creator focuses on data-tailored models) from inspection (Inspector ensures compliance), the method achieves 100% physical compliance while preserving near-baseline accuracy and robust generalization to unseen loading paths.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The concept is demonstrated with Constitutive Artificial Neural Networks (CANNs) using two different LLM backbones (Claude Opus 4.7 and Kimi K2.5). Benchmarks include brain tissue, experimental rubber, and synthetic rubber datasets.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - For Claude Opus 4.7, adding the Inspector increased the share of exported models satisfying all physical constraints from 91% to a perfect 100%.
  - For Kimi K2.5, this share increased from 37% to 56%.
  - The generated models maintained high accuracy and demonstrated remarkable generalization to loading paths outside the training data.
- **Comparison**: Compared to traditional single-agent methods that export models directly without systematic physical checks, this approach significantly enhances physical trustworthiness, transforming LLM-driven constitutive modeling into a genuinely reliable process.

#### Summary
This paper proposes a dual-agent collaborative framework that addresses the critical lack of physical consistency in LLM-based constitutive model design by separating model generation from physical inspection. Experiments demonstrate that the method not only automatically generates physically valid models but also ensures high accuracy and strong generalization. This opens a new path for automated, physics-aware model discovery, with a paradigm that is technique-agnostic and scales automatically with advancements in LLM capabilities.
