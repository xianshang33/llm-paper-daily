## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Cryptocurrency portfolio management is a sequential decision problem relying on heterogeneous signals (price trends, on-chain activity, macro conditions) whose effectiveness varies significantly across market regimes (bull, volatile, bear). While LLM-based multi-agent systems are suitable for this setting, existing systems suffer from issues in credit assignment, cold-start adaptability, and explainability.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**:
    1. **Lack of Principled Credit Assignment**: Most systems use fixed rules or heuristic confidence scores, failing to identify which agent or coalition actually improved performance.
    2. **Vulnerability to Cold-Start and Regime Shifts**: Naive online adaptation is prone to cold-start dominance and retains stale weights after market regime shifts.
    3. **Limited Transparency**: Final allocations are difficult to trace back to intermediate decisions, lacking auditability.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Market Regime Council (MRC)**: A cooperative multi-agent decision system that computes exact Shapley credits across all single, pairwise, and Grand-coalition outputs for online agent weighting.
- **Challenge 1: How to assign credit principledly to identify effective contributions?**
    - **Difficulty**: Traditional heuristic methods cannot quantify the marginal contribution of each agent to final returns, leading to unscientific weighting.
    - **Solution**: Formulates multi-agent cooperation as a transferable-utility game over all $2^N-1$ coalitions, computing exact Shapley values as online credit signals. These are combined with a Bayesian adaptive mixture that transitions smoothly from a uniform prior to evidence-based Shapley weights.
- **Challenge 2: How to handle non-stationarity due to market regime shifts and cold-start issues?**
    - **Difficulty**: Dynamic market states render static or lagging weight adjustments ineffective; sparse initial data causes weight instability.
    - **Solution**: Introduces regime-dependent multipliers to adjust agent authority based on continuous regime scores, driving downstream risk control. The Bayesian mixture stabilizes early periods, ensuring rapid adaptation to regime shifts and preventing stale weights from dominating.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Instantiated with N=3 specialist agents covering orthogonal financial information channels. At each trading period, MRC recomputes coalition-based Shapley weights from exponentially weighted performance histories and records each rebalance via a five-layer causal trace.
- **Results**: Over 1,037 trading days across 13 crypto assets and five seeds, MRC achieved a Sharpe ratio of 1.51 and a cumulative return of 440.1%.
- **Comparison**: MRC ranked first among active baselines (including other LLM and Deep RL methods) in Cumulative Return, Sharpe Ratio, and Information Ratio, while achieving the lowest Maximum Drawdown (MDD) among active methods. Ablation studies confirmed that gains stem from Shapley-weighted integration across coalition outputs rather than isolated stages.

## 4. Conclusion
The proposed Market Regime Council (MRC) addresses the lack of principled credit assignment, poor adaptability to market regime shifts, and limited explainability in existing LLM financial decision systems by modeling multi-agent portfolio management as an online cooperative game. By integrating exact Shapley value computation, Bayesian adaptive mixing, and regime-aware weight adjustment, MRC demonstrates superior risk-adjusted returns and full auditability in cryptocurrency markets, offering a new paradigm for multi-agent LLM applications in dynamic financial environments.
