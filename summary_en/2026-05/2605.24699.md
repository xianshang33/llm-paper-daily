## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are rapidly entering clinical practice. OpenAI's HealthBench Professional benchmark (525 cases from real clinician conversations) provides a rigorous evaluation standard with adversarial designs, covering 21 specialties, where 22% of cases require multi-turn integration.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: While literature shows agent architectures with tools outperform single-prompt baselines, and multi-agent frameworks like MedAgents excel in specific tasks, no prior work has evaluated multi-agent clinical pipelines on HealthBench Professional. Furthermore, existing gains are often attributed to prompt engineering rather than architectural design, and the interaction between multi-turn structures and evaluation harnesses remains under-explored.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed MDIA (Multi-agent Diagnostic Intelligence Agent)**: A coordinated 7-node specialty-routed Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) with shared memory, designed to improve clinical reasoning through system architecture and engine-level design without fine-tuning.
- **Challenge 1: Difficulty in handling specialty-specific reasoning and multi-turn context**. Clinical cases span 21 specialties and often require integrating information across multiple turns, which is hard for single models to handle with depth and consistency. **Solution**: MDIA uses a specialty classifier to route tasks to domain-expert reasoners (Gastroenterology, Ophthalmology, Neurology) or a generalist path, combined with multi-turn context preservation to ensure professional and coherent reasoning.
- **Challenge 2: Ensuring medical safety and accurate information synthesis**. Medical diagnoses involve critical drug-state safety and source reliability, where errors can have severe consequences. **Solution**: The system incorporates drug-state safety gating, site-filtered web search, and length-aware synthesis, finalized by an output synthesizer and verifier for safety and format checks, ensuring engine-level reliability.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: MDIA features an intake orchestrator with 14 medical tools (PubMed, DailyMed, UMLS, etc.) to collect structured clinical dossiers. Tasks are routed to expert nodes or a generalist path, followed by synthesis and verification. It runs on a non-fine-tuned LLM.
- **Evaluation Results**: On HealthBench Professional (n=525), using OpenAI GPT-5.4-2026-03-05 as the grader, MDIA achieved a score of 0.6272.
- **Comparison with Related Work**:
    - Outperformed the GPT-5.4 single-agent baseline (0.481) by +14.62 percentage points (pp).
    - Outperformed physician-written responses (0.437) by +19.02 pp.
    - Nominally outperformed OpenAI's ChatGPT for Clinicians (0.590) by +3.72 pp (noted as directional due to bootstrap variance).
    - The study highlighted grader variability: using Gemini 2.5 Pro as the grader resulted in a score of 0.6585, suggesting robust evaluation requires multiple independent graders.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper demonstrates that system orchestration architecture (specialty routing, multi-turn context management, safety gating) significantly shapes performance in clinical agent benchmarks, offering improvements comparable to or exceeding those from underlying foundation models. MDIA achieves state-of-the-art results on HealthBench Professional without fine-tuning. Additionally, the findings underscore the impact of grader model selection on evaluation outcomes, advocating for multi-grader cross-validation to ensure robustness.
