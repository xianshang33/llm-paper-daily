## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Delusions are significant symptoms in mental illness that fluctuate over time. Traditional clinical assessments rely on intermittent structured interviews, which miss episodic changes and suffer from recall bias. Smartphone-based ecological momentary assessment (EMA) and audio diaries capture real-time data in naturalistic settings, but manual review of large volumes of patient-generated language is time-consuming and unscalable.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current automated methods often only detect the presence of delusions without fine-grained multi-label classification of themes, affective responses, and behavioral responses. While Large Language Models (LLMs) reduce the need for training data, they are prone to false positives in clinical contexts, and effective multi-agent collaboration strategies to enhance accuracy without introducing noise remain under-explored.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a**: Novel automated, multi-agent LLM pipeline for fine-grained, multi-label extraction of language suggestive of delusional beliefs, associated affective responses, and behavioral responses from naturalistic audio diary transcripts.
- **Challenge 1**: How to reduce false positives in delusion theme classification while maintaining sensitivity to affective and behavioral interpretations? Detailed diagnostic prompt instructions successfully reduce false positives but may constrain the interpretation of affective or behavioral responses. The paper addresses this by evaluating an ensemble of three foundation models to balance these trade-offs.
- **Challenge 2**: How to handle clinically ambiguous text in multi-agent frameworks to avoid premature consensus that reduces accuracy? Complex conversational debate between agents was found to diminish accuracy on ambiguous text by inducing premature consensus. The paper demonstrates that simple majority voting establishes more robust performance compared to complex debate mechanisms.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The study developed a multi-agent LLM pipeline using an ensemble of three foundation models to process transcripts from audio diaries of individuals with moderate persecutory ideation.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - Detailed diagnostic prompt instructions effectively reduced false positives for delusion theme classification.
    - In comparing multi-agent adjudication frameworks, complex dialogue debates reduced accuracy on clinically ambiguous text.
    - Majority voting achieved the best performance, with a Micro F1 of 0.872 for delusion detection and 0.779 for delusion classification.
- **Comparison**: Compared to traditional manual review, this method offers a scalable automated solution. Compared to single-model LLM applications, the multi-agent majority voting mechanism demonstrates higher robustness and accuracy in handling clinical ambiguity.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper presents and validates a scalable multi-agent LLM pipeline for the automated detection and characterization of content suggestive of delusional beliefs in naturalistic speech. The study highlights the effectiveness of detailed prompt instructions in reducing false positives and the superiority of simple majority voting over complex agent debates in handling clinical ambiguity. This work provides a powerful technical tool for real-time monitoring of symptom fluctuations in mental illness.
