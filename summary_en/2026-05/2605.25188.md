## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Multi-agent Large Language Model (LLM) systems enhance reasoning by aggregating outputs from multiple agents. Existing approaches often rely on heavy interactions, such as debates or workflow-based collaborations.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**:
    1. **Error Propagation**: When agents exchange raw responses or reasoning traces, incorrect intermediate reasoning can be adopted and amplified, leading to confident but wrong consensus. This agreement often reflects imitation or contamination rather than independent verification.
    2. **High Communication Overhead**: Multi-round communication significantly increases token consumption, latency, and inference costs, making many multi-agent systems expensive and inefficient for large-scale deployment.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed DarkForest**: A controlled-communication coordination framework designed to improve accuracy while reducing communication overhead by limiting information flow between agents.
- **Challenge 1: How to prevent the propagation and amplification of erroneous reasoning?**
    - **Difficulty**: In traditional methods, agents directly see each other's raw outputs, making them susceptible to misleading yet plausible answers, resulting in "confident wrong consensus."
    - **Solution**: DarkForest keeps agents independent during initial answer generation. It then parses raw responses into structured candidate records and clusters semantically equivalent candidates. By estimating a calibrated belief distribution (considering agent reliability, confidence, parse quality, etc.), the coordinator receives only policy-permitted evidence, thereby isolating noise and preventing direct propagation of incorrect reasoning.
- **Challenge 2: How to retain key evidence while minimizing communication costs?**
    - **Difficulty**: Cutting off communication entirely may lead to information loss, while full communication is costly. A balance is needed to transmit only the most valuable information for final decision-making.
    - **Solution**: Through clustering and belief state estimation, the system identifies the most supported candidate clusters. The coordinator makes final decisions based solely on this calibrated and independence-corrected high-confidence evidence, achieving "less talk, higher accuracy" and significantly reducing unnecessary token exchanges.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on six different reasoning benchmarks.
- **Key Results**:
    - **Accuracy Improvement**: DarkForest achieved leading overall quality, improving upon the strongest baseline by up to 30.7% on benchmark metrics.
    - **Efficiency Improvement**: Compared to communication-heavy baselines, DarkForest reduced token consumption by up to 6.5x.
- **Comparison**: Unlike traditional debate-style methods (multi-round view exchange) or workflow systems (e.g., MetaGPT), DarkForest avoids the formation of erroneous consensus and significantly lowers computational and communication costs through independent generation and post-hoc aggregation.

## 4. Conclusion
DarkForest introduces a novel paradigm for multi-agent coordination, centered on "independent generation, structured aggregation, and controlled communication." It addresses two major pain points in existing multi-agent systems: error propagation and high communication overhead. By introducing belief distribution estimation and clustering mechanisms, the method not only significantly improves reasoning accuracy but also drastically reduces inference costs, offering a new perspective for the efficient and reliable deployment of multi-agent LLM systems.
