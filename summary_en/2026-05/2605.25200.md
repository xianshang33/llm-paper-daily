## 1. What the paper does
- **Background**: Travel planning is a realistic task for evaluating the planning and tool-use abilities of LLM agents. However, existing benchmarks typically assume only a single user, thereby avoiding one of the most challenging aspects of real-world scenarios: an agent's ability to identify and resolve conflicts among multiple users.
- **Why existing work fails**: Existing LLM travel-planning benchmarks focus on a single user. Prior work on group recommendation typically formulates the task as an optimization problem with predefined preference inputs (e.g., fixed numerical preference matrices). Consequently, they study how to optimize an itinerary when preferences are already known, rather than how an agent should interact with multiple users to discover preferences, resolve conflicts, and make decisions in a realistic conversational setting.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed GroupTravelBench**: The first benchmark for multi-user, multi-turn travel planning. Based on real user profiles, POI data, and ticket price data, it synthesizes 650 tasks divided into three difficulty levels.
- **Challenge 1: Preference Elicitation**:
  - **Description & Difficulty**: In realistic group travel planning, preferences are initially incomplete, privately held by different users, and often conflicting. An agent must proactively engage in multi-turn dialogue to gather these preferences, requiring high-level interactive understanding and questioning capabilities.
  - **Solution**: The benchmark evaluates the agent's ability to proactively engage in multi-turn dialogue to gather preferences from each user, simulating the real-world process of refining itineraries through conversation.
- **Challenge 2: Coordination and Fair Planning**:
  - **Description & Difficulty**: Agents must resolve conflicts among users through compromise or subgrouping strategies and search for travel plans that maximize overall group utility while maintaining fairness and feasibility. This is highly complex under multiple constraints (time, budget, preferences).
  - **Solution**: Beyond standard multi-step reasoning and tool use, the benchmark specifically evaluates the agent's capability to resolve conflicts and generate itinerary plans that balance group utility with fairness.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: To simulate real-world conversational itinerary planning while enabling reliable tool use and offline evaluation, the authors built an interactive sandbox environment with cached real-world tool data.
- **Evaluation Results**: A wide range of LLMs were evaluated. The results show that even frontier models still exhibit substantial weaknesses in preference coverage and group fairness.
- **Comparison**: Unlike works such as TravelPlanner which focus on single users or static constraints, GroupTravelBench highlights the deficiencies of current models in handling dynamic, multi-user interactions and complex social coordination tasks, filling a significant gap in current evaluations.

## 4. Conclusion
GroupTravelBench provides a practical and reproducible benchmark for advancing research on LLM agents for real-world travel planning. By introducing multi-user, multi-turn interaction scenarios, it emphasizes three key capabilities: elicitation, coordination, and planning. It reveals the limitations of state-of-the-art LLMs in handling group preference conflicts and fairness, pointing the way for future research.
