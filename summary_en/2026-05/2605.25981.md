## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: As LLM agents are deployed for multi-step reasoning, math, and retrieval tasks, input prompts are often perturbed by upstream models or templating systems. Practitioners need to know if agents treat surface noise (formatting, reordering) and semantic noise (paraphrasing, synonyms) equivalently or if they propagate to final answers at different rates.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Prior robustness studies focused on single-step LMs or classifiers (e.g., PromptBench, CheckList), failing to distinguish the directional gap between meaning-bearing and presentation perturbations in multi-step agent trajectories. Recent agent benchmarks measure raw success rates rather than perturbation sensitivity.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A large-scale empirical measurement study on Chain-of-Thought and ReAct agents, revealing that semantic perturbations alter final answers more frequently than surface perturbations of comparable severity, validated with held-out trace-level analysis.
- **Challenge 1: How to quantify and verify the differential impact of perturbation types on agent stability?**
  - **Difficulty**: Isolating the effect of perturbation type from severity and proving statistical significance across diverse models and datasets.
  - **Solution**: Conducted a 68-cell study across 10 LLMs and 3 benchmarks (1,530 originals, ~11,150 variants). After severity matching, semantic perturbations caused a +19.69 pp higher inconsistency rate (p<0.0001), remaining significant even when excluding specific model families.
- **Challenge 2: How to uncover the internal mechanisms driving this differential impact?**
  - **Difficulty**: Prior mechanism claims may not replicate, requiring deep inspection of intermediate reasoning steps.
  - **Solution**: Used 1,800 held-out trajectories from Qwen2.5-14B to probe mechanisms. Retracted two prior claims and proposed a "stealth-divergence" picture: semantic perturbations preserve the first action but corrupt intermediate reasoning from step 2 onward, leading to slightly deeper trajectories.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Data**: Released code, perturbation corpus, raw trajectories, and analysis scripts. Covered 10 models from 7 architectures, with held-out validation on an 11th model.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - **Main Finding**: A consistent inconsistency gap of +19.69 pp favoring semantic sensitivity (64/68 cells positive).
  - **Stress Tests**: Some cluster-bootstrap significance vanished under stricter assumptions; cross-architecture swaps broke rankings; second LLM judge agreement was moderate (κ=0.50).
  - **Held-out Validation**: Observed a small but positive effect (+1.08 pp, p=9.6×10⁻⁴) on Qwen2.5-14B.
  - **Mechanism Probes**: Semantic perturbations increased per-step similarity gaps (-5.6 to -10.5 pp) from step 2 and increased trajectory depth by 0.17 steps.
- **Comparison**: Unlike Zhu et al. (2024) which only noted accuracy degradation, this work isolates the semantic vs. surface gap. Unlike Ribeiro et al. (2020) focusing on classifiers, this targets tool-using agents. Unlike Sclar et al. (2024) highlighting formatting fragility, this explores the propagation mechanism of semantic noise.

#### Summary
This paper makes a measurement contribution by empirically demonstrating that LLM agents are more sensitive to semantic noise than surface noise. It validates this phenomenon through rigorous statistical testing and held-out samples, while revealing a "stealth-divergence" mechanism where semantic perturbations corrode intermediate reasoning after the initial step. The study retracts non-replicable prior claims and offers engineering insights for input normalization.
