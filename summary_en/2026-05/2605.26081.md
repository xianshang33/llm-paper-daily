## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Deep research agents face vast, interdependent, and uncertain information in long-horizon tasks. To overcome LLM context window limits, existing systems externalize intermediate research artifacts (e.g., outlines, evidence banks) into a persistent structured layer.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current systems (e.g., WebWeaver, OmniThink) focus on the form of the intermediate layer but leave its evolution to the LLM's implicit reasoning. Without explicit regulation, this layer is easily contaminated by mixed-quality information, propagating errors along dependencies. Consequently, these methods often rely on scaling up model size to compensate for the lack of regulation, leading to inefficiency and poor robustness.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed VeriTrace**: A cognitive-graph framework that treats the intermediate layer as the agent's "mental model" and regulates its evolution through three explicit feedback loops to continuously align task understanding with reality.
- **Challenge 1: Distraction from Noise and Passive Accumulation**. The difficulty lies in filtering valuable information from massive, mixed-quality findings to prevent the system from losing track of its research state. VeriTrace addresses this via the "Interpretive Update" loop, where new findings are actively classified (confirming, contradicting, gap-filling, or surprising) against the existing model, rather than being passively absorbed.
- **Challenge 2: Error Propagation and Model Rigidity**. The challenge is preventing error spread along dependencies and adapting the knowledge structure when it no longer fits new evidence. VeriTrace solves this through "Deviation Feedback" and "Schema Revision" loops, which detect deviations between task understanding and reality and dynamically refactor the knowledge graph schema, ensuring healthy evolution of the mental model.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: VeriTrace is implemented as a cognitive-graph architecture integrating the three regulatory loops. Experiments used matched Qwen3.5-27B backbones and were also tested with Config-DeepSeek.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - On the DeepResearch Bench (DRB), using matched Qwen3.5-27B, VeriTrace outperformed the strongest matched baseline by 4.22 percentage points (pp) on Insight and 1.49 pp on Overall metrics.
  - On DeepConsult, it achieved a 5.9 pp higher overall win rate.
  - With Config-DeepSeek, it achieved the strongest reproducible open-source result on DRB.
- **Comparison**: Unlike WebWeaver, OmniThink, and FS-Researcher, which lack explicit regulation mechanisms, VeriTrace significantly improves research quality and robustness in complex, uncertain environments through explicit feedback loops, demonstrating that explicit cognitive regulation is superior to relying solely on implicit reasoning via model scaling.

## 4. Conclusion
VeriTrace introduces a new paradigm for evolving and regulating the "mental model" of deep research agents through explicit feedback loops (Interpretive Update, Deviation Feedback, Schema Revision). This approach effectively mitigates information contamination and error propagation caused by the lack of regulation in existing systems. It achieves superior performance across multiple benchmarks, highlighting the importance of introducing explicit cognitive regulation mechanisms in deep research tasks.
