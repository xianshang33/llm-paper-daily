## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Social deduction games have become a popular testbed for probing reasoning, deception, coordination, and belief modeling in Large Language Model (LLM) and Vision-Language Model (VLM) agents. These environments combine hidden information, adversarial incentives, and long-horizon interactions.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current evaluations primarily rely on game outcomes (e.g., win rates), which fail to reveal the underlying reasons for success or failure. Furthermore, most environments are text-only, lacking recoverable visual observations and ground-truth trajectories, making it difficult to determine if an agent's dialogue is grounded in its actual perceptions and actions.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed QUACK**: An open-source multimodal social deduction environment and evaluation framework designed to audit the grounding of agent language in social reasoning.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of fine-grained behavioral attribution and consistency checks**. The difficulty lies in traditional metrics' inability to capture subtle error modes in long-horizon interactions. QUACK addresses this via a three-level evaluation system (outcomes, trajectories, utterance consistency), featuring a core "Statement Verification Pipeline" that reconstructs ground-truth trajectories to verify every claim.
- **Challenge 2: Disconnection between multimodal perception and language output (hallucinations/inconsistencies)**. It is challenging to automatically detect if agents lie or hallucinate based on visual inputs. QUACK automatically flags spatial hallucinations, unsupported accusations, deception collapse, and language-action inconsistencies to quantify agent reliability in adversarial settings.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The authors released the full engine, evaluation framework, toolkit, and logs. The framework supports both homogeneous and cross-model adversarial settings.
- **Results**: Evaluating three frontier VLMs revealed that even the strongest agents hallucinate 15.1% of their verifiable spatial claims and make over half of their accusations without grounded evidence.
- **Comparison**: Unlike prior works focusing solely on win rates or text-only interactions, QUACK provides automated auditing of multimodal grounding, exposing significant unreliability in state-of-the-art models and filling the gap between outcome-based and process-oriented evaluation.

## 4. Conclusion
QUACK addresses the lack of ground truth and fine-grained attribution in social deduction agent evaluation by introducing a multimodal environment and a statement verification pipeline. The study demonstrates that current state-of-the-art VLMs suffer from significant deficiencies in maintaining consistency between language and perception, exhibiting frequent spatial hallucinations and unsupported accusations. This work provides essential benchmarks and tools for developing more reliable and interpretable multimodal social agents.
