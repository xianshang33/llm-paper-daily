## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) have advanced significantly as interactive agents capable of reasoning, planning, and tool use, achieving strong performance on existing benchmarks. However, these agents often suffer notable performance degradation when deployed in real-world settings characterized by inherent stochasticity and imperfections.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current agent training paradigms rely on idealized assumptions, utilizing carefully curated instructions and stable, well-controlled environments. This creates a fundamental mismatch with real-world interaction dynamics, where user inputs are ambiguous and variable, and tool executions are prone to failures and anomalies, limiting agent robustness in practical applications.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed NoisyAgent**: An agentic training framework that explicitly incorporates environmental imperfections into the learning process to bridge the gap between training and deployment.
- **Challenge 1: User Noise**: Real-world user interactions involve ambiguity and variability, whereas traditional training data is overly clean. NoisyAgent addresses this by modifying user interaction patterns during training to introduce structured perturbations, forcing the agent to learn to extract intent from imperfect instructions.
- **Challenge 2: Tool Noise**: External tools often return noisy, incomplete, or failed outputs, contrary to the assumption of perfect tool execution in standard training. NoisyAgent simulates these tool execution failures and anomalies. It stabilizes training by applying noise to only a subset of rollouts and progressively increasing difficulty as the model adapts, enabling the agent to handle tool irregularities effectively.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: NoisyAgent employs hybrid rollouts combining clean and noisy trajectories. An adaptive scheduler increases noise difficulty based on the performance gap, and group-wise normalization is used to stabilize policy optimization under heterogeneous interaction conditions.
- **Evaluation Results**: Extensive experiments demonstrate that NoisyAgent consistently improves agent robustness in noisy and dynamic environments. Analysis reveals that training under noise conditions also yields performance gains on idealized benchmarks, suggesting that controlled exposure to noise promotes more generalizable reasoning and decision-making.
- **Comparison**: Unlike existing paradigms that depend on idealized environments, NoisyAgent explicitly models interaction imperfections. It significantly outperforms baselines trained without noise in terms of stability and robustness in real-world scenarios, effectively bridging the training-deployment gap.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper identifies the fundamental mismatch between idealized training conditions and real-world deployment for LLM agents. It proposes NoisyAgent, which enhances robustness by introducing user and tool noise through a progressive curriculum learning strategy. The findings highlight that modeling interaction imperfections is crucial for practical agent deployment and that noise training can enhance generalizable reasoning capabilities.
