## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: LLM-powered social agents are increasingly used to simulate online social behavior, but validating their realism remains difficult. Existing work largely relies on general-purpose benchmarks or assessments of plausibility and coherence, paying little attention to short, reactive discourse like audience replies to online news.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current evaluations often lack direct comparison with large-scale empirical audience data. Consequently, it is unclear whether synthetic reactions preserve measurable properties of real human behavior, such as hate speech prevalence, sentiment distribution, and semantic diversity, potentially leading to distorted perceptions of public opinion.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A framework for evaluating the realism of LLM-powered social agents using the Hatemedia dataset (Spanish online news). It compares 58,555 real human reactions with synthetic responses generated by five LLMs across three dimensions: hate speech, sentiment, and semantic alignment.
- **Challenge 1: Off-the-shelf models fail to reproduce the distributional properties of real discourse.**
  - **Difficulty**: Off-the-shelf LLMs tend to generate "safe" or neutral text, severely underproducing hate speech and introducing model-specific sentiment biases, resulting in a significant distributional distance from human replies.
  - **Solution**: The study systematically demonstrates that off-the-shelf models are poor proxies for real audience reactions, highlighting the necessity for distributional calibration rather than relying on default generation.
- **Challenge 2: Fine-tuning improves fidelity unevenly, creating trade-offs.**
  - **Difficulty**: While fine-tuning enhances realism, improvements are not uniform across all metrics. Optimizing for one aspect (e.g., semantic alignment) may lead to overshooting in another (e.g., hate speech prevalence).
  - **Solution**: The analysis reveals that Qwen3 offers the most balanced approximation, whereas Mistral7B achieves strong sentiment and semantic alignment but overestimates hate speech. This indicates that model selection must be aligned with specific simulation goals.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Data**: Utilized the Hatemedia dataset pairing 5,631 news items with 58,555 real reactions. Five LLMs were used to generate matched synthetic datasets under a shared experimental setting. Evaluation focused on hate speech detection, sentiment analysis, and semantic alignment.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - **Off-the-shelf Models**: Performed poorly as proxies, significantly underrepresenting hate speech and exhibiting distinct sentiment biases.
  - **Fine-tuned Models**: Showed improved but uneven fidelity. Qwen3 provided the most balanced performance, while Mistral7B excelled in sentiment and semantic alignment but overshot hate speech prevalence.
- **Comparison**: Unlike prior work focusing on textual plausibility, this study proves that "plausible" synthetic replies do not necessarily replicate the statistical distribution of public discourse, especially regarding harmful or negative content.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper concludes that off-the-shelf LLMs are insufficient for realistically simulating online news reactions due to their tendency to sanitize content and introduce biases. While fine-tuning can mitigate these issues, it introduces trade-offs between different dimensions of realism. Researchers must carefully validate the distributional properties of LLM-generated social agents to avoid distorting simulations of public opinion.
