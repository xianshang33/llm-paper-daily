## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) show great potential for mobile agents to autonomously execute human instructions. However, fully automated agents often suffer from "over-execution," attempting tasks even when they cannot resolve them.
- **Why Previous Works Failed**: Prior studies addressed this by training interactive agents to request human help when stuck. However, these agents tend to exhibit "over-soliciting" behavior, relying excessively on human intervention, which disrupts user experience and reduces efficiency.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: Mobile-Aptus, a universal confidence integration framework that enables confidence-driven proactive and robust interaction in MLLM-based mobile agents.
- **Challenge 1: How to empower agents with accurate self-assessment capabilities?**
  - **Difficulty**: Agents need to not only perform actions but also accurately judge their ability to complete tasks; standard fine-tuning struggles to produce reliable confidence scores.
  - **Solution**: In the "Interaction Capability Empowerment" stage, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) is used to teach agents to output both actions and initial confidence scores, establishing a baseline self-assessment mechanism.
- **Challenge 2: How to correct confidence bias to avoid misjudgment?**
  - **Difficulty**: Initial confidence scores may be biased, leading to unnecessary help requests or failure to ask when needed, making it hard to balance autonomy and accuracy.
  - **Solution**: In the "Confidence Bias Correction" stage, semantic similarity retrieval is combined with Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to refine confidence scores, enabling precise decisions on when to invoke human assistance.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on four popular mobile agent benchmarks: OS-Kairos, AITZ, Meta-GUI, and AndroidControl, including both offline benchmarks and real-world dynamic experiments.
- **Results vs. Baselines**:
  - **Offline Benchmarks**: Mobile-Aptus consistently outperforms all baselines, achieving an average improvement of over 17% in task success rate.
  - **Real-World Dynamic Experiments**: Surpasses baselines by 26% in task success rate while requiring only 0.64 intervention steps per instruction, significantly reducing user burden.
  - **Additional Finding**: Salient human interventions can be effectively replaced by a multi-agent system to avoid task interruption.

## 4. Conclusion
Mobile-Aptus successfully addresses the dual problems of over-execution and over-soliciting in mobile agents through a two-stage confidence integration framework. The method significantly improves task success rates while minimizing unnecessary human interventions, achieving state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmarks and enabling more efficient and robust human-agent collaboration.
