## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: As Large Language Models (LLMs) evolve into autonomous search agents, scores on existing benchmarks like BrowseComp are rising. However, it remains unclear whether these high scores reflect genuine evidence discovery or merely verification of parametric knowledge.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current static search benchmarks suffer from "Intrinsic Knowledge Dependence" (IKD). As models iterate, benchmark knowledge is absorbed into parameters, causing the effective difficulty to decay over time. This conflates what agents already know with what they can find, rewarding memory-backed verification rather than evidence-driven discovery.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed LiveBrowseComp**: A new deep-search benchmark designed to evaluate agents beyond their intrinsic knowledge coverage, forcing reliance on external real-time evidence.
- **Challenge 1: How to quantify and reveal "Intrinsic Knowledge Dependence" in existing benchmarks?**
  - **Difficulty**: It is hard to distinguish whether agents use tools for exploration or just to verify internal hypotheses, as both may yield correct answers.
  - **Solution**: Designed three diagnostic experiments: closed-book testing without tools, search testing with supporting evidence removed, and analysis of query sources. Results showed agents answer up to 44.5% of questions without tools and perform worse than closed-book baselines when supporting evidence is blocked, proving widespread IKD.
- **Challenge 2: How to construct a benchmark uncontaminated by pre-trained knowledge?**
  - **Difficulty**: Ensuring answers depend on very recent facts while excluding globally salient events that models might know via pre-training or short-term memory.
  - **Solution**: Constructed LiveBrowseComp with 335 human-authored questions based on facts published within 90 days prior to construction, sourced from six updated feeds and filtered for non-salience. On this benchmark, all agents achieved <2% closed-book accuracy, effectively isolating intrinsic knowledge.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The LiveBrowseComp dataset is publicly available on Hugging Face. Construction involved scraping from six continuously updated sources, manual question authoring, and strict filtering for recency and non-salience.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - All evaluated agents achieved less than 2% closed-book accuracy on LiveBrowseComp.
  - Search-augmented scores dropped by 25-40 points compared to BrowseComp.
  - Prior model rankings on BrowseComp did not reliably predict performance on LiveBrowseComp, indicating a reshuffling of performance evaluation.
- **Comparison**: Unlike static benchmarks like BrowseComp, LiveBrowseComp eliminates score inflation caused by parametric memory, providing a more authentic measure of real-time information retrieval and integration capabilities.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper exposes a critical flaw in current LLM search agent evaluation: static benchmarks often test memory rather than search capability. By introducing LiveBrowseComp, the authors provide a deep-search benchmark based on real-time, non-salient facts that forces agents to rely on external evidence. This benchmark not only highlights the deficiencies of current models in genuine search tasks but also offers a more reliable standard for developing robust, evidence-driven search agents.
