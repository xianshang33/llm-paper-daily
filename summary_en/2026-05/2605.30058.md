## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: While LLM agents have excelled in task-oriented abilities like planning and reasoning, user expectations in domains such as entertainment and companionship are shifting towards persistent, personalized, and psychologically consistent interactions. However, few works treat AI agents as complete human personalities where emotional dimensions are equally important.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current benchmarks primarily focus on conversational context, explicit reasoning, memory recall, and QA accuracy. These frameworks assess cognitive capabilities but lack standardized methods to evaluate human-like psychological characteristics, such as emotional consistency, value orientations, and whether behavioral decisions align with specific psychological profiles in complex situations.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed HEART-Bench**: A novel benchmark designed to systematically assess whether LLM agents can simulate coherent, human-like psychology.
- **Challenge 1: How to construct virtual characters with deep psychological backgrounds and long-term consistency?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional character settings often lack theoretical grounding and have fragmented memories, failing to simulate human psychological development across life stages.
  - **Solution**: Constructed 11 diverse human characters grounded in orthogonal Big Five personality traits. Each profile is deeply integrated with 1,000 structured autobiographical episodic memories distributed across theory-grounded developmental life stages.
- **Challenge 2: How to scientifically evaluate psychological manifestations and decision-making consistency in complex scenarios?**
  - **Difficulty**: There is a lack of unified psychological frameworks to quantify how different situations affect individual psychology, making it hard to judge if an agent's reactions match its established persona.
  - **Solution**: Guided by the DIAMONDS taxonomy (characterizing situations along eight dimensions: Duty, Intellect, Adversity, Mating, pOsitivity, Negativity, Deception, and Sociality), the authors designed 64 curated decision-making scenarios. The benchmark evaluates if agents can consolidate innate traits and memories to make behaviorally consistent decisions.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Data**: After systematic human validation and filtering, the final benchmark consists of 673 multiple-choice questions (MCQs). The dataset features expert annotations to ensure high-quality evaluation.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Compared to benchmarks like CharacterEval, PersonaHub, LoCoMo, CloneMem, PersonaLens, and PersonaMem, HEART-Bench is the only one that satisfies all six key dimensions: Big-Five grounded, lifespan coverage, episodic memory, scenario-based evaluation, expert annotation, and trait-driven decision making. Other benchmarks typically lack depth in personality theory, memory integration, or consistent decision evaluation.
- **Evaluation Results**: The benchmark provides a principled and scalable testbed for studying human-like emotions, personality consistency, and value-consistent behavioral decision-making in LLM-based agents.

## 4. Conclusion
HEART-Bench fills a critical gap in LLM evaluation regarding human-like psychology. By combining Big Five personality traits, autobiographical memory, and the DIAMONDS situational taxonomy, it goes beyond testing memory or reasoning to deeply assess whether agents possess coherent "psychology" and "personality." This serves as a vital tool for advancing higher-level Artificial Emotional Intelligence (EI) and achieving true human-like long-term identity coherence.
