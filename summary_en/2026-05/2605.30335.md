## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Multi-component LLM agents assemble probabilistic claims by routing parts of a joint problem to specialist sub-agents. Even if each sub-agent is locally calibrated and coherent, the aggregated global belief can violate basic probability axioms (e.g., probabilities summing to >1), leading to Dutch-book exposures.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current evaluation metrics (accuracy, calibration) and mitigation techniques (self-consistency, conformal prediction) operate on individual model outputs and ignore cross-component logical constraints. Intuitive LLM-side mitigations like retrieval, partition-aware prompting, and aggregator LLMs are shown to fail or regress in addressing this compositional incoherence.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a** formalization of "locally coherent, globally incoherent" failure via the **compositional residual $\epsilon^*$**, defined as the $L_2$ distance from the composed quote to the joint coherent polytope. This serves as a runtime, distribution-free certificate of system-level coherence.
- **Challenge 1: How to quantify and predict compositional incoherence?**
  - **Difficulty**: Detecting system-level probability violations at runtime without accessing underlying data distributions, and characterizing when local coherence implies global coherence.
  - **Solution**: The paper uses a product-structure dichotomy to characterize sufficiency conditions and employs a Rayleigh-quotient prediction that matches observed residuals within 7% for three out of four relation classes.
- **Challenge 2: How to repair incoherence deterministically while maintaining specialist routing?**
  - **Difficulty**: Repair must be deterministic and preserve the modular architecture, while providing sequential monitoring guarantees.
  - **Solution**: Introduces a hierarchical Boyle-Dykstra projection to deterministically repair the composition and pairs it with an anytime-valid e-process for sequential coherence monitoring.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Setup**: Evaluated on 1,876 ensemble cliques using a panel of four mid-tier LLMs.
- **Key Results**:
  - **Prevalence**: $\epsilon^* > 0$ in 33–94% of cliques, demonstrating that local-to-global incoherence is widespread.
  - **Impact**: This incoherence translates to +0.115 nats per bet of regret on 1,770 resolved bets under proportional allocation.
  - **Mitigation Gain**: The regret collapses to +0.006 when bettors themselves coherentize, validating the effectiveness of the proposed repair.
- **Comparison**: Unlike intuitive LLM-side mitigations (retrieval, prompting, aggregators) which failed or regressed, the proposed mathematical projection method provides deterministic guarantees and significantly reduces regret.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper identifies a critical failure mode in multi-component LLM agents where locally coherent sub-agents produce globally incoherent probabilistic assessments. By introducing the compositional residual $\epsilon^*$ and a hierarchical Boyle-Dykstra projection repair, the authors provide both a detection mechanism and a deterministic solution. Experiments confirm that this incoherence is common and costly, and that traditional prompt-engineering approaches are insufficient, necessitating structural, mathematically grounded repairs.
