## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Scientific research is traditionally human-intensive, requiring coordination of literature, ideas, experiments, manuscripts, and review responses over long cycles. LLM-based scientific agents offer an opportunity to automate this process.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current systems either partially or fail to meet the requirements of supporting the full research lifecycle, maintaining structured persistent memory across projects, and improving research procedures over time. There is a gap for a unified automated scientific research system.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed AutoSci**: A memory-centric agentic system for the full scientific research lifecycle.
- **Challenge 1: How to maintain structured persistent memory across projects?**
  - **Difficulty**: Existing systems typically retain state only within a single run, failing to reuse knowledge or artifacts across different research projects.
  - **Solution**: Introduces the SciMem module, which provides schema-governed research memory, separating reusable scientific knowledge (Long-Term Knowledge Memory) from project-level artifacts like ideas and experiments (Active Research Memory).
- **Challenge 2: How to achieve self-evolution and execute complex skills?**
  - **Difficulty**: Static workflows struggle with complex scientific tasks, and systems lack the ability to learn from feedback to optimize their own processes.
  - **Solution**: Combines SciDAG and SciEvolve modules. SciDAG augments difficult skills using DAG-shaped multi-agent operators and reusable templates. SciEvolve converts feedback from users, experiments, and reviews into versioned updates to SciMem, SciFlow skills, and SciDAG templates, enabling system evolution.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **System Architecture**: AutoSci consists of four core modules:
  1. **SciMem**: Manages structured persistent memory.
  2. **SciFlow**: Executes a five-stage lifecycle (literature understanding to rebuttal) via a harness controlling state, context, verification, feedback, and orchestration.
  3. **SciDAG**: Enhances complex skill execution.
  4. **SciEvolve**: Handles system evolution based on feedback.

## 4. Conclusion
AutoSci is an innovative memory-centric agentic system that addresses the limitations of persistence and adaptability in existing scientific agents by decoupling long-term knowledge from active research memory and integrating dynamic workflow orchestration with self-evolution mechanisms. It not only executes the full research lifecycle but also accumulates knowledge and optimizes its capabilities across multiple projects, providing a unified and sustainable environment for automated scientific research.
