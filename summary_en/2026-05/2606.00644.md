## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: AI research often requires decisions before future evidence exists, such as selecting bottlenecks to attack, pursuing specific directions, or positioning projects. As autonomous research agents are increasingly deployed for ideation and planning, evaluating their ability to make defensible, evidence-grounded forward-looking judgments is a central open question.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Prior benchmarks mostly evaluate answering questions over papers, literature synthesis, tool use, workflow execution, or generating paper components. None of these tasks assess whether an agent can produce open-ended research decisions (e.g., picking a bottleneck, ranking agendas) using only evidence available at a specific historical moment.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ForeSci**: A temporally controlled benchmark for evaluating forward-looking AI research judgment. It contains 500 tasks across four fast-moving AI domains and four decision families.
- **Challenge 1: Enforceable Evidence Boundary**. The difficulty lies in preventing the system from accessing post-cutoff papers or training data, which would lead to relying on hindsight rather than foresight.
  - **Solution**: ForeSci pairs each task with a cutoff-aligned offline knowledge base; post-cutoff evidence is hidden during generation and used only for validation. Answer-generation backbones are selected to precede the task cutoffs.
- **Challenge 2: Historical Inferability of Tasks**. Tasks must be grounded in signals available before the cutoff, not arbitrary future events, to ensure fairness.
  - **Solution**: Tasks are derived from pre-cutoff taxonomy branches, node-level evidence records, and method-evolution signals, ensuring decisions are historically inferable.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated native LLMs, Hybrid RAG, and three research-agent adaptations across four backbones.
- **Key Results**:
  - Explicit evidence organization improves traceability and factual support, but gains depend strongly on the decision family.
  - Diagnostics reveal a recurring "evidence-decision decoupling": agents may cite relevant evidence while forecasting the wrong research object.
- **Comparison & Significance**: Unlike prior works focusing on literature review or workflow execution, ForeSci turns forward-looking AI research judgment into a controlled benchmark for evaluating research agents specifically as decision-making systems.

## 4. Conclusion
ForeSci addresses the gap in evaluating LLM agents' ability to make forward-looking research decisions without access to future evidence. Through strict temporal control and historically inferable task design, it exposes the decoupling between evidence utilization and final decision-making in current agents, providing a crucial tool and insights for developing more reliable autonomous research agents.
