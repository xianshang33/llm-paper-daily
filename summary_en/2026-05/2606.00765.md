## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: LLM-based agents solve complex tasks through long trajectories involving reasoning, tool use, and communication. However, when failures occur, it is often unclear which agent caused the failure and which step introduced the decisive error.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current methods are insufficient. Single-pass LLM judges treat trajectories as flat inputs, suffering from positional bias and order sensitivity. Learning-based tracers evaluate steps independently, ignoring error propagation. Since errors propagate, later actions may appear incorrect only due to earlier corrupted states, making independent step-level classification ineffective.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed FALAT**: A diagnostic framework for failure attribution in LLM agent trajectories, framing attribution as a dependency-guided search problem.
- **Challenge 1: Obscured Error Roots due to Propagation**. The difficulty lies in distinguishing steps that introduce errors from those that merely inherit them, as early mistakes corrupt downstream states. FALAT addresses this by constructing an expectation of the correct solution to identify suspicious regions and tracing dependencies among decisions and outputs to isolate the true error source.
- **Challenge 2: Validating Attribution**. The challenge is confirming that the identified step is indeed the decisive cause. FALAT evaluates whether correcting a candidate step is sufficient to recover the expected outcome, thereby identifying both the responsible agent and the decisive failure step.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Benchmark**: Evaluated on the Who&When benchmark, comprising both algorithm-generated and hand-crafted multi-agent failure trajectories.
- **Results**: FALAT consistently improves attribution accuracy. Its best configurations achieve 46.0% step-level accuracy on algorithm-generated trajectories and 29.1% on hand-crafted ones.
- **Comparison**: FALAT outperforms specialized attribution baselines and direct prompting with standalone LLMs, demonstrating that dependency-aware reasoning is crucial for reliable diagnosis.

## 4. Conclusion
FALAT effectively addresses the challenge of failure attribution in long LLM agent trajectories by using dependency-guided search. It accurately locates decisive error steps and responsible agents through counterfactual validation. Experiments show it significantly outperforms existing baselines, offering a robust tool for enhancing the debuggability and reliability of agentic systems.
