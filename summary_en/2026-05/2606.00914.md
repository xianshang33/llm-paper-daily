## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: LLM agents increasingly make decisions after consuming ranked external information streams (e.g., social feeds, search results) selected by upstream rankers. However, safety evaluations typically test the model or user prompt in isolation, ignoring the upstream ranker that determines what the agent reads before acting.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Prior research on adversarial inputs focuses on individual message content, such as direct prompt injection, jailbreaking, or retrieval poisoning. These threats involve identifiable malicious payloads that can theoretically be caught by content filters. They fail to address scenarios where every individual item is benign, and manipulation occurs solely through the selection and proportion of items shown (curation), nor do they examine if such curation can steer multi-step agent decisions rather than just flipping single-turn outputs.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a** controlled protocol to isolate and quantify the causal effect of feed curation on LLM agent downstream decisions. This protocol holds the model, persona, topic, and final decision prompt fixed, varying only the composition and ordering of posts encountered during a preceding ten-turn "scrolling" phase.
- **Challenge 1: How to isolate the causal effect of feed curation?**
  - **Difficulty**: An agent's output is a function of model weights, user prompts, and information trajectories, making it hard to distinguish between inherent model preferences and changes driven by external information.
  - **Solution**: By keeping all variables constant except for the feed composition and order, any shift in decision is attributable solely to feed curation, allowing precise quantification of its impact.
- **Challenge 2: How to characterize response patterns of different models to adversarial feeds?**
  - **Difficulty**: Different models may exhibit vastly different robustness or sensitivity to the same feed manipulation, lacking a unified classification framework.
  - **Solution**: Through 2,785 decision rollouts across four modern open-instruct LLMs, the study identifies three response regimes: adversarial capitulation, default saturation, and default-direction asymmetry. The latter shows that one-sided feeds can tip decisions where the model is genuinely uncertain but cannot dislodge decisions it already favors or holds firmly.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Setup**: Experiments were conducted on four modern open-instruct LLMs from three independent labs. Decision domains included security-relevant choices, such as removing deployment approval gates or relaxing access controls.
- **Key Results**:
  1. **Dose-Response Curve**: The effect of feed manipulation follows a clear dose-response curve.
  2. **Ruling Out Artifacts**: A generator swap experiment confirmed that the effects are not artifacts of writing style.
  3. **Default-Direction Asymmetry**: In cases of genuine uncertainty, one-sided feeds shifted decision probabilities from 5% to 100% (Fisher p as low as 3 x 10^-10), but failed to change decisions the model already favored or held firmly.
  4. **Mitigation**: Two simple feed-level defenses partially mitigated the effect; frontier models retained their defaults more effectively.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Unlike traditional adversarial attacks focusing on malicious payload content, this paper demonstrates that controlling the ranking and combination of benign information (i.e., the recommender as a control surface) can effectively steer agent decisions. This highlights a blind spot in current safety evaluations, arguing for auditing the feed layer rather than just the final prompt.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper reveals a novel security threat to LLM agents: upstream recommenders/rankers can serve as practical control surfaces to steer agent decisions by curating benign information flows. The study argues that agent evaluations must extend beyond isolated tests of final prompts to audit the feed layer. While frontier models show some robustness, most models are susceptible to feed manipulation when uncertain, providing crucial empirical evidence and directions for building safer agent systems.
