## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large language model (LLM) agents increasingly rely on reusable external skills to solve long-horizon interactive tasks. To enable continual improvement without retraining the base model, many systems employ training-free skill adaptation methods.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current training-free adaptation pipelines typically update skills based on full trajectories or session-level feedback. This coarse-grained attribution causes failure signals to diffuse across unrelated steps and skills, leading to unstable or overly broad revisions, especially in long-horizon tasks where a single early error can invalidate many subsequent correct actions.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SkillAdaptor**: A training-free, step-level skill adaptation framework with explicit failure attribution, designed as a plug-in for OpenClaw-class agent harnesses.
- **Challenge 1: Credit Assignment in Long-Horizon Tasks**. In long trajectories, final failures are often caused by few early errors, but outcome-based adaptation blames the entire trajectory, leading to misdirected and unstable revisions. **Solution**: SkillAdaptor localizes the "first actionable fault step," links responsibility strictly to candidate skills, and applies targeted updates only to the deficient skills while leaving others unchanged.
- **Challenge 2: Stability and Safety of Skill Updates**. Automatically generated skill revisions may introduce noise or over-modify effective logic. **Solution**: The framework incorporates explicit acceptance checks to ensure that only verified, high-quality skill updates are applied while keeping the backbone model frozen, enabling more stable and auditable skill maintenance.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: SkillAdaptor operates as a modular post-execution component comprising a Localizer, Generator, and Qualifier. It identifies responsible steps in failed trajectories, diagnoses skill deficiencies, generates new skills, and injects them after passing acceptance checks. It is compatible with models like Kimi-K2.5, GLM-5, and GPT-5.2.
- **Evaluation Results**: Evaluated on WebShop, PinchBench, and Claw-Eval benchmarks. SkillAdaptor consistently outperforms both no-skill baselines and existing skill-adaptation baselines across all suites.
- **Comparison Advantages**: Key improvements include +1.5 points on PinchBench Avg Score%, +1.8 on Claw-Eval Avg Score, and +1.7 on WebShop success rate. These results demonstrate that step-level attribution supports more effective agent improvement than trajectory-level reflection.

## 4. Conclusion
SkillAdaptor addresses the problem of coarse failure attribution in long-horizon LLM agent tasks by shifting adaptation from trajectory-level reflection to step-level attribution. By precisely locating the first fault step and targeting specific skill updates, the method significantly outperforms existing training-free adaptation approaches on multiple benchmarks, highlighting the importance of fine-grained failure attribution for building stable and auditable LLM agent skill systems.
