## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Tool-using multi-agent LLM systems consume significant computation (tokens, tool calls, retries, code execution) before producing an answer. When a run fails, final-answer evaluation reveals the endpoint but not where the trajectory stopped making recoverable progress.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current methods focus on final accuracy, lacking granular diagnosis of intermediate processes. They cannot distinguish specific failure mechanisms (e.g., insufficient evidence, loops, tool failures) or quantify wasted computation on invalid paths, hindering targeted optimization.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a**: "Failure-Aware Observability Framework" for diagnosing wasted computation in multi-agent LLM traces.
- **Challenge 1: How to precisely define and classify failure modes in complex multi-agent systems?**
  - **Difficulty**: Interactions are complex, and binary success/failure labels fail to reflect internal states.
  - **Solution**: The paper establishes a detailed failure taxonomy distinguishing execution failure, orchestration loops, evidence failure, no-final termination, and cost waste, mapping these to specific online trace signals.
- **Challenge 2: How to detect wasted computation and locate root causes at low cost during runtime?**
  - **Difficulty**: Deep semantic analysis is expensive, while simple logs lack semantic context for real-time dead-end detection.
  - **Solution**: The framework maps recurring failure modes to online signals (tool reliability, recovery, loops, evidence availability, information change, budget pressure), combining cheap online signals with offline deep semantic metrics to capture complementary failure layers.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Instantiated in a three-agent QA system (orchestrator, search, execution). Evaluated on 165 GAIA validation traces under identical execution caps.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - Operational failures remain common: 22/53 (L1), 33/86 (L2), and 12/26 (L3) runs failed.
  - Exposed mechanisms: insufficient evidence, repeated-action loops, max-step termination, tool-failure streaks, and successful but useless executions.
  - Mean token usage rose from 8,152 (L1) to 16,389 (L3), with diverging evidence availability and sentence-level support.
- **Comparison**: A cached LLM-judge audit showed that cheap online signals and deeper semantic metrics capture complementary failure layers. This positions the framework as a superior diagnostic layer between raw logs and final accuracy, offering insights unavailable to traditional evaluation.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper highlights the necessity of failure-aware observability in multi-agent LLM systems. By mapping failure modes to online signals, the framework diagnoses "wasted computation" overlooked by traditional metrics. Experiments demonstrate its ability to identify issues like missing evidence and loops, providing a new diagnostic perspective for optimizing efficiency and reliability, bridging the gap between low-level logs and high-level accuracy.
