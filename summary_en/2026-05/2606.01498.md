## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Time series data drives critical decisions in high-stakes domains like finance and healthcare. While LLM agents can analyze data via natural language and tools, their ability to conduct reliable time series analysis across multi-turn conversations remains unclear.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current benchmarks focus on single-step tasks (e.g., forecasting, anomaly detection), ignoring practical workflows where user goals evolve, agents must build on prior analyses, and conclusions emerge from accumulated evidence. They fail to evaluate an agent's ability to maintain dialog state, chain dependent computations, or adapt when assumptions fail.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed TimeSage-MT**: A multi-turn benchmark for agentic time series reasoning, featuring 240 tasks and 2,680 dialogue turns across 8 real-world domains, ranging from basic exploration to decision-oriented analysis.
- **Challenge 1: Multi-capability Reasoning and Context Memory**. Time series reasoning requires diverse skills (data reading, method selection, configuration, memory, chaining analyses). The difficulty lies in maintaining state and leveraging prior results over long interactions. The paper addresses this by creating a reproducible pipeline for multi-turn conversations with verifiable answers and introducing TimeSage, a structured agent with a comprehensive skill library, to systematically evaluate this chained reasoning.
- **Challenge 2: Decision-oriented Uncertainty and Domain Grounding**. Agents must quantify uncertainty and make actionable decisions respecting domain constraints, not just output numbers. The challenge is LLMs' lack of deep domain understanding and uncertainty calibration. The paper exposes these gaps by designing tasks of varying depth, revealing failures in memory, uncertainty handling, and domain-based decision making through a unified evaluation protocol.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: TimeSage-MT is built via a reproducible pipeline converting real-world time series data into multi-turn conversations with verifiable answers, offering a unified evaluation protocol and public leaderboard.
- **Evaluation Results**: Frontier LLMs and the novel structured agent TimeSage were evaluated. Results showed sharp performance drops on decision-oriented tasks.
- **Comparison**: Unlike existing single-step benchmarks, TimeSage-MT highlights critical deficits in current agentic reasoning, specifically driven by failures in memory, uncertainty handling, and domain-specific decision making. It effectively distinguishes reliable reasoning from shallow pattern matching or hallucinations.

## 4. Conclusion
TimeSage-MT fills a critical gap in evaluating time series agents by simulating realistic, multi-turn, evolving analyst workflows. The study demonstrates that state-of-the-art models struggle significantly with tasks requiring long-term memory, uncertainty calibration, and domain knowledge integration. This benchmark provides a rigorous foundation and clear direction for developing more reliable time series analysis agents.
