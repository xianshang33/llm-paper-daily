## 1. What the paper does
- **Background**: Equipping language agents with world models allows them to anticipate environment dynamics and evaluate actions before execution. However, existing textual world models are typically fixed after training, failing to adapt to the on-policy state-action distributions induced by an evolving agent. Meanwhile, agent improvement methods often rely on external rewards or verifiers, limiting applicability in realistic interactive environments.
- **Why existing work fails**: Fixed world models suffer from accuracy degradation when facing distribution shifts caused by evolving agent behaviors. Policy optimization methods relying on external signals lack generality and adaptability. Optimizing either component in isolation creates a bottleneck: unreliable predictions constrain policy improvement, while suboptimal policies limit data diversity for world model adaptation.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed COMAP**: A novel framework that co-evolves textual world models and agent policies through closed-loop interaction.
- **Challenge 1: Model adaptability under non-stationary distributions**. As the agent's policy evolves, the resulting state-action distribution changes, making it difficult for a fixed world model to accurately predict future states in the new distribution. **Solution**: The world model is continuously updated via self-distillation using on-policy trajectories generated by the agent, allowing it to dynamically match the agent's evolving interaction distribution.
- **Challenge 2: Self-optimization of policy without external rewards**. Without external rewards or verifiers, agents struggle to judge action quality and improve themselves. **Solution**: At each decision step, the agent performs "future-aware reflection" by estimating the reliability of future state feedback predicted by the world model, using internal predictions as a signal to refine actions.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on benchmarks for embodied task planning, Web navigation, and tool use.
- **Main Results**: COMAP consistently outperforms competitive baselines. For instance, it achieved a +16.75% relative improvement with Qwen3-4B.
- **Comparative Analysis**: Compared to fixed world models or methods relying on external rewards, COMAP demonstrated superior long-horizon decision-making capabilities. Further analysis showed that the co-evolutionary loop improved the world model's prediction accuracy over time, leading to more effective long-term decisions.

## 4. Conclusion
COMAP addresses the limitations of fixed models and reliance on external signals by treating world modeling and policy learning as a coupled co-evolutionary process. Through closed-loop interaction and self-distillation, it achieves mutual enhancement of the world model and agent policy, significantly improving the performance of LLM agents in complex interactive environments.
