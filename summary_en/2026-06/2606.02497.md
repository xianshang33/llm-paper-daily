## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Time series forecasting has advanced rapidly with foundation models showing strong zero-shot performance in numerical extrapolation. However, in real-world scenarios, statistically plausible baselines are rarely the final forecasts used for decision-making. Before deployment, forecasts often require revision based on weakly structured business contexts such as holiday effects, campaign plans, external events, historical analogs, and expert feedback.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current research primarily focuses on improving the predictor itself or expanding input spaces via multimodal data. While recent LLM-based systems support retrieval and refinement, they typically address how forecasts are generated rather than how to contextually revise, justify, and track an already-generated baseline under operational constraints. Traditional judgmental adjustments are often manual processes lacking executability and auditability, failing to meet the need for controllable and traceable revision processes.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: The concept of "Last-Mile Forecasting" and an LLM-agent framework that sits atop a forecasting backbone to transform statistical baselines into decision-ready forecasts.
- **Challenge 1: How to translate heterogeneous, unstructured business context into specific forecast revision actions?**
  - **Difficulty**: There is a semantic gap between textual/business context and numerical forecasts. Directly asking LLMs to output free-form replacement forecasts leads to uncontrollable and unauditable processes.
  - **Solution**: The system maintains a unified forecast workspace, invokes tools to retrieve contextual evidence, and converts reasoning trajectories into explicit forecast revision actions under structural safety constraints, ensuring controllability.
- **Challenge 2: How to support long-horizon forecasting while maintaining consistency and traceability of revision logic?**
  - **Difficulty**: Long-horizon forecasting is prone to error accumulation, and complex revision logic is hard to maintain in a single pass without mechanisms for correction.
  - **Solution**: It employs a Map-Reduce style decomposition for long-horizon tasks and utilizes a memory bank for post-hoc reflection, enhancing long-term reasoning capabilities and auditability.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The framework is designed as a controllable and auditable system featuring a unified workspace, tool invocation modules, safety constraint layers, and a memory bank. It operates as an upper-layer reasoning framework without replacing existing forecasting backbones.
- **Evaluation Results**: Real-world case studies demonstrate the system's ability to bridge the gap between statistical prediction and business-ready forecasting.
- **Comparison**: Unlike traditional models focusing solely on numerical accuracy, this method emphasizes the interpretability and operability of the revision process. Compared to LLM methods that generate free-form outputs, this approach offers higher controllability and auditability through explicit actions and safety constraints, addressing key business needs such as justification for adjustments, evidence tracking, and preservation of original baselines.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the overlooked "last-mile" problem in time series forecasting: transforming statistical baselines into decision-grade forecasts aligned with business context. The authors propose an LLM-agent framework that solves challenges in context integration and process auditability through tool invocation, explicit action conversion, structural safety constraints, and memory-based reflection. This approach significantly enhances the controllability, transparency, and business adaptability of the forecasting process, offering a new paradigm for practical deployment.
