## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Clinical practice is not about selecting answers from enumerated options; it involves physicians incrementally gathering heterogeneous information and making sequential, irreversible decisions under uncertainty. Static benchmarks fail to probe these dynamics, and existing interactive medical benchmarks compromise on at least one aspect.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Data-interaction benchmarks measure data-access skills rather than clinical reasoning; diagnostic conversation benchmarks rely on LLM-simulated patients limited to short vignettes and use LLM-as-judge; code-style executable benchmarks reduce clinical reasoning to code generation. None evaluate an LLM as an attending physician managing a real, multi-stage hospitalization with deterministic verification.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ClinEnv**: An interactive benchmark that evaluates LLMs as attending physicians over real inpatient admissions using a paradigm termed "Longitudinal Inpatient Simulation".
- **Challenge 1: Complexity and Irreversibility of Real Clinical Decisions**. The difficulty lies in the sequential nature of decisions and incomplete information requiring active acquisition. ClinEnv addresses this by automatically constructing raw admissions into ordered multi-stage decision sequences, requiring the model to actively query four specialized agents (patient, nurse, laboratory, history) before committing to medications, procedures, and diagnoses.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of Dual Deterministic Evaluation for Process and Outcome**. The challenge is that traditional methods either focus only on outcomes or use unstable LLM judges. ClinEnv introduces a dual evaluation framework: it scores decision content via deterministic ontology-grounded matching (ATC for medications, hierarchical ICD F1 for diagnoses/procedures) and scores information gathering via process metrics like coverage and cost efficiency, making the "information-acquisition gap" directly measurable.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: ClinEnv features an automated pipeline that extracts structured ground-truth decisions from EHR timelines and discharge documentation without manual annotation. The environment uses four specialized agents to withhold clinical information until requested. The evaluation framework combines deterministic ontology matching with process metrics.
- **Evaluation Results**: Across seven models, the strongest achieved only a 0.31 decision F1 score. Outcome quality was sharply decoupled from process quality. Difficulty concentrated in management decisions and later stages; models recovered discharge diagnoses far more reliably than management actions (0.51 vs. 0.17 F1) and continued to issue redundant queries as cases progressed.
- **Comparison**: Unlike static benchmarks focusing only on outcomes or interactive benchmarks relying on simulated patients, ClinEnv reveals information-acquisition deficits invisible to outcome-only evaluation, demonstrating the significant gap in current LLMs' ability to simulate real attending physician behavior.

## 4. Conclusion
ClinEnv fills a critical gap in medical AI evaluation by providing a real EHR-based, multi-stage, interactive inpatient simulation environment. It strictly quantifies both the accuracy of final diagnoses and the efficiency and rationality of the information-gathering process. The results indicate that state-of-the-art LLMs face substantial challenges in simulating complex clinical decision-making processes, particularly in long-term management and active information acquisition strategies, pointing the way for future medical Agent development.
