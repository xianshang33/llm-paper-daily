## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Autonomous science is transitioning from demonstrations to infrastructure. Large Language Model (LLM) agents plan experiments, while self-driving laboratories execute them.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current systems rebuild the link between reasoning agents and physical instruments from scratch due to fragmented vendor SDKs and standards designed for deterministic software clients, not probabilistic, goal-directed agents. Existing interoperability protocols like Anthropic's MCP (agent-to-tool) and Google's A2A (agent-to-agent) fail to model the "agent-to-instrument" edge, which involves stateful, safety-critical, exclusively owned, physically embodied operations producing measurements with units, calibration, and uncertainty.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: The Lab Agent Protocol (LAP), a protocol design filling the gap in agent-to-instrument interaction.
- **Challenge 1: State Management and Exclusive Access Conflicts in Physical Instruments**. The difficulty lies in the fact that physical instruments and samples can only be exclusively used by one task at a time, and operations are state-dependent; traditional stateless APIs cannot handle resource contention. LAP addresses this by introducing first-class reservation mechanisms for exclusive locking of instruments and samples, ensuring atomicity and consistency.
- **Challenge 2: Safety of Physical Operations and Reproducibility of Measurements**. The challenge is that physical experiments involve hazardous or irreversible operations, and scientific results require physical units, calibration, and uncertainty metrics. LAP solves this via a "safety-fence" handshake with cryptographically bound operator confirmation tokens for hazardous actions, and a MeasurementResult schema that enforces physical typing (QUDT/UCUM), calibration anchoring, and uncertainty bearing, ensuring reproducibility by construction.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: LAP v0.1 is currently a design specification intended to seed an open community standard. It has no normative or implemented status yet; benefits are argued architecturally rather than empirically validated. LAP is transport-compatible with the A2A/MCP ecosystem and encapsulates rather than replaces existing device standards like SiLA 2 and OPC-UA.
- **Comparison with Related Work**:
    - **vs. MCP**: MCP standardizes agent-to-tool interactions but lacks support for physical world states, safety constraints, and physical dimensions.
    - **vs. A2A**: A2A handles agent-to-agent collaboration but does not address how agents safely and standardizedly control physical hardware.
    - **vs. Traditional Lab Automation Standards (SiLA 2, OPC-UA)**: These are designed for deterministic clients and lack support for probabilistic LLM decision-making, intent resolution, and advanced safety handshakes. LAP encapsulates these lower-level standards to provide high-level abstractions suitable for agents.
- **Evaluation Results**: The paper does not provide quantitative benchmarks. Instead, it demonstrates feasibility through an end-to-end walkthrough of a closed-loop autonomous campaign, arguing for architectural completeness.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper proposes LAP to address the lack of standards for agent-to-instrument interaction in autonomous science infrastructure. By introducing four physical-world primitives—InstrumentCard, exclusive reservation, safety-fence handshakes, and standardized measurement results—LAP bridges the gap left by A2A and MCP in physical embodiment. Although currently a design specification, its layered architecture and compatibility with existing standards lay the theoretical foundation for future open, interconnected self-driving laboratory ecosystems.
