## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Multi-Document Summarization (MDS) is critical for synthesizing knowledge from diverse textual sources amidst rapidly expanding digital information.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Early extractive/rule-based methods lack semantic coherence. Supervised deep learning and LLM models rely heavily on large labeled datasets, limiting applicability in low-resource domains and languages (e.g., Vietnamese). Existing training-free methods struggle with complex inter-document relationships and large semantic contexts due to context length limitations and lack of structural guidance.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a**: Training-free Mixture-of-Agents (MoA) framework for MDS that leverages the complementary strengths of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Knowledge Graphs (KGs).
- **Challenge 1**: Difficulty in capturing complex inter-document relationships and entity connections. **Solution**: Introduces the KGSum agent, which explicitly models entities and relationships via a Knowledge Graph, generating KG-based abstracts to overcome the loss of relational context in pure text models.
- **Challenge 2**: Limited perspective of single models and lack of consistency in training-free settings. **Solution**: Decomposes tasks into specialized agents (extractive selection, knowledge-aware abstraction, iterative refinement) and unifies outputs using an LLM-guided multi-perspective consistency mechanism, ensuring factual accuracy and coherence without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The framework employs an Extractor agent for salient sentence identification, a KGSum agent for KG construction and summary generation, and an iterative refinement module. It operates entirely on pre-trained LLMs without fine-tuning.
- **Evaluation Results**: Experiments conducted on four datasets in both English and Vietnamese demonstrate state-of-the-art or competitive performance.
- **Comparison**: Outperforms supervised models in adaptability to low-resource languages by eliminating data dependency. Surpasses other training-free methods by effectively handling complex document relationships through the integration of KGs and multi-agent collaboration.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper presents a novel training-free MoA framework that combines LLM linguistic capabilities with KG structural knowledge. It effectively addresses high data dependency, difficulty in capturing cross-document relations, and poor generalization in low-resource languages. The modular design and multi-perspective consistency mechanism ensure robustness and adaptability across different languages and domains.
