## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: LLM-based agents are increasingly solving complex tasks by interacting with external tools, retrieval systems, memory modules, and environments. While this expands autonomy, it makes agent behavior harder to verify, debug, and audit. Final-answer accuracy alone is insufficient to explain how outputs are produced, which evidence supports claims, or where failures originate.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current research largely focuses on final outcome correctness, lacking a systematic modeling of evidence tracing and execution provenance during agent execution. Existing methods fail to effectively connect retrieval grounding, claim support, tool-use safety, memory lineage, and observability, creating gaps in trust establishment, fault diagnosis, and auditing.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a**: A systematic review and conceptual framework for evidence tracing and execution provenance in LLM agents. This framework unifies a provenance perspective, covering a comprehensive taxonomy from trace sources and evidence units to provenance relations and trust functions.
- **Challenge 1: Black-box nature and unexplainability of execution processes**. The difficulty lies in the complex multi-step reasoning, tool calls, and environment interactions that obscure decision-making bases. The paper addresses this by introducing a unified provenance taxonomy (covering granularity, timing, representation, etc.), explicitly modeling the connections between retrieval, tool outputs, memory items, and final answers.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of process-oriented accountability evaluation standards**. The difficulty is that traditional evaluations focus only on final answers, ignoring process safety and rationality. The paper maps existing benchmarks and metrics to provenance-related capabilities and discusses shifting from final-answer correctness to process-level accountability, providing a direction for evaluating trustworthy agents.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation and Deployment**: As a survey paper, this work does not propose a single new algorithm or software system but structurally organizes existing methodologies. It reviews key technical directions including provenance representation, evidence attribution, tool-use provenance, runtime guardrails, provenance-bearing memory, trace-based observability, and failure diagnosis.
- **Evaluation Results and Comparison**: The paper systematically compares differences in provenance construction across existing works. It points out that current benchmarks (e.g., AgentBench, WebArena, ToolLLM) primarily focus on task completion rates and lack deep auditing capabilities for internal execution traces. The article emphasizes the necessity of shifting from "result-oriented" to "process-oriented" evaluation and highlights gaps in semantic provenance and recovery-oriented evaluation.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper fills the gap in systematic research on evidence tracing and execution provenance in the field of LLM agents. By proposing a comprehensive taxonomic framework, it unifies dispersed research areas (such as retrieval grounding, tool safety, and memory lineage) under a provenance perspective. It not only summarizes current technical progress but also identifies future open challenges, including unified trace schemas, claim-level semantic provenance, provenance-aware safety mechanisms, realistic execution-trace benchmarks, and privacy-aware audit infrastructure. This work lays the theoretical foundation for building trustworthy, debuggable, and auditable LLM agents.
