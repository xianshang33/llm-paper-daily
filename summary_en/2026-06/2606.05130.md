## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Individual-level mobility prediction is central to urban simulation, transportation planning, and policy analysis. While supervised sequence models achieve high accuracy, they require task-specific training and lack decision-level transparency (black-box nature).
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Recent LLM-based methods improve interpretability but mostly rely on static prompts and single-pass inference. They fail to seek additional evidence when mobility signals are weak or conflicting, limiting their effectiveness in ambiguous scenarios.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed AgentMob**, a training-free LLM-driven agent framework that formulates next-location prediction as adaptive evidence-controlled decision making.
- **Challenge 1: Balancing Efficiency and Cost**. The difficulty lies in the high computational cost of LLMs; performing deep reasoning for all samples is inefficient. The paper addresses this by introducing a "fast path" for routine cases based on historical regularity, triggering iterative tool use only for ambiguous cases, thus optimizing performance while reducing overhead.
- **Challenge 2: Prediction Accuracy under Weak Signals**. The difficulty is that single-context inputs are insufficient when trajectory signals are weak or conflicting. The paper solves this by enabling the LLM controller to iteratively invoke multiple tools (recent trajectories, historical behavior, stay-move likelihood, geographical evidence) for adaptive evidence gathering and cross-validation, thereby resolving ambiguities.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: AgentMob is a training-free framework requiring no large-scale labeled data for retraining. It integrates individual mobility knowledge, a toolbox (including mobility context retriever, geographical information retriever, stay-move estimator), and urban geographical knowledge.
- **Evaluation Results**: Tested on three mobility datasets (BW, YJMob100K, Shanghai ISP). Using GPT-5.4, AgentMob achieved the strongest overall performance among training-free LLM-based methods: 71.42% Acc@1 on BW, 33.14% on YJMob100K, and 33.50% on Shanghai ISP.
- **Comparison**: On non-fast-path (ambiguous) cases in the BW dataset, the LLM controller improved Acc@1 from 30.65% (same-tool statistical baseline) to 48.62%. This demonstrates its superior ability to resolve ambiguous predictions through adaptive evidence gathering compared to static prompt LLM methods and traditional supervised models.

## 4. Conclusion
AgentMob successfully transforms mobility prediction from static, single-pass inference into an adaptive, evidence-grounded, and iterative reasoning process. By distinguishing between routine and ambiguous cases, it addresses both the efficiency concerns of LLM inference and the lack of interpretability and evidence-handling capabilities in existing methods, offering a new paradigm for efficient and reliable mobility prediction.
