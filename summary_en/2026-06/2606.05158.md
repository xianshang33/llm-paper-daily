## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Multi-agent reasoning systems typically adopt a "generate-then-transfer" (Serial) paradigm, where upstream agents must complete their entire response before passing it downstream. This forces end-to-end latency to scale linearly with pipeline depth and leaves downstream agents idle during waiting periods.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Existing frameworks assume the transmission unit must be a complete response. While streaming is common within single LLMs, its application between agents and its impact on effectiveness remain underexplored. Conventional wisdom suggests incomplete context harms quality, preventing the exploitation of more reliable early reasoning steps to optimize both performance and latency.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed StreamMA**: A multi-agent reasoning system based on a streaming communication protocol that shifts the transmission unit from complete responses to individual reasoning steps, enabling pipelined execution among adjacent agents.
- **Challenge 1: High Latency and Inefficiency**. The serial protocol forces downstream agents to wait for upstream completion, causing latency to grow linearly with depth. StreamMA addresses this by streaming each reasoning step as it is generated, allowing parallel execution and significantly reducing latency.
- **Challenge 2: Misleading Downstream Agents with Late-Stage Errors**. Multi-step reasoning quality is non-uniform; early steps are often more reliable than later ones. The serial protocol forces downstream agents to condition on the full context, including error-prone late steps. StreamMA allows downstream agents to start reasoning based on the reliable early prefix. By the time degraded steps arrive, the downstream agent has formed an independent trajectory, diluting the impact of errors and surprisingly improving effectiveness.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated across eight reasoning benchmarks in mathematics, science, and code, using two frontier LLMs (Claude Opus 4.6 and GPT-5.4) and three topologies (Chain, Tree, Graph).
- **Results**: StreamMA outperforms both Serial and Single baselines in effectiveness and efficiency. It achieves an average accuracy improvement of +7.3 percentage points, with a maximum gain of +22.4 pp on HMMT 2026 (using Claude Opus 4.6-high).
- **Theoretical Analysis**: Provides the first closed-form joint analysis of stream, serial, and single protocols, deriving effectiveness ordering, speedup upper bounds, and cost ratios.
- **New Discovery**: Identifies a "step-level scaling law," showing that increasing the number of steps per agent consistently improves both effectiveness and efficiency. This represents a new scaling dimension orthogonal to and composable with agent-count scaling.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper introduces StreamMA, which addresses latency bottlenecks and error propagation in multi-agent reasoning through step-level streaming communication. The study demonstrates that streaming not only reduces latency but also enhances accuracy by leveraging high-quality early reasoning steps. Empirical results show significant improvements over existing methods across various benchmarks and models. Additionally, the discovery of the step-level scaling law offers new theoretical insights and practical directions for designing multi-agent systems.
