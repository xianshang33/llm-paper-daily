## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Persistent memory for LLM agents is a write-heavy substrate where every belief update is a versioned write, often leading to contradictions with stored claims. Production systems typically use four heuristics for resolution: last-writer-wins, evidence-weighted merge, await-confirmation, and per-rule policy.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current systems do not declare the isolation levels they assume or the write-time anomalies they admit. They lack an explicit contract for concurrency control, leading to defects in replay consistency, belief drift, and audit trails. Traditional concurrency control theories (e.g., Berenson-Adya anomalies) and bitemporal data models have not been adapted to the LLM agent write path, failing to name and defend against specific failures introduced by LLM judges.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed TOKI**, a bitemporal operator algebra that types the four common contradiction-resolution heuristics as a family of bitemporal operators with explicit isolation preconditions and provenance annotations, thereby making the write-time correctness contract explicit.
- **Challenge 1: Specific write-time anomalies introduced by LLM judges (replay inconsistency, belief-drift skew, audit erasure)**. These are difficult to address via traditional database isolation levels due to the non-deterministic nature of LLM judgments and semantic conflicts. TOKI addresses this by binding each failure to a point in classical concurrency control machinery and using a dual-row schema (main + audit row) to preserve the provenance of losing facts, providing defenses with soundness proofs.
- **Challenge 2: Maintaining replay consistency while ensuring audit integrity**. Existing baselines retaining an LLM judge on the write path often omit necessary keyed logging, leading to inconsistent results upon replay. TOKI proves that keyed logging of the adjudicating judge is necessary for replay consistency within the relational schedule model and enforces this discipline through its operator algebra, excluding all three anomalies without removing the judge.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: TOKI defines operators based on a dual-row schema, each carrying isolation preconditions and provenance annotations. It extends fold operators to handle n-ary conflict sets and provides four soundness theorems to close the contract across isolation, schema, and provenance.
- **Evaluation Results**: On a natural-workload slice of the LoCoMo dataset, TOKI's audit-row defense improved accuracy by 0.86. Ablation studies showed that removing the typed memory layer resulted in a 0.49 accuracy drop on 1,444 answerable questions.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: A verdict matrix over eight systems revealed that all baselines keeping an LLM judge on the write path admitted at least one of the three write-time anomalies. Only a content-addressed engine-layer comparator avoided them by removing the judge, whereas TOKI was the only system to exclude all three anomalies while retaining the judge. Although cross-system comparisons were underpowered and did not claim overall superiority, TOKI filled the gap in explicit contracts present in existing systems.

## 4. Conclusion
TOKI's primary contribution is a write-time correctness specification (contract) that is proved sound across isolation, schema, and provenance. It clarifies the guarantees assumed but never made explicit by production heuristics, addressing replay inconsistency, belief-drift skew, and audit erasure in LLM agent persistent memory caused by the lack of explicit concurrency control contracts.
