## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Multi-agent systems based on Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly used for tasks producing natural language artifacts (e.g., claim verification). Due to the stochastic nature of LLMs, honest agents often generate textually distinct but semantically equivalent outputs.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Classical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (BFT) relies on byte-level identity for finality, which fails for LLM proposals. Current LLM voting/aggregation methods often obscure finality control behind a single verdict, lacking fine-grained distinctions between commit types or safe aborts, and failing to provide verifiable semantic proofs.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Hierarchical Certified Semantic Commitment (H-CSC)**: A BFT-inspired protocol that converts embedding-derived finality signals into three typed outcomes: `semantic_commit`, `verdict_commit`, or a typed safe `abort`.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of byte-level consistency in LLM proposals renders traditional BFT inapplicable.**
  - **Difficulty**: Honest agents produce diverse textual expressions for the same semantic content, breaking hash/signature-based consensus.
  - **Solution**: H-CSC uses embeddings to measure semantic similarity, identifying a semantic core (2f+1 nodes) supporting a verdict within proposal groups, thus achieving consensus without byte matching.
- **Challenge 2: Balancing safety with availability when semantic rationale is dispersed but the verdict is clear.**
  - **Difficulty**: Strict semantic aggregation may incorrectly abort valid rounds due to linguistic diversity, reducing system utility.
  - **Solution**: A hierarchical mechanism issues a `verdict_commit` when there is a strong verdict margin but dispersed semantics, falling back from `semantic_commit`. This ensures high coverage while maintaining a strict safety floor.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Setup**: Evaluated on a controlled semantic-poisoning benchmark (BCS_v1, 120 episodes) and a real-world LLM agent claim-verification benchmark (MVR-50, 50 tasks).
- **Adversarial Settings**: Tested under paired static and rushing Byzantine attacks.
- **Key Results**:
  - **Safety & Correctness**: On BCS_v1, H-CSC showed low angular deviation (0.31°–2.04°) in BFT-feasible buckets and 100% abort rate in beyond-BFT scenarios (n < 3f+1).
  - **Coverage & Safety**: On MVR-50, H-CSC achieved commit rates of 0.90 (static) / 0.92 (rushing) with invalid_hmaj rates of 0.02 / 0.00. This statistically matches a strong baseline (certificate-wrapped majority, 0.88 / 0.92).
  - **Unique Advantage**: Unlike the verdict-only baseline, H-CSC emitted embedding-backed `semantic_commit` digests in 74%/72% of rounds, providing typed semantic provenance.
  - **Ablation**: A strict-semantic ablation dropped commit rates to 0.54/0.48, proving the necessity of the verdict-level fallback for coverage (+0.36/+0.44) at the same safety level.
  - **Robustness**: Cross-model checks across four LLMs kept invalid_hmaj within 0.00–0.03.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses Byzantine fault tolerance in LLM multi-agent collaboration by proposing H-CSC. It overcomes the byte-consistency limitation of traditional BFT by using embeddings for semantic consensus. The key innovation is "typed finality," distinguishing between `semantic_commit` and `verdict_commit` based on semantic cohesion. Experiments demonstrate that H-CSC matches the safety and coverage of strong baselines while offering superior semantic verifiability, effectively handling the stochastic nature of LLM outputs.
