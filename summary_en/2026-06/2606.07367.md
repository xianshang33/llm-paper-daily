## 1. What the Paper Does

**Background**
Large Language Models (LLMs) are shifting from static text generation to driving interactive agents in complex environments. However, training these agents for reliable long-horizon decision-making remains a fundamental challenge.

**Why Existing Work Falls Short**
The core difficulty lies in credit assignment: agents typically receive delayed rewards only at the end of episodes, making it hard to attribute outcomes to intermediate decisions. Existing approaches attempt to automatically derive step-wise process rewards via online Q-value search or anchor-state grouping. However, they suffer from a fundamental limitation: process-level supervision is highly distribution-sensitive. As the policy evolves, it generates out-of-distribution (OOD) actions or states, leading to catastrophic distribution shifts that render previously learned reward signals unreliable. These methods often require expensive human annotation or environment backtracking and fail to maintain reliability under distribution shifts.

## Core Contributions

**Proposed Q-Evolve Framework**
A self-evolving framework for LLM agents that unifies automatic process-reward labeling and policy learning within a principled in-distribution reinforcement learning paradigm.

**Challenge 1: Credit Assignment in Sparse-Reward Settings & Reward Reliability**
*   **Description & Difficulty**: In sparse-reward environments, evaluating intermediate steps is difficult. Furthermore, as the policy updates, the data distribution shifts, causing critics trained on offline data to become unreliable during online evaluation (distribution shift).
*   **Solution**: Q-Evolve learns an "in-distribution" critic from a hybrid off-policy dataset combining expert demonstrations and agent-generated trajectories. It stabilizes Bellman backups in sparse-reward settings using a weighted Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) objective. This allows deriving dense, reliable step-wise process rewards via advantage estimation without environment backtracking or human annotation.

**Challenge 2: Exacerbated Distribution Shift During Self-Evolution**
*   **Description & Difficulty**: Traditional online policy optimization pushes agents into unknown regions, worsening distribution shift. This makes previously learned reward signals invalid, leading to unstable training or performance degradation.
*   **Solution**: The method performs Behavior-Proximal Policy Optimization using the derived process rewards. Crucially, it evolves the agent over the same data distribution used for process reward labeling. This ensures policy updates remain within the reliable distribution of the critic, enabling iterative self-improvement without exacerbating distribution shift.

## Implementation and Evaluation

**Experimental Setup**
Evaluated on three benchmarks: AlfWorld, WebShop, and ScienceWorld.

**Results and Comparison**
*   **Performance**: Q-Evolve outperforms strong baselines in sample efficiency, robustness, and overall task performance.
*   **Comparison**: Unlike methods relying on extensive online interaction for Q-value search (e.g., Wang et al., 2024b;c) or simple advantage calculations (e.g., GiGPO), Q-Evolve maintains reward signal reliability through its "in-distribution" learning loop. The results demonstrate that stable agent self-evolution is achievable by co-evolving process-level supervision and policy within a shared distribution-grounded loop.

## 4. Conclusion

The proposed Q-Evolve framework addresses the challenges of credit assignment and distribution shift in long-horizon LLM agent tasks. By co-evolving process-level supervision and policy within a shared "in-distribution" learning loop, it achieves dense reward learning without human annotation or environment backtracking while ensuring stability. Experiments show significant improvements in performance and sample efficiency across complex interactive environments, offering a new paradigm for reliable LLM agent self-evolution.
