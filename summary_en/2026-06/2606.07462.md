## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: With advancements in foundation models and agent scaffolding, AI agents have shown proficiency in long-horizon coding and autonomous experiment execution, evolving from assistants to autonomous research agents.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current benchmarks primarily assess macro-level execution capabilities (e.g., code implementation, experiment reproduction) but lack evaluation of field sensitivity, research ethics, and nuanced scientific judgment. Consequently, frontier agents fail to fully replace human researchers as they often overlook subtle yet critical details obvious to humans.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed the AARR (Act As a Real Researcher) benchmark series and its first subset, AARRI-Bench**: Designed to evaluate whether agents can emulate the professionalism, thoroughness, and nuanced reasoning of human researchers in granular scenarios.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of granularity in evaluating "researcher-like behavior"**. The difficulty lies in the fact that success in macro tasks does not imply genuine scientific intuition or ethical awareness. AARRI-Bench addresses this by simulating real research intern activities that require high-level professional judgment and attention to detail, exposing agent failures in subtle areas.
- **Challenge 2: Insufficient robustness and completeness in complex research workflows**. The challenge is maintaining context and adhering to implicit constraints over long horizons. Extensive experiments reveal that even the best configuration (Mini-SWE-Agent with Claude Opus 4.7) achieves only a 68.3% success rate, indicating that complex scaffolding alone is insufficient and that deeper exploration of research behavior is required.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Released the AARRI-Bench dataset comprising tasks simulating real research intern activities. Conducted extensive experiments across various frontier models and agentic systems.
- **Evaluation Results**: The best-performing configuration (Mini-SWE-Agent with Claude Opus 4.7) achieved only a 68.3% success rate. Agents frequently overlooked subtle but critical details that are obvious to human researchers.
- **Comparison**: Unlike existing benchmarks focused on macro-execution, AARRI-Bench highlights significant gaps in current SOTA agents' ability to mimic human professionalism and nuanced reasoning, pointing out the missing focus on modeling specific "research behaviors" in current technical approaches.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper introduces the AARR benchmark series and its inaugural instance, AARRI-Bench, filling the gap in evaluating fine-grained research behavior and nuanced judgment. Experimental results demonstrate that while frontier agents excel in macro tasks, there is substantial room for improvement in simulating the professionalism, thoroughness, and ethical judgment of human researchers. The study emphasizes that developing researcher-like AI requires moving beyond complex scaffolding to deeply explore and model specific scientific research behaviors.
