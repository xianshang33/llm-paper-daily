## What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Fraud detection in payment, e-commerce, and telecommunications systems requires high accuracy at the individual level, robustness under severe class imbalance, and interpretability for risk managers. The field is shifting towards automated engineering processes driven by Large Language Models (LLMs).
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Automated Machine Learning (AutoML) systems search fixed numerical spaces without semantic awareness; Graph Neural Network (GNN)-based methods require costly pre-defined relational graphs and remain opaque at the individual decision level; general-purpose LLM agents do not account for the specific recall and precision constraints required in real-world fraud detection.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: SAGE, the first end-to-end LLM-driven multi-agent framework for fraud detection.
- **Challenge 1: How to quickly construct a global, realistic data portrait based on individual-level structured features without rich relational graphs?**
  - **Difficulty**: Real-world evidence consists of structured features (e.g., device fingerprints, transaction history) rather than rich graphs, making it hard for traditional methods to capture deep semantics.
  - **Solution**: SAGE introduces a six-layer Data Diagnostic Tree (DDT). It coordinates three dedicated agents to make decisions based on the DDT, using a Markov decision process guided by natural-language gradients to automatically optimize the model and build accurate data portraits.
- **Challenge 2: How to ensure the generated detection models meet strict recall/precision constraints while remaining interpretable?**
  - **Difficulty**: General LLM agents lack constraints tailored to fraud-specific reward mechanisms, leading to models that may deviate from business needs and lack transparency.
  - **Solution**: SAGE designs fraud-specific reward mechanisms to guide agents in self-reflection and optimization via natural-language gradients, ensuring the final classifier meets performance metrics while providing transparent, understandable decision logic for risk managers.

## Implementation, Deployment, Evaluation Results, and Comparison
- **Implementation & Deployment**: SAGE is a multi-agent system with open-source code. It was tested on five fraud datasets and five LLM backbones.
- **Evaluation Results**: SAGE won 96.00% of method-dataset comparisons. It improved the average F1 score by 40.86% over baselines.
- **Comparison**: Compared to traditional supervised learning, AutoML, and GNN-based methods, SAGE significantly outperforms them in performance metrics (F1) while addressing issues of semantic lack and poor interpretability, demonstrating the advantage of LLM-driven agents in constructing domain-specific classifiers.

## Summary
SAGE is the first end-to-end LLM-driven multi-agent framework designed specifically for real-world fraud detection. By introducing a Data Diagnostic Tree and specific reward mechanisms, it successfully overcomes the limitations of existing methods in semantic awareness, individual decision transparency, and business constraint adaptation. Experimental results show that SAGE achieves significant performance improvements across multiple datasets and LLM backbones, offering a new solution for automated, interpretable, and high-precision fraud detection.
