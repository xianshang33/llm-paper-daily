## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: When LLM agents fail (e.g., issuing incorrect refunds, calling wrong tools, leaking data), developers need to identify which specific step caused the failure to fix it. Existing observability tools only explain "what happened," and evaluation tools only determine "pass/fail," neither providing causal attribution.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Intuitive heuristics are often incorrect because the step executing a harmful action is rarely the step that decided on it. LLM-judge based attribution is correlational and unreliable, achieving only ~14% step-level accuracy on the Who&When benchmark.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Causal Agent Replay (CAR)**: A framework that models agent runs as Structural Causal Models (SCMs). It answers causal questions by applying do-operations to specific steps, re-executing the trajectory forward under the same stochastic policy, and measuring the shift in outcome distributions.
- **Challenge 1: Confounding in Stochastic Rollouts**. Since the policy is stochastic, intervening on a step and rolling forward resamples all downstream steps, introducing confounding variables that obscure the true effect of the intervention. CAR introduces a single-step contrastive estimator with a "point-of-commitment" rule to resolve this confound specific to stochastic forward execution.
- **Challenge 2: Credit Assignment for Interacting Steps**. Single-step methods cannot structurally handle failures caused by interactions between multiple steps. CAR provides a budget-bounded Monte-Carlo Shapley estimator that splits credit across interacting steps and reports effects with confidence intervals.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: CAR is open-source and runs on both hosted and local free models. It defines an intervention algebra of five do(·) operations over agent steps and builds the system around outcome distributions with confidence intervals.
- **Evaluation Results**: Validated against synthetic SCMs with planted ground truth. The contrastive estimator successfully recovered the pivotal step, and the Shapley estimator accurately recovered two-step interactions (Shapley values: 0.44, 0.45, ~0; efficiency sum 0.909 vs analytic 0.91).
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Unlike SOTA LLM-judge attribution methods (~14% accuracy) which rely on correlational pattern matching, CAR uses principled causal intervention. It demonstrates superior accuracy in identifying causal steps and interactions in controlled synthetic environments.

#### Summary
This paper presents Causal Agent Replay (CAR), a counterfactual attribution method for LLM agent failures. By modeling agent trajectories as Structural Causal Models and utilizing intervention experiments combined with Shapley value estimation, CAR accurately identifies the specific steps and interactions causing failures. It addresses the limitations of current observability and evaluation tools, offering a principled solution for debugging LLM agents.
