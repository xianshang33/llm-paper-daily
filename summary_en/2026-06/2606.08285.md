## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) and agentic systems are increasingly applied in financial trading, evolving from simple sentiment analysis to complex autonomous workflows and multi-agent collaboration.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: While architectural designs have become sophisticated, evaluation discipline lags behind. Reported performances are difficult to compare or reproduce due to variations in data provenance, temporal splits, execution timing, turnover treatment, and transaction cost modeling. Existing surveys mostly focus on architectures and inputs, neglecting the critical execution assumptions that determine economic interpretability.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a targeted topical review and reproducibility audit framework for execution realism in LLM-based trading systems**.
- **Challenge 1: Opaque evaluation assumptions lead to incomparable results.** The difficulty lies in the fact that trading outcomes depend heavily on information availability timing, order execution assumptions, and cost deductions, which are often vaguely reported. The paper addresses this by constructing a coded evidence matrix covering 30 primary studies, systematically assessing key dimensions like point-in-time controls, split transparency, and held-out evaluation to reveal reporting blind spots.
- **Challenge 2: Ignoring execution friction inflates strategy performance.** The challenge is that many studies overlook turnover and transaction costs, rendering active strategy results economically uninterpretable. The paper includes a 10-equity worked example to demonstrate how explicit friction and timing choices materially compress active-strategy results, highlighting the necessity of standardized reporting for execution realism.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation**: The paper does not propose a new trading algorithm but conducts a reproducibility audit. It reviews 30 trade-relevant primary studies using a coded evidence matrix to evaluate their adherence to standards in point-in-time controls, cost handling, execution semantics, universe definition, and artifact release.
- **Results**: The audit reveals that architecture reporting is generally clearer than the evaluation assumptions required to judge economic interpretability or reproducibility. Most studies lack sufficient detail for rigorous comparison.
- **Comparison**: Unlike broad surveys that summarize architectural growth and backtest performance, this work focuses specifically on "execution assumptions." It highlights the deficit in evaluation discipline and argues for a shift from merely designing better agents to establishing clearer reporting standards for execution realism.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper concludes that the next crucial step for LLM trading research is not just improving agent architecture, but establishing clearer reporting standards for execution realism, reproducibility, and evaluation comparability. Through audit and examples, it demonstrates that ignoring execution details leads to misleading results, calling for greater emphasis on evaluation discipline to ensure the economic significance and scientific rigor of research findings.
