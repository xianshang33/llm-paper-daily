## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Multi-agent Large Language Model (LLM) systems are rapidly emerging, orchestrating multiple agents to collaborate on complex tasks. However, "transparency," a cornerstone of Responsible AI (RAI), remains under-defined in these distributed architectures.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current transparency frameworks (e.g., XAI techniques, model cards) were largely developed for non-agentic, single-model systems. They fail to address the new forms of opacity introduced by agentic architectures, where task delegation, inter-agent communication, and dynamic orchestration complicate traditional notions of transparency.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Multidimensional Framework for Transparency**: Based on semi-structured interviews with 13 early adopters (builders and users) of multi-agent LLM systems, the paper synthesizes a framework focused on developers, users, and governance, positioning transparency as a situated socio-technical practice.
- **Challenge 1: Opacity arising from multi-agent interaction complexity**. The challenge lies in the difficulty of tracing decision origins due to dynamic coordination and communication between agents. The paper addresses this by identifying how practitioners conceptualize transparency through "debugging" and "reproducibility," suggesting that detailed interaction logging and state visualization are key to deconstructing this complexity.
- **Challenge 2: Misalignment of transparency expectations among stakeholders**. The challenge is the divergent and sometimes conflicting understandings of transparency among developers, end-users, and governance bodies (the "Catch-22"). The paper resolves this by analyzing complementary perspectives like "boundary-setting," "visualization," and "auditing," offering design principles to align the expectations and capacities of different audiences.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Methodology**: This is an empirical qualitative study, not a proposed algorithmic model. The researchers conducted semi-structured interviews with 13 early adopters within a large technology organization.
- **Analysis**: Thematic analysis was applied to identify recurring patterns, resulting in five key framings of transparency: reproducibility, debugging, boundary-setting, visualization, and auditing.
- **Results & Comparison**: Unlike prior work focusing on internal mechanisms of single models (XAI), this study reveals transparency in multi-agent systems as a socio-technical practice. It highlights the limitations of generic AI transparency guidelines for agentic architectures and emphasizes the need for audience-specific transparency tools, rather than providing quantitative performance metrics.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper presents one of the first empirical studies on how early adopters conceptualize and practice transparency in multi-agent LLM systems. It argues that traditional definitions of transparency are insufficient for distributed, agentic architectures. By synthesizing interview data into a multidimensional framework, the authors position transparency as a situated socio-technical practice. This framework guides future HCI and AI design to better align the expectations and capabilities of diverse stakeholders, facilitating more responsible AI deployment in complex multi-agent environments.
