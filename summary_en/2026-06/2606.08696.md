## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Counterfactual recourse aims to provide actionable feature changes to help individuals alter unfavorable decisions made by predictive models. In practice, users benefit more from multiple feasible alternatives than a single optimal explanation.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Traditional methods often return only one or a few proximity-driven solutions, lacking diversity. While Large Language Models (LLMs) can generate diverse alternatives, LLM calls are computationally and economically expensive. Existing LLM-based search frameworks (e.g., LATS) are optimized for single-path reasoning and fail to efficiently generate a large set of unique, oracle-validated counterfactuals under a fixed budget of LLM calls.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Comp-MCTS**: An agentic tree-search framework designed to maximize the yield of unique, oracle-validated counterfactuals under a fixed LLM-call budget.
- **Challenge 1: How to explore diverse intervention directions within a limited LLM budget?**
  - **Difficulty**: Blind generation leads to redundant or invalid suggestions, quickly exhausting the budget.
  - **Solution**: Comp-MCTS uses LLMs to propose multiple feature edits at each node and validates them via a black-box oracle. It allocates the budget toward novel, promising intervention directions rather than re-exploring known areas.
- **Challenge 2: How to avoid redundancy and maintain a balance between quantity and quality?**
  - **Difficulty**: Simple multi-candidate generation often produces semantically similar or duplicate results, reducing practical value.
  - **Solution**: Introduces compression-guided pruning to dynamically eliminate redundant nodes during the search. This ensures the final set has high uniqueness, sparsity, and proximity, achieving a better quantity-quality trade-off within the fixed budget.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on four real-world tabular datasets in a training-free, oracle-only setting.
- **Baselines**: Compared against single-candidate LATS-style baselines and stronger multi-candidate variants.
- **Key Results**:
  - **Yield Superiority**: Comp-MCTS substantially outperforms single-candidate LATS baselines in the yield of unique, oracle-validated counterfactuals.
  - **Efficiency and Quality Trade-off**: Against stronger multi-candidate variants, Comp-MCTS achieves comparable or higher yield at similar or lower oracle-evaluation costs on three out of four datasets.
  - **Metric Performance**: Demonstrates competitive performance in proximity, sparsity, and novelty, highlighting its balanced trade-offs among quantity, quality, and efficiency.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the problem of generating counterfactual explanations under a fixed LLM budget by proposing the Comp-MCTS framework. By reframing the task as a fixed-budget search problem and integrating LLM-based proposal generation, oracle validation, and compression-guided pruning, the method effectively resolves the tension between diversity and cost. Experiments show that Comp-MCTS provides more diverse and high-quality actionable recommendations than existing LLM-based search baselines while strictly controlling costs.
