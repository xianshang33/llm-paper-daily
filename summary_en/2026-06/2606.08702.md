## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) excel in complex continual tasks through Multi-Agent Adaptation (MAA), which involves accumulating and reusing experience. Existing approaches include memory-driven methods (retaining history) and skill/procedural-driven methods (abstracting reusable procedures).
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**:
    1. **Noise and Entanglement**: Memory-driven methods retain long, noisy trajectories (e.g., tool results, disagreements), distorting decision signals and reducing retrieval precision.
    2. **Insufficient Relation Modeling**: Skill-driven methods improve reuse efficiency but often lack adequate modeling of the complex relationships between memories and skills, resulting in weak control.
    3. **Training Dependency**: Many effective methods rely on additional training or high-quality supervision, limiting flexibility and ease of deployment in training-free scenarios.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ConMem**: A relation-aware, training-free framework that enables efficient multi-agent adaptation through cross-experience coordination.
- **Challenge 1: How to extract structured, reusable knowledge from noisy historical trajectories?**
    - **Difficulty**: Raw interaction trajectories are long, mixed, and unstructured, leading to noise interference if retrieved directly.
    - **Solution**: ConMem distills historical trajectories into structured "Memory Cards" that capture reusable strategies and cues, organizing them into a relation-aware memory graph to eliminate noise and clarify structure.
- **Challenge 2: How to effectively coordinate multiple memory fragments at runtime to resolve strategy conflicts and recover dependencies?**
    - **Difficulty**: Simple retrieval may return conflicting strategies or those lacking context, leading to execution errors.
    - **Solution**: At runtime, ConMem retrieves cards based on task needs and coordinates them via the card graph to resolve conflicts and recover dependencies, providing structured, relation-aware guidance for robust, lightweight adaptation.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: ConMem is a training-free framework that keeps the host agent architecture fixed. It works by coordinating a budgeted prompt prefix at inference time, requiring no gradient updates or extra supervision.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - Extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks and mainstream MAS architectures.
    - **Performance Gains**: Consistent improvements over existing memory architectures.
    - **Efficiency Improvements**: Pruned more than 50% of expanded candidates and reduced planning overhead by over 80%, significantly enhancing inference-time efficiency.
- **Comparison**: Outperforms traditional memory-driven methods (e.g., raw history retention) and skill-driven methods (e.g., static skill libraries), particularly in handling long-trajectory noise and strategy coordination.

#### Summary
ConMem addresses the issues of noisy historical trajectories, insufficient memory-skill relation modeling, and training dependency in MAS by introducing structured memory cards and a relation-aware graph. Without additional training, it provides robust guidance through cross-experience coordination at runtime, significantly improving adaptation capabilities and inference efficiency, offering a new paradigm for training-free MAS optimization.
