## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: As autonomous agents increasingly negotiate, purchase, deploy code, and transfer funds, Agentic Commerce is emerging. However, there is no neutral mechanism to determine if an agent fulfilled its delegated obligation, assign responsibility for failures, or dictate subsequent settlement actions.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current infrastructure, including tool protocols (MCP), inter-agent communication (A2A), payment rails (x402), and traditional payment networks (Visa, Mastercard), focuses on identity, authorization, and execution. They all assume that the "determination" of obligation fulfillment exists externally but do not produce it themselves. Payment is not clearing; authorization is not clearing; and LLM-as-judge evaluations lack the finality required for financial settlement.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed RAILS (Real-Time Agent Integrity & Ledger Settlement)**: A verification-native clearing layer for agentic commerce that bridges the gap between action execution and financial settlement.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of objective determination for obligation fulfillment**. The difficulty lies in the fact that consequences often settle before verification occurs, and traditional systems cannot catch "performance failures" (where an agent acts within authorization but fails the intent). RAILS addresses this by introducing seven primitives and a formal model of admissibility-graded verification, ensuring that no financially material settlement is supported by evidence below the obligation's admissibility floor.
- **Challenge 2: Absence of falsifiable soundness guarantees in existing verification**. Nearby approaches output simple pass/fail states or scores without rigorous logical constraints. RAILS defines a soundness property that is falsifiable against its specification, providing a strict logical bound that prevents settlements based on insufficient evidence, unlike prior mechanisms that offer only equilibrium or bare scores.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The paper specifies the RAILS protocol rather than a software library. It defines the architecture comprising per-output reliability scores, published reliability records, and a clearing function. The core consists of seven primitives: Obligation Object, Evidence Envelope, Verification Mesh, Clearing Decision, Settlement Instruction, Clearing Passport, and Finality Rules.
- **Comparison with Related Work**:
    - **MCP/A2A**: Record calls or coordinate communication but do not judge obligation satisfaction.
    - **x402/AP2/Visa/Mastercard**: Handle value transfer and authorization attestation but do not verify service delivery quality or intent alignment.
    - **LLM-as-judge**: Provides evaluation scores but lacks the legal/financial finality required for clearing.
    - **RAILS**: Uniquely states and enforces a soundness property, embedding verification natively into the clearing process.
- **Evaluation Results**: As a protocol specification paper, it primarily demonstrates the falsifiability of its soundness property through theoretical derivation. It does not provide quantitative benchmarks but argues for the logical completeness and necessity of its formal model compared to existing fragmented solutions.

## 4. Conclusion
RAILS addresses the missing primitive of Clearing in agentic commerce. It identifies a verification vacuum in the current AI infrastructure stack between tool invocation and payment execution. By introducing a formal model of admissibility-graded verification and seven core primitives, RAILS ensures the integrity and accountability of agent transactions. It distinguishes payment from clearing and provides a falsifiable soundness guarantee, preventing financial settlements based on inadequate evidence, thereby laying the theoretical and protocol foundation for the safe operation of a large-scale autonomous agent economy.
