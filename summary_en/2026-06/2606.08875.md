## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Optimizing Large Language Models (LLMs) for long-horizon caregiver agents, particularly in dementia care, requires balancing delayed task objectives with immediate environmental dynamics, such as patient distress and resistance.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current methods struggle with the trade-off between reward sparsity and evaluation costs. Trajectory-level rewards are too sparse for turn-level credit assignment, while external LLM-based evaluators are computationally expensive and prone to misinterpreting fragmented or indirect patient responses, leading to unstable training.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed T²-GRPO Framework**: Introduces Turn-Trajectory Group Relative Policy Optimization, a framework that decouples caregiver reinforcement learning into two normalized reward horizons and enforces safety via a binary hard veto.
- **Challenge 1: Credit Assignment for Immediate vs. Long-term Goals**. In long-horizon tasks, isolating the immediate impact of a single turn from sparse final outcomes is difficult, and quantifying subtle emotional changes is challenging. T²-GRPO addresses this by deriving dense turn-level rewards directly from environment state transitions in a frozen dementia patient simulator, measuring changes in patient distress and resistance.
- **Challenge 2: Fusion of Heterogeneous Reward Signals and Reward Collapse**. Turn-level (dense, immediate) and trajectory-level (sparse, global) rewards have different distributions and scales; direct summation can lead to reward dominance or collapse. T²-GRPO employs independent centered-rank normalization to combine these rewards, preserving the characteristics of heterogeneous signals and mitigating reward collapse.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The method utilizes a frozen dementia patient simulator to extract state changes (distress and resistance) as turn-level rewards. A binary hard veto mechanism is integrated to ensure interaction safety.
- **Evaluation Results**: Extensive experiments on dementia caregiver agents demonstrate that T²-GRPO outperforms competitive baselines.
- **Comparison**: Compared to methods relying on external LLM evaluators or solely sparse trajectory rewards, T²-GRPO shows substantial improvements in emotionally sensitive care scenarios, effectively handling immediate patient feedback, long-term care outcomes, and safety constraints.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the long-horizon optimization challenge in dementia caregiver agent training by proposing the T²-GRPO framework. By decoupling turn-level and trajectory-level rewards and generating dense immediate rewards from environment state transitions, combined with centered-rank normalization, it successfully balances immediate patient emotional feedback with long-term care goals. Experiments prove that this method significantly outperforms existing baselines in improving care quality, ensuring safety, and handling complex emotional interactions, offering new insights for training emotionally sensitive LLM agents.
