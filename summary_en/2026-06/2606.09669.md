## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Spatial reasoning is a foundational capability for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to perceive and operate in the physical world. Existing benchmarks predominantly rely on passive evaluation (e.g., static VQA) or simulator-specific pipelines, failing to assess general interactive spatial understanding.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current benchmarks either focus on static scene recognition without dynamic exploration or are confined to specific simulators, sensor assumptions, and action interfaces. This makes it difficult to determine if success stems from general spatial reasoning or mere adaptation to a specific simulation environment.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SpatialWorld**: A unified benchmark specifically designed to evaluate the interactive spatial understanding of multimodal agents in complex real-world tasks.
- **Challenge 1: Difficulty in unified evaluation across heterogeneous simulators**. Different backends have distinct interfaces and characteristics, hindering fair comparison. **Solution**: SpatialWorld integrates eight heterogeneous simulation backends under a shared, simulator-agnostic protocol, using a unified text-based action interface native to MLLMs for decision-making.
- **Challenge 2: Active exploration and long-horizon planning under partial observability**. Physical spaces are partially observable, requiring agents to actively navigate and update beliefs rather than relying on single-view information. **Solution**: Agents must operate under vision-only partial observability, actively gathering egocentric visual evidence through closed-loop interaction. Each task includes human-validated initial states, reference trajectories, and terminal-state verifiers for reliable evaluation.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The benchmark features 760 human-annotated tasks across diverse domains (household routines, travel, social collaboration). It evaluates 15 advanced agent models.
- **Results**: Robust spatial task solving remains highly challenging. The strongest model, GPT-5, achieved an average Task Success Rate (TSR) of only 17.4%, while the leading open-source model, Qwen-3.5, reached 14.1%.
- **Comparison & Analysis**: Unlike previous works focusing on static recognition or simulator-specific adaptation, SpatialWorld exposes a clear mismatch between task success and execution efficiency, along with significant domain-specific performance variations. It highlights bottlenecks in active exploration and long-horizon planning.

## 4. Conclusion
SpatialWorld fills the gap in evaluating interactive spatial reasoning for MLLMs by integrating multiple heterogeneous simulation environments and requiring active exploration under partial observability. The results demonstrate that despite rapid advancements, current models face significant challenges in complex real-world spatial tasks, particularly in active exploration and long-term planning, providing a rigorous testbed for future research.
