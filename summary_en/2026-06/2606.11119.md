## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) is a key approach for enhancing reasoning and agentic behaviors in LLMs. However, rollout-intensive policy optimization is computationally expensive and often suffers from insufficient reward contrast.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Prior resource allocation methods operate primarily at the prompt level, deciding which prompts to sample and how many rollouts to assign. They neglect the variation in informativeness across different turns within the same rollout. Relying solely on outcome rewards leads to sparse credit assignment and a lack of local contrast for intermediate decisions in long trajectories.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: Tree Rollout Allocation for Contrastive Exploration (TRACE), a unified framework that enhances reward contrast within a fixed sampling budget.
- **Challenge 1: Fine-grained credit assignment in multi-turn interactions**. In multi-turn agentic tasks, relying only on final outcomes makes it difficult to distinguish the quality of intermediate steps, resulting in weak policy update signals. TRACE models each ReAct-style "thought-action-observation" turn as a semantically distinct node, extending budget allocation from prompt roots to turn-level prefixes, naturally forming tree-structured rollouts to capture intermediate state values.
- **Challenge 2: Identifying high-information exploration paths under limited budget**. Blindly increasing rollout counts is inefficient; it is crucial to pinpoint paths likely to yield mixed terminal rewards. TRACE employs a shared generalizable predictor to estimate conditional success probability based on prefix history, guiding budget allocation towards roots and intermediate prefixes most likely to produce high-variance feedback, thereby amplifying policy update signals.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation**: TRACE was evaluated on typical agentic benchmarks, focusing on performance and efficiency under fixed sampling costs.
- **Comparison Results**: Compared to competitive baselines, TRACE demonstrated significant performance gains. For instance, on the Multi-Hop QA task using Qwen3-14B, TRACE improved average accuracy by 2.8 points over baselines at equal sampling cost. This validates its effectiveness in enriching outcome feedback and enhancing contrast through tree-structured rollouts.

## 4. Conclusion
TRACE addresses the inefficiency in RLVR caused by insufficient reward contrast. It breaks the limitation of traditional prompt-level budget allocation by innovatively refining allocation to prefix levels in multi-turn interactions. By leveraging tree-structured rollouts and success probability predictors, it achieves more efficient exploration and exploitation. Experiments show that TRACE significantly improves agent performance on complex reasoning tasks without increasing computational costs.
