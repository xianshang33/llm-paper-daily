## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents demonstrate strong capabilities in complex tasks. Environments, as dynamic interactive systems, are crucial for driving the continuous evolution of these models. Due to high costs, safety risks, data privacy concerns, and irreproducibility in real-world interactions, constructing simulated "agentic environments" has become key for research and benchmarking.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Despite the importance of agentic environments, existing research lacks systematic categorization and deep analysis. Previous works often focus on environment construction in specific domains or single evaluation metrics, failing to provide a unified framework from the perspective of the entire environment engineering lifecycle (modeling, synthesis, evaluation, application). This leads to fragmented knowledge, hindering progress in automated environment synthesis and agent-environment co-evolution.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Systematic Survey Framework for Agentic Environment Engineering**: Comprehensively covers four core stages: environment modeling, synthesis, evaluation, and application, viewed through the lens of the environment engineering lifecycle.
- **Challenge 1: How to systematically describe and categorize diverse agentic environments?**
  - **Difficulty**: Existing environments span numerous domains with varying attributes, lacking unified descriptive dimensions, making it difficult to compare core capabilities and development paths.
  - **Solution**: The paper introduces representative environments from the perspectives of eight attributes and eight domains, providing detailed analyses of their development paths and highlighting core capabilities, thus offering a structured view for understanding environment characteristics.
- **Challenge 2: How to achieve automated environment synthesis and effective evaluation?**
  - **Difficulty**: Manual environment construction is resource-intensive with limited scenario coverage. Automated synthesis faces paradigm differences between symbolic logic and neural representations, with inconsistent evaluation standards across paradigms.
  - **Solution**: Two paradigms for automated environment synthesis are introduced: symbolic synthesis and neural synthesis. The paper also presents suitable environment evaluation methods for each paradigm, providing technical routes for automatically building high-quality training/testing environments.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation and Deployment**: As a survey paper, this work does not propose a single new algorithmic model for deployment. Instead, it systematically reviews and categorizes existing literature on environment construction techniques, synthesis paradigms (symbolic vs. neural), and evaluation methods.
- **Evaluation Results and Comparison**:
  - **Comparison Dimension**: Rather than traditional experimental performance comparisons (e.g., accuracy improvements), the paper conducts theoretical analysis and literature comparison to highlight the lack of systematic classification in existing works.
  - **Core Analysis Results**:
    1. **Environment Modeling**: Clarifies a classification system based on 8 major attributes (e.g., dynamics, partial observability) and 8 major domains (e.g., games, robotics, social).
    2. **Environment Synthesis**: Compares the advantages, disadvantages, and applicable scenarios of symbolic synthesis (rule/logic-based) versus neural synthesis (generative model-based).
    3. **Agent-Environment Co-evolution**: Characterizes agent evolution paths in dynamic environments from four complementary perspectives: memory-centric experience evolution, orchestration-centric workflow evolution, trajectory-centric offline evolution, and exploration-centric online evolution. It also identifies three paradigms of environment evolution: neural-driven, difficulty-driven, and scaling-driven.
  - **Future Directions**: Promising directions such as Environment-as-a-Service, Multi-agent Environments, and Neural-Symbolic Environments are discussed.

#### Summary
This paper fills the gap in systematic categorization and deep analysis within the field of LLM agentic environment research. By constructing a full-lifecycle framework covering modeling, synthesis, evaluation, and application, the article not only clarifies the core capabilities and development paths of current representative environments but also deeply explores two main paradigms of automated environment synthesis and their evaluation methods. Notably, from the perspective of agent-environment co-evolution, it details four pathways for agent evolution and three paradigms for environment evolution, providing theoretical guidance and research directions for future construction of more efficient and realistic agentic training environments.
