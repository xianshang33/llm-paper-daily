## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents have significantly improved multi-turn tool-use capabilities driven by Reinforcement Learning (RL). However, existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) provides only sparse trajectory-level feedback, making it difficult to attribute success or failure to specific intermediate decisions, leading to inefficient credit assignment.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Most current methods assign credit over coarse heuristic units, such as tool-call boundaries or fixed workflows. Pilot analysis reveals that influential decision points are broadly distributed throughout the sequence, not just at tool calls, and token entropy alone is an unreliable indicator of impact, causing existing methods to miss key decision points or explore noise.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Agentic Procedural Policy Optimization (APPO)**: A framework that shifts branching and credit assignment from coarse interaction units to fine-grained decision points within the generated sequence.
- **Challenge 1: How to accurately identify high-impact branching points?**
  - **Difficulty**: Key decision points are widely distributed, and high-entropy positions often contain noise that does not affect outcomes, leading to ineffective exploration if relying solely on uncertainty.
  - **Solution**: APPO introduces a "Branching Score" that combines token uncertainty with policy-induced likelihood gains of subsequent continuations. This enables targeted exploration while filtering out spurious high-entropy positions, precisely locating decisions that truly influence results.
- **Challenge 2: How to effectively assign credit after branching to guide policy updates?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional methods assign credit at coarse granularity, failing to distinguish the differential contributions of fine-grained decisions within the same unit.
  - **Solution**: APPO introduces procedure-level advantage scaling to better distribute credit across branched rollouts, ensuring fine-grained decisions receive learning signals proportional to their actual contribution.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on 13 benchmarks against strong agentic RL baselines.
- **Key Results**: APPO consistently improves performance by nearly 4 percentage points over strong baselines.
- **Comparison**: Compared to methods relying on tool-call boundaries or fixed workflows, APPO not only enhances accuracy but also maintains efficient tool usage and behavioral interpretability. Visualizations show that rollouts resampled using APPO's Branching Score significantly outperform those based on entropy or Oracle criteria in Pass@k metrics.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the issue of coarse credit assignment in agentic RL by proposing APPO. By introducing a branching score that integrates uncertainty and likelihood gains, along with procedure-level advantage scaling, APPO transitions from coarse-unit to fine-grained decision-point optimization. Experiments demonstrate significant performance improvements across multiple benchmarks while preserving efficiency and interpretability, offering new insights for fine-grained credit assignment in long-horizon tasks.
