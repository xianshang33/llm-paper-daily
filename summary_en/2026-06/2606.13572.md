## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) show promise in general domains but struggle in specialized healthcare settings, particularly in multilingual and low-resource scenarios. In regions like rural India, patients use native Indic languages and medical images for complex queries, a use case poorly supported by existing English-centric MLLMs, limiting equitable AI healthcare access.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current medical MLLMs often use direct prediction paradigms lacking interleaved image-text reasoning required in clinical settings. Reinforcement learning approaches are constrained by base models and do not introduce new reasoning paradigms. High-quality medical Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data is costly to curate. Furthermore, research is predominantly English/Chinese-centric, leaving Indic languages underrepresented with issues of degraded logical fidelity and unstable language behavior, lacking systematic benchmarks for multilingual multimodal reasoning.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ArogyaSutra Framework and ArogyaBodha Dataset**: ArogyaBodha is a large-scale multilingual multimodal medical QA dataset covering English and seven major Indian languages, 31 body systems, 6 imaging modalities, and 21 clinical domains. ArogyaSutra is an Actor-Critic-based multi-agent framework integrating tool grounding and dual-memory mechanisms for step-wise reasoning-aware decision making.
- **Challenge 1: Low logical fidelity and unstable language behavior in multimodal medical reasoning for low-resource Indic languages**. The difficulty lies in maintaining complex medical logical consistency across languages and the scarcity of high-quality step-by-step reasoning data. The paper addresses this by using an Actor-Critic multi-agent framework with dual-memory mechanisms for step-wise decision making and distilling knowledge from stored simulation trajectories, enhancing logical coherence and language stability.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of high-quality benchmarks and data for multilingual, multimodal medical reasoning**. Existing benchmarks are mostly monolingual, text-centric, or closed-form, failing to capture real-world open-ended multimodal reasoning needs. The paper constructs ArogyaBodha from eight heterogeneous sources with expert-verified multimodal queries and unambiguous ground-truth answers, providing a reliable benchmark for evaluating logical reasoning accuracy and language fidelity across diverse clinical domains and languages.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: ArogyaSutra integrates tool grounding and dual-memory mechanisms, utilizing Actor-Critic simulation trajectories for distillation. Source code and datasets are publicly available.
- **Evaluation Results**: Experiments demonstrate that the ArogyaBodha dataset and ArogyaSutra framework significantly improve multilingual medical reasoning accuracy across all tested Indic languages. Ablation studies validate the contribution of each component.
- **Comparison**: Compared to existing English-centric MLLMs and direct prediction models, this approach achieves higher logical fidelity and reasoning capabilities for complex medical queries in Indic languages, addressing the limitations of prior work in multilingual multimodal scenarios.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the limitations of MLLMs in Indic language healthcare scenarios by introducing the ArogyaBodha dataset and the ArogyaSutra Actor-Critic multi-agent framework. By incorporating dual-memory mechanisms, tool grounding, and simulation trajectory distillation, it effectively enhances medical reasoning accuracy and logical consistency in low-resource Indic language environments, providing essential resources and solutions for promoting fairness and reliability in AI-driven healthcare services.
