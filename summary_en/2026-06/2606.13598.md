## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) built on Large Language Models (LLMs) require effective orchestrators to coordinate specialized agents for complex tasks. The field is shifting from manually designed workflows to automated, LLM-controlled orchestration.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Training capable orchestrators is hindered by two main issues. First, collecting high-quality human annotations is difficult due to strong interdependencies among orchestration strategies, task types, and sub-agent capabilities. Second, existing training frameworks (e.g., MAS-Orchestra) rely on costly full rollouts of sub-agents, leading to prohibitive computational costs and time (e.g., consuming over 1B tokens for just 100 update steps).

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Orch-RM (Orchestration Reward Modeling)**: A self-supervised framework that evaluates orchestration quality without human annotations, operating directly at the orchestration level rather than the sub-agent execution level.
- **Challenge 1: Scarcity of High-Quality Human Supervision**. Traditional RLHF relies heavily on large-scale human preference data, which is scarce for MAS orchestration. Orch-RM addresses this by leveraging intermediate artifacts from multi-agent executions to automatically construct win-lose pairs for Bradley-Terry reward model training, enabling fully self-supervised learning.
- **Challenge 2: Low Training Efficiency and High Computational Cost**. Existing methods require expensive full rollouts of sub-agents to compute rewards, resulting in high variance and latency. Orch-RM operates directly at the orchestration level, avoiding costly sub-agent rollouts, thereby significantly reducing token usage while maintaining efficient reward-guided training and test-time scaling.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: Orch-RM serves as a self-supervised reward modeling framework that can be integrated into existing MAS training pipelines for continued orchestrator optimization or to guide Test-Time Scaling.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - **Efficiency Gain**: Achieves up to 10x improvement in training efficiency in terms of token usage compared to baselines.
    - **Performance Gain**: Improves MAS test-time scaling performance by up to 8% in accuracy.
    - **Generalizability**: Gains are consistent across multiple domains, including mathematical reasoning, web-based question answering, and multi-hop reasoning.
- **Comparison**: Compared to existing frameworks like MAS-Orchestra that rely on expensive sub-agent rollouts, Orch-RM achieves a superior accuracy-efficiency trade-off, delivering better performance with substantially lower resource consumption.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper introduces Orch-RM to address the challenges of scarce supervision and high computational costs in training MAS orchestrators. By utilizing intermediate execution artifacts to generate self-supervised reward signals, Orch-RM significantly enhances both training efficiency and final performance without relying on human annotations or expensive rollouts. The work demonstrates that orchestration-level reward modeling is a scalable and effective direction for robust multi-agent systems.
