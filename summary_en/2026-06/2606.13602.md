## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Epigenomic assays (e.g., chromatin accessibility, histone modifications, DNA methylation) are crucial for understanding gene regulation but require complex analysis pipelines including alignment, peak calling, and annotation. AI agents show promise in biological data analysis, but there is a lack of standardized benchmarks to evaluate their decision-making capabilities in this domain.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current benchmarks often lack verifiability or fail to simulate specific decision points in real-world workflows. Many tasks lack deterministic grading criteria, making it difficult to accurately measure an agent's performance on tasks requiring deep scientific judgment.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed EpiBench**: A verifiable benchmark for short-horizon epigenomics analysis that evaluates whether agents can make well-defined analysis decisions from realistic workflow states and return deterministically gradable answers.
- **Challenge 1: How to construct complex bioinformatics tasks with deterministic grading criteria?**
  - **Difficulty**: Biological analysis pipelines are long and branching, with final conclusions often influenced by subjective or parameter choices, making it hard to define a single "correct" answer for automated scoring.
  - **Solution**: The paper decomposes full analysis workflows into short, gradeable decision steps (e.g., QC, peak calling, annotation) and formalizes each empirical decision into a deterministically gradable structured answer.
- **Challenge 2: How to evaluate an agent's deep scientific judgment capabilities across specific assay types?**
  - **Difficulty**: Agents may excel at code execution or file retrieval but fail when inferring scientific conclusions that require understanding specific experimental principles (e.g., differences between CUT&Tag and ChIP-seq).
  - **Solution**: The benchmark includes 106 evaluations across four major workflows (CUT&Tag/CUT&RUN, ATAC-seq, ChIP-seq, DNA methylation), comparing model performance across various task families (QC, downstream analysis, visualization) to reveal capability boundaries in specific scientific contexts.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: EpiBench comprises 106 evaluations covering four major epigenomic workflows. The study ran the benchmark on 16 model-harness pairs, with three runs per evaluation per pair, resulting in 5,088 valid trajectories. "Pi" denotes the Pi terminal coding harness.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - No system passed a majority of attempts. The top performer was GPT-5.5 / Pi with a 45.0% pass rate (143/318 attempts).
  - Followed by GPT-5.5 / OpenAI Codex (39.9%), and Claude Opus 4.8 Max / Pi and GPT-5.4 / Pi both at 39.0%.
  - Performance varied significantly by assay type and task family. Agents performed better on Quality Control (QC) tasks but struggled with downstream analyses or specific peak calling tasks requiring deeper scientific judgment.
  - Many failed runs contained parts of the correct answer; agents often found the right files and computed useful intermediate results but failed when deeper, assay-specific scientific judgment was required.
- **Comparison**: Unlike general coding or reasoning benchmarks, EpiBench is tailored to the specialized knowledge of epigenomics, revealing the limitations of current SOTA LLMs in this vertical domain—specifically, their ability to handle tool usage versus their lack of professional scientific intuition.

## 4. Conclusion
EpiBench fills a gap in the evaluation of AI agents for epigenomics by providing verifiable, deterministic grading criteria that quantify the performance of current state-of-the-art LLM agents in real-world bioinformatics workflows. The results indicate that while top models approach a 50% pass rate in some tasks, they generally fall short in scientific decision-making requiring deep domain knowledge. This benchmark provides a clear direction and evaluation standard for developing more specialized biological AI agents in the future.
