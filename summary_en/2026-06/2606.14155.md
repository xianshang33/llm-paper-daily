## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: With larger context windows and lower latency in LLMs, Context Adaptation—automating prompt engineering by iteratively revising tunable prompts from task feedback without modifying model weights—has become feasible and crucial for multi-module agentic systems.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current methods struggle with credit assignment in multi-agent systems due to black-box LLMs and discrete actions. Methods like GEPA and ACE suffer from "attribution ambiguity" due to implicit credit assignment. Graph-based methods like TextGrad rely on heuristics that are not directly coupled with the optimization objective and lack convergence guarantees.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: Graph-based Target Back-Propagation (GTBP), a context adaptation framework for agentic workflows modeled as Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs).
- **Challenge 1: Inaccurate Credit Assignment and Attribution Ambiguity**. It is difficult to attribute final errors to specific module prompts in black-box, discrete-action workflows. **Solution**: GTBP propagates local target outputs backward through the workflow graph. It uses discrepancies between targets and actual outputs to guide stage-wise prompt updates, explicitly defining the credit assignment pipeline.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of Convergence Guarantees**. Heuristic prompt updates in prior work are unstable and decoupled from the global loss function. **Solution**: The paper theoretically proves that GTBP's stage-wise prompt updates stabilize over iterations and that a sufficiently capable LLM optimizer can decrease the overall objective, providing convergence guarantees.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: GTBP models agent workflows as DAGs. After forward execution, it infers local targets for intermediate modules from the final output. Each module compares its output with the inferred target, using the resulting credit signal to update prompts without gradient access.
- **Evaluation Results**: GTBP consistently outperforms strong baselines (including TextGrad and GEPA) across three benchmarks.
- **Comparison**: GTBP achieves superior performance while maintaining comparable computational costs. Theoretical analysis confirms its stability and optimization capability compared to heuristic baselines.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses context adaptation in multi-LLM agentic systems by proposing GTBP. By introducing graph-based target back-propagation, GTBP resolves issues of inaccurate credit assignment and lack of convergence in existing methods. Both theoretical analysis and empirical results demonstrate that GTBP stably optimizes prompts and outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, offering an efficient and theoretically grounded solution for automated prompt engineering.
