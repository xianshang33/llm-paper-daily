## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: LLM agents can improve without weight updates by accumulating natural-language skills (rules, heuristics) from experience. Current systems delegate all decisions regarding skill retention and application solely to LLM judgment.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Prior methods assume LLM judgment is sufficient for the entire skill lifecycle, lacking empirical verification of a skill's actual utility. This leads to "causal heterogeneity" in skill libraries: skills that help in some contexts harm others, with opposing effects canceling out in aggregate metrics, making them invisible to global curation methods.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ASSAY Framework**: A framework that separates skill generation from curation. It computes per-skill causal attribution on a small development set, restructures the library offline, and suppresses skills with negative predicted effects for each specific test task.
- **Challenge 1: Causal Heterogeneity of Skill Utility**. Individual skills often help in some task types while hurting in others, making their net negative impact invisible to global metrics. **Solution**: The paper uses randomized masking to measure per-skill causal contributions on held-out tasks, precisely identifying skills that are harmful in specific contexts.
- **Challenge 2: Inference-time Skill-Task Matching Bottleneck**. Simply removing bad skills globally is insufficient; the key challenge is matching the right skills to the right tasks during inference. **Solution**: Introduces per-task masking to dynamically suppress skills with negative predicted effects for each test instance, preventing interference from irrelevant or harmful skills.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Setup**: Evaluated on two benchmarks (AppWorld and tau-bench) across seven base models from four providers.
- **Key Results**:
    - On the hardest split of AppWorld, DeepSeek-V3 with ASSAY achieved 69.3% task-goal completion (a 47.4% relative improvement), setting a new state-of-the-art among all published methods, including weight-tuned approaches.
    - On tau-bench retail, GPT-4.1 improved by 8.7% relatively, surpassing o4-mini, o1, and GPT-4.5 on the public leaderboard without any weight modification.
- **Comparison**: ASSAY consistently outperforms prior skill-curation approaches. Ablation studies confirm that the dominant gain comes from per-task masking, highlighting that inference-time matching is the primary bottleneck rather than just global removal of bad skills.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper identifies a systematic downside in current LLM agent skill accumulation: unchecked reliance on LLM judgment fails to account for the causal utility of skills. By proposing ASSAY, which leverages causal attribution and dynamic per-task masking, the authors effectively address skill heterogeneity and matching issues. The method significantly boosts performance across multiple mainstream models on complex interactive benchmarks without weight updates, demonstrating the importance of separating skill generation from evidence-based curation.
