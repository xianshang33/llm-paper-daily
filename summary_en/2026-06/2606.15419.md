## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) have advanced biomedical NLP, particularly in Medical Question Answering (MedQA). However, high stakes in clinical decisions require extreme factual accuracy and logical soundness, where hallucinations or logical flaws can be misleading.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current methods rely on single-model Chain-of-Thought (CoT) or majority voting ensembles. Single-model CoT lacks self-correction, while majority voting focuses only on answer agreement, ignoring reasoning quality. If multiple models share the same flawed logic, voting fails to correct systemic errors, and existing evaluations often assess reasoning post-hoc rather than integrating it into the decision loop.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Multi-Agent Peer-Reviewed Reasoning Framework**: This framework enables multiple LLM agents to act as both solvers (generating CoT with candidate answers) and peer reviewers (evaluating each other's reasoning for factual correctness and logical soundness), selecting the highest-rated reasoning chain for the final answer.
- **Challenge 1: How to prioritize reasoning quality over simple answer consensus?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional ensembles assume "majority is truth," but consistent wrong answers from flawed logic are common in complex medical tasks, making it hard to distinguish high-quality from low-quality reasoning paths.
  - **Solution**: Introduces a mutual evaluation mechanism where LLMs score the logical coherence and factual accuracy of peer reasoning chains, selecting the most logically sound path rather than just the most frequent answer.
- **Challenge 2: How to enhance robustness and interpretability without additional training data?**
  - **Difficulty**: Fine-tuning requires extensive labeled data, and black-box models lack transparency; single models are prone to prompt biases and instability.
  - **Solution**: Leverages off-the-shelf SOTA LLMs in a multi-agent setup. Role differentiation (solver vs. reviewer) stimulates emergent reasoning capabilities. The review process provides interpretability, and multi-model collaboration mitigates individual model errors, enhancing overall robustness.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The study utilized five state-of-the-art LLMs (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Phi-4, DeepSeek-LLM-7B, GPT-oss-20B). Tested on three benchmarks: HeadQA, MedQA-USMLE, and PubMedQA. Agents independently generate "reasoning-answer" pairs, cross-evaluate them, and aggregate scores to select the best output.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - Peer-reviewed reasoning consistently outperformed baselines across all datasets.
  - The best model combination achieved an average accuracy of **0.820**.
  - In comparison, the strongest single model achieved **0.777**, and CoT-based majority voting ensembles reached up to **0.789**.
  - The method scales effectively with more participating models.
  - Peer assessments reliably distinguished between high- and low-quality reasoning chains, validating its efficacy as a quality filter.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper introduces a novel multi-agent peer-reviewed reasoning method to address accuracy and interpretability in MedQA. By enabling LLMs to critique reasoning processes rather than just comparing answers, the method significantly surpasses traditional single-model CoT and majority voting baselines. Results demonstrate that emphasizing reasoning quality enhances the trustworthiness, robustness, and accuracy of medical AI systems, offering a promising direction for reliable biomedical AI.
