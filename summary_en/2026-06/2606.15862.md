## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large language model (LLM) agents have made rapid progress on short-horizon, well-scoped tasks, yet their ability to sustain coherent decisions in dynamic long-horizon environments remains uncertain.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current benchmarks mostly focus on short-horizon tasks or static environments. They lack simulations of economically grounded, partially observable, long-horizon (e.g., thousand-day scale) complex decision processes, failing to effectively evaluate agent survivability and coherence in realistic retail scenarios.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed RetailBench**: A data-grounded simulation benchmark for evaluating tool-using LLM agents in single-store supermarket operations. It models retail management as a partially observable decision process supporting thousand-day-scale simulations.
- **Challenge 1: Coherence and Survivability in Long-Horizon Decision Making**. The difficulty lies in requiring agents to handle dynamic changes like inventory aging and cash-flow constraints over hundreds of days without going bankrupt or collapsing. The paper addresses this by constructing a complex simulation environment with multi-dimensional tasks (pricing, replenishment, etc.) and a 180-day evaluation horizon, forcing agents to demonstrate long-term strategic consistency rather than short-term optimization.
- **Challenge 2: Evidence Acquisition and Deep Decision Making in Partially Observable Environments**. The challenge is that agents cannot access all state information directly and must actively gather evidence for deep reasoning rather than superficial reactions. The paper reveals current models' deficiencies in incomplete evidence acquisition and surface-level decision-making through behavioral analysis, quantifying the gap against a privileged Oracle policy.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: RetailBench simulates single-store supermarket operations, covering pricing, replenishment, supplier selection, shelf assortment, inventory aging, customer feedback, external events, and cash-flow constraints. Seven contemporary LLMs were evaluated under representative agent frameworks.
- **Evaluation Results**: Over a 180-day horizon, there was substantial variation across models. Only a small subset survived the full evaluation period. Even the strongest LLMs lagged significantly behind the privileged Oracle policy in final net worth and sales outcomes.
- **Comparison**: Behavioral analysis attributed the gap between LLMs and the Oracle to incomplete evidence acquisition, surface-level decision making, and the lack of a consistent long-horizon policy. This highlights the insufficiency of current LLM agents in achieving reliable autonomy in economically driven long-horizon decision-making.

## 4. Conclusion
RetailBench provides a controlled testbed for studying reliable autonomy in economically grounded long-horizon decision-making. The results indicate that while LLMs excel in short-term tasks, existing agents exhibit significant limitations in realistic retail environments requiring long-term coherence, complex resource management, and partial observability, particularly in long-term strategic consistency and deep reasoning.
