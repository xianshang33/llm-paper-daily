## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: As LLM agents become capable of increasingly long-horizon tasks, evaluating their performance in economic systems is becoming crucial. Economic systems are inherently multi-agent, requiring autonomous agents to communicate, negotiate, and transact while pursuing their own objectives over extended periods.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current benchmarks primarily evaluate a single agent interacting with a passive environment or multiple homogeneous firms. They fail to capture the complex dynamics of real-world economies composed of heterogeneous firms with distinct roles, nor do they adequately assess agents' capabilities in long-term multi-agent economic interactions.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed CoffeeBench**: A benchmark for evaluating LLM agents in a long-horizon multi-agent economy composed of heterogeneous firms.
- **Challenge 1: Sustained Decision-Making and State Management over Long Horizons**. The difficulty lies in requiring agents to manage cash, inventory, and pricing over a 90-day simulation to maximize cumulative net income, demanding robust long-term planning and resource management. CoffeeBench addresses this by simulating a complete supply chain (farmers, roasters, retailers) where agents must make specific business decisions in daily cycles.
- **Challenge 2: Active Communication and Negotiation among Heterogeneous Multi-Agents**. The challenge is that economic systems are not static; agents must proactively communicate and negotiate with other heterogeneous agents (suppliers, customers) having different goals to finalize transactions. CoffeeBench tackles this by assigning the evaluated agent as a roaster interacting with fixed reference agents, forcing it to initiate communications (e.g., price inquiries, payment reminders, discounts) to assess its social and economic interaction capabilities.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation and Deployment**: CoffeeBench simulates a coffee supply chain with 6 LLM agents (2 farmers, 2 roasters, 2 retailers) over 90 days. The evaluated model controls one roaster, while others are controlled by fixed reference agents. Code and agent trajectories are open-sourced.
- **Evaluation Results**: Evaluations across several recent open-weight and proprietary LLMs show that all models outperform a passive baseline that takes no actions, with most achieving positive net income.
- **Comparison and Analysis**: Behavioral analysis reveals significant differences in long-horizon economic interactions. Higher-performing models communicate more actively with other firms, whereas Claude Haiku 4.5 exhibits an "idle-drift" failure mode, repeatedly choosing inaction despite producing coherent assessments and plans. This contrasts with existing benchmarks like Vending-Bench that focus on single-agent or short-term tasks, highlighting CoffeeBench's unique value in assessing complex multi-agent dynamics.

## 4. Conclusion
CoffeeBench fills the gap in evaluating long-horizon multi-agent economic systems. By simulating complex interactions among heterogeneous firms, it reveals capability differences and specific failure modes (such as idle drift) in LLM agents within long-term business scenarios. This benchmark provides essential code and data support for future research, advancing evaluation standards for LLM agent applications in complex industries like finance and manufacturing.
