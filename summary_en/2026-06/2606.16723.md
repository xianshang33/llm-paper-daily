## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents are increasingly taking real-world actions such as screening job applicants, recommending credit, and triaging patients. However, fairness in LLMs is still predominantly measured by grading textual answers rather than evaluating the consequences of actions.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: There is a structural gap between current metrics and actual agent behavior. Answer-level fairness benchmarks do not account for actions; single-domain audits lack breadth; and capability-oriented benchmarks lack demographic instrumentation. Furthermore, the Bias Conduction Framework (BCF) demonstrates that token-level parity can coexist with action-level disparity, and agent-enhancing mechanisms (e.g., chain-of-thought) may amplify rather than mitigate bias, which existing methods fail to capture.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed AgentFairBench**: A cheap, reproducible, multi-domain benchmark (hiring, lending, medical triage) designed to measure demographic disparity in the actions of LLM agents.
- **Challenge 1: How to accurately measure subtle biases in agent actions without being misled by noise?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional statistical comparisons (e.g., comparing six-group score spreads against two-run noise differences) overstate disparity by ~2.4x due to statistic arity issues, leading to false positives.
  - **Solution**: The paper introduces an "arity-matched null methodology," computing metrics like counterfactual flip rate and Mean Absolute Score Difference (MASD) with bootstrap confidence intervals and false-discovery-rate control, ensuring significance testing against a matched noise floor.
- **Challenge 2: How to detect bias conduction across varying agent architectures?**
  - **Difficulty**: As agent agency increases (from direct response to chain-of-thought, multi-agent deliberation, and tool use), bias may be masked or amplified, making it difficult to evaluate uniformly across different scaffolds.
  - **Solution**: Grounded in the BCF, the benchmark evaluates synthetic, demographic-neutral profiles under four agent scaffolds of increasing agency, varying only name-coded race/gender signals to isolate and detect action-level disparities.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: AgentFairBench uses a NumPy-only harness, making it cost-effective (single-digit dollars per model). It features a live leaderboard with a held-out private split and a contamination canary, allowing external model submissions. Code, data, and tools are released under open licenses.
- **Evaluation Results**: In a pilot study (864 decisions plus test-retest replication), Claude Haiku 4.5 showed no demographic effects above sampling noise when evaluated against the arity-matched noise floor and omnibus group tests (0 of 120 pairwise and 0 of 9 omnibus contrasts survived correction).
- **Comparison & Validation**: A planted-bias test confirmed the instrument's sensitivity to detect disparity when present. Unlike traditional methods that might erroneously report significant differences, AgentFairBench provides a statistically rigorous foundation, demonstrating that current advanced agents may not exhibit significant discrimination under strict controls.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the critical gap between "answering" and "acting" in LLM fairness evaluation by introducing AgentFairBench. By leveraging the Bias Conduction Framework and an arity-matched null methodology, the benchmark accurately and sensitively detects demographic disparities in agent actions across high-stakes domains. The findings suggest that while current models like Claude Haiku 4.5 do not show significant bias under rigorous statistical control, the proposed framework provides an essential tool for future monitoring and mitigation of agent bias.
