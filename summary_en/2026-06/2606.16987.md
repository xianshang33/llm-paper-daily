## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Accurate Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTS) code classification is critical for customs clearance, duty assessment, and regulatory compliance in maritime logistics and smart ports. Specifically, Canada requires a complex 10-digit HTS code structure governed by legal notes and jurisdiction-specific rules.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current automated methods or general-purpose LLMs struggle with short, incomplete, or ambiguous product descriptions. Traditional single-step prediction models lack evidence grounding from official tariff documents and fail to handle the nuances of hierarchical legal structures, leading to low accuracy in fine-grained classification and poor interpretability.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A consensus-based Agentic Large Language Model (LLM) framework for Canadian 10-digit HTS code classification.
- **Challenge 1: Ambiguity in Descriptions and Lack of Legal Grounding**. Product descriptions are often vague, while correct classification depends on complex legal notes and hierarchical structures, making internal model knowledge insufficient. The paper addresses this by integrating multi-agent information retrieval and semantic retrieval over official tariff documents, enabling evidence-grounded reasoning.
- **Challenge 2: Hierarchical Consistency and Uncertainty Management**. The 10-digit code involves multiple levels (from chapters to statistical suffixes) that must be logically consistent, and the system must recognize when it is uncertain. The paper introduces element-wise voting across hierarchical components and consensus-based validation, combined with confidence estimation and human-in-the-loop escalation, to resolve inconsistencies and manage high uncertainty in fine-grained tasks.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Data**: The framework was evaluated on a private dataset of 3,300 product records labeled by domain experts, collected from logistics and delivery contexts.
- **Results**: Experiments show that exact 10-digit classification remains difficult even for advanced LLMs. Performance degrades as granularity increases, moving from coarse chapter-level predictions to fine-grained tariff and statistical suffix assignments.
- **Comparison & Conclusion**: Compared to fully autonomous single-step prediction methods, this framework offers greater interpretability and compliance. The results highlight the necessity of evidence-grounded, uncertainty-aware, and human-centered workflows over purely automated approaches for HTS classification.

#### Summary
This paper presents a novel consensus-based Agentic LLM framework to address the challenges of classifying Canadian 10-digit HTS codes in maritime logistics. By integrating multi-agent retrieval, evidence grounding, consensus validation, and human-in-the-loop mechanisms, the framework enhances interpretability and accountability. The study demonstrates that for highly specialized and legally sensitive tariff classification tasks, a hybrid workflow combining external knowledge retrieval with human expert intervention outperforms pure end-to-end deep learning or LLM prediction methods.
