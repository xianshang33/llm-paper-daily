## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Compiler optimization is critical for software performance, but predefined levels (e.g., LLVM -O3) are suboptimal for specific programs. The tuning space is vast due to the phase ordering problem and parameter settings, with effective configurations being sparse and program-dependent.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Traditional search-based autotuning is computationally expensive. Predictive modeling requires large datasets and generalizes poorly across programs and architectures. Existing LLM approaches often treat the compiler as a black box, lacking insight into internal states and struggling with complex microarchitectural effects and noisy runtime measurements.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed AutoPass**: A multi-agent framework for compiler performance tuning that uses compiler and runtime evidence to guide LLM-generated optimization decisions, operating in a training-free, inference-only setting.
- **Challenge 1: Blind decision-making due to invisible compiler internal states.**
  - **Difficulty**: Treating the compiler as a black box prevents LLMs from understanding specific pass executions and code structure changes, leading to imprecise optimization choices.
  - **Solution**: AutoPass "opens up" the compiler, allowing the LLM to query internal optimization states and analyze Intermediate Representation (IR) to orchestrate compiler options based on concrete evidence.
- **Challenge 2: Noisy runtime measurements and complex microarchitectural effects hinder regression diagnosis.**
  - **Difficulty**: Performance is influenced by various microarchitectural factors, causing measurement noise. This makes it difficult for LLMs to distinguish true improvements from regressions, leading to ineffective search oscillations.
  - **Solution**: An iterative search process uses measured runtime feedback to diagnose regressions and guide latency-improving edits, progressively refining optimization configurations for consistent performance gains.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: Implemented on LLVM and evaluated on server-grade x86-64 and embedded ARM64 systems. It requires no offline training or task-specific fine-tuning.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - Achieved a geometric-mean speedup of 1.043x over LLVM -O3 on x86-64.
  - Achieved a geometric-mean speedup of 1.117x over LLVM -O3 on ARM64.
- **Comparison**: AutoPass outperforms expert-tuned heuristics and classical autotuning methods. It addresses the high cost of search-based methods and the poor generalization of predictive models without requiring model fine-tuning.

## 4. Conclusion
AutoPass introduces a novel paradigm for compiler tuning by enabling LLMs to access internal compiler evidence (IR and optimization states) combined with runtime feedback. This approach effectively overcomes the limitations of traditional black-box tuning, achieving significant performance improvements across different hardware platforms without training, demonstrating the potential of evidence-guided LLM agents in complex system software optimization.
