## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large language model (LLM) agents are increasingly proposed as supervisory components in safety-critical systems (e.g., industrial processes, energy systems). However, their robustness under sustained, adaptive adversarial pressure remains poorly characterized.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current red-teaming benchmarks fall short in three ways: (1) They predominantly use single-turn jailbreak protocols, whereas real attackers escalate adaptively using context accumulated over sessions; (2) Harm is typically defined as policy-violating text judged by another LLM, lacking a grounded notion of physical consequence; (3) They evaluate single models in isolation, ignoring high-stakes operational settings structured around teams of role-specialized actors with explicit authority hierarchies and cross-checks.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed NRT-Bench**: A benchmark for multi-turn red-teaming of LLM agents acting as operators in a safety-critical system, instantiated in a simulated nuclear power plant control room.
- **Challenge 1: How to quantify real harm with physical consequences?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional methods rely on subjective LLM judgments of text content, failing to reflect irreversible physical damage caused by incorrect decisions in actual operations.
  - **Solution**: NRT-Bench defines harm as an objective signal rather than text judgment. A run terminates immediately when any of the six Critical Safety Functions (CSFs) is lost, attributing the cause to the specific message, thus providing a grounded assessment of physical consequences.
- **Challenge 2: How to simulate adaptive attacks in complex multi-agent collaborative environments?**
  - **Difficulty**: Real-world attacks target team collaboration vulnerabilities rather than single models, and are multi-turn and adaptive. Existing single-turn, single-model benchmarks cannot capture these collectively unsafe trajectories.
  - **Solution**: Constructed a five-role operator team, each backed by a configurable LLM. Adversaries inject messages over four channels in bounded multi-turn sessions with per-turn feedback, simulating real pressures like authority spoofing and urgency manipulation to evaluate team performance under adaptive attacks.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: Conducted in a simulated nuclear power plant control room environment with a five-role operator team and six CSFs. The simulation venue, attack dataset, and replay tooling are released for reproducible safety evaluation.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - Evaluated four frontier operator models under a fixed-attack paired-replay protocol.
  - Adaptive multi-turn attacks reliably push the operator team past safety limits: across the four models, 8.7% to 12.1% of attack sessions ended with the plant losing a critical safety function.
  - **Non-nested Vulnerabilities**: Although the four models appeared equally robust by aggregate rate, their failures barely overlapped. None of the 149 sessions defeated all four models, while one-third defeated at least one, indicating that vulnerabilities are nearly disjoint across models.
  - **Model-Dependent Defense Effects**: The same guardrail stack or safety-advisor agent could lower attack success for one model but raise it for another.
- **Comparison**: Compared to traditional benchmarks that are single-turn, text-judgment-based, and single-model focused, NRT-Bench reveals new and more complex vulnerability patterns faced by LLM agents in multi-turn interactions and team collaboration contexts.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper presents NRT-Bench, filling the gap in multi-turn red-teaming for LLM agents in safety-critical systems. By simulating a nuclear power plant control room, the study demonstrates that adaptive multi-turn attacks can effectively breach the safety defenses of current frontier models, with significant differences in vulnerabilities across models. Furthermore, the effectiveness of defense measures is highly model-dependent, suggesting limitations in universal defense strategies. This work provides a crucial benchmark and data support for evaluating the safety of LLM agents in high-risk environments.
