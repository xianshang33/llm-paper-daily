## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: The deployment of Large Language Model (LLM) agents in collaborative workflows requires serving multiple users with distinct roles, asymmetric authority levels, and strict privacy boundaries. However, contemporary LLMs are trained under a Single Principal–Agent (SPA) paradigm, optimizing exclusively for a single user's objective.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current safeguards, such as prompt engineering and fine-tuning (e.g., RLHF), offer only probabilistic soft defenses. These are vulnerable to multi-turn adversarial interactions and fail to provide deterministic guarantees for hard constraints, leading to unauthorized data access, chaotic resolution of conflicting instructions, and failure to prioritize high-authority directives.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Harness-MU**: The first model-agnostic, zero-tuning infrastructure framework for multi-user LLM agents, designed to decouple language generation from safety orchestration.
- **Challenge 1: Vulnerability of Probabilistic Safety Defenses**. Prompt-based safeguards are inherently fragile against adversarial attacks because LLMs are probabilistic. Harness-MU addresses this by treating governance constraints (authorization, restrictions, precedence) as deterministic runtime variables enforced by execution hooks rather than relying on the LLM's internal reasoning.
- **Challenge 2: Balancing Authority Conflicts and Utility**. Ensuring unbreakable permission boundaries while maximizing compliant demand satisfaction across heterogeneous users is difficult. Harness-MU solves this through systematic infrastructure orchestration, guaranteeing strict privacy preservation while significantly improving instruction-following accuracy for authorized requests.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation**: The framework was evaluated on the Muses-Bench benchmark across four frontier open-weight and proprietary models.
- **Results vs. Baselines**:
    - **Security**: Harness-MU achieved complete privacy preservation against all access-control attacks, whereas standard baselines (including most frontier LLMs) exhibited significant vulnerabilities.
    - **Utility & Accuracy**: It outperformed standard baselines by 0.28–0.39 in utility score and improved instruction-following accuracy by up to 48.9 percentage points.
    - **Advantage**: Demonstrates that infrastructure-level "Harness Engineering" yields superior safety and performance without requiring model retraining or tuning, outperforming methods that rely solely on model-centric internal safeguards.

## 4. Conclusion
Harness-MU advances the philosophy of "Harness Engineering," establishing that systematic infrastructure is essential for solving LLM multi-principal governance challenges. By decoupling deterministic governance logic from probabilistic language generation, it provides a safe, governed, and effective solution for enterprise-grade multi-user LLM deployments.
