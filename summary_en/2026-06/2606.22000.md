## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents are increasingly proposed for back-office finance tasks such as reading invoices, coding costs, reconciling systems, and moving money. Unlike chatbots that generate text, finance agents produce actions that change system states. Construction finance is high-volume, rule-bound, and exception-heavy, but unforgiving, where a single error can outweigh automation savings.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current evaluations often rely on static traces or vendor demos, failing to reflect agent performance in real multi-system software stacks (ERP, PM, email, banking portals). Traditional metrics focus on text plausibility rather than functional correctness and lack strict safety constraints for high-risk operations like fund transfers, leading to an overestimation of deployable competence based on single-attempt accuracy.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed CFAgentBench**: A reproducible, self-hostable environment and benchmark for autonomous construction-finance agents, containing 1,014 machine-gradeable task specifications across 8 domains and 77 task families.
- **Challenge 1: Difficulty in evaluating functional correctness in realistic environments**. Existing benchmarks are often static snapshots unable to verify post-operation system states. The paper constructs an executable environment with 35 mock applications using a uniform app contract. It grades tasks via deterministic state diffs, forbidden side-effect checks, and regex output validation, using LLM judges only for narrative quality, ensuring objective and functional assessment.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of safety and stability evaluation for high-risk operations**. In finance, even logically correct fund movements are unacceptable without human approval, and agents exhibit instability upon repetition. The paper introduces a "money-movement guard" where executing payments or signatures results in immediate failure, requiring agents to stage for human approval. It also contrasts pass@1 with pass@5 to reveal instability under temperature-0 decoding, demonstrating that single-attempt accuracy overstates real-world capability.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Following the WebArena pattern, CFAgentBench runs on an executable environment with 35 mock applications (31 reconciled to a company book, 4 PM platforms) categorized into 9 archetypes. Each implements a self-hostable app contract. The dataset includes a public split (n=711) and a contamination-protected private split (n=303).
- **Evaluation Results**: In an initial sweep of three open-weight models on a 40-task oracle-validated subset, the strongest agent achieved a pass@1 of 0.67 but only a pass@5 of 0.38. This indicates a 43% loss in success rate when required to repeat tasks under fixed decoding.
- **Comparison**: Compared to traditional single-success-rate metrics, CFAgentBench reveals significant heterogeneity and instability. The results show that single-attempt accuracy severely overestimates deployable competence in construction finance, highlighting stability and safety as critical barriers to adoption.

## 4. Conclusion
CFAgentBench fills a gap in evaluating autonomous agents in construction finance by providing a realistic, executable multi-system environment with strict financial safety guards. It establishes a standard closer to production needs than static benchmarks. The study emphasizes that for high-stakes domains like finance, evaluation must shift from single-task accuracy to reproducibility, functional correctness, and safety, as high single-shot accuracy does not equate to deployable reliability.
