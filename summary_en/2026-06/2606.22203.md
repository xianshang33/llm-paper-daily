## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Current studies on LLM "agent societies" primarily demonstrate emergent consensus, cooperation, or polarization. However, these works lack measurable control parameters, theoretical predictions for regime emergence, and tests to distinguish genuine social dynamics from model artifacts (e.g., sycophancy or homogenization).
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Prior research is largely demonstrative, failing to differentiate between true social interactions and model-specific biases. They lack quantitative measures of coupling gain, leading to conflated interpretations of consensus mechanisms.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A measurement protocol for per-agent coupling gain ($\\gamma$) via counterfactual perturbation, and a validity diagnostic to separate genuine averaging from model-prior artifacts.
- **Challenge 1: How to quantify inter-agent influence and distinguish its nature?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional methods assume unique social influence but struggle with quantification and sensitivity to prompt paraphrasing, making it hard to distinguish social coupling from general evidence coupling.
  - **Solution**: Directly measure $\\gamma$ by counterfactually perturbing a neighbor's opinion. The study finds $\\gamma$ is stable, model-distinguishing (0.15-0.43), and invariant to paraphrasing. Crucially, social neighbor $\\gamma$ equals impersonal numeric anchor $\\gamma$, indicating $\\gamma$ is an evidence-coupling coefficient, not uniquely social.
- **Challenge 2: How to verify if emergent consensus is a genuine group dynamic or a model-prior artifact?**
  - **Difficulty**: Models may exhibit consistency due to training priors rather than interactive averaging, a distinction often missed in existing literature.
  - **Solution**: Introduce a randomized-initial-condition diagnostic using the slope and bias of final vs. initial opinions. Applied to Chuang et al. (2023), it reveals a conflation: genuine averaging on debatable claims versus model-specific artifacts on settled facts.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Tested on 5 frontier models and 16 closed/open-source models, using interior-valued numeric facts to rule out boundary-censoring confounds.
- **Key Results**:
  1. **Dynamics**: Macro regimes are organized by classical dynamics: Friedkin-Johnsen for consensus/pluralism and signed-Laplacian/structural balance for polarization.
  2. **No Spontaneous Polarization**: Frontier LLMs do not spontaneously backfire ($\beta \\le 0$); default societies do not self-polarize. Polarization is always induced.
  3. **Context-Dependent Coupling**: Pairwise $\\gamma$ fails to predict multi-neighbour outcomes (sometimes reversing order), whereas modality-matched group coupling successfully predicts convergence (Pearson r=-0.70, p=0.008).
- **Comparison**: Re-analysis of Chuang et al. (2023) demonstrates the diagnostic's utility, exposing how previous "emergent consensus" findings conflated distinct mechanisms and contained model-specific artifacts.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper contributes a rigorous measurement protocol and validity instrument rather than new theory. By introducing measurable coupling gain $\\gamma$ and an authenticity diagnostic, it clarifies the nature of emergent consensus in LLM agent societies. It emphasizes that emergent consensus must be interpreted through coupling measured in the target interaction, providing a falsifiable framework to distinguish true social dynamics from model artifacts.
