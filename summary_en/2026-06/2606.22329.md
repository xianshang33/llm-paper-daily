## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: LLM-as-a-judge has become the dominant approach for scalable evaluation in NLP pipelines, used in benchmarks, RLHF data collection, and production quality gates.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Judge models suffer from systematic biases such as position bias, verbosity bias, and significant reliability degradation in low-resource languages. Prior studies are fragmented with incompatible setups, lacking a unified, reusable instrument to audit these biases on arbitrary judge models. Furthermore, raw accuracy metrics often hide these systemic failures.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed BabelJudge**: An open-source benchmark and reliability audit framework that measures four failure modes (position bias, verbosity bias, order inconsistency, and cross-lingual degradation) without requiring human preference labels, extended to agent trajectory evaluation.
- **Challenge 1: How to obtain reliable gold-standard labels without expensive human annotation?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional evaluation relies on costly human annotations to determine preference, which is not scalable.
  - **Solution**: Introduces "gold-labelling by degradation." By applying controlled perturbations to high-quality reference responses, the framework generates pairwise items where the reference is known to be superior by construction, eliminating annotation costs.
- **Challenge 2: How to quantify and expose systematic biases masked by raw accuracy?**
  - **Difficulty**: High raw accuracy may coexist with strong biases (e.g., preferring longer or first-placed responses), and traditional metrics fail to capture instability in low-resource languages.
  - **Solution**: Develops a composite bias-penalized reliability score that multiplicatively penalizes biases. It employs five controlled perturbation types (including verbosity probes) and nine agent-level trajectory perturbations to precisely measure specific failure modes like position and cross-lingual degradation.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: Released as a Python package supporting 11 judge backends (local, cloud APIs, OpenAI-compatible servers). It includes a multilingual corpus and perturbation engine supporting English, Hindi, Arabic, and Swahili.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - Evaluating Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-4bit, the composite reliability score drops from 0.714 in Hindi to 0.550 in Swahili.
  - Raw accuracy drops from 0.835 to 0.660, indicating that raw accuracy understates the reliability gap.
  - Order consistency in Swahili collapses to 0.480, meaning judge verdicts are near-random when slot orders are swapped—a failure mode invisible to accuracy alone.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Unlike MT-Bench or Chatbot Arena, which focus on correlation with human judgment or primarily evaluate in English, BabelJudge focuses on internal consistency and bias auditing across languages and agent trajectories, filling the gap for a unified bias audit tool.

## 4. Conclusion
BabelJudge addresses the bottleneck of human annotation in LLM judge evaluation through its innovative "degradation labeling" method. It provides a comprehensive framework for auditing position bias, verbosity bias, and cross-lingual performance degradation. The study reveals severe reliability drops in low-resource languages that are masked by raw accuracy and extends evaluation capabilities to agent trajectories. This tool offers critical infrastructure and metrics for developing more robust and fair LLM judge systems.
