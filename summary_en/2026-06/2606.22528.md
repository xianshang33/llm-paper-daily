## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Modern LLM agents increasingly rely on context compaction (summarization or eviction) to manage long-running sessions within token budgets.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current compaction mechanisms are optimized for task continuity and accuracy, often treating standing safety policies as low-salience "old" content. This creates a structural blind spot where safety constraints are silently dropped, leading to prohibited actions, a failure mode previously unaddressed as a first-class governance surface.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed "Governance Decay" and the ConstraintRot Benchmark**
- **Challenge 1: Quantifying safety constraint loss due to compaction.**
  - **Difficulty**: Isolating whether violations stem from model incapacity or context management errors is difficult without deterministic long-horizon benchmarks.
  - **Solution**: Introduced ConstraintRot, a benchmark with 1,323 episodes. Results show violation rates rise from 0% (full context) to 30% (after compaction), reaching up to 59%. If the constraint survives summarization, violations remain at 0%; if dropped, they hit 38%, proving compaction is the cause.
- **Challenge 2: Defending against adversarial attacks on the compaction mechanism (Compaction-Eviction Attack).**
  - **Difficulty**: Adversaries can inject content to bias the summarizer into omitting legitimate policies, bypassing traditional defenses.
  - **Solution**: Proposed "Constraint Pinning," a training-free mitigation that quarantines governance constraints from lossy compaction. It restores violation rates to 0% with <0.5% token overhead, effectively countering optimized injections.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Evaluation**: Tested across seven model families. Governance decay was found to be 8.3× larger for soft organizational policies than hard safety norms, highlighting vulnerability in deployment-specific constraints.
- **Attack Results**: The Compaction-Eviction Attack defeated all evaluated models. Optimized injections increased violation rates from 0% to 65% even in models immune to fixed probes.
- **Defense Performance**: Constraint Pinning reduced violations back to 0% with minimal overhead, outperforming reliance on inherent model robustness or standard prompt engineering.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper identifies context compaction as a critical safety failure surface in LLM agents, defining the "Governance Decay" phenomenon. It quantifies the risk via the ConstraintRot benchmark and demonstrates the efficacy of targeted adversarial attacks. The proposed Constraint Pinning offers an efficient, training-free defense, urging the community to treat context management as a core component of agent governance.
