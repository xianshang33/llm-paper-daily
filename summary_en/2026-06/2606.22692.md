## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Multidisciplinary tumor boards (MDTs) require rapid and precise synthesis of longitudinal electronic health records (EHRs) to reconstruct patient histories for critical clinical decisions. However, EHR data is structured for documentation rather than clinical reasoning, fragmenting patient trajectories. Clinicians currently spend significant time manually integrating these dispersed records, leading to inefficiency and potential cognitive errors due to inter-observer variability.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Traditional direct long-context prompting and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) methods have significant limitations. They often miss complex temporal relationships and incur high costs and latency because they reprocess raw text for every query. Furthermore, existing methods struggle to effectively deduplicate and synthesize coherent, clinically meaningful timelines while maintaining fidelity to source data.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed VISTA Architect**: A database-oriented AI architecture that integrates Large Language Models (LLMs) with longitudinal EHRs. It transforms complex clinical documentation into a persistent, provenance-linked knowledge graph at ingestion, eliminating repeated processing of raw records at query time.
- **Challenge 1: How to accurately capture and maintain complex temporal relationships and data provenance?**
  - **Difficulty**: Clinical events span long periods and are scattered across various document types, making it easy to lose temporal logic and difficult to verify authenticity when processed directly.
  - **Solution**: The system employs a two-layer architecture. The bottom layer is a "Source-faithful MEDS Graph" that preserves granular EHR structure and full provenance. The top layer is a "Timeline Object Architecture (TOA)" that uses graph-guided LLM extraction to synthesize a concise, deduplicated, and temporally coherent timeline of clinical events.
- **Challenge 2: How to address high latency and cost in real-time queries?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional RAG or long-context methods require re-reading and processing large amounts of raw text for each query, resulting in slow response times and high computational costs.
  - **Solution**: Adopts a "pre-computation" strategy where clinical synthesis is performed once during data ingestion. Downstream queries access the organized patient state directly, traversing to source documents only when detailed verification is needed, significantly reducing computational burden during queries.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The system was demonstrated in multidisciplinary thoracic oncology tumor boards at Stanford Medicine. Its modular design allows adaptation to other specialties through customizable event definitions and agentic tools.
- **Evaluation Results**: On a dataset of 1,180 patients, VISTA Architect achieved 96.4% accuracy (mean score 9.75/10) across 17,700 evaluations of 15 tumor board-salient variables (95% CI 96.1–96.7%).
- **Comparison with Related Work**: This performance surpassed a matched BM25 RAG baseline and recent benchmarks for LLM-based clinical extraction. Additionally, an agentic interface reduced the preparation time for a 30-patient held-out cohort to approximately 2.2 minutes without sacrificing accuracy.

#### Summary
VISTA Architect successfully addresses issues of temporal consistency, source provenance, and computational efficiency in longitudinal EHR processing by introducing a graph-database-oriented architecture. By shifting the computationally intensive clinical synthesis to the data ingestion phase, it enables efficient and accurate reconstruction of patient histories, significantly reducing clinician burden. It demonstrates superior performance over traditional RAG methods in real-world thoracic oncology scenarios, with a modular design that lays the groundwork for future expansion into other medical specialties.
