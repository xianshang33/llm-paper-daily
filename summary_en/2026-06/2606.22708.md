## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Current generative music systems (e.g., Suno, MusicGen) produce high-quality audio from text prompts, but these outputs are difficult to inspect, edit, or diagnose as musical structures. Waveforms do not directly expose note timing, voice assignment, or harmonic motion.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Existing symbolic music methods often treat music as raw token sequences. Compact formats like ABC have implicit timing, making it hard for LLM agents to reason about onsets and perform local edits. Furthermore, evaluations typically rely on human preference or global quality scores, lacking interpretable structural feedback needed for agent-driven revision.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Libretto Framework**: An agent-facing framework for symbolic music generation and revision that transforms symbolic music from raw token sequences into measurable and editable objects.
- **Challenge 1: LLMs struggle to manage structured timing and voice relationships in music.**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional symbolic representations have implicit timing, making it hard for LLMs to control onset positions and voice interleaving, leading to structurally chaotic outputs.
  - **Solution**: Libretto uses an LLM-native grammar with explicit onset slots, voices, and bar-level organization, making timing and structure directly readable and locally editable for LLMs.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of interpretable evaluation mechanisms to guide agent self-revision.**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional evaluations provide only overall scores without identifying specific defects in rhythm, harmony, or form, preventing targeted corrections by agents.
  - **Solution**: Libretto constructs a corpus-calibrated statistical space across axes such as rhythm, harmony, melody, texture, form, and variation. This allows agents to diagnose generated pieces based on their position in this statistical space, supporting retrieval, risk control, and iterative self-revision.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Tasks**: Libretto is applied to tasks including gap filling, reference-guided full-piece generation, gradual morphing, and educational music generation.
- **Evaluation Results**: By mapping music to interpretable structural axes, Libretto makes the generation process diagnosable. Compared to systems relying on likelihood or black-box aesthetic predictors, Libretto enables agents to self-correct based on statistical deviations from existing music corpora.
- **Comparison Advantages**: Unlike traditional sequence models requiring specialized deployment, Libretto leverages LLM native capabilities with structured grammar. Compared to pure audio generation models, it offers editable symbolic representations and fine-grained structural control.

## 4. Conclusion
Libretto addresses the "structural blindness" of LLM agents in music generation. By introducing an LLM-native grammar with explicit structures and a statistical evaluation space based on multi-dimensional structural axes, it transforms symbolic music into a measurable and editable object. This enhances the structural coherence of generated music and empowers agents to diagnose errors and iteratively revise outputs, offering a new paradigm for controllable music creation.
