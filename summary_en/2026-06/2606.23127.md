## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: As LLM agents are increasingly deployed in industrial workflows for recurring tasks (e.g., document processing, database queries), there is a growing need for mechanisms to improve reuse, reliability, and efficiency. Procedural memory, which distills reusable instructions and strategies from past trajectories, is a promising solution.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current understanding of procedural memory's ability to produce reusable skills is limited. Skills extracted from narrow experiences often overfit to the source context, failing when tasks, roles, or model backbones change. There is a lack of systematic benchmarks to evaluate the trade-off between specialization and generalization across these dimensions.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed AFTER Benchmark**: A benchmark comprising 382 realistic enterprise tasks spanning six professional roles and 22 procedural skills, designed to evaluate skill transfer across tasks, roles, and model backbones.
- **Challenge 1: Quantifying the Specialization-Generalization Trade-off**. It is difficult to balance skills that are specific to a workflow versus those that are broadly applicable. The paper addresses this by using controlled evaluation settings in AFTER to measure "Specificity Gain" and "Generality Gain," demonstrating that diverse experience helps achieve both high specificity and high generality.
- **Challenge 2: Uncertainty in Cross-Model and Cross-Domain Transfer**. Determining which skill sources are robust across different models is challenging due to trajectory variations. The paper shows that skills evolved from diverse multi-model execution traces achieve 73.1% accuracy in cross-model tests, outperforming all single-model sources, highlighting the importance of diversity for transfer learning.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Controlled evaluations using the AFTER benchmark across four scenarios: local improvement, cross-task transfer, cross-role transfer, and cross-model generalization.
- **Key Results**:
    - Procedural memory delivers consistent gains: a single refinement round improves aggregate performance by 3.7-6.7 points.
    - Skills evolved from diverse multi-model traces achieve 73.1% cross-model test accuracy, significantly outperforming single-model sources.
    - Some skills generalize broadly, while others become specialized to role-specific workflows and lose effectiveness under transfer.
- **Comparison**: Compared to methods relying on single models or narrow experiences, the proposed approach using diverse experiences demonstrates superior generalization capabilities and avoids source-context overfitting.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper introduces the AFTER benchmark to systematically study the control, adaptation, and evaluation of procedural memory in LLM agents. The results indicate that while procedural memory significantly boosts performance, the source of skills is critical. Skills derived from diverse multi-model experiences better balance specialization and generalization, enabling effective cross-domain transfer. These findings provide practical guidance for building and deploying procedural memory systems in production environments.
