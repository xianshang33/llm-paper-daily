## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: LLM agents increasingly load "Skills"—file-based packages of natural-language instructions written by third parties and distributed via marketplaces. These execute with user privileges, creating a new attack surface where a single malicious skill can exfiltrate data or hijack the agent.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Traditional prompt-injection defenses rely on a boundary between trusted instructions and untrusted data. However, skills consist entirely of instructions, so malicious commands blend in with legitimate ones and inherit their authority. Direct LLM scanning is too expensive for marketplace-scale deployment, and existing attention-based detectors fail because the attention signal collapses when there is no clear instruction-data separation.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Locate-and-Judge**, a two-stage detector designed specifically for the regime of mixed legitimate and malicious instructions.
- **Challenge 1: Distinguishing malicious injections within a body of pure instructions.**
  - Difficulty: Malicious content lacks clear boundaries, and attention signals are ubiquitous in legitimate instructions, causing signal collapse.
  - Solution: Leveraging the hypothesis that effective attacks must capture instruction-following attention, a lightweight "Locator" scores structural spans by attention intensity and retains only the top-K high-attention spans to isolate potential threats from noise.
- **Challenge 2: Achieving scalable, full-marketplace auditing instead of sampling.**
  - Difficulty: Running powerful LLM judgments on entire skills is prohibitively expensive at scale.
  - Solution: Concentrating the costly "Judge" phase only on the few high-attention spans identified by the Locator. This reduces costs by an order of magnitude compared to direct scanning, enabling comprehensive audits at negligible expense.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: Locate-and-Judge is deployed at marketplace scale with negligible cost. It uses a small LLM for localization and a detailed judge for the selected spans.
- **Evaluation Results**: The system flags skills with high precision, with manual confirmation verifying the majority as malicious. It surfaced dozens of live malicious skills, including those disguised as benign functionality.
- **Comparison with Related Work**:
  - **Cost & Scalability**: Offers an order-of-magnitude cost reduction compared to direct LLM-based scanning, dramatically increasing scalability with minimal loss in recall.
  - **Baselines**: Dominates keyword and regex baselines at comparable expense.
  - **Existing Tools**: Detected many malicious skills that state-of-the-art tools like SkillSpector and Cisco Skill Scanner failed to identify, demonstrating superior capability against obfuscation.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the emerging security threat of malicious skills in LLM agent marketplaces by introducing Locate-and-Judge. By decoupling localization from judgment and leveraging attention mechanisms to efficiently filter high-risk segments, it overcomes the limitations of traditional defenses and the high costs of full scanning. The method achieves high-precision detection at scale and releases a labeled dataset to support future research.
