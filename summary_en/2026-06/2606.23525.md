## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: As Large Language Model (LLM) agents tackle increasingly complex problems, their generated chains of thought and tool call traces become excessively long. These long traces accumulate stale or erroneous content (known as "context rot"), which not only consumes the context window but also anchors subsequent generations, degrading model performance.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current mitigation strategies typically rely on fixed-interval compaction triggered by token thresholds. These rigid triggers ignore the structure of the reasoning trajectory, risking the discard of partial results mid-derivation or mid-search, leading to loss of critical information or inefficient computation.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SelfCompact Framework**: A scaffold that allows the model to decide when and how to compact its context during inference, without requiring fine-tuning or external supervision.
- **Challenge 1: Models lack meta-cognitive awareness of context rot**. Unprompted models cannot reliably detect when their context is becoming ineffective. SelfCompact addresses this by introducing a lightweight rubric that specifies when to trigger compaction (e.g., sub-task resolved, trajectory converging) and when to suppress it (e.g., mid-derivation, stuck), thereby closing this meta-cognitive gap.
- **Challenge 2: Inconsistent and ill-timed use of compaction tools**. Providing a compaction tool alone leads to uneven usage, with models invoking it at unhelpful moments or not at all. SelfCompact pairs the tool with the rubric, ensuring adaptive compaction occurs only at logical breakpoints, preserving key intermediate results while clearing redundancy.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: SelfCompact consists of two inference-time elements: (i) a compaction tool invoked by the model to summarize accumulated context, and (ii) a lightweight rubric guiding trigger and suppression conditions. It requires no training and is applied directly to existing open-weight models.
- **Evaluation Results**: Empirical results are presented across six benchmarks (competitive math and agentic search) and seven models.
- **Comparison with Related Work**:
    - vs. No-compression baseline: Improves scores by up to 18.1 points on math and 5–9 points on agentic search.
    - vs. Fixed-interval summarization: Matches or exceeds performance at a fraction of the token cost.
    - Cost Efficiency: Reduces per-question token cost by 30–70%.

## 4. Conclusion
SelfCompact exposes the meta-cognitive gap in LLM agents and successfully addresses context rot in long trajectories through simple inference-time rules. It demonstrates that "when to compact" can be a capability supplied by scaffolds without additional model training. The method significantly reduces computational costs while improving agent performance in complex reasoning and search tasks, offering a new paradigm for efficient long-context agent operation.
