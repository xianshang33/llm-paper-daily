## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) offer a scalable path for agentic AI by coordinating multiple LLM-based agents. System prompts, which define agent roles and behaviors, represent a critical and accessible optimization surface for improving system performance without model fine-tuning.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: While prompt optimization has shown substantial potential in single-agent settings, extending it to MAS poses distinct challenges, notably an exponentially growing search space. It remains unclear whether, when, and by how much prompt optimization improves MAS performance, and how sensitive these gains are to system configurations.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed MAS-PromptBench**: A systematic benchmark framework to study system-prompt optimization across a broad range of MAS setups, varying in tasks, workflows, communication protocols, and team sizes. It benchmarks two prompt optimizers that naturally extend state-of-the-art single-agent methods.
- **Challenge 1: Uncertainty and Instability of Optimization Gains in MAS**. Due to complex inter-agent interactions, directly applying single-agent optimization methods can lead to significant performance degradation rather than improvement. **Solution**: The paper quantifies this variability by evaluating optimizers across diverse MAS topologies (e.g., independent vs. centralized), revealing that gains are highly dependent on the specific workflow structure.
- **Challenge 2: Exploding Search Space and Configuration Sensitivity**. Jointly optimizing prompts for multiple agents creates a vast combinatorial search space, with gains being highly sensitive to system settings. **Solution**: Through extensive experiments, the study characterizes the boundaries of effectiveness for prompt optimization in MAS, identifying conditions under which it yields significant benefits versus when it fails.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation**: The study employs two prompt optimizers extended from state-of-the-art single-agent methods (e.g., GEPA) and evaluates them across various MAS configurations. Tasks include MATH, APPS, BFCL, and HotpotQA, with workflows ranging from independent to centralized architectures.
- **Results & Comparison**:
    - **Single-Agent vs. Multi-Agent**: Results show that while GEPA consistently improves single-agent performance across all five tasks (gains of +4% to +14%), its natural extension to MAS yields highly variable outcomes, ranging from large gains to severe performance drops (e.g., -9%, -16%).
    - **Key Findings**: Prompt optimization in MAS is not universally beneficial; its effectiveness is heavily dependent on task type and workflow topology. For instance, optimization caused significant regressions in certain centralized workflows.
    - **Significance**: This work bridges the gap between the success of single-agent prompt optimization and its application in MAS, highlighting the risks of direct transfer and providing empirical evidence on the sensitivity of MAS performance to prompt changes.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper systematically investigates prompt optimization in multi-agent systems via MAS-PromptBench. It reveals that while prompt optimization is robust in single-agent scenarios, its effects in MAS are highly uncertain and configuration-dependent, sometimes leading to significant performance degradation. The work highlights the limitations of directly extending single-agent optimization strategies to MAS and emphasizes the need for more robust, MAS-specific optimization techniques, offering crucial insights for practitioners on when and how to apply prompt optimization effectively.
