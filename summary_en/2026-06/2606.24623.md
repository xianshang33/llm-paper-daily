## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) enhances LLMs with external knowledge but poses privacy risks in sensitive scenarios (e.g., healthcare) where malicious prompts can extract private data from the retrieval corpus.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current methods struggle to balance privacy preservation with contextual fidelity. Simple redaction often breaks semantic coherence, while more complex methods may fail to fully sanitize sensitive identifiers or introduce significant latency.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Multi-Agent Semantic Rewriting Framework**: A system that sanitizes retrieved content using three specialized agents for privacy extraction, semantic analysis, and reconstruction.
- **Challenge 1: How to remove sensitive identifiers while preserving core semantics?**
  - Difficulty: Naive masking disrupts context and utility. The paper addresses this by employing collaborative agents that precisely identify sensitive entities and reconstruct sentences to maintain semantic integrity without exposing private details.
- **Challenge 2: How to achieve privacy protection without adding online inference latency?**
  - Difficulty: Real-time processing adds overhead. The solution designs the rewriting as an asynchronous, one-time offline preprocessing step, ensuring zero additional latency during online inference.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Setup**: Evaluated on ChatDoctor and Wiki-PII datasets across six LLMs.
- **Privacy Results**: Significant reduction in leakage under targeted attacks. For LLaMA-3-8B, exposed instances dropped from 144 (baseline) to just 1.
- **Fidelity Comparison**: Maintains strong contextual fidelity with a BLEU-1 score of 0.122, outperforming the existing SOTA method SAGE (0.117).
- **Deployment**: Operates as an offline preprocessing module, introducing no latency to the online serving path.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper presents a novel multi-agent framework for privacy-preserving RAG. By leveraging offline semantic rewriting, it effectively eliminates targeted privacy leaks while maintaining superior semantic fidelity compared to existing methods like SAGE, all without compromising online inference performance.
