## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Birth defects are a major cause of fetal loss and long-term disability. For cases with suspected genetic etiologies, the diagnostic bottleneck has shifted from variant detection to post-sequencing interpretation. Clinicians must rank patient-specific candidate variants amidst incomplete phenotypes and heterogeneous evidence sources (population genetics, variant effects, gene-disease validity, etc.).
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Traditional automated pipelines (e.g., Exomiser) or standalone deep learning models struggle to effectively integrate highly heterogeneous evidence and handle incomplete clinical phenotypes. Existing LLM-based reranking methods often lack grounded evidence support, leading to potential hallucinations, and fail to effectively separate and synergize data-driven evidence integration, tool-based refinement, and LLM-assisted diagnostic review.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed DeepBD**, a grounded agentic workflow for variant prioritization and diagnostic interpretation of genetic birth defects.
- **Challenge 1: Effective Integration of Heterogeneous Evidence and Patient-Specific Scoring**. The difficulty lies in unifying diverse evidence sources (rules, sequences, biological context) into a coherent metric for specific patients. DeepBD addresses this via a pretrained evidence engine that learns patient-specific variant scores from structured rule evidence, sequence/variant-effect representations, and phenotype-conditioned biological contexts.
- **Challenge 2: Diagnostic Accuracy and Interpretability in Complex Clinical Scenarios**. Ranking alone is insufficient for final diagnosis; professional validation and clinical logic synthesis are required. DeepBD introduces specialist evidence modules and an agentic layer that provide tool-based refinement, candidate-pool review, and diagnosis-oriented synthesis. This ensures logical rigor and traceability through LLM-assisted case structuring and a grounded diagnostic review layer.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Dataset**: Developed and evaluated using an in-house cohort of 18,622 fetal and infant cases.
- **Metrics**: On an internal held-out solved-case benchmark, DeepBD achieved Recall@1/3/5/10 of 0.658/0.882/0.912/0.929.
- **Comparison**: DeepBD significantly outperformed standalone Exomiser, DeepRare, and prompted LLM reranking baselines evaluated on Exomiser-derived top-20 candidate variants.
- **Ablation Studies**: Analyses confirmed that rule evidence, mechanistic context, and specialist refinement provide complementary signals, validating the necessity of the modular design.

## 4. Conclusion
DeepBD demonstrates a grounded agentic approach that assigns distinct roles to data-driven evidence integration, tool-based refinement, and LLM-assisted diagnostic review. This method effectively addresses the complexities of retrospective variant prioritization in genetic birth defects, enhancing diagnostic accuracy and robustness through its modular architecture, offering a new paradigm for clinical genomic interpretation.
