## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Multi-agent LLM systems (e.g., LAMBDA) are increasingly used for automated data analysis, producing rich, multi-step outputs including code, numerical results, and diagnostics.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current benchmark scorers (like DSGym's exact_match) are often too rigid or simplistic, failing to distinguish genuine disagreements from grading artifacts. They struggle to extract intended answers from complex, interleaved outputs, leading to false negatives (e.g., treating "0.2" and "0.20" as different) and lacking robustness against grader errors.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a three-layer human-AI grading cascade and an iterative nudging mechanism**
- **Challenge 1: Accurately extracting numerical answers from rich, multimodal agent outputs is difficult, with traditional heuristics having low recall.**
  - **Difficulty**: Agent outputs interleave code, logs, and natural language, making simple "last-number" heuristics error-prone.
  - **Solution**: Developed a keyword-anchored extraction pipeline, which increased the strict grader's recall by 60 percentage points compared to the last-number heuristic.
- **Challenge 2: Automated graders produce false negatives due to minor format differences, and initial grading run success rates are low.**
  - **Difficulty**: Strict regex matching fails on semantically identical but format-different answers, and initial grading success was only 36%.
  - **Solution**: Introduced an LLM-based lenient grader (parser-independent) and an iterative "nudging" mechanism. Nudging raised grading run success from 36% to 97% and lenient-pass rates from 16% to 46%; it also confirmed that re-injecting the original question offers no benefit, identifying nudging primarily as an answer template cue.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Setup**: Applied the LAMBDA multi-agent system to 153 numerical QRData tasks from DSGym.
- **Grading Cascade**: Evaluated a three-layer cascade: strict regex matching, LLM-based lenient grading, and snippet-based human inspection.
- **Results**:
  - **Precision**: Both automated graders achieved 100% observed precision (0/70 false positives).
  - **Recall**: The lenient grader achieved 97% recall against human labels.
  - **Improvements**: Keyword-anchored extraction significantly outperformed the last-number heuristic; iterative nudging drastically improved grading success and pass rates.
  - **Insight**: Variable type was identified as the task metadata field most consistently associated with grading pipeline dynamics and outcomes.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper systematically investigates the reliability of automated graders for agentic data analysis outputs and proposes strategies to improve grading quality. By combining non-GenAI (regex) and GenAI (LLM lenient grading) with human calibration, it establishes a high-precision grading cascade. The study highlights the challenges of answer extraction from complex agent outputs and demonstrates that iterative nudging significantly enhances the robustness and success rate of the evaluation pipeline, offering key insights for evaluating agentic workflows in verifiable data science.
