## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Recent LLM role-playing systems construct character agents by extracting characters, scenes, and relations from novels. Maintaining character authenticity and consistency is critical in long-narrative role-playing.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current methods suffer from two "Out-of-Character" (OOC) failures:
    1. **Factual Overreach**: Shared retrieval or parametric memory allows characters to access facts outside their perspective (e.g., knowing truths they were not present to witness).
    2. **Stylistic Monotony**: Static character profiles flatten characters into a fixed voice, failing to adapt tone and behavior dynamically to evolving narrative situations.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed REVERIEMEM**, a three-layer memory architecture for book-based character agents designed to enforce perspective-bounded memory.
- **Challenge 1: Factual Overreach**
    - **Difficulty**: Traditional RAG systems retrieve from shared book-level memory, or LLMs use pre-trained knowledge, causing characters to access information unavailable within their specific narrative perspective, breaking logical boundaries.
    - **Solution**: REVERIEMEM introduces an **Episodic Layer** for first-person scene memories and a **Semantic Layer** for visibility-tagged facts. By strictly restricting access to information visible only within the character's perspective, it ensures high fidelity to knowledge boundaries.
- **Challenge 2: Stylistic Monotony**
    - **Difficulty**: Static profiles cannot capture the complex emotional and behavioral shifts of a character across different contexts, leading to flat and repetitive dialogue styles.
    - **Solution**: REVERIEMEM incorporates a **Personality Layer** that stores situation-dependent speech and behavior patterns. This allows characters to dynamically adjust their expression based on the current narrative state, generating more vivid and context-grounded narratives.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Benchmark Construction**: The authors constructed **KBF-QA**, a benchmark with 4,386 questions across eight novels, specifically to test knowledge boundaries.
- **Key Results**:
    - **Knowledge Boundary Fidelity**: REVERIEMEM improved Knowledge Boundary Fidelity by **34.6 percentage points** over the strongest prior method.
    - **Narrative Quality**: On the BOOKWORLD five-dimension pairwise narrative protocol, REVERIEMEM achieved a win rate of approximately **79%**.
- **Comparison Conclusion**: Experiments demonstrate that perspective-bounded memory significantly reduces factual overreach errors and enhances the quality of character-grounded narrative generation, outperforming existing methods based on static profiles or shared memory.

#### Summary
This paper addresses factual overreach and stylistic monotony in long-narrative role-playing by proposing REVERIEMEM, a three-layer memory architecture. By coordinating episodic, semantic, and personality layers, it achieves perspective-bounded memory management and dynamic style adaptation. Extensive evaluations on the custom KBF-QA benchmark and the existing BOOKWORLD benchmark confirm that this method significantly outperforms prior art in maintaining knowledge boundary fidelity and enhancing narrative vividness, offering a new solution for building high-fidelity novel-based character agents.
