## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Computer-use agents translate Vision-Language Model (VLM) predictions into executable GUI clicks. Reliable Uncertainty Quantification (UQ) is critical for rejection, calibration, miss-severity ranking, and defining spatial safety regions, as each prediction triggers a system action.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current evidence on post-hoc UQ is fragmented across isolated model-dataset pairs. It remains unclear whether UQ method rankings remain stable when the agent architecture, benchmark, or observable interface (e.g., access to logits vs. API-only) changes, hindering the selection of robust UQ strategies.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Argus Benchmark**: A cross-regime benchmark for post-hoc UQ in single-step executable GUI grounding, covering both open-weight and closed-source scenarios.
- **Challenge 1: Generalizability of UQ Methods Across Open and Closed Models**. The difficulty lies in the inaccessibility of internal states (logits, hidden states) in closed-source models and the behavioral divergence across vendors. The paper addresses this by evaluating a 27-method matrix across 4 open VLMs and 4 datasets, and an 8-method API-compatible matrix across 3 closed-source vendors. It reveals that while UQ rankings are highly stable within a fixed model across datasets (Spearman rho up to 0.969), they degrade significantly across model classes and interfaces (average rho of only +0.08 for closed-source transfer), proving that closed-source UQ must be reranked on-target rather than extrapolated.
- **Challenge 2: Score-Level Discrimination is Insufficient for Spatial Safety in Deployment**. The challenge is that uncertainty scores alone do not define safe execution zones, and coverage degrades under distribution shifts. The paper employs split-conformal prediction to generate click regions, showing that while calibrated locally weighted disks can shrink radii by 40-60%, coverage suffers under calibration-test or interface mismatches, highlighting the need for regime-aware UQ selection.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Setup**: Evaluated seven families of UQ methods: logit-based scores, sampling/consistency measures (e.g., semantic entropy), hidden-state/density estimators (e.g., Mahalanobis, SAPLMA), attention-based scores, verbalized confidence, and conformal prediction.
- **Key Results**:
    - **Stability**: Hidden-state and density methods are the most stable in the open-weight family. CoCoA-1MCA, Focus, sampling-based scores, and verbalized self-assessment win in specific regimes.
    - **Comparative Findings**: Attention, verbalized, and VLM-native families lose AUROC across all datasets when model classes change, whereas density methods remain stable. Logit-family degradation on ScreenSpot-Pro is partly confounded by scale issues.
    - **Closed-Source Limitations**: Due to weak cross-tier transfer, UQ recommendations for closed-source models cannot be generalized from open-source findings and require independent evaluation.
- **Deployment Insights**: Conformal click regions demonstrate that score discrimination is insufficient; calibration is essential for shrinking safety radii, but practitioners must account for coverage degradation under distribution shifts.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper introduces the Argus benchmark to systematically evaluate UQ for computer-use agents. It uncovers a "selective transfer" phenomenon: UQ rankings are stable within a model across datasets but fail across architectures and interfaces, particularly from open to closed sources. The study identifies hidden-state and density methods as most robust for open weights, while emphasizing that closed-source UQ requires target-specific reranking. Furthermore, it highlights that combining UQ with conformal prediction for spatial safety is crucial for deployment, though sensitive to distribution mismatches. This work provides a reproducible foundation for regime-aware UQ selection in GUI agents.
