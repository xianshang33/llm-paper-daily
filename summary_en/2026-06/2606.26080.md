## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training has become the dominant paradigm for Large Language Model (LLM) agents. However, evaluating the quality of agent behavior at each step (process reward) is crucial for long-horizon, multi-turn interactions, while existing Outcome Reward Models only provide coarse final signals, failing to guide credit assignment effectively.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Building Process Reward Models (PRMs) for agents is prohibitively difficult due to long horizons, irreversible actions (e.g., sending emails), and stochastic environment feedback. These factors make traditional Monte Carlo estimation and large-scale human annotation infeasible. Furthermore, domain-specific PRMs often lack generalization across different tasks or environments.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: "Progress Advantage," an implicit step-level scoring signal derived directly from standard RL post-training, eliminating the need for dedicated reward model training.
- **Challenge 1: How to obtain fine-grained step-level reward signals without expensive annotation or simulation?**
  - **Difficulty**: The complexity and irreversibility of agent environments make traditional data collection methods (human labeling or MCTS) unscalable.
  - **Solution**: The paper proves that under a general stochastic MDP, the log-probability ratio between the RL-trained policy and its reference policy exactly recovers the optimal advantage function. This "Progress Advantage" is available as a byproduct of RL post-training, offering an annotation-free, domain-agnostic signal.
- **Challenge 2: How to ensure the generality and effectiveness of this implicit signal across diverse tasks and models?**
  - **Difficulty**: Explicitly trained reward models often overfit to specific tasks and fail to generalize.
  - **Solution**: Since Progress Advantage stems from the mathematical essence of policy optimization, it is independent of task-specific feature engineering. Experiments demonstrate its robust generalization across multiple benchmarks and model families, outperforming confidence-based baselines and even specialized trained reward models.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The method requires no additional training pipelines or annotated data. It computes the log-probability ratio using logs from the behavior policy and reference policy generated during standard RL post-training, allowing seamless integration into existing LLM agent systems.
- **Evaluation Results**: The effectiveness of Progress Advantage was validated across three applications: test-time scaling, uncertainty quantification, and failure attribution, using five benchmarks and four model families.
- **Comparison with Related Work**:
  - **vs. Confidence-based Baselines**: Progress Advantage consistently outperforms traditional baselines relying on model output confidence.
  - **vs. Dedicated Process Reward Models (PRMs)**: Despite requiring no task-specific training, Progress Advantage surpasses the performance of explicitly trained PRMs, indicating that the implicit signal extracted from RL is more robust and generalizable.

#### Summary
This paper uncovers a neglected "free lunch" in RL post-training: Progress Advantage. By theoretically proving that the log-probability ratio between the trained and reference policies serves as the optimal advantage function, it provides high-quality, annotation-free step-level evaluation for LLM agents. This approach addresses the core challenges of high cost, difficulty, and poor generalization in process reward modeling for agents. It demonstrates superior performance across multiple applications compared to state-of-the-art methods, offering practical guidance for monitoring and optimizing real-world agent systems.
