## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning is widely used in LLM-based agents. However, prior work indicates that verbalized CoT is not always faithful and may reflect post-hoc rationalization, where the model knows the answer before generating the reasoning.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current evaluations typically focus only on final task success rates, failing to distinguish whether performance improvements stem from genuine reasoning-based action revision or from the model becoming better at predicting actions directly from the prompt. This "shortcut" learning makes it difficult to diagnose the true source of gains from CoT training.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A fine-grained diagnostic framework for attributing CoT training gains in LLM agents, along with a method to improve generalization via selective masking of action token supervision.
- **Challenge 1**: It is difficult to distinguish whether CoT training enhances reasoning-based action revision or direct prompt-based action prediction, as final performance metrics can mask the actual contribution of the reasoning process. The paper addresses this by comparing "prompt actions" (predicted without CoT) and "CoT actions" (predicted after reasoning). It finds that prompt action quality improves substantially during training, while the relative advantage of CoT over prompt actions remains stable, indicating that training primarily boosts direct prediction capabilities rather than widening the reasoning gap.
- **Challenge 2**: Models become less reliant on CoT in later training stages, limiting out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization. The authors observe that later checkpoints are less likely to revise actions based on CoT, showing increased reliance on the prompt. To mitigate this, they propose selectively masking action token supervision on a fraction of training examples, which forces greater reliance on reasoning and improves OOD generalization.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation**: The study combines offline step-level comparisons, online evaluation on unseen tasks, and conflicting-trace tests. It also includes attention and gradient analyses to explain the structural advantage of prompts in action prediction.
- **Results**:
    1. Prompt action quality improves significantly across training checkpoints.
    2. The relative advantage of CoT actions over prompt actions remains constant during environment interaction, suggesting CoT training does not expand the benefit of reasoning but rather improves direct prompt-based prediction.
    3. Later checkpoints show reduced likelihood of revising actions in response to CoT, indicating heightened dependence on the prompt.
    4. The proposed selective masking intervention effectively enhances out-of-domain generalization.
- **Comparison**: Unlike prior works that assume CoT inherently boosts reasoning or focus solely on final accuracy, this paper reveals the "shortcut" phenomenon in CoT training and demonstrates that simple supervision interventions can yield more robust agents than merely scaling CoT data.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper investigates the actual mechanism of CoT training gains in LLM agents, revealing that the primary benefit lies in improving the model's ability to predict actions directly from the prompt, rather than significantly enhancing reasoning-based action revision. Addressing the issue of excessive prompt reliance in later training stages, the authors propose a simple selective masking strategy that effectively improves out-of-distribution generalization. These findings are crucial for understanding the nature of CoT and optimizing agent training strategies.
