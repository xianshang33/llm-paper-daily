## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Multi-agent LLM loops (e.g., Writer-Critic patterns) typically rely on a fixed `max_iterations` cap for termination. This syntactic "kill-switch" is blind to content quality, leading to token overspending on easy inputs and premature truncation on hard ones.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Fixed iteration strategies are content-agnostic. Previous attempts to justify convergence using Banach contraction principles were theoretically unsound due to the non-deterministic and non-Lipschitz nature of LLM generation.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Semantic Early-Stopping Mechanism and Rigorous Evaluation Framework**: The method halts loops when consecutive draft embeddings converge in meaning (cosine distance) and quality metrics stabilize, supported by machine-checked theoretical proofs.
- **Challenge 1: How to ensure theoretical correctness and honesty in termination conditions?**
  - **Difficulty**: LLM generation's stochasticity invalidates traditional contraction mapping theorems, leading to overclaimed mathematical guarantees in prior work.
  - **Solution**: The paper proves deterministic termination and well-definedness, verified via machine checking. It treats distance sequence convergence as an empirically tested conjecture rather than an absolute theorem, providing a more honest theoretical footing.
- **Challenge 2: How to efficiently and fairly evaluate the cost-quality trade-off of different stopping policies?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional re-running of policies incurs high LLM costs and introduces variance, preventing strict paired comparisons.
  - **Solution**: Introduced a "judge-efficient" evaluation protocol that generates each question's full trajectory once, replays all stopping policies over identical drafts, and caches LLM-judge calls. It strictly separates "operational tokens" (policy cost) from "evaluation tokens" (measurement instrument).

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Empirical study on multi-hop Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) using HotpotQA (60-question test split).
- **Key Results**:
  - **Judge-Free Semantic Stopper**: Reduced operational tokens by **38%** compared to the `max_iterations` baseline while maintaining parity in quality (Delta-IS = -0.004, p = 0.81).
  - **Full Quality-Gated Variant**: Proved counter-productive because the cost of per-round LLM judging outweighed the savings from early stopping.
  - **Oracle Analysis**: An oracle selecting the best round achieved a +0.115 Information Score improvement over all practical policies (p ≈ 4e-11), reframing the problem from "when to stop" to "which round is best."
- **Comparison Conclusion**: Semantic early stopping significantly outperforms fixed iteration in token efficiency when no extra judging cost is incurred. Real-time quality gating must be implemented cautiously as its computational overhead can negate early-stopping benefits.

#### Summary
This paper addresses inefficient termination in LLM agent loops by proposing a semantic early-stopping mechanism. By discarding unrealistic mathematical assumptions and establishing a machine-verifiable theoretical basis alongside a efficient replay-based evaluation protocol, the study demonstrates that semantic early stopping significantly reduces token consumption without sacrificing quality, provided no additional judging overhead is introduced. The research highlights that the core future challenge lies in low-cost identification of the "best iteration round" rather than merely deciding "when to stop."
