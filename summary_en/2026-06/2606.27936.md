## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Commercial data brokers widely collect fine-grained location data and trade it as "anonymized." While prior research established the uniqueness of mobility traces, traditional re-identification attacks required significant manual effort by skilled analysts, limiting their practical scale.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current Statistical Disclosure Control (SDC) practices rely on de facto anonymity, assuming re-identification is costly and hard to scale. This assumption fails to account for advances in agentic AI, leading to an underestimation of re-identification risks under standards like GDPR Recital-26.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: An end-to-end re-identification pipeline powered by Agentic AI, where Large Language Model (LLM) agents autonomously search the open web, cross-reference public records and social media, and resolve raw coordinate sequences to candidate identities without human intervention.
- **Challenge 1: Complexity of Automated Intelligence Gathering**. The difficulty lies in automatically extracting evidence related to specific spatio-temporal trajectories from unstructured web data. The paper addresses this by using LLM agents to autonomously decompose investigative tasks, iteratively executing search, retrieval, and reasoning, mimicking advanced OSINT analyst workflows.
- **Challenge 2: Scalability and Cost-Effectiveness of Re-identification**. The challenge is that traditional methods cannot process massive datasets at low cost. The proposed method demonstrates that agents can re-identify targets in minutes for just a few dollars, fundamentally shifting the threat model and making large-scale automated attacks feasible.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation**: The pipeline was evaluated on a spatio-temporal dataset with simulated location points anchored around true home and work addresses, focusing on high-risk disclosure scenarios.
- **Results**: The Agentic AI successfully re-identified 18 out of 25 re-identifiable individuals (72%) and 18 out of 43 cases overall (41.9%).
- **Comparison**: Unlike previous studies requiring hours of manual labor, this approach achieves full automation, significantly reducing time and economic costs, thereby proving the feasibility of large-scale re-identification using public sources in real-world settings.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper reveals an emerging, scalable threat to mobility microdata privacy posed by Agentic AI. It demonstrates that LLM agents can automate complex re-identification tasks at minimal cost, challenging the implicit assumptions of anonymity in traditional SDC practices. These findings necessitate a re-evaluation of privacy risks by data custodians and regulators, particularly under GDPR, where re-identification is now shown to be "reasonably likely" and inexpensive.
