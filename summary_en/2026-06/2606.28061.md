## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are evolving from standalone text generators into agents that invoke external tools and execute multi-step tasks. As these agents handle complex workflows, privacy protection during tool invocation has become a critical concern.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Conventional function-calling benchmarks focus primarily on task completion and API correctness, while existing privacy benchmarks typically assess final responses or static privacy judgments. Neither approach captures the flow of purpose-bound information across multi-tool execution trajectories, failing to evaluate whether private data is unnecessarily exposed during intermediate steps.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ToolPrivacyBench**: A benchmark framework designed to audit "Purpose-Bound Privacy" in tool-using LLM agents, ensuring that private information is routed only to authorized tools and downstream sinks.
- **Challenge 1: Difficulty in tracking fine-grained privacy flows in multi-tool workflows**. It is challenging because a private field may be necessary for one tool but forbidden for another, and traditional methods lack control over intermediate processes. **Solution**: The paper introduces a "Policy Knowledge Base" that explicitly records private atoms, tool purposes, and allowed/forbidden field-tool relationships. It performs knowledge-based trajectory auditing by comparing backend audit logs with this policy.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of comprehensive metrics balancing task success and privacy compliance**. The difficulty lies in the fact that successful task execution often masks privacy risks, as existing benchmarks treat these aspects in isolation. **Solution**: ToolPrivacyBench formalizes a "need-to-know disclosure boundary," evaluating whether each tool receives only the information necessary for its stated purpose, thereby identifying cases where tasks are completed but privacy is over-disclosed.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Dataset Composition**: The benchmark comprises 2,150 cases, including 1,150 fully synthetic privacy-sensitive business workflows and 1,000 cases adapted from existing multi-tool and function-calling benchmarks, each paired with a policy knowledge base.
- **Evaluation Scope**: Nine widely used LLM agents were evaluated.
- **Results**: The results demonstrate that successful tool execution does not imply appropriate privacy disclosure. Agents often transmit unnecessary private information through intermediate tool calls while completing tasks, revealing significant deficiencies in adhering to the "need-to-know" principle.
- **Comparison Advantage**: Unlike prior works that focus solely on final outputs or task success rates, ToolPrivacyBench provides fine-grained auditing of information flow during multi-step interactions, filling the gap in privacy evaluation for dynamic tool-use scenarios.

## 4. Conclusion
ToolPrivacyBench addresses the lack of privacy evaluation for LLM agents in multi-tool collaboration scenarios. By introducing a policy knowledge base and trajectory-level auditing, it formalizes and quantifies "Purpose-Bound Privacy" risks. The study confirms that current agents frequently suffer from privacy over-disclosure, leaking sensitive data even when tasks are successfully completed. This benchmark provides essential evaluation standards and directions for developing more privacy-compliant agent systems.
