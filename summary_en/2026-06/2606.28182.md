## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Embodied agents operating in decentralized and partially observable environments have gained significant attention. Large Language Model (LLM)-based agents are widely used for high-level decision-making and natural language communication.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Existing LLM-based agents often exhibit behaviors misaligned with their partners or inconsistent with the environment state, leading to inefficient cooperation and poor task success. Specifically, current methods lack effective mechanisms to align agents with diverse partners and task specifications, and they lack learning-based self-evolution capabilities, relying instead on non-learning pipelines or knowledge distillation from stronger models, which prevents autonomous improvement through interaction.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed LLawCo (Learning Laws of Cooperation)**: A novel framework that enables embodied agents to autonomously align with both their partners and task objectives.
- **Challenge 1: Difficulty in Behavioral Alignment**. In complex environments, agents struggle to rapidly adapt to partner-specific behaviors and constraints, resulting in suboptimal coordination. **Solution**: LLawCo allows agents to reflect on past failures to extract misaligned behavioral patterns and derive high-level behavioral laws (e.g., "Talk when necessary", "Wait for partner"). These laws are explicitly incorporated into the agents' chains of thought via supervised fine-tuning, ensuring alignment with task requirements and partner behavior.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of Autonomous Evolution**. Existing frameworks rely on static prompt engineering or external distillation, limiting adaptive capabilities in long-horizon tasks. **Solution**: By automatically learning and internalizing behavioral laws from interaction failures, LLawCo empowers agents with self-evolution capabilities, allowing them to continuously optimize collaboration strategies without supervision from stronger external models.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Benchmark Introduction**: The authors introduce PARTNR-Dialog, a large-scale multi-agent communicative and cooperative planning benchmark built on the PARTNR environment.
- **Experimental Results**: Experiments on existing tasks and the new PARTNR-Dialog benchmark demonstrate significant improvements in cooperative efficiency and task success rates.
- **Comparison**: Across four backbone LLMs, LLawCo achieves average success rate improvements of 4.5% on the PARTNR-Dialog benchmark and 6.8% on the TDW-MAT benchmark compared to state-of-the-art open-source communicative agent frameworks.

## 4. Conclusion
LLawCo draws inspiration from Asimov's Laws of Robotics to propose a method for embodied multi-agents to learn high-level cooperation laws from failures. This approach addresses the challenges of behavioral alignment and lack of self-evolution in partially observable environments. By incorporating learned behavioral laws into the reasoning process via supervised fine-tuning, agents better understand task requirements and partner behaviors. Experiments confirm that LLawCo outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods across multiple benchmarks, significantly enhancing multi-agent collaboration success rates.
