## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: In multi-agent Large Language Model (LLM) systems, output distributions evolve through repeated interactions. Bias from one agent can propagate across the network, coupling outputs in ways that single-model benchmarks fail to capture.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: The community lacks a reusable measurement framework for this phenomenon. Current efforts are limited to single-model bias probes (e.g., BBQ, StereoSet) or qualitative multi-agent case studies, lacking principled metrics to quantitatively answer questions about modality amplification, temporal persistence, or calibration validity.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: The Contagion Tensor framework and the Coupling Amplification Factor (CAF).
- **Challenge 1: Lack of a unified, falsifiable metric for coupling.**
  - **Difficulty**: Prior works used conflicting symbols and definitions, making it difficult to perform unified, unitless, baseline-referenced comparisons across modalities, agents, and time steps.
  - **Solution**: Defined the Contagion Tensor $T \\in \\mathbb{R}^{M \times N \times T}$ and derived the CAF metric family ($CAF = E[T_{condition}] / E[T_{baseline}]$). This provides unitless measurements with bootstrap confidence intervals, making output-distribution coupling quantitatively falsifiable for the first time.
- **Challenge 2: Distinguishing genuine coupling from simulator design artifacts.**
  - **Difficulty**: Significant effects observed in simulations (e.g., super-linear image effects) might stem from specific module designs rather than model behavior, which traditional methods struggle to isolate.
  - **Solution**: Introduced a transferable ablation protocol. By modularly disabling specific components (e.g., image perturbation modules) and comparing CAF shifts, the method distinguishes genuine coupling from artifacts. For instance, an apparent super-linear image effect collapsed when a specific module was disabled, revealing it as an artifact.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Experimental Design**:
  - **Simulation**: Used a complete $2 \times 2 \times 2$ block-orthogonal design to evaluate the strongest CAF variant ($CAF_{base}$) with modality-specific ablations.
  - **Real-API Experiments**: Conducted supplementary experiments on DeepSeek-Chat and GPT-4o-mini, covering text-only and real-vision conditions.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - **Simulation Ablation**: An initial super-linear image effect (CAF = 1.40) collapsed to sub-linear (CAF = 0.87, $\\Delta = -0.53$) when the image perturbation module was disabled, while text conditions remained unaffected. This identified the "bifurcation" as a design artifact.
  - **Real Model Validation**:
    - Uniform personas yielded CAF ≈ 1.0 for text-only communication in both models.
    - Diverse personas drove convergence (CAF = 0.88).
    - In GPT-4o-mini, text condition (C3) had CAF = 1.02, while real vision (C5) had CAF = 1.72 [1.700, 1.733], a delta of +0.70. This validated the simulation's prediction of a genuine super-linear effect for vision after accounting for artifacts.
- **Comparison**: Unlike prior works offering only qualitative descriptions or static single-model bias measures, the CAF framework quantitatively distinguishes dynamic coupling strength in multi-agent systems and offers higher audit credibility through its ablation protocol.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper introduces the Contagion Tensor and Coupling Amplification Factor (CAF), filling the gap in quantitative measurement of output-distribution coupling in multi-agent LLM systems. By combining rigorous simulation ablation with real-API experiments, it provides not only a falsifiable measurement tool but also a methodology to distinguish genuine coupling from design artifacts. The findings confirm that visual modalities can significantly amplify coupling under certain conditions, provided simulator biases are excluded. This framework establishes essential tools and standards for future safety auditing and performance evaluation of multi-agent systems.
