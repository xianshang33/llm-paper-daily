## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly capable in mathematical reasoning, but their generated proofs are often unreliable. Interactive theorem provers like Lean 4 ensure correctness via kernel checking but are limited by the scope of available formalized knowledge (e.g., Mathlib).
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: State-of-the-art automated provers (e.g., DeepSeek-Prover-V2, Goedel-Prover) are fine-tuned on Mathlib-centric data. They struggle to generalize to domains underrepresented in Mathlib, such as Combinatorics on Words (CoW), due to a lack of necessary vocabulary and supporting lemmas.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: LAMP (Lean-based Agentic framework with MCP and Proof Repair), a multi-agent framework, and the first Lean 4 formalization of CoW containing 8 modules and 93 core declarations.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of Domain Knowledge and Poor Generalization**. Existing models rely on implicit knowledge from training data and fail in new domains like CoW lacking formal foundations. **Solution**: LAMP provides explicit, structured domain knowledge at inference time via an ontology, rather than relying on fine-tuning, allowing agents to access CoW-specific definitions and relationships.
- **Challenge 2: Coordination of Complex Proof Synthesis**. CoW proofs are long and case-heavy, making direct generation by single models difficult. **Solution**: LAMP coordinates a Planner, Builder, and Verifier using the Model Context Protocol (MCP) to access the domain ontology, iteratively synthesizing and repairing proofs to ensure kernel verification.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: LAMP integrates Planner, Builder, and Verifier agents connected via MCP to a custom CoW ontology. A benchmark suite of 90 theorems across 8 modules and three difficulty levels was created.
- **Results**: LAMP synthesized kernel-verified proofs for 96.7% of the 90 CoW theorems.
- **Comparison & Ablation**: 
    - LAMP substantially outperformed both unscaffolded baselines and existing specialized provers.
    - Ablation studies showed that removing the tool-grounded architecture or the Planner/Builder separation resulted in a ~12 percentage point drop in performance, even with the same backbone model, validating the architectural design.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the gap in formalized knowledge for Combinatorics on Words (CoW) in the Lean 4 ecosystem by creating a foundational library and proposing the LAMP framework. By injecting structured domain knowledge at inference time instead of fine-tuning, LAMP overcomes the generalization limitations of current provers. Achieving a 96.7% success rate, LAMP significantly surpasses state-of-the-art methods, offering a robust paradigm for extending formal mathematics to new domains.
