## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) data augmentation is critical for improving the generalization of data-driven SAR interpretation models. However, SAR images are governed by complex radar imaging geometry and electromagnetic scattering mechanisms, leading to poor model generalization under unseen acquisition conditions when training data is limited or biased.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current augmentation workflows are hindered by heterogeneous dataset formats, task-dependent metadata requirements, diverse generation methods with varying assumptions/risks, and weak validation of generated samples. Traditional transformations lack semantic diversity, while manual or fixed-rule selection methods suffer from trial-and-error inefficiencies, method mismatches, and a lack of reliable, reproducible decision-making processes.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: SAGA (SAR Augmentation and Generation Agent), a schema-grounded and benefit-aware agent framework for task-oriented SAR data generation and augmentation.
- **Challenge 1: Difficulty in strategy selection amidst heterogeneous data and complex constraints**. SAR datasets vary widely in format and metadata, and generation methods have different costs and risks, making it hard to match user needs automatically. **Solution**: SAGA extracts observable dataset facts and validates executable schemas. It employs validator-constrained planning to select feasible augmentation strategies from natural language requests, separating semantic proposal from deterministic validation to ensure executability and reliability.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of reliable validation for generated sample quality and downstream utility**. Generated SAR data may contain artifacts, distribution shifts, or data leaks, with no quantified assessment of their actual benefit to downstream tasks. **Solution**: SAGA integrates multi-dimensional evaluators (quality, distribution, SAR artifacts, duplicates, leakage, and optional downstream task performance) to rigorously assess generated data, supporting evidence-qualified augmentation claims and enhancing reproducibility.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on controlled agentic benchmarks and downstream SAR interpretation tasks.
- **Baselines**: Compared against rule-based methods, LLM-only approaches, ReAct-style agents, and fixed-augmentation baselines.
- **Key Results**:
    - **Schema Grounding & Skill Planning**: SAGA demonstrated superior ability in understanding dataset structures and planning viable augmentation skills.
    - **Invalid Sample Rejection**: Significantly improved the identification and rejection of invalid samples that violate SAR physical characteristics or task requirements.
    - **Downstream Utility**: Training with data augmented by SAGA yielded higher performance improvements in downstream SAR interpretation tasks compared to all baselines, proving the practical value of its generated data.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the issues of heterogeneity, weak validation, and unreliable decision-making in SAR data augmentation by proposing the SAGA agent framework. By decoupling semantic proposal from deterministic execution and introducing multi-dimensional quality and utility assessments, SAGA achieves task-driven and quality-assured SAR data generation. Experiments show that SAGA outperforms existing baselines in schema understanding, strategy planning, and downstream task gains, providing a reliable and reproducible automated solution for SAR data augmentation.
