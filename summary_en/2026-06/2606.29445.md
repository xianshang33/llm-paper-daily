## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have achieved remarkable performance on Video Question Answering (VideoQA) benchmarks. However, existing evaluations primarily focus on shallow visual cue perception (e.g., object recognition, attribute judgment) and rarely examine whether MLLMs can learn deeper knowledge or procedural skills from video tutorials and generalize them to downstream long-horizon agentic tasks.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current benchmarks fail to measure "Video In-Context Learning" capability—the ability to extract actionable knowledge from videos and apply it to new environments. Traditional VideoQA stays at low-level understanding, whereas real-world complex tasks (e.g., software operations, device configuration) require high-level procedural understanding and long-horizon planning. Existing methods lack both an effective evaluation system for this gap and optimized keyframe extraction techniques.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: VG-GUIBench, a new benchmark designed to evaluate whether MLLM-based GUI agents can follow video tutorials to complete corresponding GUI interactive tasks; and TASKER (Task-driven And Scene-aware Keyframe searchER), a generalized keyframe extraction algorithm.
- **Challenge 1: How to evaluate the generalization capability from video learning to agentic action?**
  - **Difficulty**: Existing datasets lack paired data of video tutorials and specific GUI operation tasks, making it difficult to quantify the model's ability to learn from observation and execute long-horizon tasks.
  - **Solution**: Constructed VG-GUIBench, which includes video tutorials, task specifications, and corresponding GUI interaction environments. This forces models to derive logical steps or navigate UIs by watching tutorials, bridging the evaluation gap from low-level perception to high-level agentic action.
- **Challenge 2: How to efficiently extract key frames that are critical for both task and scene in long videos?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional keyframe extraction often ignores task relevance or scene dynamics, leading to information redundancy or loss of key steps, which severely impacts MLLM performance in VideoQA and agentic tasks.
  - **Solution**: Proposed the TASKER algorithm, which jointly considers task relevance and scene dynamics to identify the most informative frames. This ensures extracted keyframes contain necessary operational steps and retain important visual state changes, thereby enhancing downstream task performance.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Results**: TASKER achieved significant performance improvements on both VideoQA and video-guided agentic task benchmarks.
- **Comparison with Baselines**:
  - On the EgoSchema full set, TASKER outperformed the best baseline by 2.0%.
  - On the NExT-QA dataset, TASKER outperformed the best baseline by 1.8%.
- **Conclusion**: The experiments highlight the potential of generalized keyframe extraction methods in video understanding tasks and demonstrate that VG-GUIBench effectively reveals the deficiencies and progress space of current models in learning procedural skills.

#### Summary
This paper aims to advance video understanding from the traditional VideoQA paradigm (low-level understanding) to the video-guided agentic task paradigm (high-level understanding). By introducing the VG-GUIBench benchmark and the TASKER keyframe extraction algorithm, the authors not only provide a new standard for evaluating MLLMs' ability to learn from video tutorials and execute complex GUI tasks but also demonstrate an effective method to significantly boost model performance across various video understanding tasks through optimized keyframe selection. This work emphasizes the hierarchical leap from perception to action, offering important directions for the future development of multimodal agents.
