## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Motion-language agents require bidirectional capabilities: Motion-to-Text (M2T) understanding and Text-to-Motion (T2M) generation. While foundational models perform well in static settings, autonomous agents in dynamic environments must continuously incorporate new motion concepts (e.g., novel athletic styles) without catastrophic forgetting.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Most existing systems are trained in static settings with all data available upfront. They struggle with the stability-plasticity trade-off in continual learning, especially given the inter-task interference in bidirectional shared-parameter models and the significant kinematic and semantic differences across motion domains. Current methods fail to effectively handle task-agnostic inference in sequential learning scenarios.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a** LoRA variant framework built on a frozen Large Language Model backbone, specifically designed to mitigate inter-task interference in bidirectional motion-language learning, incorporating a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture.
- **Challenge 1: How to achieve task-specific knowledge isolation to prevent interference without task labels?**
  - **Difficulty**: Inference occurs without knowing the specific task sequence, and soft mixing often confuses features from different tasks.
  - **Solution**: Proposed an autoencoder-based router that automatically selects task-specific experts at inference time (hard selection), eliminating the need for explicit task labels and maintaining expert isolation.
- **Challenge 2: How to achieve near-zero forgetting in both M2T and T2M directions while maintaining high quality?**
  - **Difficulty**: Shared parameters between M2T and T2M can cause negative transfer, and token-level metrics may not reflect downstream generation quality.
  - **Solution**: Demonstrated that hard expert selection significantly outperforms soft blending, preserving old task performance while learning new ones. Validated using a newly established five-task benchmark derived from semantic clustering of HumanML3D.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Benchmark**: Established a reproducible five-task benchmark by semantically clustering motion descriptions from HumanML3D. The model integrates LoRA variants and MoE architecture on a frozen LLM backbone.
- **Evaluation Results**: The method achieved near-zero forgetting in both M2T and T2M directions while maintaining high generation and captioning quality.
- **Comparison**: Hard expert selection via routing significantly outperformed soft expert blending in quality metrics, indicating that preserving expert isolation is critical for continual learning. The study also highlighted a divergence between token-level accuracy and downstream generation quality, suggesting limitations in current evaluation protocols.

#### Summary
This paper addresses the continual learning challenge for motion-language agents in dynamic environments by proposing a solution based on LoRA variants and a Mixture-of-Experts architecture. By introducing a label-free router based on an autoencoder, it successfully mitigates inter-task interference and catastrophic forgetting. Experiments prove that hard expert selection mechanisms effectively maintain performance stability in bidirectional tasks. This work not only provides an efficient continual learning framework but also reveals inadequacies in current evaluation metrics, laying the groundwork for future research on lifelong motion-language agents.
