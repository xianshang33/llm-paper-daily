## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed as autonomous financial agents initialized with explicit behavioral mandates (e.g., "preserve capital") intended to govern decisions throughout deployment.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current financial benchmarks measure knowledge retrieval, exam performance, or short-term returns at a single point in time. They fail to assess whether agents consistently adhere to their assigned behavioral profiles over long horizons. As market context accumulates, mandate influence erodes—a phenomenon termed Mandate Salience Decay (MSD)—which remains unquantified in objective financial settings.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed FinPersona-Bench**: A simulation benchmark that decouples observable price from hidden fundamental value in a synthetic market, enabling falsifiable evaluation across three failure modes.
- **Challenge 1: How to objectively quantify mandate adherence decay over long periods?**
  - **Difficulty**: Static evaluations cannot capture the erosion of mandate influence due to accumulating context, nor distinguish between reasoning failures and mandate失效.
  - **Solution**: The paper formalizes Mandate Salience Decay (MSD) and designs a 200-trading-day simulation. It objectively measures the weakening of mandate influence by comparing target vs. actual cash allocation over time.
- **Challenge 2: How to identify specific failure modes across different market contexts?**
  - **Difficulty**: Financial complexity allows agents to violate core risk constraints while maintaining coherent local reasoning, with varying impacts across market states (calm, crash, bubble).
  - **Solution**: By decoupling price from fundamental value, the benchmark specifically evaluates three failure modes: trading without signal in calm markets, panic-selling during crashes, and ignoring fundamental value during speculative bubbles.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated 18 leading frontier and open-source LLMs, each assigned one of three behavioral profiles ranging from strict capital preservation to aggressive growth.
- **Key Results**:
  - MSD compounds over time and is model-dependent.
  - In crash scenarios, the behavioral gap between static agents and those receiving periodic mandate re-grounding grew 4.4x from the first to the final quarter of the simulation.
  - Mandate re-grounding effects are not uniformly positive: it consistently helps conservative agents in low-signal markets but actively worsens behavior for aggressive agents in the same setting.
- **Comparison**: Unlike existing works focusing on short-term performance or static knowledge, this study reveals the severity of behavioral drift in long-term deployment. It demonstrates that reliable long-term operation requires selective, mandate-aware re-grounding strategies based on agent profile and market regime.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses "Mandate Salience Decay" in autonomous financial agents, where initial behavioral instructions lose influence as context accumulates. Through the FinPersona-Bench benchmark, the authors quantify this phenomenon and find that simple periodic mandate refreshing is not always effective and can even harm aggressive agents. The study suggests that reliable long-term deployment necessitates selective, mandate-aware re-grounding strategies tailored to the agent's specific behavioral profile and current market conditions.
