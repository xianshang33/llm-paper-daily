## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: AI-native applications are shifting shopping from page-based browsing to intent-driven experiences orchestrated by LLM agents.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Common designs wrap LLMs around existing search/recommendation pipelines, forcing complex intents through low-bandwidth interfaces, creating a gap between language understanding and item-space fulfillment. While generative recommendation uses Semantic IDs (SIDs), existing models mainly generate retrieval candidates rather than translating flexible intents directly into item-space outcomes.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ShopX**: A foundation model for "Intent-to-Item" fulfillment in agentic shopping, unifying intent understanding, execution planning, and flexible SID-native item-space operations.
- **Challenge 1: Disconnection between Language Intent and Item Execution**. The difficulty lies in the lossy, low-bandwidth interaction between LLMs and traditional retrieval systems, which struggles with complex/multi-turn intents. ShopX addresses this by employing a model-native fulfillment framework where the model directly plans and composes SID-based operations (e.g., SID beam-search, ranking, bundling), reducing lossy hand-offs.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of Fine-grained Item-Space Operation Capabilities in General LLMs**. The challenge is enabling LLMs to precisely manipulate item IDs while retaining general knowledge and instruction-following abilities. ShopX solves this by designing semantically recoverable, LLM-operable SIDs and a specialized training recipe that equips general LLMs for flexible multi-turn item fulfillment.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: ShopX is deployed via a serving harness that defines a model-facing action protocol, exposing support for context access, catalog grounding, and state management. The model executes SID-native operations within this framework.
- **Evaluation Results**: Evaluated on single- and multi-turn fulfillment tasks derived from anonymized Taobao production logs. Results show that model-native fulfillment improves overall framework behavior compared to tool-mediated agentic systems, especially for complex or ambiguous requests.
- **Comparison**: Outperforms related works like ChatRec, RecMind, and InterRecAgent in metrics such as constraint grounding, catalog coverage, and personalization.

## 4. Conclusion
ShopX bridges the gap between language understanding and item retrieval in agentic shopping by unifying intent understanding, planning, and item-space operations into a single foundation model. Its core innovations include LLM-operable Semantic IDs (SIDs) and a tailored training strategy. Experiments demonstrate its superiority over traditional tool-mediated agent systems in handling complex shopping intents, paving the way for next-generation AI-native shopping experiences.
