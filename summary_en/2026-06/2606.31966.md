## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) show great potential as embodied agents, but most existing research focuses on single-agent tasks. Many real-world scenarios, such as household assistance, require multi-agent collaboration.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current multi-agent benchmarks rely heavily on textual descriptions, shared symbolic states, or pre-processed observations, reducing collaboration to mere plan coordination or dialogue. They fail to address the fundamental challenges of embodied multi-agent collaboration, where agents must coordinate based on partial, dynamically changing visual observations, resolve perceptual inconsistencies, avoid spatial conflicts, and act under physical constraints.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed MECoBench**: A systematic multimodal embodied cooperation benchmark featuring an evaluation platform with diverse real-world tasks, two cooperation structures (independent and interdependent), and three collaboration modes (isolated, centralized, decentralized).
- **Challenge 1: Perception-Action Coupling and Coordination Complexity in Embodied Environments**. The difficulty lies in coordinating agents using dynamic visual inputs while balancing collaboration gains against coordination costs. The paper addresses this by establishing an evaluation framework that quantifies the trade-off between collaboration benefits and complexity, revealing that collaboration is not always superior to single-agent performance and depends on specific configurations.
- **Challenge 2: Impact of Multimodal Communication Mechanisms on Collaboration Efficiency**. The challenge is determining which communication methods (text vs. visual) and collaboration topologies maximize performance in visually grounded environments. The paper demonstrates through comparative experiments that communication is essential for collaboration gains, but the optimal mode depends on team size and model capability. Furthermore, collaboration enhances robustness under noisy priors and exploration conditions.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: MECoBench provides an evaluation platform supporting various MLLMs. It incorporates diverse real-world embodied tasks and defines clear cooperation structures and modes.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    1. Collaboration generally improves task completion rates, but benefits depend on balancing gains with coordination complexity.
    2. Communication is critical for collaboration gains; the best collaboration mode varies with team size and model capability.
    3. Collaboration improves robustness in scenarios with noisy priors and during exploration.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Compared to benchmarks like FurnMove, CoELA, and RoCoBench, MECoBench is unique in supporting multimodal observations, variable team sizes, multiple cooperation structures, and diverse communication media. Existing benchmarks are often limited to fixed team sizes, text-only interactions, or lack true multimodal perceptual synergy.

## 4. Conclusion
MECoBench fills the gap in research on multimodal embodied agent collaboration by providing a systematic testbed. The findings indicate that while collaboration enhances performance and robustness, communication mechanisms and collaboration modes must be carefully designed to manage coordination complexity. This work offers significant insights into the mechanisms and limits of multimodal embodied collaboration.
