## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: LLM agents are increasingly operating in long-horizon tasks where single trajectories can involve hundreds or thousands of actions. In such settings, outcome-only rewards are too sparse to provide adequate guidance on the quality of intermediate actions.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current dense supervision methods (e.g., intrinsic confidence, self-distillation) are typically evaluated by measuring the downstream performance of full training pipelines. This approach is expensive, conflates signal quality with training engineering confounders, and makes it difficult to compare different methodological families that require distinct training setups.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed QVal**: A training-free testbed for directly evaluating dense supervision signals by measuring how well a method's scores align with the Q-values of a strong reference policy (Q-alignment).
- **Challenge 1: High Cost and Inefficiency of Evaluation**. Traditional evaluation requires running complete RL training loops, which is computationally prohibitive. **Solution**: QVal employs a training-free approach that directly correlates predicted scores with reference Q-value rankings, allowing for rapid assessment without any training runs.
- **Challenge 2: Conflation of Signal Quality and Engineering Factors**. Performance metrics are often skewed by specific training hyperparameters, making fair comparison impossible. **Solution**: QVal decouples signal quality from training engineering, providing a unified benchmark that allows direct comparison across diverse methodological families (e.g., probability-based vs. self-distillation).

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The authors instantiated QVal-v1.0, covering four diverse environments, seven methodological families, and six open-weight model backbones, conducting over 1,200 evaluation experiments.
- **Results**: The study finds that simple prompting baselines consistently outperform recent, more complex dense supervision methods from the literature. Performance clusters strongly by methodological family, and these findings hold robustly across different model sizes, environments, and observation modalities.
- **Comparison**: Unlike traditional evaluations that rely on downstream task success, QVal reveals that many complex methods do not offer superior signal quality compared to simple baselines, highlighting the importance of pre-training signal validation.

## 4. Conclusion
QVal provides an efficient, low-cost, and fair evaluation framework for dense supervision signals in long-horizon LLM agents. By introducing the "Q-alignment" metric, it successfully isolates signal quality from training engineering, enabling researchers to iterate on supervision methods before committing to expensive RL training. The findings challenge the assumed superiority of complex supervision methods, emphasizing the effectiveness of simple baselines and offering a scalable benchmark for future research.
