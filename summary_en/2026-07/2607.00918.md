## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: While Large Language Models (LLMs) excel at creative fiction, they struggle to maintain narrative consistency and coherent plotlines in long-form stories, especially in multi-character environments where balancing goals, actions, and relationships is complex.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current multi-agent narrative systems (e.g., StoryVerse, IBSEN) rely heavily on textual memory, which limits their ability to generate coherent long-range stories. Furthermore, existing evaluation methods lack robust frameworks for detecting hallucinations within generated long-form narratives.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Unified Framework**: Introduces MAGNET for generation and ATLAS for verification.
- **Challenge 1: Character Inconsistency and Plot Discontinuity**. The difficulty lies in maintaining logic over long contexts. MAGNET addresses this by using persona-grounded character agents that propose actions based on a shared world state and evolving story goals, ensuring long-term coherence.
- **Challenge 2: Detecting Subtle Hallucinations in Long Texts**. Traditional methods struggle to track logical consistency across scenes. ATLAS solves this by employing a graph-based pipeline that compares scene-level world representations to detect inconsistencies and hallucinations.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation**: The framework was evaluated using an LLM editor, pairwise rubric scoring, and the ATLAS detection pipeline.
- **Results vs. Baselines**: In generating 100-page stories, MAGNET reduced annotations by 41% and hallucinations by 50% compared to a single-model baseline. Compared to the state-of-the-art IBSEN framework, it reduced annotations by 34% and hallucinations by 45%. Pairwise rubric evaluations confirmed these improvements in narrative coherence.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper demonstrates that high-quality long-form narratives can emerge from explicit world-state tracking and goal-driven multi-agent interactions. The combination of MAGNET and ATLAS provides a foundation for controllable and structurally coherent long-form narrative generation, effectively addressing the limitations of previous approaches in consistency and hallucination detection.
