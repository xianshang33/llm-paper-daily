## 1. What the paper did
- **Background**: Computer-assisted synthesis planning (CASP) relies on large libraries of reaction rules to assign deterministic, interpretable labels to chemical transformations. However, the long-tail distribution of chemical reactions makes manual encoding intractable.
- **Limitations of existing work**: Current tools depend on fixed rule sets that cannot adapt to new chemistries. Manually constructing SMIRKS rules requires significant chemical intuition and scales poorly, leading to insufficient coverage of rare transformations.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a**: Multi-agent framework based on Large Language Models (LLMs) that automatically classifies reactions and generates verifiable reaction rules, enabling self-expansion of the reaction taxonomy.
- **Challenge 1**: How to automatically extract generalizable rules from massive, long-tailed chemical data while ensuring accuracy and computability?
  - **Difficulty & Solution**: Traditional methods rely on inefficient human expertise. This paper employs multi-agent collaboration where LLMs generate SMIRKS rules, integrated with a verification loop that tests and refines these rules against a corpus of 665,901 US patent reactions to ensure determinism.
- **Challenge 2**: How to build a classification system that maintains high precision while dynamically adapting to new chemical spaces?
  - **Difficulty & Solution**: Fixed taxonomies (e.g., RXNO) are coarse and rigid. The method automatically expands the standard taxonomy from 68 to 14,073 classes without human curation. A lightweight fingerprint classifier achieves high accuracy on unseen reactions and supports on-demand extension beyond the training distribution.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Processed 665,901 US patent reactions. The system includes a verification loop to validate generated rules against the corpus.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - Expanded the classification taxonomy from 68 to 14,073 classes.
  - Achieved 97.7% classification accuracy on unseen reactions using a lightweight fingerprint classifier.
- **Comparison with Related Work**:
  - Performance matches leading proprietary classifiers.
  - Advantages include finer resolution of chemical transformations and the ability to extend to out-of-distribution chemistry, whereas existing proprietary tools are limited by fixed rule sets.

#### Summary
This paper presents a general pathway to turning generative models into reliable, self-expanding symbolic systems. By leveraging a multi-agent LLM framework for automated rule generation and verification, it constructs a "living" reactivity database. This approach addresses the intractability of manual encoding for long-tail reactions, offering a more adaptive and granular solution for computer-assisted synthesis planning.
