## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Software development is a complex task requiring cooperation among diverse roles. Large Language Models (LLMs) have enabled autonomous multi-agent frameworks that automate requirements analysis, coding, testing, and refinement through role-based collaboration.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current approaches typically assume that intermediate agent outputs are equally reliable without explicitly modeling uncertainty. This makes them vulnerable to "hallucination propagation," where incorrect decisions from early phases are transferred to downstream agents, negatively impacting final software quality.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed UA-ChatDev**: An uncertainty-aware multi-agent software development framework that integrates uncertainty quantification into agent interactions to enhance reliability.
- **Challenge 1: How to efficiently and accurately assess agent confidence?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional uncertainty estimation methods are often computationally expensive or incompatible with black-box LLM APIs, making real-time deployment difficult.
  - **Solution**: The paper introduces a lightweight uncertainty estimation mechanism based on token-level log probabilities to assess response confidence without additional inference overhead.
- **Challenge 2: How to correct errors effectively when uncertainty is high without compromising efficiency?**
  - **Difficulty**: Blindly verifying all outputs increases latency, while ignoring high-uncertainty outputs leads to error accumulation.
  - **Solution**: It employs phase-aware threshold calibration to selectively trigger retrieval-based verification only when uncertainty exceeds acceptable levels, balancing reliability and resource usage.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Extensive experiments were conducted on the SRDD benchmark, comparing UA-ChatDev against existing single-agent and multi-agent software development frameworks.
- **Key Results**: UA-ChatDev consistently outperforms baselines across metrics including completeness, executability, consistency, and overall quality.
- **Ablation Studies**: Further ablation studies and communication analyses verify that uncertainty-aware interactions significantly enhance code execution reliability by mitigating hallucination propagation.

## 4. Conclusion
UA-ChatDev addresses the issue of hallucination propagation in multi-agent collaboration by introducing token-probability-based uncertainty estimation and dynamic verification mechanisms. This approach significantly improves the quality and reliability of automatically generated software without substantial computational overhead, offering a new perspective for building more robust LLM-driven software development workflows.
