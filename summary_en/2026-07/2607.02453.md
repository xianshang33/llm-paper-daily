## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Since the launch of ChatGPT, open-source agentic frameworks have proliferated, making framework selection a critical yet risky decision for engineering teams due to potential technical debt and ecosystem instability.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current evaluation metrics (e.g., GitHub Stars, PR counts) are often misunderstood and susceptible to manipulation. Stars reflect hype rather than actual adoption, and contributor metrics fail to distinguish between one-off fixes and sustained engagement, thus failing to capture true ecosystem health and sustainability.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a longitudinal ecosystem health assessment framework** for open-source multi-agent systems, analyzing Awareness, Adoption, and Retention using data from 15 major frameworks (800k+ stars, 1M+ user profiles).
- **Challenge 1: Surface-level popularity metrics (like Stars) are misleading and do not reflect deep technical adoption.**
  - **Difficulty**: Stars are driven by hype cycles and inorganic activity, creating a divergence between visibility and actual developer engagement.
  - **Solution**: Introduced "Contributor Density" (contributors per 1,000 stars). The study found AutoGPT had low density (<9/1000) despite massive stars, while LangChain (41/1000) and Pydantic-AI showed higher density, proving this metric better identifies deeply adopted frameworks.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of quantified understanding of cross-framework interactions and long-term sustainability makes it hard to assess ecosystem stickiness.**
  - **Difficulty**: Isolated metrics cannot reveal a framework's infrastructural role, and short-term contributions do not predict long-term viability.
  - **Solution**: Analyzed cross-framework contributions, revealing LangChain as shared infrastructure attracting 82.5% of cross-ecosystem contributors. Additionally, retention analysis showed steepest drop-off in the first 30 days, stabilizing at 90 days, providing a temporal benchmark for sustainability.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Data & Method**: Longitudinal analysis of 15 major open-source AI agent repositories from late 2022 to early 2026, utilizing GitHub REST/GraphQL APIs to collect stars, commits, PRs, and user profiles.
- **Results & Comparisons**:
  1. **Popularity vs. Adoption**: AutoGPT gained 111k stars in a month but had significantly lower contributor density compared to LangChain. MetaGPT and LangFlow showed high visibility but low engagement (density <5), demonstrating that visibility does not equal adoption.
  2. **Limitations of Institutional Backing**: Openai-agents-python had a limited contributor base despite official support, indicating that institutional backing alone does not ensure community depth.
  3. **Infrastructural Dominance**: LangChain emerged as the central hub, absorbing the majority of cross-ecosystem contributors, whereas other frameworks remained more isolated.
  4. **Retention Dynamics**: Universal pattern of highest churn in the first 30 days, stabilizing by day 90. This contrasts with prior works focusing merely on growth rates, highlighting the critical window for contributor retention.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper demonstrates that relying on GitHub Stars is insufficient for evaluating the health of open-source agentic frameworks. By introducing metrics like "Contributor Density," "Cross-Ecosystem Engagement," and "Retention Curves," it provides a more robust evaluation model. The findings highlight LangChain's role as foundational infrastructure due to its high density and cross-pollination, while exposing the shallow adoption of many hyped frameworks. The study offers data-driven guidance for engineering teams to prioritize deep adoption and community sustainability over superficial popularity.
