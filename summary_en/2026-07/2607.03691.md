## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Coding agents rely on "agentic scaffolding" as middleware to orchestrate system prompts, tool execution, context management, and iterative reasoning. These scaffoldings evolve at extreme velocities (over two releases per day), yet practitioners often attribute quality regressions to the underlying Large Language Model (LLM) rather than the scaffolding itself.
- **Limitations of Prior Work**: Existing studies typically fix the scaffolding and vary the model, lacking a longitudinal study that isolates how scaffolding evolution independently impacts agent quality (effectiveness and efficiency).

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: The first controlled longitudinal study framework designed to isolate and quantify the impact of agentic scaffolding evolution on coding agent quality.
- **Challenge 1: Difficulty in disentangling the impact of model capabilities from scaffolding design on final performance.**
  - **Why it's hard**: Rapid concurrent iterations of both models and scaffoldings create confounding variables, making attribution difficult.
  - **Solution**: By fixing the underlying LLM and varying only the scaffolding versions, the study evaluates 35 sequential releases of Qwen Code CLI, precisely isolating the independent impact of scaffolding evolution.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of fine-grained explanation for the root causes of quality fluctuations under high-frequency releases.**
  - **Why it's hard**: With thousands of issues and extremely high release frequencies, it is challenging to pinpoint specific code changes or architectural components causing quality regressions or stagnation.
  - **Solution**: Combines quantitative benchmarking (SWE-bench Verified) with qualitative analysis to trace quality fluctuations to specific development patterns (e.g., feature additions, fix-heavy releases) and high-risk architectural components, establishing causal links via Pull Request-level evidence.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Empirically analyzed the evolution of five major open-source scaffoldings (Codex, Qwen Code, Gemini, OpenCode, OpenHands). Conducted a deep dive into 35 sequential releases of Qwen Code CLI, evaluating each against 50 stratified SWE-bench Verified tasks with a constant LLM.
- **Key Results**:
  1. **Effectiveness Stagnation**: Despite continuous development and increasing codebase complexity, there is no statistically significant improvement in SWE-bench resolve rates across releases for a fixed LLM version.
  2. **Efficiency Degradation**: Later scaffolding versions consume nearly double the computational tokens and tool calls without corresponding quality gains.
  3. **Attribution**: Identified development patterns correlating with quality fluctuations and localized regressions to specific high-risk architectural components.
- **Comparison**: Unlike prior work focusing on performance gains from model upgrades, this paper reveals that blind, rapid scaffolding iteration can lead to resource waste without substantial performance improvements, and even cause quality regressions when the model is held constant.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper demonstrates through rigorous controlled experiments that bottlenecks in coding agent quality often lie not in the underlying LLM, but in the rapidly evolving scaffolding layer. The findings reveal a "high input, low return" phenomenon in current scaffolding development, where computational costs increase significantly without improving task resolution rates. The paper calls for new "Agentic QA" practices in scaffolding development, emphasizing fine-grained monitoring of architectural components and development patterns to avoid ineffective iterations and resource waste.
