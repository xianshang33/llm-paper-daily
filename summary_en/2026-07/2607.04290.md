## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: 5G/6G networks are evolving towards AI-native architectures, specifically O-RAN, which separates control logic into non-real-time (rApp) and near-real-time (xApp) applications. Vehicle-to-Everything (V2X) communication is highly dynamic, requiring simultaneous support for services with diverse latency and reliability needs, such as teleoperated driving and HD map sharing.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: While Large Language Models (LLMs) are proposed for network control, their high inference latency, hallucinations, and lack of control guarantees make them unsuitable for direct near-real-time packet scheduling. Existing Reinforcement Learning methods optimize trade-offs but lack the natural language understanding and flexible policy generation of LLMs, while direct LLM integration fails to meet strict V2X deadline requirements.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Agentic-V2X Architecture**: An architecture leveraging a small, locally deployed language model where policy creation (non-real-time rApp role) is decoupled from policy execution (near-real-time xApp role) for deadline-aware 5G NR V2X scheduling.
- **Challenge 1: Conflict between LLM latency and real-time scheduling**. LLMs are too slow and unstable for millisecond-level scheduling. The paper addresses this by using the small LLM as a periodic non-real-time policy creator that generates structured policies (priorities, weights, constraints), while a lightweight controller executes validated policies at faster intervals suitable for scheduling.
- **Challenge 2: Policy invalidity or safety risks due to LLM hallucinations**. LLMs may generate unexecutable or unsafe configurations. The paper introduces a validator module to check and repair policies before enforcement, ensuring validity and safety constraints are met, followed by execution via scheduler-weight adaptation in ns-3/ns3-ai.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The framework is implemented in the ns-3 network simulator with ns3-ai. The LLM generates policies based on scenario summaries and telemetry, which are validated and then enforced by an xApp-like controller adjusting scheduler weights.
- **Evaluation Results**: Evaluated over 126 runs against Proportional Fair (PF), static expert policies, heuristic xApp, static LLM policies, and adaptive LLM-rApp/xApp policies. Metrics included deadline-constrained packet reception ratio, tail latency, violations, throughput, fairness, and safety.
- **Comparison**: The adaptive LLM-rApp/xApp design consistently generated valid, executable policies. It outperformed PF in mean critical reliability at high densities and showed favorable latency/throughput trade-offs at medium densities. However, statistical analysis revealed it was not the best aggregate method for critical reliability, remaining below the strongest static policies. This positions Agentic-V2X as a safe, executable small-LLM-assisted policy generation architecture rather than a universally dominant scheduler.

## 4. Conclusion
Agentic-V2X proposes a practical architecture that addresses LLM latency and safety issues in V2X scheduling by decoupling non-real-time policy generation from near-real-time execution. While it does not surpass the best static policies in overall performance, it demonstrates competitiveness in dynamic adaptability and critical reliability, validating the feasibility of small LLMs as safe policy-generation assistants for future 6G AI-native control.
