## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Evaluations of Large Language Model (LLM) agents in software tasks typically focus solely on outcome metrics (whether the task is solved), ignoring the mediating role of the "Harness" (execution framework). The Harness controls observations, available actions, failure repairs, and logging.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Prior research treats the execution system as neutral infrastructure, assuming that with a fixed task and base LLM, the agent's reasoning remains consistent. This overlooks how Harness manipulations of information flow alter the agent's multi-step beliefs, affecting subsequent decision paths. These implicit belief biases remain unquantified and uncorrected, even when final success rates are unchanged.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Belief-Rollout Diagnostic and Cross-Harness Belief Divergence Metric**: This method elicits structured K-step belief trajectories (covering progress, risk, recoverability, etc.) to quantify belief differences under different Harness settings, decomposing divergence into an "arrival term" (immediate interface shifts) and a "growth term" (horizon-dependent changes).
    - **Challenge 1: How to quantify the non-explicit impact of the Harness on the agent's internal state?**
        - **Difficulty**: Traditional evaluations only look at final outputs, missing intermediate belief shifts; beliefs are implicit and hard to measure directly.
        - **Solution**: The paper designs a belief-rollout diagnostic that forces the agent to generate structured multi-step belief trajectories under different Harness views, allowing direct observation and quantification of belief divergence caused by Harness interventions (e.g., blocked actions, compressed repairs).
    - **Challenge 2: How to eliminate Harness-induced belief bias without retraining?**
        - **Difficulty**: Different Harnesses create inconsistent observation spaces, making direct alignment difficult; retraining is costly, and there is a lack of general debiasing protocols.
        - **Solution**: Introduced BIWM (Belief-Invariant World-Modeling), a training-free protocol. It canonicalizes observations, logs censored branches, expands repair traces, records verification masks, and executes risky branches in shadow mode to align belief trajectories across different Harness views.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on controlled coding tasks and public benchmark stress tests.
- **Key Findings**: The study shows that operations like blocking actions, compressed repairs, selective verification, and cost-aware evidence pruning often preserve terminal success but significantly alter the beliefs driving later decisions. This proves that Harness design is a critical experimental variable in agent evaluation, not just an implementation detail.
- **Comparison and Effectiveness**: Compared to traditional outcome-only evaluations, the proposed diagnostic reveals hidden belief divergences. The BIWM protocol effectively aligns belief trajectories across different Harnesses, reducing decision bias caused by interface discrepancies, thereby improving evaluation fairness and interpretability while maintaining performance consistency.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper highlights a critical overlooked issue in LLM agent evaluation: Harness design induces belief divergence in agents, influencing their decision-making processes even when final task outcomes are identical. By proposing the belief-rollout diagnostic and the BIWM protocol, the authors not only quantify this bias but also provide a training-free solution to normalize observations and align beliefs. This work emphasizes the importance of treating Harness design as a core experimental variable in agent evaluation, offering new perspectives and methods for building more robust and fair agent assessment systems.
