## 1. What the paper did
- **Background**: Reinforcement Learning (RL) is the dominant paradigm for training Large Language Model (LLM) agents on long-horizon tasks. However, sparse and delayed rewards often lead to "trajectory neglect," where agents lose focus on task goals and interaction history at intermediate steps.
- **Why existing work fails**: Prior works using Shannon-entropy-based uncertainty signals conflate inherent state complexity with agent confidence, providing unreliable estimates of decision reliability. Furthermore, existing fine-grained advantage estimation methods optimize all steps indiscriminately, lacking a selective mechanism to precisely locate and address outlier steps caused by trajectory neglect.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: Selective Trajectory-Aware Policy Optimization (STAPO), a hierarchical group-based RL framework.
- **Challenge 1: How to accurately identify trajectory neglect?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional Shannon entropy cannot distinguish whether high entropy stems from genuine state complexity (many plausible actions) or agent confusion (low-quality actions).
  - **Solution**: Proposed "normalized entropy," which measures confidence deviations relative to the agent's average behavior under a given state. This strengthens the association between low-quality actions and trajectory neglect, allowing for more accurate localization of outlier steps.
- **Challenge 2: How to optimize outlier steps while maintaining training stability?**
  - **Difficulty**: Directly applying strong penalties or rewards to outlier steps can lead to training instability or overfitting to local noise.
  - **Solution**: Introduced a joint mechanism combining "trajectory-aware reward" and "trajectory-independent penalty." This mechanism specifically optimizes steps identified by normalized entropy, enhancing trajectory awareness while preserving overall training stability through group sampling and specific penalty terms.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Extensive experiments were conducted on benchmarks including ALFWorld, WebShop, and Search-Augmented QA.
- **Results**: STAPO achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance across these tasks. Compared to prior methods like GiGPO and RLVMR that attempt to address credit assignment issues, STAPO substantially alleviated trajectory neglect.
- **Effectiveness**: Results demonstrate that STAPO not only outperforms baselines in final task success rates but also exhibits greater robustness during long-horizon interactions, validating its effectiveness and robustness for agentic tasks.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the trajectory neglect problem in LLM agents caused by sparse rewards in long-horizon tasks, highlighting the limitations of existing Shannon-entropy-based methods. By proposing normalized entropy to more precisely measure confidence deviations and constructing the STAPO framework for selective optimization of outlier steps, the method achieves SOTA performance on multiple complex benchmarks. It effectively enhances trajectory awareness and training stability for agents engaged in long-horizon reasoning.
