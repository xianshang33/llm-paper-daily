## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: As Large Language Model (LLM) agents rapidly evolve from simple chatbots to autonomous systems handling complex tasks like deep research, their deployment in production environments is increasing. This creates an urgent need for rigorous and realistic evaluations of their capabilities.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Most current benchmarks evaluate agents in simplified, idealized settings. They rely on pre-packaged tool interfaces, overlook critical intermediate steps, and assume clean, fully specified inputs. Consequently, they underestimate the difficulty of real-world deployments where uncertainty, noise, and the need for proactive tool exploration are ubiquitous.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed AgentGym2**: A new evaluation framework with task instances grounded in real-world end-to-end working demands, moving beyond simple reasoning and planning to measure practical execution capabilities.
- **Challenge 1: Tool Discovery and Composition in Real Environments**. The difficulty lies in the fact that necessary tools are not provided upfront; agents must proactively explore the environment to discover and compose tools for unseen tasks. AgentGym2 addresses this by creating scenarios that require active exploration and dynamic tool composition, testing the agent's ability to operate in non-prescribed environments.
- **Challenge 2: Robustness to Noise and Underspecified Information**. The challenge is that real-world user requests are often ambiguous or underspecified, and information sources can be incomplete or misleading. AgentGym2 incorporates tasks with noisy and incomplete data to evaluate the agent's robustness and its ability to execute end-to-end procedures despite uncertainty.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: AgentGym2 covers diverse domains including humanities, law, economics, and technology, focusing on real-world end-to-end tasks. The code and dataset are open-sourced on GitHub and Hugging Face.
- **Evaluation Results**: Experiments on 15 proprietary and open-source models reveal that even state-of-the-art systems like Gemini-2.5-Pro and GPT-5 struggle significantly on AgentGym2. This highlights a substantial gap between current agent capabilities and the demands of real-world applications.
- **Comparative Analysis**:
    - **Performance vs. Model Size**: Performance generally improves with parameter scale, but even large models (e.g., Qwen3-235B, Nex-N1-671B) do not achieve perfect scores, indicating inherent difficulties in the tasks.
    - **Performance vs. Interaction Rounds**: There is a negative correlation between performance and average interaction rounds, suggesting that efficient planning improves outcomes, though complex tasks inherently require more interactions.
    - **Performance vs. Cost**: High performance is often associated with higher costs, but AgentGym2 effectively differentiates models based on cost-efficiency, showing significant variance in performance per dollar spent.

## 4. Conclusion
AgentGym2 addresses the limitations of overly simplified existing benchmarks by introducing de-idealized, real-world environments. It specifically evaluates agents on tool exploration, composition, and robustness to noise. The results demonstrate that current SOTA LLM agents are still insufficient for handling the complexity and uncertainty of real-world scenarios, providing a crucial benchmark for guiding future agent development.
