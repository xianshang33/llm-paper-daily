## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents are typically improved by modifying prompts, models, or hand-crafted workflows, while the execution "harness" (the control layer deciding when to observe, retrieve, call tools, check, revise, etc.) is treated as fixed infrastructure.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current harnesses are often static artifacts composed of handwritten rules, fixed templates, or static execution graphs. These designs fail to adapt control logic based on task difficulty, trajectory state, or intermediate success/failure. For instance, "always check" wastes budget on complete solutions, while "submit after draft" fails on unverified drafts. The core issue is rigid control sequences rather than a lack of model knowledge.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Learnable Harness Control Framework via Offline RL**: Formalized harness operation as a finite-horizon Harness MDP where a lightweight controller selects structural execution actions while keeping the LLM executor frozen.
- **Challenge 1: High cost and low quality of online exploration**. Online RL in agent environments is expensive and prone to generating invalid trajectories. **Solution**: Employed offline reinforcement learning using a finite offline rollout buffer. The controller is trained via Advantage-Weighted Regression (AW) using only terminal task-rubric rewards, avoiding the pitfalls of online exploration.
- **Challenge 2: Difficulty in distinguishing final task quality from control process reliability**. Traditional metrics only measure final answer correctness, ignoring the robustness of the control policy. **Solution**: Introduced a post-hoc "Harness Maturity Score" to separate final task quality from whether the harness follows reliable execution patterns. This separation provides a finite-buffer view: final quality gains require high-return support in the offline buffer, while process behavior shifts when aligned with advantage weights.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated across six controlled domains and two public benchmark adapters.
- **Key Results**: The learned controller consistently improved verification behavior and selectively enhanced final task quality. The largest gains were observed in adapted tau-bench retail, adapted AgentBench DB-Bench, and coding tasks with a calibrated structural verifier.
- **Comparisons**:
    - **vs. Behavior Cloning**: Ablation studies showed that gains are not merely due to imitation, validating the effectiveness of RL advantage weighting.
    - **vs. Forced CHECK**: Improvements were not simply due to adding more checks, but from intelligently deciding when to check.
    - The results confirm that harness control is a learnable layer for frozen LLM agents, though offline support limits the extent to which better process control translates to better final answers.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper argues that the execution harness of LLM agents should not be viewed as fixed infrastructure but as a learnable control layer. By modeling it as an MDP and training a lightweight controller with offline RL, the method significantly optimizes execution flows (e.g., verification, retrieval, submission decisions) without updating LLM parameters. The proposed Harness Maturity Score further decouples process quality from outcome quality, revealing the limitations imposed by offline data distributions on performance gains. This work offers a new perspective and method for enhancing the efficiency and quality of frozen LLM agents.
