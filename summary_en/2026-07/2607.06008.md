## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large language model (LLM) agents have demonstrated strong capabilities in long-horizon tasks requiring planning, tool use, and environmental interaction. However, most existing benchmarks implicitly assume a monolingual setting, where reasoning, tool invocation, and output generation occur entirely in a single language (typically English).
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Real-world applications often involve multilingual inputs and outputs within a unified workflow. Existing multilingual benchmarks (e.g., XGLUE, M-MMLU) focus on static, isolated prediction tasks without execution dynamics, while agent benchmarks (e.g., SWE-bench, WebArena) study execution complexity under monolingual assumptions. These two lines of research treat multilinguality and agentic reasoning as orthogonal, failing to explore how language variation affects the stability of reasoning and decision-making trajectories in long-horizon tasks.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed PolyWorkBench**: A benchmark designed to evaluate LLM agents on multilingual long-horizon workplace workflows.
- **Challenge 1: Execution and Evaluation in Multilingual Long-Horizon Tasks**. Agents must process heterogeneous multilingual inputs, perform iterative reasoning, and invoke tools, where language switching can cause reasoning breaks or error accumulation. The paper addresses this by constructing 67 tasks across five domains (commerce, knowledge work, legal analysis, localization, manufacturing), forcing agents to handle multilingual interactions within coherent workflows.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of Comprehensive Evaluation Metrics**. Traditional methods struggle to simultaneously measure functional correctness of tool/code execution and semantic consistency of multilingual outputs. The paper proposes a hybrid evaluation framework combining structural grading, executable verification, and LLM-based semantic assessment to capture both aspects effectively.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: PolyWorkBench comprises 67 tasks across five key domains. The evaluation utilizes a hybrid framework incorporating structural scoring, executable code/command verification, and LLM-based semantic consistency checks.
- **Evaluation Results**: Empirical results show that state-of-the-art LLM agents suffer significant performance degradation in multilingual workflow settings compared to their monolingual counterparts.
- **Comparison & Analysis**: The analysis reveals that multilinguality introduces compounding effects across reasoning and execution steps, where early linguistic misunderstandings amplify in subsequent steps. This highlights the critical need to jointly model language variation and procedural decision-making, a dimension overlooked by existing monolingual benchmarks.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper identifies the gap in current LLM agent benchmarks regarding multilingual interaction dynamics and introduces PolyWorkBench. By simulating realistic multilingual long-horizon workflows, it exposes the performance bottlenecks of SOTA agents in handling multilingual contexts. The proposed hybrid evaluation framework provides a vital tool for future research on the robustness and consistency of multilingual agents, emphasizing the coupling of language capability and execution logic in general agent development.
