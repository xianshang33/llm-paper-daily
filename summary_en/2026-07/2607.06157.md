## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Deliberation is crucial in human collaboration for aligning information and reaching consensus. While LLMs have advanced in multi-agent collaboration and decision-making, research often overlooks scenarios with incomplete information.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current studies typically focus on pure deliberation or collaboration with full information sharing. They fail to address how LLM agents can effectively collaborate through deliberation when each agent possesses only partial and asymmetric observations, a common reality in complex tasks.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Scalable Benchmark for Deliberative Collaboration**: The paper formalizes deliberative collaboration as a cooperative joint decision-making problem with partial and asymmetric observations. It introduces a benchmark across multiple domains (e.g., menu design, task allocation) where agents must exchange information to reach a joint decision with a shared reward.
- **Challenge 1: Information Alignment under Partial Observability**. Agents struggle to infer the global state from local views, hindering consensus. The paper addresses this by designing tasks requiring explicit information exchange, evaluating how well agents align beliefs during deliberation, and identifying failure modes in current models.
- **Challenge 2: Coupled Complexity of Reasoning and Decision Making**. Agents must not only align information but also perform complex combinatorial optimization for final decisions. The paper evaluates this by incorporating external mathematical tools and diagnostic analysis, revealing that while models may fail in deliberation or reasoning, the deliberation process itself can offer reflection and error correction opportunities, sometimes outperforming centralized baselines.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Protocol**: A reference scaffold and evaluation protocol were instantiated to systematically evaluate a range of representative LLMs. The underlying decision problems are formulated as combinatorial optimization tasks with finite decision spaces, enabling rule-based numerical rewards.
- **Evaluation Results**: Results show that complex deliberative collaboration tasks continue to challenge state-of-the-art LLMs. Even with external mathematical tools, models often fail in either the information alignment phase or the complex reasoning phase.
- **Comparison & Insights**: Compared to centralized baselines, diagnostic analysis reveals that deliberation provides opportunities for reflection and error correction, potentially improving performance. This highlights the strengths, limitations, and properties of current LLM-based multi-agent systems.

## 4. Conclusion
This work establishes a foundation for evaluating and improving LLM agents in deliberative collaboration. By formalizing joint decision-making under partial observability and introducing a scalable benchmark, the paper systematically reveals the limitations of current LLMs in handling information asymmetry and complex joint reasoning. It also underscores the potential of deliberation mechanisms to enhance decision quality through reflection, providing valuable directions for future multi-agent system development.
