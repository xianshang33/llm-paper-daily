## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: The rapid expansion of biomedical literature makes it difficult to extract precise and reliable answers from dispersed, heterogeneous, and sometimes conflicting documents. BioASQ Task B serves as a key benchmark for evaluating evidence-grounded reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs) by requiring systems to generate exact and ideal answers from evidence snippets.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Previous approaches typically apply a single prompting strategy or inference pipeline to all question types. However, Yes/No, Factoid, and List questions have distinct reasoning requirements and evaluation metrics. A one-size-fits-all strategy fails to address the need for decision stability in Yes/No questions, precise entity identification in Factoid questions, and high-coverage verification in List questions, leading to insufficient robustness and accuracy in complex biomedical contexts.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: An "Answer-Type-Aware" LLM framework for BioASQ 14b Task B that selects specific inference procedures based on question type, combining ensemble prediction and agent-based verification to enhance reliability.
- **Challenge 1**: Yes/No questions are sensitive to the ordering of evidence snippets and suffer from instability when facing dispersed or conflicting evidence. **Solution**: The paper introduces snippet shuffling and self-reflection mechanisms. Shuffling reduces sensitivity to input order, while self-reflection calibrates decisions, thereby improving judgment stability.
- **Challenge 2**: List questions require retrieving multiple correct entities while avoiding unsupported or redundant answers, a task difficult for single models to balance between recall and precision. **Solution**: A multi-agent architecture is employed, decomposing the task into collaborative steps: evidence extraction, candidate generation, answer verification, and final aggregation, ensuring comprehensive coverage and strict validation.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Preliminary experiments on BioASQ 13b were conducted to identify optimal strategies for each question type. The final framework was evaluated in the official BioASQ 14b Task B challenge. Specific pipelines included: shuffling/reflection for Yes/No; full-snippet input with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) in-context learning for Factoid; and multi-agent collaboration for List questions.
- **Evaluation Results**: The framework demonstrated competitive performance across multiple batches in the official evaluation. Notably, it achieved first place in the Factoid subtask of Batch 4. These results highlight the effectiveness of combining type-specific inference, ensemble methods, and agent-based verification.
- **Comparison**: Compared to baseline methods using uniform prompting strategies, this type-specific approach significantly improved answer robustness and evidence grounding, particularly excelling in tasks requiring high-precision entity matching and complex logical verification.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the challenges of dispersed evidence and complex reasoning in biomedical QA by proposing an Answer-Type-Aware LLM framework. By designing dedicated inference pipelines for Yes/No, Factoid, and List questions (such as snippet shuffling, CoT context learning, and multi-agent collaboration), it effectively resolves the limitations of single-strategy approaches. Experiments show superior performance in the BioASQ 14b challenge, including a first-place finish in the Factoid subtask, validating the value of refined, modular reasoning processes in domain-specific QA.
