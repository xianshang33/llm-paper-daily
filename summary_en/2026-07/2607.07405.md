## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Tool-using LLM agents exhibit a "silent wrong-state" failure mode. In policy-permissive environments, tools execute any well-formed call even if it violates domain policies (e.g., cancelling a non-refundable booking). This results in a corrupted system state without any tool errors or exceptions, making the violation invisible to the agent's self-report.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current benchmarks and models rely on the LLM's reasoning to adhere to natural language policies. However, 78% of failures in the studied domain are silent policy violations that are reproducible across seeds, indicating they are not mere sampling noise. Simply using stronger models or resampling is unreliable because the root cause is the lack of deterministic enforcement at the action boundary.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a** lightweight intervention: deterministic, read-only pre-execution gates that inspect proposed tool calls against the current database state before allowing writes.
- **Challenge 1: Silent violations are undetectable by the agent**. Since tools do not raise errors, the agent cannot detect the failure from its trace. The proposed method addresses this by intercepting违规 writes deterministically at the action boundary, independent of the model's self-reflection.
- **Challenge 2: Reliance on probabilistic reasoning is unstable**. Enhancing model reasoning is costly and inconsistent. The method decouples policy enforcement from probabilistic generation by using deterministic code checks, significantly improving success rates where policies are at risk of being violated.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: Evaluated on the $\tau^2$-bench airline domain using a suite of four deterministic gates. These gates perform read-only checks before write operations.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - On gpt-4o-mini, full-benchmark success increased from 29.6% to 42.0% (+12.4pp; P=0.0012).
    - The improvement was reproduced on a disjoint 15-seed set (+12.3pp; P=0.0008).
    - The effect was concentrated in tasks where gates fired: +19.2pp success rate on 26/50 firing tasks, with negligible change on non-firing tasks.
- **Comparison with Related Work**:
    - **Negative Controls**: In self-enforcing retail domains and BFCL, the gates provided little benefit, confirming the mechanism is specific to policy-permissive tool environments.
    - **Frontier Models**: Even on gpt-5.2 (default reasoning), silent violations persisted. The same gate suite improved success from 61.2% to 71.6% (+10.4pp), demonstrating that the failure mode exists in frontier models and the solution is generalizable.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper identifies and quantifies a critical "silent policy-violation" failure mode in tool-using LLM agents. It demonstrates that relying on model reasoning for policy enforcement is unreliable when tools do not enforce constraints themselves. By introducing deterministic pre-execution gates, the authors show that system reliability can be significantly recovered at low cost without modifying the model. The work advocates for a "Reason Less, Verify More" paradigm, emphasizing deterministic code-based verification at the action boundary over probabilistic generation for ensuring policy compliance.
