## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: As Large Language Models (LLMs) shift towards post-training with Reinforcement Learning (RL), traditional synchronous batch-interleaved RL pipelines prove inefficient for long-horizon agentic tasks due to GPU idle time waiting for slow rollouts. Asynchronous RL has emerged as a more efficient alternative by updating the model as rollouts arrive.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current asynchronous RL systems prioritize throughput over training stability and task effectiveness. Specifically, the widely-used GRPO framework relies on group-wise sampling, which is incompatible with asynchronous training. This mismatch causes latency-driven off-policy issues because groups must wait for all members to finish, hindering performance in dynamic online environments.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Single-rollout Asynchronous Optimization (SAO)**: A novel optimization framework designed to address stability and off-policy challenges in asynchronous agentic RL.
- **Challenge 1: Severe off-policy effects and poor generalization in asynchronous settings**. Trajectories generated by different versions of old models lead to unpredictable distribution shifts, harming stability. SAO addresses this by replacing group-wise sampling with single-rollout sampling (one rollout per prompt), significantly reducing off-policy effects and improving generalization, further enhanced by practical value-model training designs.
- **Challenge 2: Training instability during asynchronous optimization**. Asynchronous updates can cause gradient explosions or oscillations. SAO introduces a strict double-side token-level clipping strategy to constrain update magnitudes, ensuring stable training for over one thousand steps.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: SAO has been successfully deployed in the agentic RL pipeline for training the open-source GLM-5.2 model (750B-A40B), demonstrating its scalability.
- **Evaluation Results**: SAO consistently outperforms GRPO and its variants across multiple agentic coding and reasoning benchmarks, including SWE-Bench Verified, BeyondAIME, and IMOAnswerBench. For instance, on SWE-Bench Verified, SAO achieved 76.0% accuracy compared to GRPO's 54.8%; on BeyondAIME, SAO reached 97.3% versus GRPO's 88.3%.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Unlike previous asynchronous RL works that focus mainly on efficiency, SAO significantly improves task effectiveness and stability. It also demonstrates superior adaptability in simulated online learning settings where environments evolve, a scenario where traditional synchronous methods or standard GRPO struggle.

## 4. Conclusion
The proposed SAO framework effectively mitigates off-policy effects and training instability in asynchronous RL through single-rollout sampling and strict token-level clipping. Experiments show that SAO not only surpasses GRPO on reasoning and coding benchmarks but also exhibits strong online adaptability. Its successful application in training the massive GLM-5.2 model establishes SAO as a new paradigm for efficient and stable agentic RL post-training.
