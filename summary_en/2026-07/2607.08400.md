## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: LLM agents reach users via resellers who may rebrand or substitute models. Attribution relies on trajectory logs (tool calls, observations, actions) stored and processed by resellers. Watermarks must survive adversaries with full read/write access to this evidence.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Existing agent watermarks read attribution directly from the log, failing against adversaries who delete or rewrite logs. Deletion desynchronizes position-based keys, while rewriting alters content; no single key handles both threats.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed TRACE**: A two-channel robust attribution watermark using complementary embeddings to achieve distortion-free action choices, self-synchronization under deletion, and unconditional invariance under rewriting.
- **Challenge 1: Desynchronization due to deletion**. Deleting steps breaks position-based keys, making detection difficult. **Solution**: TRACE introduces a "Selection Channel" keyed on local content with a distortion-free sampler. This keeps the agent's distribution provably unchanged and allows detection to resynchronize after deletions.
- **Challenge 2: Content alteration due to rewriting**. LLM rewriting changes text content, breaking content-based watermarks. **Solution**: TRACE introduces a "Tally Channel" that sets record counts per decision group, keyed solely on the log's skeleton (structure). This structural information remains untouched by any strength of LLM rewriting.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on ToolBench and ALFWorld datasets.
- **Performance**: TRACE matches the success rate of unwatermarked agents, demonstrating its distortion-free nature.
- **Robustness Results**:
    - **Selection Channel**: Achieves detection scores near z = 100 on long-horizon trajectories and remains detectable under 70% step deletion.
    - **Tally Channel**: Remains exactly unchanged under LLM rewriting of any strength.
- **Comparison**: Unlike existing methods that fail under log manipulation, TRACE is the first to simultaneously achieve distortion-free behavior, deletion self-synchronization, and rewriting invariance.

## 4. Conclusion
TRACE addresses core challenges in LLM agent trajectory watermarking through a novel two-channel design. It uses a content-based selection channel to resist deletion and a structure-based tally channel to resist rewriting. Theoretically, it proves that the watermark signal is bought with decision entropy, forcing adversaries to corrupt trajectories to remove the watermark. The method provides strong attribution robustness without compromising agent performance.
