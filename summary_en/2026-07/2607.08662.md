## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: LLM-based web search agents are evolving from simple factoid QA to complex, research-oriented tasks requiring both deep (multi-hop) and wide (broad coverage) search capabilities.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Single-agent systems (e.g., ReAct) struggle with long trajectories and limited context, failing to balance depth and width. Existing multi-agent systems improve coverage via parallelism but suffer from limitations in recursive depth, adaptive collaboration, and evidence-grounded expansion, often relying on static initial plans or fixed collaboration modes that misalign with dynamic Web structures.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed WebSwarm**: A progressive recursive delegation framework that jointly constructs task decomposition, recursive expansion, and agent collaboration during inference.
- **Challenge 1: How to dynamically balance search depth and width, avoiding the limitations of static planning?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional methods rely on fixed pre-search plans, unable to adapt to emerging evidence, leading to inefficiency or missed information in complex Web environments.
  - **Solution**: WebSwarm dynamically instantiates agentic search nodes, each coupling a local objective with a search mode. Nodes can solve objectives or delegate to child nodes, returning evidence upward to allow parent nodes to expand, revise, or aggregate the search process recursively based on real-time findings.
- **Challenge 2: How to improve multi-agent collaboration efficiency and reduce redundant exploration?**
  - **Difficulty**: Parallel or recursive searches often lead to redundant paths and lack awareness of Web information organization, wasting resources.
  - **Solution**: WebSwarm probes how task-relevant information is organized on the Web to ground node expansion and reuses process-level experience across homogeneous sibling nodes, guiding efficient and grounded search expansion.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experiments**: Evaluated on BrowseComp-Plus, WideSearch, DeepWideSearch, and GISA benchmarks, covering deep, wide, and interleaved tasks.
- **Results**: WebSwarm consistently outperforms single-agent (e.g., ReAct) and multi-agent baselines (e.g., Vanilla Multi-Agent, Plan-then-Orchestrate) across all task types, demonstrating higher accuracy and more effective search paths.
- **Analysis**: Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of recursive delegation, Web probing, and experience reuse. The system shows robustness across varying task difficulties and Web tool efficiencies, with strong model generalization.

## 4. Conclusion
WebSwarm addresses the limitations of existing search agents in handling complex deep-and-wide tasks through a progressive recursive delegation mechanism. Its key innovations include dynamic node instantiation, Web-structure-guided search, and experience reuse among sibling nodes. Experiments demonstrate significant improvements over state-of-the-art baselines, offering a new paradigm for building efficient, adaptive multi-agent search systems.
