## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Deployed LLM agents rely on "agentic context," specifically mutable persistent system-level instructions updated from operational experience while the model and tools remain fixed.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Over long evolution horizons, flat-text maintenance makes verification increasingly difficult as accumulated instructions grow and interact. Implicit relationships in linear text lead to context collapse, unbounded memory growth, and guideline conflicts, making global verification dependent on inefficient long-context processing.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: Graph-Regularized Agentic Context Evolution (GRACE), which maintains persistent instructions as a typed semantic graph and validates updates within local typed neighborhoods of modified nodes.
- **Challenge 1: Verification Complexity in Long Horizons**. Global consistency verification becomes infeasible as context length grows. GRACE addresses this by structuring instructions into a typed semantic graph, restricting validation to the local neighborhood of modified nodes for efficient, localized structural verification.
- **Challenge 2: Usability of Accumulated Instructions under Distribution Shift**. Stale assumptions and conflicts arise during distribution shifts. GRACE provides a consolidation mechanism that reconstructs accepted graph updates as incremental edits to the textual instruction checkpoint, ensuring accumulated content remains usable and consistent.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: Evaluated within a fixed telecom agent harness derived from $\tau^2$-bench under a controlled distribution-shift protocol.
- **Results**: Across five independent replications, GRACE improved strict reliability (measured by pass^3) from a Gemini 2.5 Flash zero-shot baseline of 0.091 to 0.673±0.136 at the final checkpoint.
- **Comparison**:
    - GRACE (0.673) significantly outperformed the Gemini 3.1 Pro zero-shot reference (0.242) on the same held-out set.
    - GRACE (0.673) substantially exceeded the flat-text HCE baseline (0.191±0.051).

## 4. Conclusion
The paper identifies two requirements for reliable long-horizon context evolution: a structural substrate for local verification and a consolidation mechanism for usability. GRACE effectively addresses verification challenges and consistency issues in long-cycle evolution through graph regularization, significantly enhancing agent reliability under distribution shift.
