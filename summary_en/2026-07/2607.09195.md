## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents are increasingly central to AI-driven scientific discovery, with the potential to autonomously propose hypotheses, test them, and revise beliefs based on evidence.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current agents bury hypotheses, tests, and belief updates in unstructured logs or free-text reasoning, lacking mechanisms for auditing. Most existing works automate predefined workflows or optimize for external goals without explicitly tracking the "hypothesis-test-evidence-belief" cycle, leading to opaque reasoning where evidence is often ignored and belief revisions are untraceable.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed the Hypothesis Evolution Protocol (HEP)**: An agent harness that formalizes hypothesis generation, evaluation, and evolution as explicit, auditable operations.
- **Challenge 1: Black-box nature and lack of auditability in scientific reasoning**. The difficulty lies in the unstructured internal states of current agents, making it hard to trace how hypotheses evolve. HEP addresses this by enforcing a structured protocol that explicitly records hypotheses, test designs, evidence, and belief updates, making the reasoning chain inspectable and verifiable.
- **Challenge 2: Absence of robust closed-loop belief revision**. Many agents generate hypotheses but fail to effectively update beliefs in light of contradictory evidence. HEP implements a complete "hypothesis-test-evidence-belief" cycle, ensuring agents dynamically adjust confidence levels based on empirical data, mimicking authentic scientific inquiry.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The authors deployed HEP-equipped LLM agents on materials science research tasks. The protocol acts as a generic architecture layer independent of the underlying model.
- **Evaluation Results**: Agents equipped with HEP successfully executed the full scientific cycle, which planning-style agents lack. The agents demonstrated better utilization of the protocol as the capability of the base LLM increased.
- **Comparison**: Unlike agents that merely execute predefined workflows or rank hypotheses, HEP agents showed generalization across research questions and provided transparent reasoning paths. The paper highlights that while existing agents ignore evidence in ~67% of traces, HEP aims to rectify this by enabling auditable belief updates.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper introduces the Hypothesis Evolution Protocol (HEP) to address the opacity and lack of auditability in LLM-based scientific agents. By structuring the discovery process into an explicit cycle of hypothesis, test, evidence, and belief, HEP makes the reasoning of AI scientists inspectable, verifiable, and buildable upon. Initial applications in materials science demonstrate that this approach fosters more reliable and interpretable autonomous research, marking a significant step toward auditable AI scientists.
