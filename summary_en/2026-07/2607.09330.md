## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Teams of embodied agents powered by heterogeneous Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in physical AI applications like smart factories and service robotics. Efficient coordination mechanisms are required to operate reliably under limited network resources.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current coordination frameworks rely on multi-round natural language (NL) conversations, which introduce three coupled challenges: 1) Communication overhead grows rapidly with team size; 2) Coordination quality is constrained by the heterogeneous capabilities of agents (the "short-board" effect); 3) Iterative negotiation causes action delays. Existing solutions like shared blackboards still rely on NL text, failing to address ambiguity and high bandwidth costs.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed LDT-Coord**: A networked coordination framework built upon a lightweight Digital Twin (DT).
- **Challenge 1: Decoupling Communication Overhead and Heterogeneity**
  - **Description & Difficulty**: Traditional methods depend on NL reasoning for coordination, leading to excessive communication loads and performance bottlenecks caused by the weakest LLM in the team.
  - **Solution**: Each agent independently selects an action and reports only the decision and structured temporal constraints on shared resources to the DT server. This decouples coordination performance from NL reasoning abilities, eliminating verbose negotiations.
- **Challenge 2: Conflict Resolution and Communication Efficiency Optimization**
  - **Description & Difficulty**: Resolving cross-agent conflicts while minimizing reporting frequency to save bandwidth is a complex control problem in partially observable environments.
  - **Solution**: The DT executes a training-free, rule-based orchestrator to resolve conflicts and return instructions. Furthermore, agent reporting control is formulated as a Constrained Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (C-POMDP) and solved using the PPO-Lagrangian algorithm to dynamically optimize reporting strategies.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation**: Performance was validated through simulations.
- **Comparison Results**:
  - **Task Success Rate**: LDT-Coord achieves a success rate comparable to conventional coordination methods.
  - **Communication Efficiency**: It reduces communication overhead by more than 70x compared to traditional approaches.
  - **Robustness**: The system maintains robustness under LLM heterogeneity, effectively mitigating the impact of varying agent capabilities.

#### Summary
This paper addresses the collaboration challenges of heterogeneous LLM embodied agent teams under resource-constrained networks by proposing LDT-Coord. By introducing a lightweight Digital Twin and structured constraint reporting, the framework decouples coordination from expensive natural language dialogues. Combined with a rule-based conflict resolver and RL-based communication control, the method significantly reduces communication overhead while maintaining high task success rates, offering a scalable solution for large-scale heterogeneous embodied agent coordination.
