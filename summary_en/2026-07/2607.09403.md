## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Worldbuilding is a foundational task in game design and literary creation, involving dozens to hundreds of interconnected concepts such as geography, races, and history. Traditional manual construction is inefficient and prone to inconsistencies.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: While Large Language Models (LLMs) offer new possibilities for automated content generation, their application to worldbuilding faces three main challenges: context explosion that grows linearly with the process, the tension between creative diversity and content consistency, and the lack of automated quality assurance. Existing methods struggle to simultaneously manage long contexts, ensure logical consistency, and provide automated review.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed AutoWorldBuilder**, a multi-agent collaborative system addressing these challenges through five integrated components.
- **Challenge 1: Context Explosion**. As worldbuilding progresses, the context window fills up, leading to performance degradation and high costs. The paper proposes a four-layer context compression mechanism that achieves approximately 90% token reduction, effectively mitigating the burden of long contexts.
- **Challenge 2: Conflict between Creativity and Consistency & Lack of QA**. Generating numerous concepts often leads to logical conflicts without effective review. The paper introduces an iterative review system with specialized Auditor agents, improving proposal pass rates from 42% to over 85%, and employs a structured concept network for conflict detection to ensure self-consistency.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation Details**: The system includes a structured concept network with conflict detection, a DAG-based hybrid batch scheduler grouping tasks by semantic locality, four-layer context compression, an iterative review system, and a skill-driven agent architecture supporting zero-code extension.
- **Experimental Setup**: Experiments were conducted on 20 diverse worldbuilding tasks using GPT-OSS 120B and DeepSeek v3.2 as LLM backends.
- **Results**: The system achieved a 95.0% success rate. It generated 56–103 self-consistent concepts per world in 18–31 minutes, with zero conflicts detected by the review pipeline.
- **Comparison**: Compared to traditional methods or basic LLM applications, AutoWorldBuilder significantly improved proposal pass rates (from 42% to >85%) and achieved zero-conflict delivery, demonstrating its effectiveness in knowledge-intensive multi-agent tasks.

## 4. Conclusion
AutoWorldBuilder successfully addresses context explosion and consistency issues in LLM-based worldbuilding through hierarchical context compression, semantic-locality scheduling, and separation of generation and review. The system demonstrates efficient, high-quality automated construction capabilities for game and literary creation, and its architectural patterns are transferable to other complex, knowledge-intensive multi-agent LLM applications.
