## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Model (LLM) agents requires effective orchestration of diverse expert models and tools. Current systems often rely on static assignment or coarse-grained matching, routing all steps to fixed generalist models and ignoring the advantages of specialized experts for specific sub-problems.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Existing frameworks typically call APIs based on coarse-grained matching between tasks and expert functions, overlooking critical factors like performance variability and cost efficiency among functionally similar alternatives. Furthermore, coarse-grained routing fails to exploit sub-task structures, and the lack of reliable measures for an agent's true success probability leads to assigning critical logic to overconfident but incompetent agents, causing reasoning chains to collapse.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Agora Framework**: A framework that introduces an incentive-compatible auction mechanism for dynamically allocating tasks to expert models and tools. It treats reasoning steps as tradeable items, ensuring critical logic is routed to the most capable solver rather than the most overconfident one.
- **Challenge 1: Structural Alignment and Lack of Fine-Grained Routing**. Existing methods perform coarse-grained routing at the query level, failing to utilize the atomic sub-task structures generated by planners. Agora addresses this by operating in two phases: a Planner decomposes queries into atomic units, and an Auction treats these units as tradeable items, enabling precise, step-level dynamic routing.
- **Challenge 2: Trustworthy Valuation and Overconfidence**. Agents often hallucinate certainty, making dynamic allocation risky without a reliable measure of true competence. Agora introduces a hierarchical calibration strategy combining static baselines with online adaptation (embedding-based binning and online refinement) to standardize confidence estimation, filtering out hallucinated certainty and ensuring the auction is driven by genuine competence.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Evaluation Setup**: Extensive evaluations were conducted across five benchmarks covering text, scientific-code, and multimodal tasks.
- **Baselines**: Compared against matched single-model, traditional routing, and cascade baselines under comparable candidate pools.
- **Results**: Agora outperforms all baseline methods across the benchmarks. The results demonstrate that Agora not only improves reasoning accuracy but also exposes a controllable cost-quality trade-off through a single auction parameter, allowing for optimized computational costs while maintaining high performance.

## 4. Conclusion
Agora addresses the challenges of dynamic task allocation in LLM agent reasoning by introducing an auction-based mechanism. It overcomes the structural limitations of coarse-grained routing and the valuation issues caused by agent overconfidence through a hierarchical calibration strategy. Empirical results show that Agora significantly enhances reasoning performance across various complex tasks and provides flexible cost control, offering a new paradigm for building efficient and reliable collaborative LLM agent systems.
