## 1. What the paper did
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) achieve high scores in medical knowledge assessments, but clinical reasoning requires actively deciding what to investigate under uncertainty. Clinical decision-making in hematologic oncology involves the sequential integration of incomplete and evolving information.
- **Why existing work fails**: Current evaluations often focus on static knowledge Q&A, failing to capture the core challenge of "active information seeking under uncertainty" in real-world practice. While models possess medical knowledge, they exhibit systematic failures in information-seeking behaviors when simulating authentic clinical workflows, limiting diagnostic accuracy.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed an** agentic evaluation framework for hematologic oncology, requiring models to proactively request clinical data across three sequential rounds before committing to a diagnosis and treatment plan.
- **Challenge 1: Declining information utilization efficiency**. The difficulty lies in models ceasing exploration of critical data in later rounds. The study found that information utilization is the strongest predictor of diagnostic accuracy, yet it collapsed from 57% to 26% in the final round, leaving crucial molecular and cytogenetic data unchecked.
- **Challenge 2: Decoupling of reasoning coherence and final accuracy**. The difficulty is that model reasoning traces are locally coherent (91% above threshold) but decorrelated from global correctness. Error analysis identified search satisficing, anchoring, and premature closure as dominant failure modes, revealing cognitive biases similar to those of novice clinicians.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation**: The study evaluated 32 frontier models. The best-performing model achieved only 68% overall accuracy.
- **Key Findings**: Information utilization (the fraction of available data actually requested) strongly correlated with diagnostic accuracy (R = 0.69, P < 0.001). However, models tended towards "search satisficing," stopping their search once a plausible explanation was found, thereby ignoring subsequent critical evidence.
- **Comparison & Insight**: Contrary to the view that LLMs lack medical knowledge, this study demonstrates that the primary limitation is not knowledge insufficiency but a systematic failure in information-seeking under uncertainty. The phenomenon of high reasoning scores but low accuracy highlights the limitations of current evaluation metrics that focus solely on reasoning fluency.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper reveals that the main bottleneck for current LLMs in clinical oncology is not a lack of medical knowledge, but a systematic failure in information-seeking under uncertainty. Models exhibit cognitive biases similar to novice clinicians, such as anchoring and premature closure. Future improvements should focus on enhancing models' active information acquisition capabilities in dynamic, uncertain environments rather than merely expanding knowledge bases.
