## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: While Large Language Models (LLMs) show promise in news summarization, they struggle with long-document summarization due to input length limits, context processing constraints, and hallucination issues.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Traditional segmenting-and-aggregating methods often result in incomplete or unfaithful summaries. Existing multi-agent frameworks, while improving capabilities through role-playing or question decomposition, lack a refined mechanism for collaboratively optimizing core semantics and surface-level errors, failing to simultaneously ensure semantic consistency and linguistic accuracy.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed an Expert-Editor Stepwise Questioning Multi-Agent Framework**
- **Challenge 1: Difficulty in capturing core semantics and information loss in long documents**. The difficulty lies in LLMs' inability to accurately identify key terms and main ideas within ultra-long contexts. The method addresses this by introducing an "Expert" agent that focuses on semantic understanding and extracting key clues about the main idea, guiding the Author agent to reflect and revise the summary through stepwise questioning to ensure coherence, fluency, and faithfulness.
- **Challenge 2: Surface-level errors and inconsistency with the original text**. The difficulty involves grammatical errors and factual deviations during generation. The method addresses this by introducing an "Editor" agent that focuses on literal errors (grammar, syntax) and consistency checks against the original text, providing targeted revision clues to enhance overall reliability.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Datasets**: Experiments were conducted on two representative long-document scientific datasets: Arxiv and PubMed.
- **Metrics**: Widely recognized automatic evaluation metrics were used for quantitative assessment.
- **Results & Comparison**: The results demonstrated that the proposed method effectively improves long-document summarization quality compared to traditional segmenting-and-aggregating approaches. Furthermore, the multi-agent interaction significantly reduced the generation of irrelevant content, validating the effectiveness of the collaborative guidance mechanism between the Expert and Editor.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper proposes a multi-agent collaboration framework inspired by the academic peer-review process. By simulating the division of labor where "Experts" focus on content substance and "Editors" focus on formal norms, the method utilizes a stepwise questioning mechanism to guide LLMs in iteratively optimizing long-document summaries. This approach effectively addresses issues of information incompleteness and unfaithfulness, with experiments on Arxiv and PubMed datasets verifying its superior performance over traditional methods.
