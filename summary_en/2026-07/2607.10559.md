## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) offer a highly modular platform for adsorptive gas separation. However, their vast reticular design space makes inverse design challenging under simultaneous constraints of chemical validity, separation performance, and structural diversity.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Traditional high-throughput screening relies on fixed databases, limiting exploration of the unknown chemical space. Existing generative models or optimization algorithms often struggle to simultaneously ensure chemical validity, high performance, and structural diversity, lacking mechanisms to effectively leverage feedback from failed attempts.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed LEMO Agent**: A large-language-model (LLM) agent framework for closed-loop inverse design of gas-separation MOFs in MOFid space.
- **Challenge 1: How to ensure chemical validity and rationality of generated structures in a vast combinatorial space?**
  - **Difficulty**: Arbitrary combinations of metal nodes, organic linkers, and topologies often result in chemically invalid or unstable structures.
  - **Solution**: LEMO Agent couples language-based candidate generation with MOFid standardization and explicit validity checking. It uses an iterative "generate-validate-evaluate-remember" cycle, leveraging feedback from both successful and failed candidates to guide chemically constrained searches.
- **Challenge 2: How to avoid local optima and maintain structural diversity while improving separation performance?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional optimization methods tend to converge to a few known high-performing structures, sacrificing diversity and making it hard to balance performance with novelty.
  - **Solution**: The framework introduces structured design memory and multi-island exploration strategies, combined with Transformer-based property prediction. This allows the agent to broadly explore linker, metal, and topology choices, enriching high-performing candidates while maintaining broad chemical and topological diversity.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: LEMO Agent integrates LLM generation, MOFid standardization, validity checking, Transformer-based property prediction, structured memory, and multi-island exploration. Selected candidates were reconstructed, evaluated via Grand Canonical Monte Carlo (GCMC) simulations, and passed through an experimental down-selection workflow based on chemical feasibility and ligand purchasability, leading to initial wet-lab synthesis and SEM characterization.
- **Evaluation**: The agent was evaluated on CH4/N2 and CO2/N2 separation tasks.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Compared to representative generative, optimization, and agentic baselines, LEMO Agent enriched the pool of high-performing candidates, improved predicted separation performance, and maintained broader chemical and topological diversity. Experimental validation confirmed the feasibility of translating computational designs into synthesized materials.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper demonstrates that LLM agents can serve as interpretable and scalable design engines, accelerating MOF discovery beyond conventional fixed-library screening. LEMO Agent effectively addresses challenges in chemical validity, performance optimization, and diversity preservation in inverse design through a closed-loop feedback mechanism. It provides viable candidates for experimental synthesis, proving the practical value of AI-driven material inverse design in scientific research.
