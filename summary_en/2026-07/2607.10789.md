## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Computational imaging is crucial for recovering hidden signals from indirect, noisy measurements in fields ranging from black hole imaging to medical CT/MRI. However, constructing correct reconstruction pipelines is laborious and demands deep domain expertise, even for scientists.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: General coding benchmarks fail to expose the systematic challenges LLMs face in scientific computational imaging. They lack the depth to evaluate physical modeling, inverse problem solving, and domain-specific conventions, leading to an inaccurate assessment of AI agents' capabilities in complex scientific workflows.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Imaging-101 Benchmark**: A benchmark comprising 57 expert-verified computational imaging tasks across six scientific domains. Each task is grounded in peer-reviewed literature and canonicalized into a standardized four-stage pipeline: preprocessing, forward physics modeling, inverse solver, and visualization.
- **Challenge 1: Algorithm Selection and Physical Convention Handling**. The difficulty lies in selecting appropriate mathematical algorithms for specific physical contexts and correctly handling complex physical conventions. Imaging-101 addresses this through planning and function-level unit test tracks, exposing systematic LLM failures in algorithm matching and physical logical consistency.
- **Challenge 2: Full Pipeline Integration and End-to-End Reconstruction**. The challenge involves seamlessly integrating preprocessing, modeling, solving, and visualization, where errors in any stage cause total failure. The benchmark uses an end-to-end reconstruction track to evaluate agent coordination across the entire pipeline, highlighting the fragility of current LLMs in long-chain scientific task integration.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Evaluation Setup**: Seven frontier LLMs were evaluated on Imaging-101 across three tracks: planning, function-level unit tests, and end-to-end reconstruction.
- **Results and Comparison**: Results show that even top-performing LLMs struggle significantly with computational imaging tasks, performing far worse than on general coding benchmarks. Specific gaps were identified in algorithm selection accuracy, physical constraint enforcement, and multi-stage code integration. Compared to general programming tasks, scientific imaging tasks' reliance on rigorous logic and domain knowledge leads to significantly higher error rates, proving that general benchmarks cannot replace domain-specific ones.

## 4. Conclusion
Imaging-101 fills the gap in standardized LLM evaluation for scientific computational imaging. By systematically assessing LLM performance on real-world scientific tasks, the paper reveals specific shortcomings in algorithm selection, physical modeling, and pipeline integration. The findings suggest that achieving reliable computational imaging assistance requires developing skill-augmented, domain-specialized AI agents rather than relying solely on general-purpose LLMs.
