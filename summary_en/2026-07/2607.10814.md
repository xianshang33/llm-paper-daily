## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Evaluating LLM agents in hidden-information multi-agent environments (e.g., Werewolf) is challenging. Final outcomes are high-variance and rarely reveal why an agent made a specific decision, making it difficult to distinguish whether failures stem from missing evidence, poor belief tracking, prompt-induced behaviors, or load-induced LLM faults.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current evaluations often focus on fully observable states or tasks where the state can be reconstructed from prompts. In partially observable, adversarial settings with deceptive communication, win rates alone are noisy signals that fail to explain internal reasoning defects or separate strategy effects from confounding factors like system load.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed an Auditable Belief-Conditioned LLM Agent Framework**: This framework operates under strict code-level information isolation, maintains an external belief state over hidden roles, logs belief updates and belief-action deviations as structured evidence, and supports a defensive offline improvement loop.
- **Challenge 1: How to quantify and diagnose agent decision logic in high-noise environments?**
  - **Difficulty**: In high-variance games like Werewolf, single game outcomes do not reflect decision quality, and LLM internal reasoning is opaque, making it hard to pinpoint error sources.
  - **Solution**: Introduced an external belief layer as an auditable cognitive baseline. By recording belief snapshots and action traces, the framework transforms opaque agent behavior into replayable structured evidence for fine-grained diagnosis.
- **Challenge 2: How to distinguish the direct impact of belief content from other confounding factors (e.g., load, forced consumption)?**
  - **Difficulty**: It is difficult to determine if performance gains come from belief accuracy or extra computational load/intervention strategies, especially when direct action-belief consistency is low.
  - **Solution**: Conducted multi-arm experiments (belief-disabled, active-belief, kernel-ablation, etc.) and seed-paired comparisons to isolate strategy effects from load confounds. The study found that active belief conditions correlated with significantly better good-side outcomes, despite low direct action-belief consistency (~0.21) and complex mechanisms, demonstrating the framework's value in revealing these nuances.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: A 9-player Werewolf environment was built where LLM agents operate under strict information isolation. The system records structured game events, decision traces, belief snapshots, and evaluation reports, enabling offline review of bad cases before strategy changes.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - Experiments spanned 1,080 frozen games across multiple conditions.
  - In a 200-seed A0/A1 paired comparison, the good-side win rate increased significantly from 0.205 to 0.390 (paired McNemar test $p < 0.001$) with the active belief condition, along with fewer irreversible witch-poison errors.
  - Direct action-belief consistency was found to be low (~0.21).
  - Counter-intuitively, giving beliefs only to werewolves helped the good side more than giving them only to the good side, arguing against a simple "holder-benefit" account.
- **Comparison**: Unlike traditional black-box evaluations, this framework not only demonstrated performance improvements but also exposed low consistency and complex mechanisms. It rejected unreliable forced-consumption interventions with evidence and successfully separated strategy effects from load confounds, providing deeper insights.

## 4. Conclusion
The primary contribution of this paper is the auditable framework itself, rather than just performance optimization. The framework makes LLM agent behavior in hidden-information games measurable and interpretable. By positioning external belief as an auditable cognitive baseline, the study turns opaque agent behavior into replayable evidence, supporting safer and more controlled agent iteration. Although active belief is associated with better outcomes, its exact mechanism remains unresolved; the framework's value lies in its ability to expose these issues and provide empirical evidence.
