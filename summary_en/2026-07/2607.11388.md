## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have empowered digital agents for computer use. However, real-world tasks are often long-horizon, involving evolving contexts with accumulated observations, intermediate edits, failed attempts, and partial executions.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current agents typically operate over raw interaction history. As context grows, critical information becomes buried, making task states ambiguous and difficult to interpret, verify, or recover from, which ultimately limits reliable long-horizon execution.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed StructAgent**: A state-centered framework that introduces a unified state for maintaining compact, verifiable task progress and a structured workflow that regulates progress through verifier-backed state transitions.
- **Challenge 1: State Ambiguity and Lack of Verifiability in Long-Horizon Tasks**. The difficulty lies in extracting clear causal progress from massive, unstructured raw history, leading to inaccurate state assessment. StructAgent addresses this by introducing a "Unified State" that abstracts task progress into a compact, structured representation, enforcing that all state updates are backed by a verifier to ensure interpretability and truthfulness.
- **Challenge 2: Inefficient Error Recovery and Planning**. The challenge is that agents relying on raw history struggle to pinpoint error sources and perform targeted fixes, often leading to repetitive failures. StructAgent solves this via a "Structured Workflow" that manages state transitions, enabling explicit progress checkpointing, evidence-driven task completion, and targeted failure recovery, allowing for precise backtracking and correction based on solid evidence.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Extensive experiments were conducted on benchmarks including OSWorld-Verified, Mind2Web, and Minecraft, utilizing various LLM and VLM backbones.
- **Key Results**:
    - On OSWorld-Verified, StructAgent significantly improved performance: Qwen3.5-9B success rate increased from 27.0% to 46.9%; Qwen3.5-27B from 31.6% to 62.2%.
    - Achieved a new open-source state-of-the-art (SOTA) of 78.9% success rate using the MiniMax-M3 model.
    - The framework generalized beyond desktop environments to Minecraft, demonstrating its design's generality.
- **Comparison Advantage**: Compared to existing methods relying on raw history, StructAgent consistently improved performance across different backbone sizes. Its structured state management provided significant robustness advantages, particularly in tasks requiring long-term memory and complex reasoning.

## 4. Conclusion
StructAgent addresses state management and workflow control in long-horizon digital agents by introducing a unified causal structure. By moving away from raw interaction history to a verification-centric unified state and structured workflow, it enhances the interpretability and verifiability of task progress. This approach enables efficient error recovery and precise planning. Experimental results show that StructAgent significantly outperforms existing methods on multiple benchmarks, setting a new performance standard for open-source digital agents and demonstrating great potential in complex, long-horizon automation tasks.
