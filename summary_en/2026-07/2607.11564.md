## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Researchers organize collected papers into personal folder hierarchies in reference managers (e.g., Zotero). Routing new papers to the correct folder is a recurring task, formalized here as Personalized Hierarchical Paper Routing (PHPR): assigning incoming papers to user-specific hierarchies without per-user training.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Standard hierarchical text classification assumes fixed, shared taxonomies. However, personal folders are private, evolving folksonomies where meanings are defined by contained papers rather than just folder names (which may be topical, shorthand, venue-based, or process-oriented). Zero-shot baselines relying solely on names fail to capture this content-defined semantics, performing poorly on metadata-driven or implicit topic folders.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: PaperRouter-Agent, a training-free LLM agent that grounds routing decisions in folder member papers rather than folder names alone.
- **Challenge 1: Ambiguous and Non-standard Folder Semantics**. Folder names are often shorthands or metadata (e.g., "CVPR", "To Read"), making name-only matching insufficient. **Solution**: The agent retrieves and inspects existing member papers within folders to verify fit by comparing semantic similarity with the new paper, thereby understanding the true intent of the folder.
- **Challenge 2: Large Search Space and Noisy Feedback**. Personal libraries have many folders, and past user rejections provide valuable but hard-to-use signals. **Solution**: The agent narrows the candidate hierarchy, retrieves folder-specific evidence, and incorporates similarity-gated feedback from past rejections to refine routing decisions and avoid repeated errors.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: A training-free LLM agent pipeline involving candidate narrowing, evidence retrieval, member inspection, and feedback integration. Designed to be low-cost and practical.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - **Real Personal Libraries**: Overall Recall@1 improved from 0.39 to 0.61, and Recall@3 from 0.57 to 0.83. The largest gains were seen in organizational folders defined by metadata (venue/year), where Recall@1 jumped from 0.09 to 0.50, a area where single-shot methods collapse.
    - **Public Benchmark (LaMP-2)**: Accuracy improved from 44.5% to 51.5% (+9.0 macro-F1) over the single-shot baseline.
- **Comparison**: Outperforms name-only zero-shot baselines, demonstrating robustness in handling multimodal papers (e.g., correctly routing CLIP) and metadata-oriented folders, addressing the semantic gap that causes baseline failures.

## 4. Conclusion
PaperRouter-Agent successfully addresses the challenge of hierarchical routing in personalized literature management by introducing a "content-grounded" mechanism. It moves beyond traditional name-dependent methods by analyzing the semantic content of existing folder members to interpret user-specific classification logic. Experiments show significant accuracy improvements in both real-world scenarios and public benchmarks, particularly for non-topical (e.g., venue/year) folders, offering an efficient, low-cost automation solution for personal knowledge management.
