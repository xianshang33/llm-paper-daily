## What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), particularly Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), is routinely applied to supervised fine-tuned checkpoints to create stronger agents.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Prior work often assumes GRPO improves performance on capable models but lacks a deep understanding of whether it adds new skills or merely reshapes existing behaviors in small (4B-8B) language and vision-language Web agents, especially when tasks are already largely mastered. The boundary conditions for GRPO's effectiveness and its failure mechanisms were previously undefined.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Controlled Null Result and Its Mechanistic Explanation**: Through large-scale controlled experiments, the paper demonstrates that GRPO fails to improve success rates on saturated tasks and can even be harmful, while elucidating the underlying internal model mechanisms.
- **Challenge 1: Distinguishing between algorithmic ineffectiveness and experimental setup issues.**
  - **Difficulty**: If GRPO yields no improvement, it is difficult to determine if the method is inherently ineffective for saturated tasks or if the training pipeline, reward function, or hyperparameters are flawed.
  - **Solution**: The authors tested the identical harness, reward, and recipe on tasks where rewards are reachable by sampling. Success rates increased by 22 percentage points, proving the pipeline works. This establishes that the "null result" on mastered tasks is due to saturation, not system failure, and GRPO only helps when there is headroom (sampled policy outperforms greedy).
- **Challenge 2: What is the specific mechanism behind GRPO's failure or harm on saturated tasks?**
  - **Difficulty**: Explaining why moderate learning rates cause degradation while high learning rates cause collapse, with different impacts across model components.
  - **Solution**: Double dissociation analysis reveals that degradation from moderate learning rates localizes to Attention and MLP blocks, while collapse from high learning rates is not traceable to a single group, with causally inert embedding changes dominating weight movement. Additionally, effective rank in late layers tracks capability changes in 4B models but decouples in 8B models, indicating scale-dependent mechanisms.

## Implementation, Deployment, and Evaluation Results
- **Experimental Setup**: A control grid of 18 runs varying learning rate, KL weight, seeds, initialization, and clipping. Evaluation used 25 evaluation seeds and 6 training seeds, covering text trajectories and Set-of-Marks screenshot observations, scaling backbones to 8B.
- **Key Results**:
  - No configuration credibly improved the success rate of a strong supervised baseline on largely mastered tasks.
  - On text trajectories, moderate to high learning rates significantly degraded performance; harm was only nominal under Set-of-Marks.
  - In contrast, on tasks with headroom, the same setup yielded a 22% success rate increase.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Unlike studies blindly applying RLVR expecting universal gains, this paper defines GRPO's limitations: it cannot add new skills to saturated policies but only improves when sampled policies outperform greedy ones. It also uncovers a scale-dependent coupling between effective rank and capability in small models, a phenomenon previously under-explored.

## Summary
This paper rigorously demonstrates that for 4B-8B small language and vision-language Web agents, applying GRPO on largely mastered tasks does not improve performance and can cause degradation or collapse due to inappropriate learning rates. GRPO's effectiveness depends on the existence of "headroom." The study further reveals internal failure mechanisms: moderate learning rates impair Attention and MLP modules, while high learning rates cause global collapse. These mechanisms correlate with effective rank changes in small models but decouple in larger ones. These findings provide crucial insights into the application boundaries of RLVR and the mechanics of reinforcement learning in small models.
