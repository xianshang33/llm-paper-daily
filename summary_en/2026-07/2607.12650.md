## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are prone to hallucinations in empirical reasoning, producing conclusions lacking evidentiary support or rigorous logical derivation. While tool access allows LLMs to retrieve external information, it alone does not ensure governable reasoning.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current tool-based LLM approaches suffer from two main flaws: accepted outputs do not necessarily descend from attested evidence (broken evidence chains), and accepted deductions do not withstand formal scrutiny (unverified logical validity). Thus, tool access alone cannot eliminate unsupported "Verified" outputs.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed EG-VAR (Evidence-Grounded Verified Agentic Reasoning)**: A Lean 4-based tool-calling architecture where the Lean kernel is the sole minter of "Verified" claims via tool-attestation axioms and declared source lifts.
- **Challenge 1: Ensuring outputs strictly descend from attested evidence**. The difficulty lies in LLMs potentially hallucinating sources or misassociating data. EG-VAR addresses this via Theorem 3.1, guaranteeing that every verified output structurally descends from an attested tool call, using the Lean kernel as the single trust anchor to enforce evidence provenance.
- **Challenge 2: Guaranteeing formal correctness of deductive reasoning**. The difficulty is that natural language reasoning often contains logical loopholes. EG-VAR addresses this via Theorem 3.2, ensuring all inference chains are kernel-checked. Any output failing formal verification or lacking sufficient evidence honestly "Abstains" with a replayable audit trail.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: EG-VAR employs Lean 4 as a formal sidecar, with the LLM acting as the deployment-time formalizer. This makes the target proposition, source scope, evidence boundary, proof obligation, and abstention conditions fully auditable.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - On a TableBench numerical reasoning subset (n=120), EG-VAR achieved a perfect 120/120 accuracy, compared to 95% for the same-tool baseline.
    - In counterfactual stress tests (5 domains x 2 models), EG-VAR maintained 100% source faithfulness, while the same-tool baseline dropped to 80-90%, and the no-tool baseline to 50-80%.
    - Residual semantic-formalization error rates were 3.3% for Sonnet and 1.7% for Opus.
- **Comparison**: Unlike traditional same-tool baselines, EG-VAR eliminates unsupported "Verified" outputs entirely. It transforms formalization errors, source authority disputes, and ambiguities into explicit audit targets, significantly enhancing the governability of high-stakes empirical claims.

#### Summary
EG-VAR addresses LLM hallucinations in empirical reasoning by introducing a Lean 4-based formal verification mechanism. It demonstrates superior accuracy and source faithfulness compared to existing methods in both numerical reasoning and stress tests. Furthermore, it provides a technical governance interface that makes high-risk AI decision processes fully auditable. As typed sidecars become prevalent in datasets and APIs, the formalization burden is expected to be amortized into reusable infrastructure, fundamentally improving the trustworthiness of LLM reasoning.
