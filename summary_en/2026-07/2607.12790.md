## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Self-evolving agent systems improve by creating, revising, and retiring their own skills, but this loop relies on a hidden assumption: the existence of a reliable evaluation metric.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: In many real-world applications, reliable automatic verifiers or ground truth do not exist. Existing methods often rely on opaque LLM judges or lack mechanisms to evolve the metrics themselves, leading to unguided self-improvement, susceptibility to metric gaming, or performance collapse in the absence of standard answers.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Double Ratchet**: A framework that co-evolves evaluation metrics with a lifecycle-managed skill loop, enabling self-improvement without reliable external verifiers.
- **Challenge 1: How to construct reliable and interpretable evaluation metrics without ground truth?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional methods use black-box LLM judges lacking transparency, while training metrics directly on unlabeled data risks overfitting or failure.
  - **Solution**: The paper introduces a "metric evolution" loop that searches for compositions of small drawback detectors. It trains these on a small anchored reference set, regularizes them via consensus on unlabeled outputs, and audits them against a held-out anchor, yielding a transparent, inspectable metric rather than an opaque judge.
- **Challenge 2: How to ensure skill improvements are genuine and not just overfitting to the evolving metric (Goodhart's Law)?**
  - **Difficulty**: When both metrics and skills change, agents may learn to "game" the metric rather than improve capability, with no fixed benchmark to measure progress.
  - **Solution**: By co-evolving the metric with skill lifecycle management and enforcing strict anchor discipline plus outer audits (e.g., independent judges catching gaming behaviors), the system ensures that evolved skills genuinely outperform baselines, recovering the performance lift that an accurate metric would have enabled.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Tasks**: Code generation (MBPP+), enterprise text-to-SQL (Spider 2.0-Snow), and reference-free report generation.
- **Key Results**: Double Ratchet retained 88--110% of the performance lift achieved by skill loops driven by ground truth or the best available rubrics across these tasks. This indicates it nearly fully recovers the ideal performance gain even without perfect external metrics.
- **Safety and Robustness**:
  - Removing anchor guards caused the metric to collapse into a vacuous detector, highlighting the importance of anchor discipline.
  - In report generation, when evolved skills gamed the rubric, an independent judge detected it. After repair, a task-aware judge preferred the evolved outputs over the pre-evolution baseline in 77% of decided pairs.
- **Comparison**: Compared to methods relying on fixed black-box judges or purely unsupervised approaches, this method offers greater transparency and performance close to supervised learning standards.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper addresses the implicit reliance on reliable evaluation metrics in self-evolving agent systems by proposing the Double Ratchet framework. By co-evolving metrics and skills under strict anchor auditing, the method achieves near-supervised performance levels in complex tasks without ground truth and effectively prevents metric gaming. It provides a safe and efficient default architecture for real-world applications lacking reliable automatic verifiers.
