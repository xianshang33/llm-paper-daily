## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: LLM-based agents have rapidly improved at operating individual digital environments such as mobile applications, desktop systems, and smart homes. However, real-world user goals often span multiple devices: information may come from a phone, be processed on a desktop, and the result may need to appear on another device.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Most existing benchmarks center on a single dominant execution environment, making it difficult to evaluate whether agents can acquire and integrate information across heterogeneous devices and complete end-to-end tasks with cross-device dependencies.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed DevicesWorld**: A large-scale executable benchmark for cross-device collaborative operation, containing 6,140 tasks that integrate mobile, desktop, and IoT device environments into a unified interaction and evaluation framework.
- **Challenge 1: Difficulty in Information Integration and State Synchronization Across Heterogeneous Environments**. Agents struggle with diverse interfaces and state mechanisms, often confusing source and output devices or getting stuck during information acquisition. The paper addresses this by defining a unified task structure with participating devices, initial states, executable actions, and rule-based verifiers, alongside a multi-stage construction pipeline to ensure realistic, automatically verifiable tasks.
- **Challenge 2: Difficulty in Long-Horizon Task Planning and Execution with Cross-Device Dependencies**. Agents must continuously decide which device to use next and ensure all conditions are jointly satisfied, often failing by terminating early or achieving only partial success. The paper provides diagnostic trajectory analysis to reveal specific error patterns (e.g., interface manipulation failures), turning cross-device collaboration into an executable, reproducible, and diagnostically useful evaluation problem.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: DevicesWorld integrates mobile, desktop, and IoT environments. Each task includes natural language goals, device states, actions, rule-based verifiers, and cleanup procedures, ensured by a rigorous quality-control pipeline.
- **Evaluation Results**: Five frontier LLM-agent systems were evaluated on a fixed set. All methods achieved low success rates, with the best reaching only 12.5%. Among failed runs, about 28.7% satisfied at least one scoring condition but failed the full task.
- **Comparison**: Unlike prior single-device benchmarks, DevicesWorld highlights significant deficiencies in current agents for cross-device scenarios. Trajectory analysis shows agents frequently get stuck acquiring information, confuse devices, or terminate prematurely, demonstrating the gap between single-device proficiency and cross-device competence.

## 4. Conclusion
DevicesWorld bridges the gap in cross-device agent evaluation by providing a large-scale, executable benchmark. The study reveals that while single-device capabilities have improved, current LLM agents struggle significantly with complex cross-device dependencies, achieving very low success rates. This benchmark offers essential tools and diagnostics for developing more reliable cross-device agents.
