## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Automatic Scientific Discovery (ASD) aims to enable machines to autonomously uncover natural laws, shifting from mere data-fitting tools to the generation and refinement of mechanistic models. Advances in Symbolic Regression (SR) and Large Language Model (LLM)-based agents suggest potential for recovering equations from data while incorporating domain priors.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current approaches either focus on narrow equation-discovery benchmarks or broad end-to-end automation pipelines, leaving biological systems relatively underexplored. Furthermore, existing LLM-driven scientific automation often over-promises on empirical reliability; purely numerical fitting can yield equations that are trajectory-compatible but biologically incorrect, lacking rigorous construction and criticism of mechanistic hypotheses.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed MEDA System**: An LLM- and SR-powered agentic framework designed specifically for discovering Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) models of biological and biologically inspired dynamical systems.
- **Challenge 1: Ensuring biological plausibility beyond simple data fitting.**
  - **Difficulty**: Pure numerical fitting can produce mathematically valid but biologically meaningless equations, as multiple forms can fit the same data trajectory.
  - **Solution**: MEDA incorporates knowledge-guided formalization by retrieving background knowledge, defining admissible variables, and generating mechanistic constraints, ensuring candidate ODEs adhere to biological principles.
- **Challenge 2: Reasoning and extrapolation to unseen variants.**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional methods struggle with open-ended discovery and generalizing to scenarios outside their training distribution.
  - **Solution**: Leveraging LLM reasoning capabilities, MEDA performs reasoning-based extrapolation to unseen variants and conducts open-ended discovery with or without experimental data, achieving strong structural recovery.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Setup**: MEDA's workflow includes background knowledge retrieval, variable definition, constraint generation, candidate ODE proposal, fitting, and evaluation. It was evaluated on canonical model retrieval, reasoning-based extrapolation, and open-ended discovery tasks.
- **Results**:
  - MEDA successfully recovered correct state variables across all settings.
  - It achieved strong structural recovery in both retrieval and extrapolation tasks.
  - The system produced biologically plausible, discovery-oriented models.
- **Ablation & Comparison**:
  - Ablation studies identified "knowledge-guided formalization" and "mechanistic constraints" as load-bearing components.
  - In contrast, numerical fitting alone preserved trajectory compatibility but often resulted in biologically incorrect equations.
  - Compared to state-of-the-art systems that struggle with memorization-resistant benchmarks, MEDA demonstrates superior reliability by integrating domain priors effectively.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper introduces MEDA, an agentic framework combining LLMs and Symbolic Regression to automate ODE discovery in biological systems. By integrating background knowledge retrieval, mechanistic constraints, and rigorous evaluation, MEDA addresses the limitations of traditional data-fitting methods regarding biological plausibility. Experiments demonstrate its effectiveness in canonical model retrieval, extrapolation to unseen scenarios, and open-ended discovery, highlighting the critical role of embedding domain knowledge into automated scientific discovery workflows.
