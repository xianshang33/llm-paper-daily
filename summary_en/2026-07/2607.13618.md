## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: LLM agents are increasingly evaluated on long-horizon decision tasks (e.g., supply chain management) where the state driving cost is hidden and unobservable. Final costs do not reveal whether failure stemmed from misperceiving the world (perception failure) or failing to act correctly despite correct understanding (the knowing-doing gap).
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current benchmarks cannot separate these failure modes. Their reference policies (Oracles) either access privileged information unavailable to the agent (conflating "not knowing" with "not acting") or lack a reasonable baseline entirely, making it impossible to attribute performance bottlenecks accurately.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed STOCKTAKE Benchmark**: A 26-week supply-chain replenishment benchmark modeled as a factored Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) with six hidden factors. Its key innovation is a "Fair Oracle" computable via exact Bayes filters per factor, driven by the identical observation stream the agent receives.
- **Challenge 1: How to fairly separate perception from control failures?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional Oracles have god-like access to ground truth, failing to reflect the agent's inference upper bound under information constraints, thus obscuring the "knowing-doing gap."
  - **Solution**: STOCKTAKE computes a Skill Score bounded by a "symptom-blind base-stock floor" (0) and the "Fair Oracle" (1). By grading weekly written rationales, it separately measures "stated-belief detection lag" and "knowing-doing rate," isolating state estimation (perception) from control (action).
- **Challenge 2: How to identify and quantify different directions of action failure?**
  - **Difficulty**: Even with correct diagnosis, agents may fail due to under-response or over-response (costly actions), which are indistinguishable in traditional metrics.
  - **Solution**: By correlating stockout rates in correctly diagnosed weeks with skill scores, STOCKTAKE reveals two faces of failure: under-response and actions whose cost exceeds their protective value. Low-skill models showed lower stockout rates in diagnosed weeks, indicating their failure stems from costly, inefficient actions rather than lack of response.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Setup**: Evaluated Claude Sonnet 5, GPT-5.4, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, and Grok 4.5 across 50 seeds with curated stress profiles.
- **Key Results**:
  - **Perception**: All models detected 84-88% of hidden failures, typically within one week of onset.
  - **Control (Skill Score)**: Despite similar perception, skill scores varied widely (0.62 to -0.23). Two models performed below the symptom-blind baseline.
  - **Knowing-Doing Gap Analysis**: In persistent stress weeks, 34-43% of correctly diagnosed weeks still resulted in stockouts. Counter-intuitively, lower-skill models had fewer stockouts in diagnosed weeks, suggesting their failure arises from taking actions that are too costly rather than insufficiently responsive.
- **Comparison**: Unlike benchmarks like RetailBench that use privileged info, STOCKTAKE demonstrates that the current bottleneck for agents lies in translating correct beliefs into cost-effective actions, not just in perception.

## 4. Conclusion
STOCKTAKE resolves the evaluation challenge in partially observable environments by introducing a Fair Oracle based on exact Bayesian filtering. It clearly distinguishes between perception and control failures, revealing that top-tier models struggle not with detecting hidden states, but with converting correct cognition into economically efficient actions. This benchmark provides precise metrics for future improvements in agent reasoning scaffolding and policy elicitation.
