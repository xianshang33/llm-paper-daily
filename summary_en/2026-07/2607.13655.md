## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Explainable Reinforcement Learning (XRL) aims to enhance the transparency and interpretability of Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies, which is crucial for safety-critical and human-centric scenarios. While logic-based approaches in Explainable AI (XAI) offer compact, human-readable abstractions of decision-making, there is a lack of systematic quantification for the degree of explainability of these logical representations.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current XRL research largely relies on user studies, which are tailored to specific audiences and lack shared, objective evaluation metrics. Furthermore, the field of logic-based XAI lacks a principled method to quantify the explainability of logical rules, often relying on common-sense assessments or simple propositional fragments rather than rigorous metrics.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a framework using Inductive Logic Programming (ILP) to extract symbolic representations of RL policies, along with a novel set of explainability metrics.**
- **Challenge 1: How to extract symbolic rules from black-box RL policies that are both accurate and human-readable?**
  - Difficulty: RL policies are typically high-dimensional continuous neural networks, creating a significant semantic gap and alignment difficulty when mapping to discrete logical spaces.
  - Solution: The paper employs Inductive Logic Programming (ILP) to learn first-order logic rules from agent behavior trajectories, transforming complex neural policies into compact symbolic representations that align with agent behavior.
- **Challenge 2: How to objectively and systematically quantify the explainability of these symbolic rules without relying on subjective judgment?**
  - Difficulty: "Explainability" is a vague concept lacking a unified mathematical definition and computational standard, making it hard to compare different methods or training stages.
  - Solution: The paper defines a new set of metrics, including activation rate, feature coverage, syntactic distance, and semantic distance. These metrics quantitatively assess the alignment between symbolic rules and agent behavior, the role of features in decision-making, and policy evolution.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Setup**: Experiments were conducted across various RL domains, covering both single-agent and Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) settings. Symbolic representations were extracted via ILP, and the new metrics were applied for analysis.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - **Beyond Global Return**: The proposed metrics highlight action-specific learning dynamics, offering finer insights than merely focusing on global cumulative return.
  - **Fine-grained Feature Insights**: Compared to classical global feature importance estimation methods, this framework provides detailed insights into the specific roles of domain features in decision-making.
  - **MARL Pattern Discovery**: In multi-agent environments, the method successfully uncovered patterns of coordination, specialization, and adaptation among agents.
  - **Policy Transfer & Generalization**: The metrics provided crucial insights for the transfer and generalization of action-specific policies.
- **Comparison**: Unlike traditional methods reliant on subjective user studies or lacking quantitative standards, this approach offers objective, reproducible metrics that enable more systematic debugging and optimization of RL agents.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper advances XRL by introducing Inductive Logic Programming (ILP) to extract symbolic representations of RL policies and defining a new set of objective, planning-oriented explainability metrics (such as activation rate, feature coverage, and syntactic/semantic distances). This work not only moves XRL beyond reliance on subjective user studies but also provides a principled way to quantify the explainability of logical rules in XAI. Experiments demonstrate that these metrics reveal deep insights into learning dynamics, feature roles, and coordination patterns in both single-agent and multi-agent systems, laying the groundwork for building more robust and trustworthy RL applications in safety-critical environments.
