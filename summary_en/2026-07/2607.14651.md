## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Persistent external memory enhances LLM agent continuity but introduces durable security vulnerabilities. Adversarial content injected via standard interaction channels can be retained across turns and distort downstream behavior.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current evaluations are fragmented, focusing on simplified threat models (e.g., isolated single-record injections) and narrow memory abstractions. There is a lack of a unified benchmarking framework for realistic attack regimes, such as compositional corruption and context-triggered dormant behaviors. Furthermore, the effectiveness of emerging write-time defenses remains poorly understood beyond direct injection settings, with limited visibility into failure propagation.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed MemPoison**: A comprehensive benchmark and analysis framework featuring 1,227 hand-validated cases across four attack types, three injection channels, and three representative memory substrates, evaluated on ten model families (seven open-weight, three closed-weight).
- **Challenge 1: Lack of systematic evaluation for complex attack patterns**. The difficulty lies in capturing隐蔽 attacks involving multi-record composition or context triggers. The paper addresses this by introducing a three-tier taxonomy: (L1) direct single-record corruption, (L2) compositional multi-record corruption, and (L3) context-triggered dormant corruption, covering the full spectrum from simple to complex attacks.
- **Challenge 2: Unexplained structural blind spots in write-time defenses**. The difficulty is that while traditional defenses block L1 attacks, they fail against L2/L3 attacks without clear reasons. The paper uses Mechanistic Influence Decomposition (MID) to reveal these structural blind spots, demonstrating how seemingly benign records become harmful through joint retrieval composition or trigger-conditioned activation, thus explaining the root cause of defense failures.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on seven open-weight and three closed-weight model families across three representative memory substrates.
- **Key Results**: Baseline write-time defenses (e.g., consistency checks) substantially suppress direct L1 attacks but fail to reliably suppress L2 (compositional) and L3 (context-triggered dormant) attacks.
- **Comparison**: Unlike prior work focused on single-record injections, MemPoison reveals a distinct defense frontier. MID analysis shows that existing defenses have structural blind spots, admitting seemingly harmless records that later cause harm via composition or specific contexts. This indicates that static filtering is insufficient against advanced threats.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper presents MemPoison, the first comprehensive benchmark and analysis framework for persistent memory threats in LLM agents. By establishing a three-level attack taxonomy (L1-L3), the study uncovers structural blind spots in current write-time defense strategies when handling compositional and context-triggered attacks. The findings strongly advocate for shifting from static filtering to adaptive, context-sensitive memory defense mechanisms to address increasingly sophisticated persistent memory security risks.
