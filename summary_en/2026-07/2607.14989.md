## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are evolving from static text generators into general agents capable of understanding user intents, invoking external tools, and completing complex tasks through interaction. These agents show strong potential in software development, office automation, and business workflows.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current agent benchmarks often focus on limited scenarios, tool ecosystems, or interaction formats. This makes it difficult to systematically characterize model capabilities across heterogeneous application settings, lacking a comprehensive evaluation of general agents in diverse real-world contexts.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed OmniaBench**: A benchmark for evaluating general agents across diverse scenarios with explicit state spaces.
- **Challenge 1: Insufficient Breadth and Realism of Scenario Coverage**. Existing benchmarks lack coverage of broad domains like ToC, ToB, and ToE, and often use oversimplified scenarios. **Solution**: The paper derives application-oriented scenario knowledge from app stores, product documents, industry resources, web retrieval, and human refinement, creating a hierarchical taxonomy with 90 level-1 and 354 level-2 domains, based on which executable environments are constructed.
- **Challenge 2: Poor Diversity and Controllability of Task Synthesis**. Traditional methods struggle to generate multi-step interactive tasks that are both logical and have controllable difficulty levels. **Solution**: Tasks (single-turn and multi-turn) are synthesized via four complementary routes: DAG, DAG-S, Solver, and Program. Additionally, a ten-dimensional capability taxonomy and eight compositional atomic difficulty factors are introduced to support fine-grained evaluation.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Dataset Scale**: OmniaBench contains 1,431 tasks, including a challenging subset of 644 tasks designed to reduce evaluation costs and mitigate data contamination risks after public release.
- **Evaluation Results**: The benchmark presents substantial challenges to current frontier models. Even top-performing closed-source models like Claude-Sonnet-5 and GPT-5.6-Sol achieved Overall Pass@1 scores of only 58.54 and 57.14, respectively. Open-source models such as Qwen and DeepSeek series showed lower performance, indicating a significant gap.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Unlike prior works focusing solely on tool calling or single-domain interactions, OmniaBench reveals significant differences in model performance across domains and capabilities. Analysis highlights persistent limitations in planning, constraint maintenance, and adaptive correction in current models.

## 4. Conclusion
OmniaBench provides a broad and diagnostic benchmark for characterizing the capability boundaries of general agents. By constructing a hierarchical taxonomy covering wide industry domains and employing diverse task synthesis mechanisms, it addresses the lack of scenario diversity and evaluation granularity in existing benchmarks. Experimental results indicate that while frontier models have made progress, they still face severe challenges in handling complex, multi-step real-world agent tasks, particularly in long-horizon planning and state maintenance.
