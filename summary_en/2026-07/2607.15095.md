## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Coalition formation is central to parliamentary democracy, involving complex negotiations over policy objectives and ideological compromises. Large Language Models (LLMs) offer new avenues for computational political science, such as simulating legislative voting and generating coalition compromises.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: LLMs trained with Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) are biased towards neutrality and helpfulness, which contradicts steadfast partisan behavior. Prompt-based personas suffer from degraded reasoning and lack domain grounding, while multi-agent debates often amplify conformity and dominant-party bias, failing to accurately simulate real partisan positions and negotiations.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a multi-agent framework called "Digital Pantheon"**, which combines Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to simulate and audit political coalition formation.
- **Challenge 1: How to maintain steadfast partisan stance in LLM agents instead of neutrality?**
  - Difficulty: RLHF-induced neutrality is hard to override with simple prompts, and personas tend to degrade during interaction.
  - Solution: Used DPO to inject aggressive party-specific personas at the parameter level, combined with SFT, embedding party commitments into the model weights to ensure consistent ideological alignment.
- **Challenge 2: How to ensure factual accuracy and interpretability of negotiation content?**
  - Difficulty: LLMs are prone to hallucinations, and black-box negotiation processes make it hard to trace specific party contributions.
  - Solution: Introduced a per-party RAG pipeline to bound agents to their official manifestos; proposed Multi-Layered Information Lineage Topology (MILT) and Coalition Influence Score (CIS) to trace the provenance of agreement clauses and quantify party influence, enabling interpretable auditing.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The framework was operationalized on the 2019 Flemish election scenario, deploying partisan agents in a hub-and-spoke negotiation arbitrated by a formateur.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - Across three independent simulations, the framework yielded a stable winner and ranking (N-VA ahead of CD&V and Open Vld), consistent with historical outcomes.
  - Manifesto-anchored lineage reliably predicted the materialization of real-world clauses, whereas hallucinated content did not.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Compared to traditional game-theoretic approaches (which capture structure but not language-mediated deliberation) and generic LLM agent simulations (limited by neutrality bias and hallucinations), this method offers higher fidelity through parameter-level alignment and RAG grounding. It provides a transparent, scalable testbed for ex-ante exploration of party compatibility.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper presents the Digital Pantheon framework, addressing the lack of steadfast partisan stance and interpretability in LLM-based political simulations by combining DPO persona injection and RAG factual grounding. Experiments demonstrate that the method not only reproduces the coalition outcomes of the 2019 Flemish election but also provides transparent negotiation auditing via MILT and CIS metrics, offering a reliable tool for computational political science.
