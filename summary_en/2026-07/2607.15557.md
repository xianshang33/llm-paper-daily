## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: As Large Language Models (LLMs) evolve into autonomous agents capable of long-horizon tasks, extending agent capabilities via externalized skills (e.g., SKILL.md files) has become an industry consensus. Public repositories host a vast and growing number of these skills, but they are fragmented, redundant, and of uneven quality.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Prior research lacks a corpus that is simultaneously broadly aggregated, strictly quality-gated, and cleared for open redistribution. Previous evaluations often relied on hand-picked "oracle" skills or simulated environments, failing to characterize the true end-to-end value of community skill libraries in real-world agent tasks.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SkillCorpus Framework**: An end-to-end framework for aggregating, curating, matching, and evaluating the open skill ecosystem at scale.
- **Challenge 1: Noise and Low Quality in Massive Open Skills**. The abundance of duplicate, invalid, or unsafe open-source skills hinders direct utilization. The paper addresses this by filtering ~821,000 crawled skills down to 96,401 high-quality skills through a multi-stage pipeline, organized by a 16-class taxonomy and three quality facets (utility, robustness, safety).
- **Challenge 2: Efficient Matching of Skills to Tasks**. Precisely retrieving skills relevant to specific tasks from a large library is difficult. The paper introduces a fine-tuned retrieval-and-selection stack that accurately matches task-relevant skills, ensuring precise skill invocation.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Setup**: End-to-end evaluation was conducted across three benchmarks (SkillsBench, GDPVal, QwenClawBench), two agent harnesses, and two open backbone models, including a frontier robustness check.
- **Results**: Integrating SkillCorpus yielded consistent gains across all three benchmarks, with the largest improvement on SkillsBench (+7.5 percentage points).
- **Comparison & Analysis**: Unlike prior works using manual skills or uncurated libraries, SkillCorpus demonstrates that a curated, retrieval-served community corpus significantly improves real agent performance. Operational analysis attributes these gains to "coverage boundaries" and "harness boundaries," clarifying the scope and limitations of its effectiveness.

## 4. Conclusion
SkillCorpus provides the first end-to-end account of when and where a curated community corpus improves real-world agent tasks. It offers a high-quality, taxonomically organized skill dataset and empirically validates the effectiveness of retrieval-augmented skill invocation, serving as critical infrastructure and evidence for building more robust LLM agent ecosystems.
