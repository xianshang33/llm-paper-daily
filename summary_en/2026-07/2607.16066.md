## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: The telecommunications sector is at a critical inflection point with the maturation of 5G and the early architectural formulation of 6G. Modern networks are characterized by extreme heterogeneity, ultra-dense deployments, and stringent requirements for latency, reliability, and QoS. There is a converging vision for architectures demanding unprecedented automation, closed-loop intelligence, and operational autonomy, where Zero-Touch Service Management (ZSM) and Intent-Based Networking (IBN) are pivotal.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Existing surveys typically treat Large Language Model (LLM)-based Agentic AI and 5G/6G networks in isolation. They leave protocol integration, evaluation methodologies, and alignment with standardization bodies (such as 3GPP and ETSI) underexplored, failing to provide a cohesive framework that translates high-level service expectations into actionable, verifiable network behaviors.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a** two-part tutorial-and-survey framework that bridges the gap between Agentic AI and Next-Generation Networks (NGNs), formalizing the control, management, and AI-native planes of 5G/6G while mapping agentic capabilities to standardization efforts.
- **Challenge 1: The Integration Gap due to Domain Isolation**. Current literature lacks deep analysis on how LLM agents specifically integrate into 5G/6G protocol stacks and standards. **Solution**: Part I formalizes the network planes and covers agentic foundations (reasoning, planning, tool use); Part II maps these capabilities directly onto 5G/6G control surfaces and major 6G initiatives, aligning theoretical AI concepts with standardized network architectures.
- **Challenge 2: Difficulty in Evaluating the Paradigm Shift from Rule-Based to Goal-Driven Autonomy**. Traditional automation is reactive, whereas Agentic AI is proactive, lacking unified evaluation and alignment standards. **Solution**: The paper systematically reviews core agentic foundations (reasoning, memory, multi-agent coordination) and identifies open challenges shaping autonomous telecommunications, providing taxonomies and directions for evaluating agent efficacy in networks.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: As a survey and tutorial, this paper does not propose a single new algorithm or code deployment but provides architectural guidance. It details how agentic AI capabilities (e.g., reasoning, planning) can be integrated into 5G/6G network functions, specifically mapping them to the work of standardization bodies like 3GPP and ETSI.
- **Evaluation Results**: The paper evaluates the landscape through comparative literature analysis and taxonomy development. Table 1 summarizes related surveys, highlighting this paper's unique contributions in "protocol integration," "evaluation," and "standardization alignment." It addresses gaps in existing works regarding agent communication security, lifecycle management, and cross-domain collaboration.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Unlike existing surveys that focus solely on LLM applications in 6G [12] or only on Agentic AI autonomy [15], this work uniquely combines both domains. It specifically focuses on the standardization path and protocol-level integration, offering a complete view from AI fundamentals to standardized network implementation.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper presents a comprehensive tutorial and survey on LLM-powered Agentic AI in 5G/6G networks. It identifies the lack of protocol integration and standardization alignment in current research and addresses this through a two-part structure: first formalizing network planes and agentic foundations, and second mapping agentic capabilities to specific network control surfaces and standards. The article provides a clear roadmap for researchers and practitioners from theoretical concepts to standardized practice, highlighting key challenges for future autonomous telecommunications networks.
