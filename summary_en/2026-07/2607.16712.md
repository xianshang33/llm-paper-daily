## What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Depression affects over 280 million people globally, but traditional assessment tools like BDI-II require trained clinicians, limiting scalability. Automated conversational systems offer a promising direction for broadening screening access by inferring symptoms through natural dialogue.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Standard LLM approaches struggle because the task prohibits direct questions about mental health, requiring indirect inference. Additionally, there are no ground truth labels during submission for tuning, and there is a trade-off between diagnostic thoroughness and conversational efficiency. High-performance proprietary models are costly, while open-source models often lack the reasoning and instruction-following capabilities needed for high-quality clinical simulation interviews.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A Hybrid Multi-Agent LLM System with Structured Algorithmic Guidance for conversational depression screening.
- **Challenge 1**: Open-source models (e.g., Gemma 27B) have weaker reasoning and instruction-following capabilities compared to proprietary models (e.g., GPT-5-nano), leading to unstable interview quality. **Solution**: The system introduces a precomputed dialogue tree to standardize interview openers and follow-ups, using algorithmic constraints to compensate for the model's limitations and ensure rigorous dialogue structure.
- **Challenge 2**: Data sparsity due to the prohibition on direct questioning leads to missing symptom data, and single-model outputs may be biased. **Solution**: The method employs a reliability-weighted consensus aggregation mechanism inspired by the Weaver framework, combined with a cluster-based imputation step for unprobed symptoms, enhancing the robustness of BDI-II scoring and symptom identification.

## Implementation, Deployment, and Evaluation Results
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The pipeline evolved from a monolithic prototype to a baseline multi-agent architecture, and finally to a hybrid configuration replacing the paid GPT-5-nano interviewer with the open-source Gemma 27B. The system was fully automated across all 20 personas in the eRisk 2026 Task 1.
- **Evaluation Results**: Hybrid Run 3 achieved an ADODL score of 0.9063, ranking 3rd among all complete submissions and placing the team 2nd overall among 21 teams.
- **Comparison**: This performance outperformed the paid proprietary model baseline (Run 1, ADODL 0.8841) while reducing the per-persona API cost to approximately one-quarter. This demonstrates that with sufficient algorithmic supervision, weaker open-source models can compete with or surpass stronger proprietary models in conversational interviewing roles.

## Summary
This paper presents DS@GT's solution for eRisk 2026, highlighting the enhancement of open-source LLMs through structured algorithmic guidance (dialogue trees, weighted aggregation, and clustering imputation). The approach not only achieved superior performance at a significantly lower cost compared to proprietary baselines but also validated the feasibility of combining algorithmic supervision with open-source models for sensitive conversational tasks, offering a new pathway for low-cost, scalable automated mental health screening.
