## What the Paper Does
- **Background**: While Large Language Model (LLM) agents are increasingly equipped with tools, existing research typically provides the tools or instructions directly. Current benchmarks report a single success rate for complex tasks, failing to distinguish between "tool use" and "tool discovery," and cannot measure a model's propensity to build tools without explicit prompting.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Existing benchmarks conflate tool discovery with recall from pretraining data and often mandate tool creation, thereby failing to assess the agent's intrinsic disposition as a tool builder. They lack fine-grained decomposition and evaluation of the cognitive stages involved in tool discovery, such as component gathering, process recognition, and efficient deployment.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed the Lomekwi Framework and Environment**: Inspired by cognitive science, the paper decomposes tool discovery into three separable behaviors: Curiosity (ability to discover components), Recognition (ability to discover the creation process), and Efficiency (ability to use the tool effectively after creation).
- **Challenge 1: How to quantify and decouple the sub-capabilities of tool discovery?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional benchmarks only focus on final success, making it hard to distinguish whether a model fails due to missing components, inability to conceive the creation process, or poor execution efficiency.
  - **Solution**: The paper formalizes these dimensions, decomposing the probability of solving a task into the product of recognition, curiosity, and efficiency, plus a brute-force probability. It designs the parameterized Lomekwi environment to independently observe progress in each dimension.
- **Challenge 2: Does the relationship between model scale and tool discovery capability follow intuition?**
  - **Difficulty**: It is generally assumed that larger models are better at all tasks. However, in complex combinatorial optimization and creative tasks, larger models may underperform due to overfitting to pretraining knowledge or search strategy biases.
  - **Solution**: By introducing a class of combinatorial games and a real-world emulation environment, the paper provides evidence that "Recognition" inversely scales with model size, meaning larger models may be weaker at identifying how to construct new tools from scratch.

## Implementation, Deployment, and Evaluation Results
- **Implementation & Environment**: The authors developed a family of parameterized Lomekwi environments that are uncontaminated by pretraining data. Tool creation is optional, allowing for the measurement of the model's proactive tendency to build tools. Code and environments are open-sourced.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - Experiments confirm that curiosity, recognition, and efficiency are dissociated in practice; some agents excel in one dimension while underperforming in others.
  - **Key Finding**: The study observes inverse scaling in "Recognition," where the ability to discover the tool creation process does not monotonically improve with model size and may even decline in specific combinatorial games.
  - **Comparison**: Compared to existing discovery tasks like Voyager, the Lomekwi framework provides a more granular analysis of failure modes. Unlike traditional benchmarks that mandate tool creation, Lomekwi reveals the true willingness and capability gaps of models in autonomous tool construction without explicit instructions.

## Summary
This paper highlights the limitations of current LLM tool-use benchmarks, which conflate tool use with discovery and lack fine-grained evaluation. By proposing the Lomekwi framework, it decomposes tool discovery into curiosity, recognition, and efficiency, accompanied by a clean testing environment. The study not only validates the separability of these dimensions but also uncovers an inverse scaling relationship between model size and tool recognition capability, offering new insights and evaluation standards for understanding the autonomous tool creation abilities of LLM agents.
