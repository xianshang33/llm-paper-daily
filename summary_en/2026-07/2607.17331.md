## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems are the operational backbone of modern organizations but still delegate almost all operational decision-making to human specialists. Classical rule-based automation cannot reason about exceptions, and monolithic AI assistants degrade when coordinating across functional boundaries.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current workflow automation relies on rigid, predefined rules that fail in novel situations. Monolithic LLM assistants struggle with the complexity of cross-functional coordination, leading to degraded performance due to excessive context and tool selection spaces, preventing end-to-end autonomous execution.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Agentic ERP Architecture**: An expert-system architecture combining role-aligned Large Language Model (LLM) agents, a risk-tiered human-in-the-loop harness, and a graph-based orchestrator to execute end-to-end business workflows on production ERP backends.
- **Challenge 1: High-Complexity Sequential Decision Making and Tool Selection**
  - **Description & Difficulty**: Autonomous ERP operation is a constrained sequential-decision problem over structured enterprise states. The combinatorial explosion of available modules and APIs makes single-step tool selection extremely difficult.
  - **Solution**: The paper introduces a "role-aligned" agent decomposition strategy. By breaking down global complex problems into domain-specific sub-problems, it theoretically proves a measurable reduction in per-step tool-selection complexity, allowing agents to focus on their specialized domains.
- **Challenge 2: Coupling of Generation and Evaluation & Cross-Functional Coordination Failure**
  - **Description & Difficulty**: In traditional methods, action generation and evaluation are tightly coupled without transparent standards, leading to inconsistency and lack of interpretability in cross-functional crisis management.
  - **Solution**: A graph-based "Planner–Executor–Reflector–Responder" orchestration mechanism is designed. It decouples generation from evaluation using externalized grading criteria and "sprint contracts," ensuring decisions are inspectable and compliant with expert system norms.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Evaluation Framework**: The system was evaluated at three levels: (1) A scenario-based task suite; (2) A comprehensive comparison of six orchestration paradigms on cross-functional crisis tasks; (3) A 365-day agent-in-the-loop simulation against rule-based RPA and no-intervention baselines.
- **Comparison Results**: The proposed multi-agent method significantly outperformed baseline methods across all evaluation levels.
- **Key Metrics**: Under the same demand stream, Agentic ERP sustained a simulated year of operation with zero stockouts, whereas the rule-based baseline accumulated hundreds of stockouts, demonstrating superior handling of dynamics and exceptions.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper demonstrates that role-aligned LLM agents under human oversight can transition ERP systems from passively recording transactions to actively executing operational decisions. Agentic ERP provides a reference architecture and an evaluation protocol for autonomous enterprise resource planning, addressing the limitations of traditional automation in handling exceptions and the coordination deficits of monolithic AI.
