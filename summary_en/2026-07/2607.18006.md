## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large language models excel in reasoning but incur prohibitive training and inference costs. Enhancing multi-step logical reasoning for compact models (≤4B parameters) under limited budgets remains a critical challenge.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning methods suffer from high computational costs, training instability, and dependence on reward design. Test-time scaling methods (e.g., Chain-of-Thought, multi-agent debate) improve robustness but lack parameter-efficient optimization specifically tailored for compact models, failing to achieve optimal performance-cost balance under resource constraints.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed MADA-RL Framework**: A post-training framework for parameter-efficient reasoning in compact models using Multi-Agent Debate-Aware Reinforcement Learning. It specializes models into Generator and Critic roles and fine-tunes only a small subset of parameters via LoRA adapters.
- **Challenge 1: How to ensure Critics learn to correct errors rather than merely imitate correct answers?**
  - **Difficulty**: Static mean-reward normalization leads to imprecise credit assignment, causing critics to replicate correct answers instead of identifying and correcting generator errors.
  - **Solution**: Introduced "Counterfactual Critic Advantage," a dynamic, role-conditioned baseline. It redefines the critic's advantage as its reward minus the per-instance accuracy of the generator ensemble. This explicitly optimizes critics to outperform generator consensus, yielding more targeted credit assignment.
- **Challenge 2: How to significantly boost reasoning capabilities in compact models with minimal parameter update costs?**
  - **Difficulty**: Full fine-tuning is computationally expensive and prone to overfitting or collapse, making it hard to unlock model potential with limited data.
  - **Solution**: Employed a lightweight multi-round protocol for deploying specialized agents and fine-tuned only a tiny fraction of parameters via LoRA. The method uses 16x fewer trainable parameters than full fine-tuning baselines while significantly improving accuracy on mathematical reasoning benchmarks.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: MADA-RL specializes compact models (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B) into Generators and Critics, using LoRA for parameter-efficient fine-tuning. During deployment, these specialized agents interact via a lightweight multi-round debate protocol.
- **Evaluation Results**: Across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks, MADA-RL improved the accuracy of DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B from 39.9% to 41.9% (+2.0 points, p < 0.001). It sits on the accuracy-trainable-parameter Pareto front, using 16x fewer trainable parameters than full fine-tuning baselines.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: MADA-RL approaches but does not surpass the strongest baselines (e.g., DeepScaleR, STILL-3), which are trained on substantially larger datasets. A controlled study isolated the source of gains, showing that the counterfactual advantage yielded the highest critic improvement rate, confirming that trained critics learn to correct generator errors rather than imitate them.

## 4. Conclusion
MADA-RL successfully addresses the challenge of enhancing reasoning in compact models under resource constraints by introducing Counterfactual Critic Advantage and multi-agent debate mechanisms. It significantly improves mathematical reasoning accuracy while drastically reducing trainable parameters (16x less than full fine-tuning), demonstrating the potential of parameter-efficient fine-tuning combined with structured debate signals for low-cost, high-performance reasoning.
