## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: LLM-based agents processing external content are vulnerable to prompt injection and multi-turn manipulation. Most existing safety benchmarks evaluate defenders against fixed attack pools collected prior to evaluation.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current benchmarks (e.g., PAIR, TAP, Crescendo) release static post-hoc attack sets, failing to capture the dynamic nature of real-world adversarial interactions where attackers adapt based on defender responses. This leads to an underestimation of risk (e.g., 0-1% ASR when only scoring the first turn).

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Adaptive Adversaries Benchmark**: A 21-scenario benchmark for adaptive multi-round attacks against memoryless LLM defenders.
- **Challenge 1: Static pools miss dynamic strategy evolution**. It is difficult to simulate real-time adaptation using static datasets. The paper addresses this by employing an autonomous LLM attacker that observes prior defender responses and pivots strategies across rounds, treating each defender response as a fresh interaction.
- **Challenge 2: Single models/scenarios provide incomplete coverage**. Relying on a single attacker or scenario creates blind spots. The paper demonstrates that pooling three frontier LLM attackers (Claude Opus 4.6, GPT-5.4, Gemini) uncovers 1.4-2.2x more unique successful attacks than the best single attacker, with low similarity to existing benchmarks.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Built on the AgentBeats platform, releasing a runnable protocol with 21 evaluation scenarios, orchestrator, baselines, and a multi-attacker CLI. Also releases 945 transcripts, an attack-replay dataset, and 18,422 battle records.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - **Adaptive vs. Static**: ASR increases from 0-1% (first turn only) to 5.4-14.0% (15 adaptive rounds).
    - **Multi-Model Benefit**: Combining three attackers reveals significantly more unique vulnerabilities.
    - **Model Specificity**: Claude Opus 4.6 and GPT-5.4 tie in aggregate ASR (5.4%) but show sharp differences in specific scenarios (e.g., 60% vs 7% ASR in one case).
    - **Scenario Discriminability**: 13 of 21 scenarios distinguish defender pairs, but rankings vary widely (Kendall's W = 0.19), indicating no single best defender.
- **Comparison**: Generated attacks have low cosine similarity (0.02-0.14) to existing benchmarks, proving they cover novel attack vectors missed by static pools.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper highlights the bias in current LLM safety benchmarks due to static attack pools. By introducing the "Adaptive Adversaries" benchmark with multi-round adaptive attacks and multi-model combinations, it provides a more realistic assessment of LLM agent security. Results show that adaptive attacks significantly increase success rates and that diverse attackers and scenarios are essential for uncovering distinct defender weaknesses.
