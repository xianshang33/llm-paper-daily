## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: As Large Language Model (LLM) agents are increasingly deployed in fields like software engineering and customer support, defenses against prompt injections are typically evaluated on a per-session basis. However, in real-world deployments, attackers can distribute campaigns across independent agents, teams, and runtimes, leaving each local guardrail with only sparse, isolated fragments of the attack.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current per-session defenses classify individual interactions, while many multi-agent defenses assume coordinated tasks, shared memory, or exposed runtime graphs. They fail to address scenarios involving independent injections from the same adversary, asynchronous arrivals, and no shared runtime state, where only proxy-observable telemetry (prompts, tool calls, timing) is available.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Cross-Agent Asynchronous Campaign Attribution Framework and A²FV Method**: The paper formalizes the task of linking sessions from the same latent adversarial campaign without shared runtime state, labels, or attacker identity oracles.
- **Challenge 1: How to link dispersed attack fragments without shared context?**
  - **Difficulty**: Attacks are distributed across different agents and time, so local defenses see only isolated events without a global view.
  - **Solution**: Introduced Asynchronous Attribution Fingerprint Vectors ($A^2FV$), a lightweight proxy-side reference protocol that scores pairwise campaign similarity using proxy-observable tool usage, timing, and prompt residue to link related sessions.
- **Challenge 2: How to validate effectiveness under realistic evasion and establish a benchmark?**
  - **Difficulty**: Lack of standardized benchmarks for cross-agent asynchronous attacks and the need to prove signals are not solely style-based.
  - **Solution**: Constructed the SCD-v1 benchmark, featuring benign traffic, isolated attacks, multi-session campaigns, and matched non-oracle evasion. Experiments showed $A^2FV$ achieved 0.82 pairwise AUC on SCD-v1. Cross-style controls and stress tests confirmed the signal is partly style-sensitive but not reducible to style alone, maintaining separability under controlled evasion.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: $A^2FV$ is designed as a lightweight proxy-side protocol leveraging tool-use structures, prompt-level stylometric residue, and timing metrics. It does not require complex runtime graphs or shared memory, making it suitable for deployment at operational gateways for logging, policy enforcement, and quota management.
- **Evaluation Results**: On the SCD-v1 benchmark, $A^2FV$ achieved a pairwise AUC of 0.82 for campaign linking. The strongest fixed signals came from structural and stylometric residue, with timing serving as a diagnostic channel.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: In contrast, score-only adaptations of per-session detectors and chunked LLM judges performed near chance levels on the same task. This highlights the limitations of traditional single-session defenses and coordinated multi-agent defenses in handling such asynchronous distributed attacks.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper establishes cross-agent campaign attribution as a distinct evaluation layer for securing LLM agents in the wild. By proposing the $A^2FV$ method and the SCD-v1 benchmark, the authors demonstrate that asynchronous attack sessions can be effectively linked via proxy-observable features, even without shared state or attacker identity. This finding underscores the importance of shifting from single-session safety to multi-session latent campaign pattern recognition, offering new directions for enhancing LLM agent security in complex deployment environments.
