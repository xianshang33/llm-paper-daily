## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Shippers are increasingly delegating carrier selection to autonomous Large Language Model (LLM) agents within supply chain workflows. Digital Freight Matching (DFM) platforms are evolving from algorithmic recommendations to direct agent-based decision-making.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Previous studies focus on traditional algorithmic matching or static supplier screening, lacking analysis of dynamic market behaviors driven by real LLM agents, endogenous capacity constraints, and the impact of information design on market concentration. They fail to address the "algorithmic monoculture" risk arising when thousands of shippers rely on a few foundation models with shared preferences.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A simulation framework using shipper agents built on commercial LLMs (GPT, Claude, Gemini) to model a 30-day digital freight matching market, identifying concentration risks and effective platform information design remedies.
- **Challenge 1: Choice convergence among LLM agents leads to high market concentration.**
  - **Difficulty**: Different LLM models show strong initial preference consistency, causing a single carrier to attract up to 76% of requests on day one, creating monopoly risks.
  - **Solution**: The study finds concentration spikes when candidate lists exceed ~10 carriers. By controlling the length and structure of candidate lists displayed to shippers, platforms can intervene in this convergence process.
- **Challenge 2: Traditional trust signals (ratings, quality display) fail to mitigate volatility and concentration.**
  - **Difficulty**: Displaying true quality instead of estimated ratings does not reduce concentration or its variability, as quality only affects perception, not outcomes. Vendor diversification, list randomization, and popularity displays also show no significant effect.
  - **Solution**: The paper proposes and validates "disclosing remaining daily capacity" as an information design strategy. This provides real-time supply-demand status, dispersing demand, reducing concentration by one-third, and doubling shipper surplus.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: An agent-based simulation with 50 shipper agents powered by OpenAI (GPT), Anthropic (Claude), and Google (Gemini) LLMs. The market implements DFM rules: waterfall tendering, daily capacity limits, congestion-responsive spot prices, and accumulating ratings.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  1. **Rapid Convergence**: All models converged on the same primary carrier on day one.
  2. **List Length Effect**: Concentration rose steeply once candidate lists exceeded ~10 carriers.
  3. **Ineffective Interventions**: Vendor diversification, list shuffling, popularity displays, and true quality displays failed to significantly reduce concentration or variability.
  4. **Effective Intervention**: Disclosing remaining capacity was the only effective lever, significantly lowering concentration and increasing shipper surplus.
- **Comparison**: Unlike traditional algorithmic recommendations or static rating systems, this study highlights that information transparency (specifically real-time capacity) is more effective than reputation mechanisms in regulating market health in LLM-agent-dominated markets.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper presents the first simulation of freight procurement by populations of real LLM agents, revealing risks of algorithmic monoculture and market concentration. It identifies platform information design, particularly disclosing remaining capacity, as a more effective regulatory lever than model choice or regulation. These findings provide crucial theoretical and practical guidance for designing healthier, more competitive LLM-driven markets.
