## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Design Rule Check (DRC) closure is a major bottleneck in advanced-node physical design. Despite rule-aware detailed routers, residual Design Rule Violations (DRVs) often require manual Engineering Change Order (ECO) iterations. This process is labor-intensive as repairs must handle complex geometric interactions, preserve connectivity, and avoid introducing new violations.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current LLM-based studies focus on rule interpretation or code generation, or are limited to small-scale standard-cell layouts. They struggle to scale to block-level designs with multi-layer rules. Furthermore, LLM agents lack task-specific skill files for new designs, leading to cold-start problems and an inability to leverage past repair experience.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed EvoDRC**: A self-evolving agentic framework for block-level DRC repair. It decomposes layouts into bounded regions, assigns LLM agents to each, and uses local DRC analysis, connectivity checks, and impact preview tools for feedback-driven repair.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of Initial Repair Knowledge (Cold Start)**
  - **Difficulty**: Constructing effective repair strategies from scratch for new designs is difficult due to the absence of specific domain knowledge.
  - **Solution**: EvoDRC initializes layer-specific repair skills by distilling knowledge from an unrelated reference design, providing a foundational capability for the agents.
- **Challenge 2: Inefficient Trial-and-Error and Lack of Adaptability**
  - **Difficulty**: Local edits can cause ripple effects, and static strategies fail to adapt to diverse violation patterns across different designs.
  - **Solution**: The framework continuously collects traceable repair experiences from the target design. These operations and their outcomes are stored in a knowledge database to evolve and optimize repair skills, transforming trial-and-error into experience accumulation.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: EvoDRC decomposes block-level layouts into bounded regions managed by LLM agents. It employs external tools for local DRC analysis, connectivity checking, and impact previewing in a closed loop. Repair histories are stored to drive skill evolution.
- **Evaluation Results**: Experiments on seven block-level designs from the DAC26 DRC Benchmark show that EvoDRC achieves a 73.5% overall reduction in violations compared to the reported baseline.
- **Comparison**: Compared to traditional manual ECO iterations (taking days/weeks) and existing LLM methods limited to small scales or static rules, EvoDRC demonstrates scalability and efficiency in complex block-level designs, building scalable knowledge bases within hours.

## 4. Conclusion
EvoDRC introduces a novel self-evolving agentic framework to address DRC closure challenges in advanced-node physical design. By combining initial skills distilled from reference designs with real-time experience collected from target designs, it effectively overcomes cold-start and adaptability issues in applying LLMs to EDA. Experiments confirm significant DRV reduction and reduced manual ECO effort, offering a new paradigm for automated physical design flows.
