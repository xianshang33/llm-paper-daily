## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems are increasingly adopting multi-agent architectures involving complex processes like query formulation, evidence organization, and multi-step reasoning.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Most prior work optimizes components in isolation (e.g., self-reflection or query rewriting only for the final generator), lacking coordination across the pipeline. This allows errors from early stages (such as incomplete retrieval or poorly structured evidence) to propagate downstream, as evaluation signals from the final generation stage are rarely used to improve upstream components like retrievers or graph constructors.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed GRADRAG**: A framework for cross-component prompt adaptation in agentic RAG systems, modeling the RAG pipeline as a computational graph and propagating structured evaluation feedback to update upstream agents.
- **Challenge 1: Cascading Error Propagation**. Errors in upstream retrieval or evidence structuring persist because components are optimized locally. GRADRAG addresses this by employing an Evaluator to critique downstream answers and supporting evidence, generating actionable feedback that a Prompt Optimizer uses to iteratively update upstream adaptive agents (e.g., retrievers, graph constructors), thereby correcting errors at the source.
- **Challenge 2: Coordination and Efficiency in Multi-Agent Systems**. Determining when to stop iterations and how to coordinate updates across multiple components is difficult. GRADRAG's Evaluator triggers an early stopping mechanism when outputs are deemed satisfactory, ensuring performance gains are realized within few iterations (typically two), thus avoiding the overhead of infinite recursive optimization.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on SQUALITY and QMSUM benchmarks under two retrieval paradigms: (1) Flat chunk-based retrieval with IRCoT-style query refinement; (2) Graph-based retrieval that constructs and iteratively enriches entity-relation graphs.
- **Baselines**: Compared against one-step refinement baselines that update only the final generator.
- **Results**: GRADRAG consistently outperforms baselines in both settings. It achieves a 12-15 percentage point net preference margin in LLM-judged pairwise comparisons, with most gains realized within the first two refinement iterations, demonstrating high efficiency.

## 4. Conclusion
GRADRAG addresses the issue of error propagation in multi-agent RAG systems caused by isolated component optimization. By modeling the pipeline as a computational graph and introducing a cross-component feedback loop, it effectively leverages downstream evaluation signals to optimize upstream retrieval and evidence construction. Experiments show that the method significantly improves performance in document QA and summarization tasks without requiring extensive iterations, offering a new paradigm for coordinated multi-agent RAG.
