## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: In long-horizon Reinforcement Learning (RL) for LLM agents, weak policies often repeat similar failures, generating uninformative rollout trajectories that limit effective policy optimization.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Existing skill-centric methods improve exploration by optimizing, filtering, or internalizing reusable skills. However, they focus on the skills themselves rather than designing them as adaptive, training-time support for the evolving policy. They fail to provide dynamic guidance tailored to the current policy's specific needs, leading to either insufficient support or excessive reliance.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed PATS (Policy-Aware Training Scaffolding)**: A policy-centric training paradigm that reframes skills as dynamic, disposable training scaffolds.
- **Challenge 1: Weak policies get stuck in local optima in long-horizon tasks, producing homogeneous failure trajectories and sparse RL signals.**
  - **Difficulty & Solution**: Balancing sufficient guidance to complete tasks with maintaining behavioral diversity for learning is difficult. PATS converts rollout groups from the latest policy into "evidence cards" and dynamically adjusts the context for subsequent rollouts using task-specific evaluation. This concrete guidance helps weak policies solve challenging tasks while preserving useful trajectory variation.
- **Challenge 2: Avoiding over-reliance on explicit guidance and reducing inference costs as the policy improves.**
  - **Difficulty & Solution**: Fixed skill libraries or prompts lead to redundant context, increasing token usage and potentially stifling autonomy. PATS dynamically revises or removes redundant context as the policy improves, reducing dependence on explicit guidance. The training scaffold is discarded at deployment, ensuring a lightweight and independent final model.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: PATS optimizes the policy using standard RLVR (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards) with environmental rewards. Crucially, the training scaffold is used only during training and is discarded at deployment, incurring no additional inference cost or latency.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - On ALFWorld and WebShop benchmarks, PATS outperforms strong baselines by up to 18.6%.
  - Across seven search-augmented QA benchmarks, PATS remains competitive while using 32.1% fewer prompt tokens than the baseline.
- **Comparison**: Compared to methods like SkillRL and SKILL0, PATS expands context early in training to provide support and contracts it as validation performance improves. In contrast, SkillRL's context grows continuously, and SKILL0 uses staged withdrawal. PATS demonstrates superior performance and token efficiency through its dynamic adjustment mechanism.

#### Summary
PATS introduces a novel policy-centric RL training paradigm that treats skills as dynamic, disposable training scaffolds. By dynamically adjusting training-time context support, it effectively addresses the low exploration efficiency of weak policies in long-horizon LLM agent tasks. This approach helps weak policies overcome bottlenecks and reduces redundancy as they mature, achieving both performance gains and resource efficiency without any deployment overhead.
