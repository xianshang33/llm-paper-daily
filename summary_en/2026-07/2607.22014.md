## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are emerging as core reasoning modules for embodied agents. However, it remains unclear how well frozen, general-purpose MLLMs can solve long-horizon embodied tasks from a single high-level instruction without task-specific adaptation.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current benchmarks (e.g., VLN, Object Navigation, ALFRED) primarily evaluate path-level instruction following or step-level subgoals. They do not directly assess the ability of frozen MLLMs to coordinate perception, planning, control, and reporting for complex "mission-level" tasks. Furthermore, existing aerial benchmarks focus mainly on route following or object search, lacking comprehensive evaluation of spatial reasoning and adaptive decision-making in aerial viewpoints.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed MissionBench**: A benchmark for mission-level evaluation of MLLMs in aerial 3D environments, comprising 120 missions across five simulated 3D environments and four task families (Reporting, Inspection, Manipulation, Patrol).
- **Challenge 1: Multi-step Planning and Adaptive Reasoning in Long-Horizon Tasks**. The difficulty lies in the agent's need to autonomously decide where to go, how to position itself, and what to report using only egocentric observations and action history, without aerial-specific fine-tuning. The paper addresses this by creating a closed-loop evaluation environment that requires iterative decision-making over multiple time steps, quantifying zero-shot capabilities in multi-step embodied tasks.
- **Challenge 2: Spatial Perception and Pose Localization in Aerial Viewpoints**. UAV missions are viewpoint-sensitive, requiring agents to reach informative poses and maintain visual contact, which is challenging due MLLMs' weaknesses in depth estimation and relative positioning. The paper addresses this by designing tasks requiring precise pose reporting and object recognition (e.g., reading license plates), revealing the need for coordinated capabilities beyond simple spatial perception.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: MissionBench is built on simulators like AirSim. Agents operate using only egocentric observations and action history, without fine-tuning for aerial scenarios. The study evaluated 22 open-source and closed-source MLLMs.
- **Evaluation Results**: The strongest model achieved a success rate of less than 35%, compared to 84.4% for humans, highlighting the difficulty of multi-step embodied tasks.
- **Comparison & Analysis**: Despite significant variations between model families, clear gains from scaling were observed, indicating that larger general-purpose models possess stronger zero-shot embodied capabilities. Compared to existing works focusing only on navigation or simple search, MissionBench demonstrates that mission-level competence requires coordinating multi-step planning and adaptive reasoning, not just spatial perception.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper introduces MissionBench, filling the gap in evaluating general-purpose MLLMs for long-horizon, mission-level aerial embodied intelligence. The results show that state-of-the-art MLLMs still fall far short of human performance in these complex tasks, but scaling up model size significantly improves zero-shot performance. This underscores the importance of closed-loop evaluation and highlights both the promise and risks of scaling general models for embodied AI.
