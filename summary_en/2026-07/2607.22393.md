## 1. What the paper does
- **Background**: Vision-language model (VLM) agents are increasingly using tools to act on 3D scenes rather than just describing them. Acting on a scene is a stronger test of an agent's 3D understanding.
- **Why existing work fails**: Existing 3D benchmarks either score textual responses to static images/scans without changing the scene, or evaluate single-object operations/static edits with limited metrics. There is no benchmark that evaluates an agent's ability to perform coordinated actions on complete, multi-object 3D scenes.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SceneActBench**: A benchmark for visually conditioned action across five 3D tasks under a unified agent-environment loop.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of evaluation for coordinated action in complex multi-object scenes**. It is difficult to assess how agents handle interactions across multiple objects simultaneously. SceneActBench addresses this by creating 520 task cases from 210 source instances (furnished rooms, articulated objects, dynamic scenes), requiring agents to act on a 3D environment based on visual inputs to match a reference state.
- **Challenge 2: Absence of unified, fine-grained geometric accuracy metrics**. Traditional methods rely on text matching or binary success/failure, lacking precision. SceneActBench evaluates each final output against hidden ground truth using task-specific geometric metrics within a fixed agent loop to ensure fair comparison.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Tasks**: The benchmark includes five tasks: Layout, Camera, Articulated, Reconstruction, and Dynamic. It utilizes 100 furnished rooms, 100 articulated objects, and 10 multi-object dynamic scenes.
- **Evaluation Results**: Evaluated across eleven proprietary VLM configurations, overall scores range from 38.6 to 50.2. Top performers include Claude Opus 4.6 High (50.2), GPT 5.4 Medium (48.9), and GPT 5.4 High (48.7). The lowest score was from MiniMax M3 High (38.6).
- **Comparison**: No single model performs consistently well across all tasks. Performance varies significantly across different 3D capabilities (e.g., spatial reasoning vs. dynamic reasoning), highlighting significant gaps in current VLMs' general 3D action capabilities.

## 4. Conclusion
SceneActBench fills the gap in evaluating VLM agents' ability to perform visually conditioned actions in complete, multi-object 3D scenes. By introducing a unified evaluation framework based on geometric precision, it reveals the inconsistencies and limitations of current mainstream VLMs in 3D understanding and action, providing a crucial benchmark for future improvements in 3D interactive agents.
