## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Long-horizon LLM agents accumulate facts in external memory stores to use as premises for downstream reasoning. If a hallucinated fact is written at any step, it persists as a false premise for all subsequent steps, a failure mode termed "memory contamination."
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current memory management focuses on retrieval efficiency and capacity, ignoring write-time correctness. Admission criteria based on utility, novelty, or recency are structurally blind to contamination because hallucinated facts can be just as relevant and recent as correct ones, leading to compounding errors that existing mechanisms cannot resolve.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ConsistencyGate**: A model-agnostic, training-free write-time admission gate that uses self-consistency scoring to decide whether to commit a candidate fact to memory.
- **Challenge 1: How to accurately assess the factual correctness of candidate facts without additional training data or fine-tuning?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional methods struggle to distinguish semantically relevant but factually incorrect content, lacking specialized discriminators for write-time correctness.
  - **Solution**: ConsistencyGate queries the LLM K times before writing to obtain a soft support score. A fact is admitted only if the average score exceeds a threshold, leveraging the model's own self-consistency for verification.
- **Challenge 2: How to achieve efficient real-time verification while maintaining low latency?**
  - **Difficulty**: Multiple sampling rounds for verification significantly increase inference latency, making it unsuitable for latency-sensitive applications.
  - **Solution**: The authors propose a log-probability variant that reduces the verification process to a single forward pass, significantly lowering computational costs while maintaining effectiveness, thus enabling deployment in latency-sensitive scenarios.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Benchmark Construction**: To evaluate performance on natural data, two real-conversation benchmarks (LoCoMo-Contam and MSC-Contam) were constructed by planting controlled single-detail corruptions in long conversations. A structured synthetic corpus (MemContam) was also created to isolate near-oracle upper-bound performance.
- **Experimental Results**: Tested across four different LLM backbones, ConsistencyGate significantly reduced memory contamination rates on all benchmarks compared to the "Write-everything" baseline.
- **Comparison**: Unlike traditional utility- or recency-based admission policies, ConsistencyGate effectively intercepts hallucinated facts. While the verification cost is concentrated on facts implicitly stated in the source context, the method significantly improves downstream Question Answering accuracy (QA F1), demonstrating its effectiveness in preventing error propagation.

#### Summary
This paper addresses the under-explored issue of "memory contamination" in LLM agents, where write-time hallucinations pollute subsequent reasoning. The authors propose ConsistencyGate, a self-consistency-based write-time admission gate that verifies factual support rather than utility. The method requires no fine-tuning and offers a low-latency variant. Experiments show that it effectively reduces memory contamination and improves downstream task performance across multiple benchmarks, filling the gap in existing memory management regarding write-time correctness control.
