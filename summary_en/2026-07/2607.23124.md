## 1. What the Paper Did

**Background**
Large language model (LLM) agents have advanced rapidly, but progress remains fragmented across domains, capabilities, task difficulties, and interaction settings. Existing research often focuses on specific scenarios or single capabilities, lacking a unified scaling framework that covers To-Consumer (ToC), To-Business (ToB), and To-Employee (ToE) applications.

**Why Existing Work Falls Short**
Current methods struggle with "full-scenario agentic scaling" due to the lack of a unified taxonomy to align data synthesis, post-training, and evaluation. This misalignment limits generalization in complex, multi-step, tool-dependent tasks. Furthermore, traditional approaches lack fine-grained diagnostic signals, making it difficult to perform targeted optimizations for specific failure modes.

## Core Contributions

**Proposed AgentOmnia Framework**
A comprehensive framework coordinating task-space definition, data synthesis, post-training, evaluation, and iterative improvement for full-scenario agent scaling.

**Challenge 1: Construction of Full-Scenario Task Space and Data Synthesis**
*   **Challenge**: Building massive, high-quality, stateful training data covering broad domains and capabilities is prohibitively expensive manually and hard to ensure diversity and correctness.
*   **Solution**: AgentOmnia combines bidirectional environment-task synthesis with tool-dependency, program-structured, and solver-based pipelines. It constructs 5,018 stateful environments, 255,375 tools, and 52,361 tasks, using programs and verifiers to provide correct supervision signals.

**Challenge 2: Post-Training and Targeted Evolution for Complex Tasks**
*   **Challenge**: Agents often fail in long-horizon planning and tool use. Traditional fine-tuning struggles to correct complex logical errors, and there is a lack of mechanisms to automatically learn from evaluation failures.
*   **Solution**: The framework employs Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), online agentic reinforcement learning, and a rollback curriculum. Crucially, it translates evaluation failures into Product Requirement Documents (PRDs) to guide targeted self-evolution, significantly improving pass rates on challenging subsets.

## Implementation and Evaluation

**Implementation Details**
*   **Base Model**: Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking-2507.
*   **Benchmarks**: Released OmniaBench and evaluated under a unified protocol alongside τ²-Bench, DeepPlanning, and VitaBench.
*   **Data Scale**: Constructed a large-scale dataset of code-driven stateful environments and tasks.

**Evaluation Results and Comparisons**
*   **Significant Improvement**: Pass rate on the OmniaBench challenging subset increased from 9.16% to 37.11%. The macro-average score across four benchmarks rose from 22.86% to 41.69%.
*   **Outperforming Larger Models**: AgentOmnia-30B surpassed Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking-2507 on all four benchmarks and exceeded Qwen3.5-35B-A3B in macro-average score.
*   **Leading Baselines**: Under a unified evaluation protocol, AgentOmnia led other agentic post-trained baselines on OmniaBench and maintained the highest macro-average across the four benchmarks.
*   **Broad Gains**: Improvements spanned ToC/ToB/ToE scenarios, 10 capability dimensions, 8 atomic difficulty factors, and 76 out of 90 level-1 domains, demonstrating general rather than category-specific improvements.

## 4. Conclusion

AgentOmnia addresses the fragmentation in agent development by establishing an extensible "Domain × Capability × Atomic Difficulty" taxonomy. Its core innovations lie in the automated synthesis of large-scale stateful environments and a PRD-guided self-evolution mechanism. Experiments demonstrate that a 30B parameter model enhanced by AgentOmnia achieves performance surpassing much larger models across full-scenario benchmarks, providing an effective framework and empirical evidence for the scalable deployment and continuous evolution of industrial-grade agents.
