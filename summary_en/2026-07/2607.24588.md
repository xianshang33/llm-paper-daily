## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Early warning of extreme weather is crucial for mitigating societal, economic, and environmental risks. However, traditional expert-centered warning workflows are costly, labor-intensive, and difficult to scale across the entire warning-to-action process.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: While Large Language Model (LLM) agents have advanced the automation of weather-related tasks, existing studies focus on isolated scientific tasks. They overlook the chain of interdependent processes required for operational, end-to-end extreme-weather early warning.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SIREN**, an experience-grounded LLM agent framework for end-to-end extreme-weather early warning.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of comprehensive benchmarks for end-to-end warning capabilities.** Existing benchmarks often focus on single tasks, failing to capture the interdependencies in complex warning chains. The paper constructs SIREN-Bench, a comprehensive benchmark with 600 QA instances across 19 tasks, covering four individual warning procedures and an end-to-end chain, thereby revealing capability gaps in existing frameworks and providing a standardized evaluation tool.
- **Challenge 2: Existing agents lack the ability to leverage historical experience for handling complex heterogeneous data.** Extreme weather warning requires integrating multi-source heterogeneous evidence and mimicking expert decision-making based on historical cases. SIREN addresses this by combining an agentic execution environment that integrates heterogeneous weather evidence and tools with a family of agent harnesses that exploit historical cases through retrieval, skill distillation, and predictive modeling.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The SIREN framework features an agentic execution environment for integrating heterogeneous weather evidence and tools, along with agent harnesses that utilize historical cases via retrieval augmentation, skill distillation, and predictive modeling.
- **Evaluation Results**: Extensive experiments on SIREN-Bench reveal significant capability gaps in existing weather agent frameworks.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: SIREN outperforms existing weather-agent baselines on both individual warning procedures and end-to-end warning chains, demonstrating its superiority in handling complex, interdependent warning processes.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the high costs of traditional methods and the fragmentation of existing LLM agent research in extreme weather early warning by proposing the SIREN framework. By constructing the comprehensive SIREN-Bench, the authors highlight the limitations of current methods and develop an experience-grounded agent system. By integrating heterogeneous data and simulating expert experience utilization mechanisms, SIREN achieves superior performance in end-to-end warning tasks, offering a new solution for automated and scalable meteorological warning systems.
