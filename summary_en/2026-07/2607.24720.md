## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Multi-turn long-horizon planning is a critical capability for foundation model agents, yet it remains unclear how to fundamentally improve it. Existing models are trained on uncontrollable and opaque Internet data, making it difficult to identify how planning abilities are acquired, shaped, and integrated.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Due to the lack of precise control over data quality, task length, and planning patterns, existing research cannot systematically decouple the specific impacts of pre-training and post-training stages on long-horizon planning capabilities, nor quantify the deep impact of suboptimal trajectories or different teacher models.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a unified and controlled multi-turn environment and systematic research framework**: This framework allows precise control over task length, data quality, planning knowledge, and planning patterns, enabling a systematic study of long-horizon planning evolution across pre-training, single-teacher post-training, and multi-teacher post-training stages.
- **Challenge 1: Unclear mechanisms of planning ability acquisition during pre-training**. The difficulty lies in distinguishing the sources of atomic skills versus long-horizon compositional generalization and quantifying the impact of low-quality data. The paper finds that atomic skills alone are insufficient for compositional generalization, while a small amount of long-horizon data significantly improves capability. Explicit world model construction via Chain-of-Thought (CoT) state transition modeling yields stronger long-horizon generalization than direct action prediction, whereas suboptimal trajectories severely impair performance due to error accumulation.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of strategies for shaping and integrating planning abilities during post-training**. The challenge involves distinguishing general planning patterns from task-specific knowledge and resolving conflicts from multiple knowledge sources. Using mutual information analysis, the paper shows that On-Policy Distillation (OPD) has a broader effective region than GRPO under low-quality data and long horizons, as it provides more consistent update directions. In Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation (MOPD), compatible patterns enable cross-environment generalization, partially shared patterns support continual learning, while completely conflicting patterns cause severe catastrophic forgetting and interference.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The study utilizes a custom unified controlled multi-turn environment for experiments, covering analyses from pre-training data distributions to comparisons of GRPO, OPD, and MOPD post-training algorithms. Code, models, and datasets are open-sourced.
- **Evaluation Results and Comparisons**:
    - **Pre-training Stage**: Experiments demonstrate that explicit world model construction (via CoT state transition modeling) outperforms direct action prediction in long-horizon generalization. A small amount of high-quality long-horizon data is more effective than large amounts of atomic skill data.
    - **Post-training Stage (Single-Teacher)**: Comparing GRPO and OPD, results show that OPD outperforms GRPO when pre-training data quality is low and the planning horizon is long, because OPD with an ideal teacher provides more consistent update directions than GRPO's sparse credit assignment. However, distilling unseen multi-path procedures from a teacher with different underlying knowledge can impair the student's existing world model.
    - **Post-training Stage (Multi-Teacher)**: MOPD integrates capabilities by converging to shared planning pattern distributions among teachers. Experiments verify that compatible patterns enable cross-environment generalization, while conflicting patterns lead to significant performance degradation and interference, revealing the physical mechanisms of multi-source knowledge integration.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper systematically reveals the "physics" of long-horizon planning capabilities in foundation model agents, detailing how abilities are acquired during pre-training, shaped during post-training, and integrated across stages. The study highlights the importance of explicit world model construction, identifies the advantages of OPD in long-horizon low-quality data settings, and clarifies the critical role of pattern compatibility in multi-teacher distillation for cross-environment generalization and continual learning, providing theoretical insights and practical guidance for developing stronger agentic foundation models.
