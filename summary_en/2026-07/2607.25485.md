## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Health AI is evolving from simple question-answering systems to agentic systems that converse with patients, reason about health records, and act on their behalf (e.g., scheduling, prescription management). These systems require evaluation against risks like diagnostic errors and unsafe care.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current benchmarks primarily assess medical knowledge through isolated question-answering or clinician-facing tasks. They fail to evaluate real-world scenarios involving sustained conversations and tool usage, missing specific failure modes of autonomous agents (e.g., unverified tool outputs), making static benchmarks insufficient for modern healthcare agents.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed PatientAgentBench**: A reproducible, clinician-validated benchmark framework for evaluating patient-facing health AI agents.
- **Challenge 1: How to comprehensively evaluate multi-dimensional agent performance in complex interactions?**
  - **Difficulty**: Agent behavior involves dialogue, tool use, and clinical reasoning, which single metrics cannot fully capture.
  - **Solution**: Created a sandbox environment with 1,200 scenarios and used an "LLM-as-a-Jury" mechanism to score conversations across six dimensions using over 100 conversation-agnostic, clinician-grounded criteria.
- **Challenge 2: How to ensure alignment between automated evaluation and human expert judgment?**
  - **Difficulty**: Automated scoring may deviate from clinical safety standards, lacking credibility.
  - **Solution**: Validated by licensed clinicians annotating shared conversations, achieving 79-93% adjacent agreement between the jury and experts, matching or exceeding inter-rater agreement among clinicians.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The framework includes a sandbox where foundation models are wrapped as agents with healthcare tools, conversing with simulated patients. The code is open-sourced.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - Benchmarked 10 models across four families.
  - **Triage Quality** was the most discriminating dimension: pass rates ranged from 32% for the weakest models to 88% for the strongest. Agents often acted on administrative requests without clinical screening.
  - **Clinical Safety and Workflow Accuracy**: Weakest models frequently failed and fabricated actions; frontier models failed in only 1-3% of cases, mainly due to unverified tool outputs or omitted crisis resources in emergencies.
  - Even the strongest models scored only 4.25/5 overall, indicating gaps remain despite improved capabilities.
- **Comparison**: Unlike static benchmarks that only test medical knowledge, PatientAgentBench reveals failure modes that only emerge in sustained, tool-using conversations with realistic patient records, demonstrating the insufficiency of existing static benchmarks.

## 4. Conclusion
PatientAgentBench fills the gap in evaluating patient-facing health AI agents. By simulating real interactions and using clinically validated automated evaluation, it exposes specific defects in current models regarding triage, safety, and workflow accuracy. The framework provides a crucial standard for developing safer and more reliable health AI agents, highlighting the necessity of dynamic, multi-turn interaction evaluation in healthcare AI safety.
