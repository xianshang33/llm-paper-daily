## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Actuarial risk modeling in the insurance industry is a high-stakes decision-support setting requiring high accuracy, fairness, and compliance. Large Language Model (LLM)-based Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) offer potential for automating complex workflows like data preparation and modeling, but LLMs' probabilistic nature introduces reliability issues.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current approaches lack transparency ("black box") and fail to adequately address how errors and uncertainty arise and propagate in multi-step agent workflows. Errors from one agent can cascade, leading to undetectable biased outcomes, which is unacceptable in regulated actuarial contexts.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A runtime uncertainty monitoring framework for LLM-based MAS using Bayesian Networks.
- **Challenge 1: How to quantify raw LLM output uncertainty meaningfully?**
  - **Difficulty**: Token-level log-probabilities cannot be directly treated as probabilities of correctness and are biased by output length.
  - **Solution**: The paper transforms length-normalized log-probability summaries into calibrated task-level confidence estimates, rather than treating them as direct success probabilities, yielding more reliable local uncertainty metrics.
- **Challenge 2: How to model and track uncertainty propagation across agents?**
  - **Difficulty**: Agents are interdependent; uncertainty in early steps affects later ones. Independent evaluation misses these cascading effects.
  - **Solution**: A Bayesian Network is introduced to capture dependencies between workflow stages, incorporating calibrated confidence estimates to enable end-to-end runtime uncertainty propagation monitoring.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The framework features a central hub with specialized agents (data prep, modeling, review, explanation). It leverages token-level log-probabilities, normalizes and calibrates them, and inputs them into a Bayesian Network.
- **Evaluation Results**: Results show the framework reproduces baseline actuarial performance while providing additional insights into workflow stability and runtime uncertainty propagation.
- **Comparison**: Compared to traditional LLM MAS lacking uncertainty quantification, this approach offers a structured representation of uncertainty propagation, enhancing interpretability and safety monitoring in high-stakes workflows.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the transparency and safety challenges of LLM-based MAS in high-stakes actuarial modeling. By combining calibrated token-level log-probabilities with Bayesian Networks to model dependency-driven uncertainty propagation, the proposed framework provides robust runtime monitoring, ensuring greater reliability and explainability for critical decision-support systems.
