## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM)-based Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) are shifting from traditional text-based communication to "Latent Communication," which transmits continuous internal representations (e.g., embeddings, hidden states, KV caches) to avoid information loss from discretization and reduce overhead.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Prior studies primarily rely on end-task performance improvements to validate latent communication. However, such gains may stem from increased computation, context reuse, or redundant reasoning rather than genuine information transfer. End-to-end accuracy alone cannot distinguish whether the receiver actually utilizes sender-specific, task-relevant information, nor can it disentangle the contributions of message presence, example-specific content, and independent agent value.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Causal Audit Framework**: This method applies controlled message replacements at the boundary where sender-generated representations enter the receiver, decomposing and quantifying different causal effects in latent communication.
- **Challenge 1: Distinguishing genuine information transfer from confounding factors (e.g., increased computation)**. This is difficult because traditional black-box evaluations cannot decouple message content from internal model computations. The paper addresses this by introducing four message settings (including replacing with other-example messages or empty messages) to isolate "message presence," "example-specific content value," and "independent agent value," thereby precisely quantifying the causal contribution of each component.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of fine-grained metrics for receiver sensitivity and information encoding**. Existing methods fail to determine if the receiver truly "understands" the sender's intent. The paper designs five specific measurements, including encoded sender information and receiver sensitivity to message presence/identity. These reveal complex interaction mechanisms masked by aggregate accuracy, such as reversed contribution directions between small and large models.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Experiments were conducted on Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B models using datasets GSM8K, ARC-C, and MATH-500. The study employed a latent relay architecture and used controlled variable substitution in the latent space for causal analysis.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - On GSM8K, the overall performance effect for Qwen3-4B was -1.00 percentage points, decomposed into a -6.17 point effect retained by other-example messages and a +5.17 point effect attributable to example-specific content; these directions reversed for the 8B model.
    - On MATH-500, the 15.00 point gain for Qwen3-4B comprised 8.33 points from other-example message retention and 6.67 points from example-specific content, while the 8B gain was dominated by the former.
    - Self-substitution comparisons further demonstrated that example-specific content and other-agent value are distinct.
- **Comparison**: Unlike prior work that only reports overall accuracy gains, this paper reveals the risk of "spurious correlations" in latent communication. It demonstrates that aggregate accuracy is insufficient to identify how latent messages affect receivers, advocating for controlled message comparisons as a standard evaluation protocol.

#### Summary
The paper argues that while latent communication offers higher representational capacity, it does not guarantee that receivers utilize task-relevant information. By introducing a causal audit framework, the authors deconstruct multiple effects within latent communication, finding significant differences and even reversals in how different model sizes depend on message components across tasks. The study concludes that end-task accuracy alone cannot reveal the true mechanisms of latent communication and calls for the adoption of controlled message comparisons as a standard evaluation paradigm to ensure genuine and effective information transfer in multi-agent systems.
