## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents are increasingly adopted in real-world security operations with access to host artifacts and command-line interfaces (CLIs), making it critical to assess their security capabilities thoroughly.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current cybersecurity benchmarks focus on "pre-compromise" settings, where agents operate in clean, idealized environments to find or exploit vulnerabilities before an attack. Even defensive benchmarks often simplify tasks to analyzing isolated alerts or logs, ignoring real post-attack artifacts like persistence mechanisms and erased traces. This leaves the "post-compromise" incident response workflow (investigation, attack chain reconstruction, and remediation) underexplored.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SecRespond**: The first benchmark for evaluating LLM agents on the post-compromise incident-response workflow.
- **Challenge 1: Proactive Forensics and Silent Intrusion Detection in Complex Environments**. The difficulty lies in the agent's need to proactively inspect disk snapshots for hidden intrusion traces rather than relying solely on explicit alerts. SecRespond addresses this by providing forensic disk snapshots of compromised hosts and requiring agents to generate detailed reports on intrusions, baseline risks, and vulnerability risks, thereby testing their proactive investigation capabilities.
- **Challenge 2: Generating Comprehensive and Verifiable Remediation Plans**. The challenge is not just identifying issues but formulating a thorough plan to eradicate threats and restore system security. SecRespond instantiates this task across 10 cyber ranges (covering 4 entry-point types, 21 ATT&CK techniques, and 5 OSs), requiring agents to produce concrete remediation plans based on their findings, strictly evaluating their end-to-end capability from detection to repair.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: SecRespond constructs 10 cyber ranges, each derived from a distinct compromised cloud host. The evaluation uses the OpenCode agent harness to test 23 frontier LLMs.
- **Evaluation Results**: Results show that while current agents can reliably uncover problems exposed by alerts, they struggle to proactively investigate disks for silent intrusions and produce comprehensive, verified remediation plans. No model achieved complete detection and remediation on any single range.
- **Comparison**: Unlike existing benchmarks focusing on CTF challenges, vulnerability mining, or isolated alert analysis (e.g., CyberModelArena), SecRespond reveals a fundamental bottleneck in current agents' ability to handle real-world post-compromise incident response workflows, highlighting their limitations when facing authentic attack remnants.

## 4. Conclusion
SecRespond fills the gap in evaluating LLM agents for post-compromise incident response. By introducing a benchmark based on real compromised host disk snapshots, the study exposes significant deficiencies in state-of-the-art LLM agents regarding proactive forensics and comprehensive remediation planning. This indicates that substantial technical hurdles remain in building agents capable of handling real-world incident response, particularly in dealing with stealthy threats and complex system state recovery.
