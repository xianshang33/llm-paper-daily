## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large language model (LLM) agents are increasingly expected to assist users in completing complex tasks, particularly within office-suite workflows. However, existing benchmarks offer limited support for evaluating whether agents can execute these long-horizon tasks at a reasonable cost.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current benchmarks lack economic grounding, making it difficult to directly compare human labor costs with LLM inference costs or perform value-weighted evaluations. This limits the ability to assess the cost-effectiveness and delivery quality of agents in real-world scenarios.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed OmegaUse-OfficeVal**: A benchmark comprising 100 long-horizon office-suite tasks derived from real practitioner requests, with an average human labor time of 2.32 hours per task, paired with economic signals.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of Economic Evaluation Metrics**. The difficulty lies in traditional benchmarks focusing only on task completion rates while ignoring time and monetary costs, failing to reflect real-world business value. The paper addresses this by pairing each task with "human labor time" and "task price proxy" signals, enabling direct comparison between human and LLM costs and value-weighted evaluation.
- **Challenge 2: Difficulty in Stable Automated Evaluation of Long-Horizon Complex Tasks**. Office tasks (e.g., DOCX, PPTX, XLSX) have diverse and subjective outcomes, rendering traditional string matching ineffective. The paper develops code-based verifiers from fine-grained rubrics, generated and revised by experts, to resolve discrepancies between human and code evaluations, ensuring stable and automated quality scoring.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The benchmark includes 100 tasks and 220 input files covering DOCX, PPTX, XLSX, and PDF formats. All code and datasets are fully open-sourced. The evaluation pipeline involves generating verification code from fine-grained rubrics and resolving human-code evaluation discrepancies.
- **Evaluation Results**: Several frontier LLMs were evaluated against a human baseline. Results show that while all evaluated LLMs are substantially cheaper and faster than human workers, they have not yet reached human-level deliverable quality.
- **Comparison**: Unlike existing productivity or office automation benchmarks, OmegaUse-OfficeVal introduces economic grounding, providing a trade-off analysis of quality, cost, and time, rather than relying solely on accuracy metrics.

## 4. Conclusion
OmegaUse-OfficeVal fills the gap in evaluating the economic efficiency of LLM agents in long-horizon office tasks. By introducing human labor time and task price proxies, the benchmark quantifies economic efficiency alongside task completion quality. Although current LLMs offer advantages in cost and speed, their delivery quality in complex office tasks needs improvement to match human experts. This work provides essential evaluation standards and data support for developing more economically viable agents in the future.
