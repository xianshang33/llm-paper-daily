## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: As Large Language Models (LLMs) are deployed in high-stakes domains like healthcare and law, understanding their deceptive capabilities is critical for safety. Existing benchmarks (e.g., MMLU, GSM8K) are saturated and lack robust evaluation of adversarial behaviors such as persuasion and deception.
- **Limitations of Prior Work**: Traditional game-theoretic settings (e.g., Prisoner's Dilemma) fail to capture the complexity of social interactions involving secret-keeping and strategic deception. Existing social deduction games (e.g., Werewolf, Avalon) either lack plausible deniability or mandate bluffing, failing to reflect real-world scenarios where deception is optional and reward-driven.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ParliamentBench**: An open-source benchmark framework based on the game *Secret Hitler* to evaluate LLMs on deception, persuasion, and reasoning under information asymmetry.
- **Challenge 1: How to quantify complex social reasoning and deception?**
  - **Difficulty**: Social deception involves multi-turn interactions, hidden information, and dynamic strategies, making it hard for traditional metrics to isolate specific reasoning abilities from deceptive consistency.
  - **Solution**: Introduced three novel metrics that isolate social deduction, logical reasoning, and deceptive consistency, allowing for a more granular assessment of model capabilities.
- **Challenge 2: How to conduct large-scale, realistic adversarial evaluations in a controlled environment?**
  - **Difficulty**: LLM-to-LLM or LLM-to-human experiments are costly and hard to standardize, lacking baselines from real human player data.
  - **Solution**: Conducted approximately 1,600 simulated matches (LLM vs. LLM and LLM vs. Human) and compared results against 25,000 online games played by humans, providing a reproducible sandbox.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated 16 LLMs in *Secret Hitler*, a game featuring a liberal majority and a secret fascist minority, involving nomination, voting, and policy enactment phases.
- **Key Results**:
  - **Top-Tier Cluster**: GPT-5.4, Kimi K2.5, Grok 4.1 Fast, and DeepSeek 3.1 Terminus demonstrated strong performance in both cooperative and deceptive roles.
  - **Underperforming Models**: The weakest models fell below random (33%) and simple algorithmic (45%) baselines.
  - **Deception Consistency Issue**: Most LLMs struggled to maintain a consistent deceptive persona throughout the game, with deception retention dropping below 50%.
- **Comparison**: By comparing with extensive human gameplay data, the study reveals that while frontier models approach or exceed average human performance in specific social reasoning tasks, they still lag behind skilled humans in maintaining long-term deceptive strategies.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper fills the gap in evaluating LLMs' complex social deception and reasoning capabilities through ParliamentBench. The findings indicate that while frontier models excel in short-term tactics and immediate reasoning, they exhibit significant deficiencies in maintaining long-term consistent deceptive strategies. This provides essential tools and insights for future improvements in LLM safety and robustness in high-stakes environments.
