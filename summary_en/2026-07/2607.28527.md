## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large language model (LLM)-based multi-agent systems enhance complex problem-solving through task decomposition, specialization, and information exchange. Current self-improvement methods mainly focus on adapting outputs, prompts, or memory for individual agents.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Existing multi-agent frameworks typically treat communication topology as a fixed design choice or an offline optimization target. They lack the ability to dynamically adjust the collaboration architecture during inference based on specific task instances, limiting adaptability to complex and varying requirements.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed MANTA**: A framework for Multi-Agent Network Topology Adaptation that enables communication structures to self-evolve at inference time.
- **Challenge 1: How to dynamically generate initial topology at inference?**
  - **Difficulty**: Different tasks require different collaboration patterns; designing from scratch is inefficient, while fixed templates lack flexibility.
  - **Solution**: MANTA initializes a task-conditioned topology before execution by leveraging prior structural experience, enabling cross-task knowledge transfer.
- **Challenge 2: How to monitor and optimize insufficient collaboration structures during execution?**
  - **Difficulty**: Requires real-time identification of collaboration bottlenecks and structural adjustments without interrupting the task, updating model weights, or exceeding budget constraints.
  - **Solution**: During deployment, it monitors collaboration traces and applies bounded structural updates when the current organization is insufficient. Updates can modify agent roles, links, execution order, visibility, and validation paths while preserving the task interface and agent budget.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Evaluation Setup**: MANTA was evaluated against representative single-agent and multi-agent baselines on five benchmarks spanning information seeking, tool use, planning, workflow execution, and mathematical reasoning.
- **Results**: MANTA achieved the highest average score of 74.0, outperforming the strongest baseline by 5.8 percentage points.
- **Comparison Advantage**: It obtained the best result on the PlanCraft benchmark. The results demonstrate that inference-time self-improvement can extend to the architecture of collaboration, showing superiority over fixed topology or offline optimization methods.

#### Summary
MANTA breaks the limitation of fixed topology in traditional multi-agent systems by achieving self-evolution of communication structures during inference. By combining task-conditioned initialization with online structural updates based on collaboration traces, MANTA dynamically adjusts agent interactions, significantly improving performance on complex tasks. This work proves the feasibility and effectiveness of extending self-improvement mechanisms from content to architecture.
