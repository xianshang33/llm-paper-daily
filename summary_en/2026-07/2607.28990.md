## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Data-driven scientific discovery is a hypothesis-driven empirical research process requiring agents to understand context, select statistical methods, execute analyses, and draw verifiable conclusions. Existing LLMs and agents are unreliable in this setting, struggling with statistical method applicability and causal reasoning.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current scientific discovery benchmarks provide evaluation tasks and ground-truth criteria but lack hidden scientific evidence states for large-scale process-supervised training. General agent environments focus on web interaction or ML engineering, not real-data hypothesis verification. Furthermore, prompting and generic multi-turn training fail to produce goal-directed interactions, while traditional RL struggles with long-horizon credit assignment due to the lack of fine-grained rewards for intermediate steps.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SciDisco Framework**: A scalable framework for training Scientific Discovery agents in process-verifiable environments.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of executable and verifiable environments for process supervision**. The difficulty lies in existing datasets being static files without task goals or verifiers, preventing progress checks during interaction. SciDisco addresses this via SciThèque, which compiles hypotheses, datasets, hidden evidence graphs, and verifiers into task environments where analytical progress can be checked in real-time.
- **Challenge 2: Credit assignment in long-horizon tasks and lack of behavioral priors**. The challenge is that scientific discovery involves long sequences where outcome-only rewards lead to invalid early explorations and make it hard to attribute credit to specific steps. SciDisco solves this by using DAG-grounded trajectory synthesis to create verifier-filtered demonstrations and employing DiscoPO to assign turn-level credit to actions that produce verifiable analytical evidence, guiding robust policy learning.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: SciDisco consists of three components: SciThèque (environment construction), DAG-grounded trajectory synthesis (high-quality demonstration generation), and DiscoPO (turn-level credit assignment RL optimization).
- **Evaluation Results**: Experiments show that SciDisco-14B achieves state-of-the-art performance on hypothesis-driven scientific data analysis benchmarks.
- **Comparison**: Compared to prior agentic code frameworks and reasoning scaffolds (e.g., Majumder et al., 2025; Gu et al., 2024), SciDisco significantly improves reliability and performance in complex scientific data analysis by introducing process supervision and turn-level credit assignment, overcoming limitations in handling statistical applicability and causal reasoning.

#### Summary
This paper addresses the challenges of long-horizon analysis and lack of process supervision in data-driven scientific discovery by proposing the SciDisco framework. By constructing the SciThèque environment with hidden evidence and verifiers, and combining DAG trajectory synthesis with DiscoPO's turn-level credit assignment, the authors successfully train agents capable of robust interaction. SciDisco-14B achieves SOTA results, demonstrating the effectiveness of process-verifiable environments and fine-grained RL signals in enhancing LLM scientific reasoning capabilities.
