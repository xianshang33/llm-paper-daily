## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Multi-agent systems (MAS) are increasingly deployed for complex tasks. However, when outputs are incorrect, users must manually inspect agent trajectories for failure attribution and provide feedback for repair. While automated failure attribution has seen recent progress, automated mechanisms for MAS repair remain largely unexplored.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: The only existing work, DoVer, uses a sequential exploration strategy that limits the search space under high token costs. Furthermore, it focuses on repair at the orchestrator level, limiting its applicability to agentic systems without a centralized orchestrator.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed MARS Framework**: A search-based framework that formulates MAS repair as a Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) process, navigating the vast space of potential repairs via diagnosis-guided expansion and taxonomy-augmented evaluation.
- **Challenge 1: How to efficiently search the vast repair space while reducing token consumption?**
  - **Difficulty**: Standard MCTS evaluates complete simulations via full rollouts, leading to prohibitive computational and token costs.
  - **Solution**: MARS employs "partial rollout" (re-executing the system for only k steps) combined with diagnosis-guided expansion (Rollback, Guided Repair, Continuation). This significantly reduces token consumption while allowing fine-grained control over trajectory exploration.
- **Challenge 2: How to evaluate the quality of partial repairs without complete trajectories?**
  - **Difficulty**: Partial rollouts do not yield final task outcomes, rendering traditional outcome-based evaluation ineffective.
  - **Solution**: Introduces a novel "taxonomy-augmented evaluation" mechanism that characterizes common failure patterns and generates dynamic reward signals for MCTS, enabling informed search decisions even before a complete trajectory is available.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Benchmark**: Introduced StateMAS, a large-scale MAS repair benchmark with 1,310 replayable multi-agent failure trajectories across four agent architectures and four LLM backbones.
- **Results**: Experiments on StateMAS demonstrate that MARS consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods, achieving an absolute improvement of 3.0% to 12.1% across all settings.
- **Efficiency**: MARS maintains comparable token consumption costs to existing methods while delivering superior performance.
- **Ablation Study**: Confirms that both taxonomy-augmented evaluation and diagnosis-guided expansion are critical to the observed performance gains.

#### Summary
This paper addresses the gap in automated MAS repair by proposing MARS, which models repair as an MCTS process. By leveraging partial rollouts and taxonomy-augmented evaluation, MARS effectively tackles the challenges of search efficiency and intermediate state assessment. The introduction of the StateMAS benchmark further supports future research. Experimental results show that MARS significantly outperforms current SOTA methods in both repair success rate and cost efficiency.
