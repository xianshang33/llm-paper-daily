## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: As LLMs evolve into autonomous scientific agents, evaluating their ability to conduct experiments is crucial. A typical ML workflow involves executing baselines, inspecting metrics/logs, and adjusting parameters iteratively.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current benchmarks focus on static code generation, paper replication, or final answer correctness, failing to assess whether agents can interpret experimental evidence to guide subsequent hyperparameter decisions. Traditional HPO benchmarks abstract away logs and procedural context, leaving the capability to convert empirical feedback into effective configuration changes insufficiently examined.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed AgentHPOBench**: A sequential benchmark comprising 30 executable machine learning tasks across seven research categories, designed to evaluate LLM agents as sequential hyperparameter optimizers in executable research repositories.
- **Challenge 1: How to evaluate sequential decision-making based on empirical feedback?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional benchmarks lack the requirement to interpret experiment logs and intermediate states, making it hard to measure if an agent truly "understands" results versus blind searching.
  - **Solution**: AgentHPOBench implements a sequential intervention mechanism where agents observe accumulated configurations, metrics, and logs at each step before proposing the next valid configuration, forcing the use of historical evidence.
- **Challenge 2: How to fairly compare diverse agents and traditional optimizers under a unified protocol?**
  - **Difficulty**: Existing works have fragmented evaluation dimensions (e.g., code modification, architecture design), lacking a standardized environment for pure hyperparameter tuning capabilities.
  - **Solution**: Established a unified evaluation protocol comparing 12 widely used LLM agents against conventional HPO baselines on the same 30 executable tasks, clearly delineating the performance boundaries between LLM-based policy optimization and traditional algorithmic optimization.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The benchmark includes 30 executable tasks from recent ML research repositories. Each task starts with a validated baseline run, and agents interact via APIs/interfaces to receive observations (configs, metrics, logs) and output new hyperparameters.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - Evaluated 12 mainstream LLM agents and traditional HPO baselines.
  - Results show that current agents exhibit measurable experimental optimization abilities across domains.
  - However, clear limitations remain in sustained iterative refinement, complex log diagnosis, and consistent progress toward reference performance.
- **Comparison**: Compared to traditional HPO benchmarks (e.g., HPO-B, LCBench), AgentHPOBench is closer to real-world research workflows by emphasizing log interpretation and sequential decision-making. Compared to general scientific benchmarks (e.g., MLGym, PaperBench), it focuses specifically on the core环节 of hyperparameter optimization, providing finer-grained capability assessment.

## 4. Conclusion
AgentHPOBench fills the gap in evaluating LLM agents' ability to perform sequential hyperparameter optimization in real research environments. By introducing executable tasks with log and intermediate state observations, it reveals that while current agents have preliminary optimization capabilities, they significantly lag in deep reasoning and sustained iteration, providing a vital tool and direction for developing more powerful autonomous scientific agents.
