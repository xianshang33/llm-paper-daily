## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: LLM agents are increasingly deployed in scientific research, a domain critical for reliability and characterized by densely interconnected knowledge. In this context, hallucinations are particularly damaging as a single error in a foundational concept can propagate through multi-step reasoning, corrupting entire trajectories.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current hallucination benchmarks operate at a surface level, treating facts in isolation and relying on uniform accuracy metrics that ignore topological structures. They fail to explain why hallucinations arise or trace them to specific reasoning steps, and they poorly transfer to scientific domains where error propagation is a key failure mode.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SCHEMA**: The first evidence-grounded, topology-aware evaluation framework for hallucinations in scientific agents.
- **Challenge 1: How to quantify and trace cascading hallucinations?**
  - **Difficulty**: In scientific reasoning, small early factual errors amplify along knowledge graph paths; traditional point-wise accuracy fails to capture this structural damage.
  - **Solution**: SCHEMA automatically constructs scientific concept graphs and introduces a "trajectory hallucination pipeline" that audits intermediate reasoning at scale using a topology-weighted severity score (HSw), quantifying the propagation impact of errors.
- **Challenge 2: How to distinguish "false successes" where the final answer is correct but the reasoning is flawed?**
  - **Difficulty**: Models may reach correct conclusions via structurally flawed reasoning, causing a decoupling between terminal accuracy and trajectory honesty, which traditional evaluations miss.
  - **Solution**: A multi-agent counterfactual attribution module pinpoints the causal mechanisms behind failures. The framework reveals that hallucinations concentrate at highly connected knowledge hubs and demonstrates that terminal accuracy alone is an insufficient signal of reliability.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Task Synthesis**: SCHEMA builds scientific concept graphs from benchmark seeds and literature, synthesizing graph-grounded tasks including claim verification, multi-hop reasoning, open-ended explanation, and experimental code generation.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - Hallucinations are found to concentrate at a small set of highly connected knowledge hubs.
  - Reveals a decoupling between final-answer accuracy and trajectory honesty: models often reach correct conclusions through structurally flawed reasoning.
- **Comparison**: Unlike existing methods relying on expert verification or coarse statistical metrics, SCHEMA provides mechanism-level evaluation, explaining the origin of hallucinations and tracing their sources. It overcomes the poor transferability of existing open-world benchmarks to scientific domains.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the severe issue of hallucination propagation in scientific agents by proposing the SCHEMA framework. By constructing scientific concept graphs and employing topology-aware evaluation, it resolves the limitations of traditional benchmarks that ignore knowledge interconnectivity and structural reasoning flaws. The results indicate that for high-stakes scientific applications, terminal accuracy is insufficient for measuring reliability, necessitating mechanism-level evaluation grounded in knowledge topology.
