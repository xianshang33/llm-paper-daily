## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Multi-agent LLM pipelines orchestrate specialized agents into structured workflows, passing intermediate outputs to solve complex tasks. This design introduces a security gap absent in single-agent settings: once an agent accepts adversarial content, it propagates as trusted input throughout the pipeline.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current security analyses largely treat vulnerabilities as properties of individual models or isolated inputs, rather than emergent phenomena from collaboration. Existing evaluation frameworks largely evade these structural vulnerabilities because they lack explicit verification mechanisms for data exchange across agent boundaries.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a**: Systematic analysis and attack framework highlighting that the absence of "boundary verification" in multi-agent LLM pipelines leads to structural fragility, identifying implicit trust assumptions across content, identity, and execution intent boundaries as root causes.
- **Challenge 1: Silent propagation of adversarial content across boundaries**. The difficulty lies in intermediate outputs being implicitly trusted by downstream agents, allowing injected payloads to bypass isolation. The paper addresses this by identifying the lack of sanitization and validation at content boundaries, revealing how retrieved content can override agent directives and propagate.
- **Challenge 2: Unverifiable identity and execution plans**. The difficulty is that agent identity is inferred from routing position rather than verifiable credentials, and downstream agents are not enforced to adhere to prescribed plans. The paper addresses this by analyzing the missing identity and delegation boundaries, demonstrating how agent impersonation and plan deviation become distinct attack surfaces.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The study leverages annotated production traces from GAIA and SWE-Bench benchmarks to show these vulnerabilities exist in benign deployments. It further operationalizes these failure modes in a controlled multi-agent setting.
- **Evaluation Results**: Evaluations were conducted on GPT-5-mini, Claude Sonnet 4.5, and Kimi K2.5 under identical pipeline configurations. Results reveal that attack success aligns with pipeline structure rather than model capability.
- **Comparison**: Unlike existing work focusing on single-agent prompt injection or isolated model robustness, this study demonstrates that adversarial vulnerability is fundamentally an architectural property, motivating a shift towards pipeline-level defenses rather than just model-level hardening.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper unveils structural security vulnerabilities in multi-agent LLM systems caused by the lack of boundary verification. By analyzing implicit trust assumptions across content, identity, and delegation boundaries, it proves that adversarial content can silently propagate between agents. Experiments indicate that this vulnerability is an inherent architectural attribute, independent of specific model capabilities, calling for a community shift from point defenses to system-level pipeline boundary verification mechanisms.
