## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly integrated into autonomous robotic systems for task planning and control. However, this integration exposes them to prompt injection attacks, which can lead to unsafe decisions and physical harm. Multi-agent systems (MAS) exacerbate these risks through cross-agent contamination and broader attack surfaces.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Prior research has primarily focused on general-purpose multi-agent applications or prompt injections in single-agent architectures. There is a lack of systematic investigation into prompt injection attacks within LLM-based multi-agent robotic systems, particularly regarding how attacks propagate across agent workflows and how indirect injections via perception modules can cause physical harm.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A systematic evaluation framework for prompt injection attacks in LLM-based multi-agent robotic systems, covering both direct injections into task instructions and indirect injections through perception modules.
- **Challenge 1**: Complexity of attack propagation in multi-agent environments. The difficulty lies in ensuring malicious inputs propagate through intermediate agents without disrupting the intended final output while still inducing adversarial behavior. The paper addresses this by analyzing how attacks spread via shared prompt structures, quantifying the impact based on prompt composition and targeted agents.
- **Challenge 2**: Bridging the gap between malicious text and executable physical commands. Attackers must optimize malicious content so that the LLM's output is interpreted as executable commands by the robotic control system. The paper demonstrates through experiments that prompt injections can reduce task completion rates and successfully induce adversarial physical actions across varying attack complexities.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The study constructed an experimental environment for an LLM-based multi-agent robotic system, simulating scenarios with direct task instruction injections and indirect injections via perception modules. Experiments covered both single-agent and multi-agent settings with varying attack goals and strategies.
- **Evaluation Results**: Results showed that prompt injection attacks significantly reduced task completion rates and successfully induced adversarial actions. The study found that attacks can propagate between agents through shared prompt structures, with impacts varying by prompt composition and agent role. Architectural changes were also shown to affect LLM queries and subsequent attack success rates.
- **Comparison**: As the first study to systematically investigate prompt injections in multi-agent LLM robotic systems, this work fills a gap in literature regarding cross-agent contamination and physical consequence assessment. Unlike prior studies limited to single-agent or pure software environments, this research highlights the expanded attack surface and physical safety risks inherent in multi-agent interactions.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper presents the first systematic investigation of prompt injection attacks in LLM-based multi-agent robotic systems. By evaluating direct and indirect injection strategies, it reveals the mechanisms of attack propagation across agents and their negative impact on physical tasks. The findings indicate that while multi-agent architectures enhance collaboration, they also expand the attack surface, making systems more vulnerable to cross-agent contamination. This study provides critical insights into the security vulnerabilities of LLM-driven cyber-physical systems and emphasizes the need to address physical safety risks associated with prompt injections in system design.
