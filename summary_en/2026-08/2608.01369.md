## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Constructing executable chemical process models remains manually intensive. Chemical engineers must translate underspecified requests into coupled decisions regarding unit operations, thermodynamics, streams, specifications, degrees of freedom (DoF), initialization, and solver repair, where a single error can invalidate the model.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Prior LLM applications focus on experimental protocols or record extraction rather than executable simulation models. Existing multi-agent approaches using black-box simulators struggle with error diagnosis, lack gradient information, and are hard to reproduce because failures are distributed across flowsheet connections, configurations, and solver diagnostics.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed CRAFTS**: A Collaborative Role-Adaptive Fine-Tuning framework for LLM agents in chemical process simulation. It decomposes simulation building into bounded subtasks assigned to seven roles, with deterministic IDAES/Pyomo gates between stages.
- **Challenge 1: Accurate Translation of Complex Engineering Semantics**. The difficulty lies in converting unstructured natural language and Process Flowsheet Diagrams (PFDs) into strict simulator contracts. CRAFTS addresses this by fine-tuning three schema-critical roles (Visual, Topology, Specification) to generate typed intermediate representations (VisualGraphIR, TopologyIR, SpecIR), ensuring precise capture of unit, port, and thermodynamic decisions.
- **Challenge 2: Error Diagnosis and Repair in Simulation Execution**. Black-box simulators make debugging difficult due to lack of equation access. CRAFTS uses white-box simulators (IDAES/Pyomo) and integrates Debug and Optimization roles. Semantic artifacts must pass engineering gates before constructors and solvers are attached, enabling bounded repair and eligible optimization.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Data**: The authors introduced OpenIDAES-450, a dataset of 450 IDAES process simulation cases. The workflow is built on LangChain/LangGraph, with fine-tuning applied to the Visual, Topology, and Specification roles, while other roles use untuned Qwen.
- **Results**: On a frozen held-out split of 82 cases, CRAFTS completed the prescribed validation and execution contract for 91.5% of cases. It achieved F1 scores of 0.815 for units, 0.791 for streams, and 0.782 for directed connections.
- **Comparison**: Unlike prior works relying on black-box simulators, CRAFTS leverages white-box simulation with full equation access and gradient information. This significantly improves error diagnosability and reproducibility, demonstrating the effectiveness of role specialization and deterministic engineering gates.

## 4. Conclusion
CRAFTS successfully mirrors the staged workflow of chemical engineers by decomposing complex simulation tasks into seven collaborative roles. By combining role-specific fine-tuning, typed intermediate representations, and deterministic engineering gates based on white-box simulators, the framework effectively addresses the challenges of translating natural language into executable simulation models, achieving high accuracy and reliability in automated chemical process modeling.
