## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Training LLM-based multi-agent systems with Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) is gaining traction. Parallel work argues that such systems should be judged by behavior, not just reward.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: In spatial cooperative games, small frozen LLMs prompted with low-level actions fail completely (zero reward). Three main obstacles exist: 1) Grounding Gap: Small models cannot reliably map high-level goals to primitive actions, often hallucinating spatial preconditions; 2) Horizon Problem: Long episodes with sparse team rewards provide little credit signal for optimization; 3) Specialization Problem: Shared-weight training averages conflicting gradients, causing agents to collapse into identical behaviors rather than cooperating.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a training paradigm for small LLMs as spatial multi-agent policies using an Options framework**: Combines symbolic option libraries with per-agent LoRA fine-tuning to overcome zero-reward failures.
- **Challenge 1: Grounding gap and long-horizon credit assignment**. Difficulty: Small models struggle with low-level spatial logic, and sparse rewards hinder optimization. Solution: Introduces symbolic "Options" (short-horizon macro-actions executed by a symbolic planner) and mechanically synthesized feasibility guards. This abstracts temporal decisions and filters invalid actions, effectively shortening the horizon and improving grounding.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of role specialization in cooperation**. Difficulty: Joint training leads to behavioral homogenization. Solution: Uses Per-Agent Multi-Agent GRPO (PA-MAGRPO) to train private LoRA adapters for each agent, enabling distinct policy learning and genuine collaborative division of labor.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Option libraries are drafted by coding models from game source code. Feasibility guards are synthesized from cheap random-policy burn-in rollouts, ensuring no manual authoring or reward tuning. Each agent uses a private LoRA adapter trained via PA-MAGRPO.
- **Results**: The method lifts frozen small backbones (2-4B parameters) from zero reward to competent play across three spatial games and four model variants.
- **Comparison & Insights**: Unlike works relying solely on reward metrics, behavioral audits reveal that reward and cooperation decouple. A rising reward curve may indicate one agent dominating the task while the other idles. True cooperation emerges only when necessary. Thus, reward alone is an unreliable proxy for cooperation, necessitating behavioral evaluation.

#### Summary
This paper addresses the failure of small LLMs in spatial multi-agent tasks by proposing a framework combining symbolic option abstraction with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA + PA-MAGRPO). By automating the construction of option libraries with feasibility guards, it solves grounding and horizon issues. Independent policy optimization resolves role homogenization. Crucially, the study highlights the importance of behavioral auditing, demonstrating that high rewards do not equate to effective cooperation, offering a new perspective for evaluating LLM-based multi-agent systems.
