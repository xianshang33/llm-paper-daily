## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: As Large Language Models (LLMs) evolve into autonomous agents capable of complex real-world tasks, personalization has become critical. User preferences are rarely explicit; they are dynamic, evolving, and implicitly embedded within long-term interaction histories.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current benchmarks for agent personalization largely rely on static preference snapshots, fixed interaction logs, or Question Answering (QA) over predefined profiles. These approaches fail to capture the complexity of evolving preferences and neglect "preference-conditioned task execution." This leads to a "knowledge-to-action gap," where agents possess relevant knowledge but fail to apply it during decision-making.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed IBA-Bench benchmark and IBA-Agent framework** to address implicit behavioral alignment.
- **Challenge 1: The Knowledge-to-Action Gap**
  - **Description & Difficulty**: Agents can recall static preferences (e.g., liking spicy food) and recent events (e.g., tooth extraction) but fail to synthesize these signals during task execution (e.g., ordering dinner), leading to inappropriate actions. The difficulty lies in connecting seemingly unrelated temporal signals to infer implicit constraints.
  - **Solution**: IBA-Bench evaluates this using longitudinal histories with noise and implicit cues. IBA-Agent addresses this via broad retrieval and trajectory-level alignment to proactively infer constraints and reconcile conflicts.
- **Challenge 2: Dynamic Preference Synthesis and Conflict Resolution**
  - **Description & Difficulty**: User preferences change over time and contain noise. Static profiling cannot handle this evolution or resolve conflicts between multiple latent constraints.
  - **Solution**: IBA-Agent moves beyond static extraction by synthesizing dynamic signals through trajectory-level alignment, enabling better decision-making in complex, evolving scenarios.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Benchmark**: Introduced IBA-Bench, constructed from longitudinal interaction histories across nine application domains, specifically designed to test implicit behavioral alignment.
- **Results**: Experiments show that effective personalization remains a significant challenge for state-of-the-art (SOTA) LLM agents. Current models struggle to bridge the knowledge-to-action gap.
- **Comparison**: The proposed IBA-Agent substantially improves behavioral alignment in complex scenarios compared to existing SOTA methods, demonstrating superior capability in handling implicit constraints and conflicting priorities.

#### Summary
This paper identifies the "knowledge-to-action gap" in current personalized LLM agents, where agents fail to apply known user preferences during task execution. To address this, the authors introduce IBA-Bench, a benchmark featuring noisy, long-term interaction histories, and IBA-Agent, a framework utilizing broad retrieval and trajectory-level alignment. Results indicate that IBA-Agent significantly outperforms existing methods in aligning agent behavior with implicit, evolving user constraints, offering a new direction for developing truly personalized autonomous agents.
