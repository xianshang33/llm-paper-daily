## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: LLM-Agent benchmark evaluations are often expensive and time-consuming, yet partial task outcomes are available long before the full run completes. There is a temptation to report these partial scores to save costs or speed up feedback.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Simple partial scores do not indicate whether the observed tasks support the same conclusion as the completed evaluation. Early tasks may miss critical parts of the benchmark (e.g., specific domains or difficulties), and prioritizing cheaper tasks can distort the sample distribution. Rules that only resolve easy comparisons may appear accurate but leave many comparisons unresolved, making partial scores insufficient for reliable decision-making.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ParEvalLayer**: A decision layer that reads paired outcomes for two agent systems and a pre-chosen comparison policy to determine if current partial data supports a decision.
- **Challenge 1: Representativeness Bias and Uncertainty in Partial Data**. The difficulty lies in early or low-cost tasks potentially failing to represent the overall benchmark distribution, leading to judgments inconsistent with full-data conclusions. ParEvalLayer addresses this by applying an explicit comparison policy (including required improvement margin, coverage requirements, etc.) to categorize the state as "better," "not better," "needs more evidence," or "abstain," thereby quantifying uncertainty rather than just outputting a score.
- **Challenge 2: Achieving Early Stopping While Maintaining Decision Accuracy**. The challenge is determining when to stop evaluation to save resources without sacrificing the correctness of the comparison. ParEvalLayer applies the policy at each step using only observed outcomes and verifies if the judgment matches the complete data conclusion. It achieves consistent decisions with full evaluations after observing only 15%-25% of task outcomes in several benchmarks.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation Method**: The study evaluates ParEvalLayer by replaying completed public benchmark data, simulating early stopping at various points. At each point, ParEvalLayer applies the policy using only observed outcomes; if a comparison judgment is reached, it is checked against the ground truth from the full dataset.
- **Results**: With the main comparison rule, three public benchmarks reached the same decision as the completed evaluation after observing only 15% to 25% of task outcomes. Other benchmarks required more data.
- **Comparison & Insights**: Compared to traditional methods that only report partial scores, ParEvalLayer reveals variations in data requirements across benchmarks. It demonstrates that reporting partial scores alone is insufficient; reports should also state the decision rule and the number of unresolved comparisons, providing a more transparent evaluation view.

## 4. Conclusion
ParEvalLayer addresses the difficulty of making decisions based on partial data in LLM-Agent evaluations. It serves not just as an early-stopping mechanism but as a transparent decision layer that determines if current evidence is sufficient according to preset policies. Experiments show that the method maintains high decision consistency with full evaluations while significantly reducing evaluation costs (requiring only a small fraction of task results), highlighting the importance of explicitly stating decision rules and unresolved states in evaluation reports.
