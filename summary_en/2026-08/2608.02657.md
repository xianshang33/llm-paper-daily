## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Agentic LLMs operating in open environments are vulnerable to Indirect Prompt Injection (IPI) attacks, where malicious instructions are hidden within external tool outputs. While many efforts address this threat, little is known about the internal mechanisms of Agentic LLMs when exposed to IPI, a condition termed "IPI exposure."
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current activation-based monitoring primarily focuses on single-turn or direct prompt injection scenarios. However, IPI attacks against agents involve mixed-source contexts, stochastic arrival in multi-turn dialogues, and behaviorally directive but superficially harmless content, making existing monitoring methods ineffective for these隐蔽 threats.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A comprehensive analysis framework for IPI exposure signals in Agentic LLMs, covering Probing, Defense, and Explanation, and introduced AGRI, a probe-gated reasoning-based defense mechanism.
- **Challenge 1: How to accurately detect if the model internally perceives an IPI attack?**
  - **Difficulty**: IPI attacks are embedded in complex tool returns and may be adaptive or cross-lingual, making traditional output checks insufficient for detecting the model's true internal state.
  - **Solution**: Trained simple linear probes on pre-generation hidden states across six models (up to 753B parameters). These probes achieved >90% AUROC on unseen attacks, instructions, and tasks, demonstrating high robustness against adaptive attacks and in cross-lingual settings.
- **Challenge 2: How to bridge the gap between "recognizing risk" and "taking safe actions"?**
  - **Difficulty**: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) measurements revealed a "recognition-action gap": although models encode risk signals internally, they often fail to translate them into safe defensive actions.
  - **Solution**: Introduced AGRI, a defense that prepends anti-injection reasoning on demand when the probe detects high-risk signals. In difficult AgentDojo settings, AGRI substantially reduced attack success rates (e.g., from 34.6% to 0% on Qwen3.5-27B) while largely maintaining utility on clean tasks.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation**: Used the AGENT D OJO benchmark to collect large-scale multi-turn agent trajectories. Evaluated six open-source LLMs (8B–753B).
- **Probing Results**: Linear probes achieved >90% AUROC across unseen scenarios, proving the high predictability of internal signals. This extends prior work by validating signal existence in complex agentic interactions rather than just direct injection.
- **Defense Performance**: AGRI significantly improved security without compromising normal task performance. For instance, it reduced the attack success rate to 0% on Qwen3.5-27B (from 34.6%). This offers a more fundamental defense based on internal states compared to traditional prompt engineering or post-hoc filtering.
- **Explainability**: The proposed framework identified natural language explanations correlated with probe signals. Findings showed model-specific profiles: some align with direct IPI exposure claims, while others align with indirect operational cues, offering new insights into internal safety mechanisms.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper deeply investigates the internal mechanisms of Agentic LLMs under IPI attacks. It first confirms the presence of highly predictable IPI exposure signals in hidden states; second, it reveals the recognition-action gap and proposes AGRI to effectively bridge it; finally, it provides explainable analysis showing how different models process IPI signals. This work offers significant theoretical and practical tools for understanding and defending against advanced prompt injection attacks in agentic systems.
