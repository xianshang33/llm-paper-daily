## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Low-Earth-orbit (LEO) Earth observation missions are shifting from raw data storage and forwarding to onboard intelligent processing. Due to limited communication windows, bandwidth constraints, and time-sensitivity, relying solely on ground-side processing is no longer optimal. Onboard AI allows for filtering low-value scenes, identifying high-value events, and returning compact mission artifacts before full image downlink.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current research often focuses on the execution efficiency of single models (e.g., detectors) and lacks a comprehensive "task layer." A standalone detector cannot interpret mission intent, coordinate local tool calls, expose execution states, or return machine-consumable artifacts under power and communication constraints. Existing solutions fail to address the system-level orchestration of language and vision services on resource-constrained edge SoCs.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SAT-Edge-Agent**: A Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) edge-agent orchestration system deployed on a Commercial Off-The-Shelf (COTS) ARM-based heterogeneous edge SoC. It coordinates a local OpenAI-compatible language service with a YOLO-style oriented object detection endpoint.
- **Challenge 1: Multi-service Coordination and Latency Bottlenecks under Resource Constraints**
  - **Description & Difficulty**: Running both Large Language Model (LLM) services and computer vision models on low-power edge devices while meeting strict real-time and power constraints makes it difficult to identify latency sources and optimization directions.
  - **Solution**: Through detailed profiling, the study reveals that most visible latency occurs outside detector execution (i.e., in LLM inference and pre/post-processing), with detector time accounting for only ~2.5-3% of total agent latency. By separating machine-oriented structured results (from the detector) from operator-facing natural language summaries (from the LLM), the method clarifies optimization boundaries and demonstrates the feasibility of end-to-end orchestration on COTS hardware.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of Reproducibility and Standardized Evaluation**
  - **Description & Difficulty**: The field of satellite edge computing lacks unified HIL testing benchmarks, making cross-study comparisons difficult and often overlooking system-level metrics (e.g., CPU/NPU utilization, end-to-end latency distribution).
  - **Solution**: Established a reproducible HIL boundary using two fixed FAIR1M workloads (single-image and serial two-image requests), each repeated 20 times. The paper provides sanitized request records, JSON outputs, and reproduction scripts. Results show 100% success rate, with detailed reporting of P95 latency, CPU utilization, and NPU load fields, offering a standardized observational baseline for future research.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The system runs on an ARM-based heterogeneous edge SoC. The architecture includes a browser workspace and a FastAPI agent coordinating a local OpenAI-compatible language service and an internal YOLO-style oriented object detection endpoint, which returns FAIR1M metadata-backed structured results.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - **Success Rate**: 100% (20/20 attempts) for both workloads.
  - **Latency**: Mean full-agent latency was 29.353s (P95 31.166s) for single-image requests and 60.937s (P95 66.882s) for dual-image requests.
  - **Component Timing**: Detector mean times were 861.386ms and 1510.920ms, representing only 2.93% and 2.48% of total agent latency, respectively. This indicates the bottleneck lies in non-detector components.
  - **Resource Utilization**: Mean CPU utilization was ~20.5%. The NPU load field showed a mean of 100%, though noted as a shared-accelerator software metric rather than calibrated occupancy.
- **Comparison**: Unlike prior works focusing solely on model accuracy or isolated inference speed, this paper emphasizes system-level orchestration and reproducibility. While it does not claim new detection accuracy or flight readiness, it fills the evaluation gap between "models" and "operational agents" by providing a robust HIL boundary and detailed performance decomposition.

## 4. Conclusion
SAT-Edge-Agent demonstrates the feasibility of orchestrating satellite onboard intelligence agents on COTS edge hardware. Its core contribution lies not in novel detection algorithms or geolocation methods, but in constructing a complete, reproducible hardware-in-the-loop test framework. It reveals that in resource-constrained environments, the primary bottleneck for end-to-end latency is language service and system orchestration rather than visual detection. This work establishes a critical benchmark and reference architecture for the development and evaluation of future satellite edge intelligence systems.
