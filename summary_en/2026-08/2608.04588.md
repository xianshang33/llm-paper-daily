## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Agentic systems coordinating specialized LLM-based agents have emerged as a promising paradigm for solving complex tasks. However, most existing systems primarily optimize for task success, giving limited consideration to execution efficiency under practical constraints like executor capability and computational cost.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Existing router-based methods have limited ability to reason over rich, evolving task contexts, multi-step dependencies, and intermediate execution feedback. They often generalize poorly to unseen executors and lack explicit awareness of heterogeneous executor costs and capabilities, focusing mainly on performance-only routing.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed EASy**: A trainable agentic framework that jointly optimizes task performance and computational efficiency through reinforcement learning.
- **Challenge 1: How to achieve context-sensitive coordination balancing cost and capability?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional routing fails to handle evolving contexts and multi-step dependencies while ignoring cost differences arising from executor heterogeneity.
  - **Solution**: EASy equips the LLM-based orchestrator with explicit knowledge of capability and cost profiles of heterogeneous executors, enabling coordination that goes beyond simple performance-based routing.
- **Challenge 2: How to efficiently decompose complex tasks and adapt to intermediate results?**
  - **Difficulty**: Complex tasks require logical decomposition for parallelization and dynamic adjustment based on intermediate outcomes, which static workflows cannot handle.
  - **Solution**: Introduces a "milestone-plan-act" workflow that decomposes tasks into manageable milestones, constructs dependency-aware execution graphs, assigns suitable executors, parallelizes independent steps, and adapts subsequent decisions based on intermediate feedback.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: To train the orchestrator, the authors developed a tree-structured rollout procedure to explore alternative milestone decompositions and execution plans. A multi-component reward function was designed to capture task correctness, execution efficiency, and trajectory completeness.
- **Evaluation Results**: Extensive experiments were conducted on benchmarks for mathematical reasoning, embodied decision-making, and deep research.
- **Comparison**: Results show that EASy consistently achieves stronger performance-efficiency trade-offs compared to strong agentic baselines, maintaining high task success rates while significantly reducing computational costs or improving execution speed.

## 4. Conclusion
EASy addresses the neglect of execution efficiency in current agentic systems by introducing an RL-trained orchestrator with explicit awareness of executor costs and capabilities. Its core "milestone-plan-act" mechanism and tree-search training strategy enable optimal balance between performance and efficiency in complex, dynamic environments, offering a new paradigm for building practical and efficient LLM-based agentic systems.
