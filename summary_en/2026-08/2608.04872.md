## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Symbolic Regression (SR) aims to discover closed-form mathematical equations from data, a critical task for scientific discovery. Existing Large Language Model (LLM)-guided methods typically rely on a unified "proposal-evaluation" loop.
- **Limitations of Prior Work**: Current approaches compress heterogeneous search failures (e.g., invalid programs, parameter instability, redundant terms) into a single scalar score and a uniform prompt. This oversimplification ignores that different failure modes require distinct evidence and corrective strategies, leading to ineffective search adjustments, such as exploring new structures when the primary issue is syntactic invalidity.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed A-SR**: A self-evolving agentic framework that shifts the control unit from simple expression edits to role-conditioned evidence views, coordinating formula discovery through hierarchical mechanisms.
- **Challenge 1: Confusion and Inefficiency in Handling Heterogeneous Failures**. The difficulty lies in the fact that different errors (syntax, fit, complexity) require distinct repair strategies, which a single score cannot differentiate. A-SR addresses this by using an online evaluator-reward role policy to characterize feedback (reliability, productivity), update role-level utilities, and route elite motifs, failure traces, and diagnostics to specialized agents.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of Adaptivity and Knowledge Accumulation**. The challenge is to dynamically adjust search strategies within a run and accumulate priors across runs. A-SR achieves self-evolution at two timescales: adapting the search process via state-routed process memory without updating LLM parameters during a run, and distilling recorded trajectories into open-source LLMs as role-conditioned proposal priors across runs.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on four LSR-Synth scientific domains in LLM-SRBench and four real-world scientific discovery tasks.
- **Comparison Results**:
    - On synthetic datasets, A-SR with Llama3.1-8B improved Acc@0.01 from a baseline of 25.79% to 48.30%.
    - With Qwen3-4B using LoRA fine-tuning (A-SR-LoRA), Acc@0.01 improved from 24.58% to 38.29%.
    - In real-world tasks, A-SR achieved the best In-Distribution (ID) or Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Normalized Mean Squared Error (NMSE) on 7 out of 8 reported metrics.
- **Advantage Analysis**: Compared to traditional genetic programming, neural-guided search, and existing LLM-guided methods, A-SR significantly enhances the ability to discover accurate and robust equations through refined role coordination and memory routing, particularly excelling in complex failure scenarios.

#### Summary
A-SR is an innovative self-evolving agentic framework designed to address complex search problems in symbolic regression. By introducing role-based coordination protocols, online evaluation feedback, and state-routed memory, it overcomes the limitation of traditional methods that oversimplify heterogeneous failures. The method not only significantly outperforms existing baselines on synthetic benchmarks but also demonstrates superior accuracy and generalization in real-world scientific discovery tasks, proving the potential of hierarchical coordination and multi-agent collaboration in enhancing LLM-based scientific reasoning.
