## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Multi-agent LLM systems relay Key-Value (KV) caches instead of text, attributing performance gains to the exchange of "latent thoughts." This attribution implies a causal claim: the receiver utilizes content specific to the example, not just the presence of a cache.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Prior studies rely solely on benchmark performance deltas to validate latent communication. However, these gains could stem from generic interface effects or helpful perturbations rather than true example-specific information transfer. Benchmark improvements alone cannot establish the causality of "latent thought" transmission.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Causal Audit Framework**: The paper introduces an intervention-based audit that swaps relayed caches with deranged (mismatched-example), zeroed, or moment-matched random counterparts, evaluating released systems without retraining.
- **Challenge 1: Distinguishing true semantic transfer from generic interface/architectural effects.**
    - **Difficulty**: Performance boosts may result from the model adapting to additional inputs rather than processing specific transmitted information.
    - **Solution**: Introduced "mismatched cache" controls. Results showed that in scenarios requiring private information, mismatched caches caused performance to drop to baseline, proving content specificity.
- **Challenge 2: Quantifying the actual contribution of latent communication in natural benchmarks.**
    - **Difficulty**: In standard benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K), the receiver can often solve tasks independently, making it hard to isolate the marginal benefit of the cache.
    - **Solution**: Employed a pre-registered five-seed protocol with Holm-corrected TOST (Equivalence Testing). Results indicated that in natural settings, the advantage of example-specific cache transfer was statistically equivalent to zero within a ±2.8 point margin.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The audit was conducted on unmodified, released systems across three model families (Qwen3, Mistral-Nemo, phi-4) and five checkpoints.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - **Calibrated Regime**: When private information was required, correct relays achieved ~100% accuracy, while answer-irrelevant relays dropped to 23-25%. This contrast was replicated across Qwen3-4B/8B/14B, Mistral-Nemo-12B, and phi-4.
    - **Natural Regime**: On GSM8K, ARC-Challenge, and MedQA, the benefit of example-specific cache transfer was equivalent to zero within the error margin. For instance, zeroing the relay cost 14.7 points, but using a mismatched cache only cost 0.4 points, indicating gains were largely due to the cache's presence, not its specific content.
- **Comparison with Related Work**:
    - **LatentMAS**: Native relay showed ceiling effects, confirming effectiveness when private info is critical.
    - **KVComm**: Showed partial transfer via layer subsets.
    - **C2C**: No detected example-specific transfer advantage with its released projector.
    - **Conclusion**: Benchmark deltas alone do not prove latent thought transmission; mismatched-cache audits are necessary.

#### Summary
This paper reveals through rigorous causal auditing that performance gains from relaying KV caches in multi-agent LLMs are not always due to example-specific "latent thought" transmission. While cache content is crucial in controlled scenarios requiring private information, gains in standard natural benchmarks primarily stem from interface effects or the general presence of state, rather than specific semantic content. The study highlights the importance of distinguishing between "cache effects" and "pairing effects" and releases audit tools to promote more rigorous system evaluation.
