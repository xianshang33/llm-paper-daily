## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: In India, accessing social benefits requires filling out forms, but the most needy populations are often illiterate. Currently, frontline health workers manually enroll beneficiaries one by one, which is inefficient and strains their limited capacity.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Traditional IVR systems rely on touch-tones or simple speech recognition, offering poor user experience and low completion rates. Existing LLM-powered voice agents perform well in quiet, standard conditions but degrade significantly in real-world rural acoustic environments (noise, channel distortion) and struggle to meet strict latency and cost constraints.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed FormBharo**: A voice agent that combines Large Language Models (LLMs) with deterministic, rule-based validation and flow control to automatically fill structured enrollment forms via phone calls for low-income mothers in rural India.
- **Challenge 1: Error propagation in real-world speech**. Real-speech transcription errors cause a ~41 percentage point drop in form completion compared to reference transcripts, and component-level metrics do not predict end-to-end performance. **Solution**: The system integrates rule-based controls that recover many turn-level extraction errors, allowing smaller, cheaper models to match or surpass frontier models in form completion rates.
- **Challenge 2: Multi-objective optimization for deployment**. No single model optimizes accuracy, cost, and latency simultaneously, and errors can both propagate and cancel out across the pipeline. **Solution**: The authors released FormVoiceAgentBench for end-to-end evaluation and used a Pareto-based weighted-sum scalarization to select a deployable configuration that balances these three competing objectives.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: FormBharo is being piloted with the NGO ARMMAN to enroll low-income Hindi-speaking mothers in maternal and child health programs. The architecture pairs LLMs for understanding/generation with hard rules for slot validation and dialogue flow.
- **Benchmark**: Released FormVoiceAgentBench, featuring 3,760 multi-turn conversation tests across 960 simulated calls with human-recorded Hindi audio, evaluating transcription, extraction, response generation, and end-to-end form completion.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - Form completion drops by up to ~41% when LLMs process error-prone real-speech transcripts instead of reference texts.
    - Rule-based controls significantly improve robustness, enabling smaller, cheaper models to match or exceed the form completion performance of frontier models like GPT-5.5.
    - Component-level metrics (e.g., extraction accuracy) diverge from end-to-end metrics: GPT-5.5 achieves 99.8% extraction accuracy on reference transcripts but ranks lower in end-to-end form completion, highlighting the necessity of end-to-end evaluation.
    - A Pareto-optimal configuration was selected to balance accuracy, cost, and latency for practical deployment.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the challenge of social benefit registration for illiterate populations in rural India by introducing FormBharo. By combining LLMs with deterministic rule-based controls, it mitigates performance degradation caused by real-world acoustic errors and achieves efficient automated form filling under strict resource constraints. The study underscores the importance of end-to-end evaluation, demonstrating that component performance does not guarantee task success, and provides a framework for deploying voice AI in resource-constrained settings through new benchmarks and Pareto optimization.
