## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Modern chronic care management is episodic, leaving patients without continuous support between hospital visits, leading to high readmission rates. Existing digital health tools fail to bridge this gap.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**:
    1. **Stateless Sessions**: Traditional medical LLMs (e.g., Med-PaLM) are session-bound and cannot retain patient history, allergies, or symptom changes across separate conversations.
    2. **Memory Update Risks**: RAG-based approaches often use append-only vector databases, which can lead to "temporal hallucinations" when clinical facts change. They struggle to overwrite obsolete information (e.g., old dosages), posing safety risks.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ECHO (Enhanced Care & Health Observer)**, a locally-deployable agentic health assistant system integrating agentic orchestration, hybrid safety guardrails, and multimodal speech assessment.
- **Challenge 1: Consistency and Safety of Long-Term Memory Across Sessions**
    - **Difficulty**: Maintaining continuity of patient state over long-term care while correctly updating or overwriting obsolete clinical facts to prevent advice based on outdated history.
    - **Solution**: ECHO employs an agentic chatbot orchestrated via LangGraph using a ReAct loop. It combines SQLite for transactional data with a Hindsight temporal knowledge graph for unstructured longitudinal memory. This structure allows new clinical facts to supersede rather than just accumulate old ones, ensuring a safe and consistent patient profile.
- **Challenge 2: Real-Time Safety Interception and Intent Recognition in Healthcare**
    - **Difficulty**: Identifying explicit crisis signals and jailbreak attempts with ultra-low latency, while accurately classifying boundary-case clinical intents to prevent harmful outputs, outperforming general-purpose LLMs.
    - **Solution**: A two-stage hybrid safety layer is introduced. Stage 1 uses a rule-based interceptor for explicit crises/jailbreaks (<1ms). Stage 2 uses a Signed Graph Neural Network (GNN) with APPNP-style propagation for boundary cases. This achieved 88.8% accuracy and 90.6% unsafe recall on a Turkish health dataset, outperforming zero-shot baselines like Llama 3.3 70B.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: ECHO is implemented as a web application running entirely on consumer hardware. No patient data is transmitted externally, ensuring compliance with GDPR and KVKK.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - **Agentic Capability**: Achieved a 94.9% tool-execution pass rate on a 59-scenario benchmark using GPT-5 Mini.
    - **Safety Classification**: The hybrid safety layer achieved 88.8% accuracy and 90.6% unsafe recall on a dataset of 2,537 annotated Turkish health queries, significantly outperforming zero-shot LLM baselines including Llama 3.3 70B.
    - **Speech Assessment**: The multimodal module (Whisper acoustic + BERT text encoding) reached a mean macro F1 of 0.652 for emotion, depression, and pain estimation.
- **Comparison**: Unlike session-bound models (Med-PaLM) or append-only RAG, ECHO solves memory consistency via temporal knowledge graphs. Its specialized Signed GNN outperforms general LLMs in specific medical intent classification.

#### Summary
ECHO is an end-to-end localized agentic health assistant for chronic care management. It addresses memory consistency in long-term care through temporal knowledge graphs, ensures safety via a two-stage hybrid guardrail, and enhances emotional awareness through multimodal speech analysis. The system demonstrates high performance in clinical tool usage and safety classification while strictly preserving patient privacy, offering a viable path for locally deployed medical AI.
