## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Evaluating the strength of Large Language Model (LLM) agents in imperfect-information games (e.g., poker) is costly and noisy. Since the required number of games is unknown, fixed-budget evaluations either waste resources after results are decisive or stop prematurely. Naive optional stopping with ordinary confidence intervals invalidates statistical guarantees, inflating false positives.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: The existing Action-Informed Value Assessment Tool (AIVAT) significantly reduces variance via conditional mean-zero corrections but does not provide guidance on when to stop. Conversely, applying traditional Confidence Sequences (CSs) directly to raw, high-variance outcomes requires excessive interactions. There was no effective combination of both to achieve efficient, statistically guaranteed early stopping.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed AV-AIVAT (Anytime-Valid AIVAT)**: A general statistical interface that combines AIVAT's variance reduction with continuously monitored Confidence Sequences for agent evaluation in imperfect-information games.
- **Challenge 1: Causal Leakage in Online Learning**. The difficulty lies in improving the value model during evaluation without letting the current game's score influence its own correction. The paper solves this by designing a "pre-action conditional-kernel interface," ensuring the online value model learns only from past games, thus preventing any game from contributing to its own correction term.
- **Challenge 2: Theoretical Bounds and Certification for Efficiency**. The challenge is determining when variance reduction translates to fewer required games and providing auditable finite-sample guarantees. The paper establishes structural payoff bounds for Leduc Hold'em and characterizes a "width floor" determined by the CS bet cap and this bound. It separates asymptotic screening (AsympCS) from exact certification (EB-CS), allowing evaluation to stop immediately when evidence suffices while providing all data needed for third-party verification.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated across 15 LLM agent configurations, spanning 71,439 paired Heads-Up No-Limit Hold'em (HUNL) hands.
- **Key Results**:
    - **Variance Reduction**: AIVAT achieved a median 54x variance reduction.
    - **Stopping Efficiency**: At a nominal 95% confidence level and ±1 Big Blind (BB) precision, raw outcomes required a median 74x more hands than AIVAT-corrected outcomes to stop under Asymptotic CS (AsympCS). This implies AV-AIVAT is 74x cheaper than using raw outcomes.
    - **Exact Certification**: For Empirical-Bernstein CS (EB-CS), which requires independently justified bounds, descriptive HUNL runs showed a median stopping-time ratio of 1.37x, demonstrating efficiency gains even under strict finite-sample guarantees.
- **Comparison**: Unlike fixed-budget evaluations (e.g., Libratus's fixed 120,000 hands) or naive optional stopping, AV-AIVAT dynamically determines stopping time while maintaining statistical validity, significantly reducing computational costs and expert time.

## 4. Conclusion
AV-AIVAT successfully transforms variance reduction into an efficient, auditable early-stopping mechanism. By separating asymptotic screening from exact certification, it addresses the high cost and noise in evaluating LLM agents in imperfect-information games. The method ensures statistical validity at any stopping point and allows for third-party re-verification, offering a rigorous and economical new standard for LLM agent evaluation in gaming environments.
