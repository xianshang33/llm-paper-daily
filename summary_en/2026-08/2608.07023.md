## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: HR platforms face persistent challenges in organizing thousands of unstandardized, multilingual expertise declarations, which directly impacts downstream tasks like accurate talent matching. Free-text declarations often lead to multilingual fragmentation, niche jargon, compound skills, and rapid temporal drift.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Traditional methods are limited. Rigid top-down approaches (e.g., relying on ESCO) offer structural consistency but fail to capture emerging or localized concepts. Purely generative bottom-up approaches are flexible but lack structural constraints, leading to semantic fragmentation, redundant clusters, and relationship hallucinations. Additionally, LLMs suffer from hallucinations and a lack of grounding in verifiable metadata.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed an Agentic Hybrid Top-Down and Bottom-Up Knowledge Graph Generation Pipeline**: This framework grounds an LLM in the Wikidata multilingual KG while using an agentic reflection pattern to synthesize emerging concepts, resulting in a scalable, explicable, and self-healing skills KG.
- **Challenge 1: Balancing structural consistency with flexibility for emerging skills.**
  - **Difficulty**: Static ontologies miss new trends, while open generation causes structural chaos.
  - **Solution**: The hybrid architecture anchors recognized concepts to stable Wikidata entities (top-down grounding) while dynamically creating new nodes and metadata for unrecognized skills via agentic reflection (bottom-up synthesis), ensuring both stability and adaptability.
- **Challenge 2: Handling multilingual noise, fragmentation, and semantic redundancy.**
  - **Difficulty**: Multilingual variations, abbreviations, and noise in free text lead to fragmented nodes representing the same concept.
  - **Solution**: A five-stage process (entity reconciliation, multilingual canonicalization, active curation, deduplication, and iterative recovery) allows the system to autonomously adapt to noisy skill mentions across five European languages, converging fragmented expressions (e.g., "Web PM") into unified concept nodes.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The fully automated agentic pipeline is instantiated on Wikidata and operates through five stages: entity reconciliation, multilingual canonicalization, active curation, deduplication, and iterative recovery of unmapped concepts. It processes unstructured, noisy text from the Malt platform across five European languages.
- **Evaluation and Comparison**:
  - Compared to rigid top-down methods (e.g., ESCO-based), this approach successfully captures emerging skills (e.g., "Web Project Management") that static ontologies miss.
  - Compared to chaotic bottom-up methods, it eliminates semantic fragmentation (correctly linking "Project Mgmt" and "Web PM") and link hallucinations (e.g., erroneous links to "Excel") through grounding and agentic reflection, providing clearer structure and verifiable metadata.
  - The resulting skills KG is highly scalable, self-healing, and enables the derivation of structured taxonomies.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper presents a novel hybrid approach to knowledge graph generation that effectively addresses the organization of multilingual, unstandardized skill data in HR. By combining authoritative grounding from Wikidata with the flexible generative capabilities of LLM agents, it overcomes the inflexibility of top-down methods and the lack of structure in bottom-up approaches. The proposed pipeline autonomously adapts to noisy data, generating high-quality, interpretable, and dynamic skills knowledge graphs that support robust talent matching applications.
