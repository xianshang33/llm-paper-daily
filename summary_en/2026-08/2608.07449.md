## What the Paper Does
- **Background**: LLM agents increasingly adapt to recurring tasks by accumulating "skills"—lightweight, reusable textual artifacts loaded into context without weight updates. The field is shifting from static skill synthesis to skill evolution, where skills are refined through iterative execution, failure diagnosis, and text-space updates.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current frameworks suffer from two main issues: (1) Lack of explicit diagnosis-outcome feedback, where diagnostic patches are committed without verifying their actual impact; (2) Treating deletion as a generic edit rather than a dedicated mechanism for knowledge consolidation, leading to unregulated skill growth filled with redundancy and conflicting heuristics.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SkillProx**: A proximal-gradient-inspired forward-backward framework that couples closed-loop diagnostic evolution with utility-aware proximal refinement, balancing task loss and skill complexity.
- **Challenge 1: Unverified Forward Updates**. Existing methods treat LLM-generated diagnoses as valid updates without verification, risking performance regression. **Solution**: The forward stage re-executes diagnosis-driven edits on the same task batch, rolls back regressions, and feeds measured outcomes back into subsequent diagnoses, creating a closed feedback loop.
- **Challenge 2: Unregulated Skill Growth**. Iterative patching causes skills to expand uncontrollably with redundant or conflicting information. **Solution**: The backward stage decomposes skills into auditable knowledge units, estimates their contributions via a frozen leave-one-out utility audit, and applies validation-gated consolidation, demotion, or removal to maintain a concise and effective skill set.

## Implementation, Evaluation, and Comparison
- **Evaluation Setup**: Experiments were conducted on in-distribution and out-of-distribution benchmarks across multiple backbone LLMs.
- **Results**: SkillProx improved average accuracy by 3.0 percentage points over the strongest gradient-based baseline (e.g., SkillGrad).
- **Ablation Studies**: Demonstrated the complementary effects of closed-loop diagnosis and proximal refinement, showing that both components are necessary for optimal performance.

## Summary
SkillProx addresses the critical issues of unverified updates and structural bloat in LLM agent skill evolution. By mimicking forward-backward splitting algorithms from optimization theory, it ensures update effectiveness through closed-loop feedback and maintains knowledge health via backward utility auditing. This approach enables the co-evolution of task capability and skill structure, significantly enhancing agent robustness and accuracy across diverse scenarios.
