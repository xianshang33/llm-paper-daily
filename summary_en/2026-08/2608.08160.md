## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: The rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) is revolutionizing AI for Games by enabling open-ended and fluid interactive storytelling. However, existing research has largely overlooked the critical challenge of maintaining long-horizon logical consistency and narrative integrity against unconstrained user interventions.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current narrative agents often fail to adhere to established story facts over long interactions, especially when faced with adversarial user inputs. High linguistic quality does not guarantee commitment preservation, and there is a lack of benchmarks that can automatically evaluate long-horizon consistency.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed NCP-Bench**: A benchmark for evaluating "Narrative Commitment Preservation" (NCP) in interactive narratives.
- **Challenge 1: Contradiction between Long-Horizon Consistency and Adversarial Interventions**. The difficulty lies in users' high-agency interventions (e.g., "speedrunning") inducing model deviations from preset plot trajectories, leading to logical contradictions. The paper addresses this by constructing environments with structured narrative specifications (trajectories, commitments, initial facts) and automatically checking consistency during interactions between player and narrator agents.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of Standardized Automatic Evaluation**. Traditional evaluations rely on human judgment or single-turn quality, failing to measure overall narrative integrity over hundreds of turns. The paper derives 100 narrative environments from movie synopses with clear achievement commitments and fact constraints, enabling automatic and objective assessment of fact conflict rates and survival rates.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: NCP-Bench consists of 100 narrative environments based on movie plots, each with structured narrative specifications. Experiments were conducted on various state-of-the-art LLMs, simulating interactions between player and narrator agents.
- **Evaluation Results**: The experiments revealed a substantial long-horizon consistency gap. Even the best-performing model (GPT-5.2) achieved only a 42% survival rate after 20 turns. Fact conflict rates ranged from 40% to 68% across models. Only isolated runs satisfied all achievement commitments within the 100-turn limit.
- **Comparison**: The results demonstrate that current SOTA models, despite generating high-quality language, frequently produce logically conflicting content under adversarial interventions, highlighting severe deficiencies in long-horizon narrative control.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper identifies the overlooked issue of "Narrative Commitment Preservation" in LLM-based interactive storytelling and introduces the NCP-Bench benchmark. The study finds that current top-tier LLMs struggle to maintain logical consistency and narrative integrity in long-horizon interactions with adversarial user inputs, exhibiting high fact conflict rates and low survival rates. This provides a crucial evaluation standard and research direction for developing more robust and consistent narrative AI agents.
