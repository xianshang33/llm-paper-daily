## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Knowledge Distillation is widely used to transfer capabilities from large teacher models to smaller student models to reduce computational costs. However, as distillation scales, concerns arise about whether student models inherit not just functional knowledge but also behavioral patterns, specifically regarding "self-identity" representation.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current research primarily focuses on improving student model capability or reducing bias, overlooking the "accidentally learned" behaviors during distillation. This opacity raises issues regarding model ownership, safety, and accountability, with a lack of systematic frameworks to evaluate self-identity consistency in LLMs.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed STEMMA**: A multi-modal, multi-agent adversarial framework designed to evaluate self-identity consistency in Large Language Models (LLMs).
- **Challenge 1: How to effectively probe hidden inconsistencies in model self-identity?**
  - **Difficulty**: Direct queries about identity usually yield standard, consistent responses, masking potential behavioral biases or implicit features inherited from teacher models.
  - **Solution**: STEMMA employs role-specific agents collaborating to indirectly probe models using manually designed adversarial prompts. This approach reveals vulnerabilities in identity representation under pressure through multi-turn interactions and diverse probing angles.
- **Challenge 2: How to quantify and compare identity consistency across different models?**
  - **Difficulty**: There is a lack of unified metrics to measure a model's ability to maintain stable self-cognition under inducement attacks, with significant variance in responses across different architectures.
  - **Solution**: The paper defines Attack Success Rate (ASR) as the core metric. Testing across various open-source and closed-source models demonstrates STEMMA's effectiveness in capturing differences in identity consistency.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: STEMMA is a multi-agent framework featuring agents specialized in generating adversarial prompts and analyzing responses. The study utilizes a curated set of manual adversarial prompts designed to bypass conventional defenses and probe self-identity.
- **Evaluation Results**: Experiments on a range of models show that most are vulnerable to inconsistencies in self-representation. Attack Success Rates (ASR) varied significantly, ranging from 0% to as high as 82%, indicating that many models fail to maintain stable self-identity under adversarial conditions.
- **Comparison**: Unlike prior work focusing on general performance or safety, this study targets "identity consistency." Results highlight that even models performing well in standard conversations exhibit significant identity inconsistencies under STEMMA's adversarial probing, addressing a gap in understanding the side effects of distillation.

#### Summary
This paper highlights an overlooked risk in knowledge distillation: student models may inherit behavioral patterns and identity traits from teacher models, leading to output homogeneity and ambiguous accountability. To address this, the authors propose STEMMA, a framework using multi-agent collaboration and adversarial prompts to systematically assess LLM self-identity consistency. Experiments reveal significant vulnerabilities in current mainstream models, emphasizing the need for greater transparency and attention to identity consistency in model development and deployment.
