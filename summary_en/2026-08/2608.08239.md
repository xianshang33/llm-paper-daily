## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: LLM routers promise efficiency by matching each request to the cheapest adequate model and are increasingly applied per step within multi-step agents. However, current evaluation methods mimic single-turn router evaluations by replaying logged trajectories and substituting outputs from different models.
- **Problem with Existing Work**: This "replay evaluation" assumes an open-loop system where substituting a model at one step does not affect the rest of the trajectory. In reality, agent actions determine subsequent observations, forming a closed loop with the environment. Static evaluations ignore this dependency, leading to assessments that do not reflect real-world performance.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Branching-Rollout Protocol**: An open-source harness for counterfactual evaluation of per-step model switching in agents.
- **Challenge 1: Quantifying the magnitude and structure of the replay gap**. The difficulty lies in distinguishing true divergence caused by model switching from baseline noise due to sampling or environment replay, while managing the high cost of live branching. The paper addresses this by forking live SWE-bench agent trajectories at controlled points, rebuilding the environment, and comparing against same-model control forks to isolate noise, thereby precisely measuring divergence.
- **Challenge 2: Validating the effectiveness of replay evaluation in predicting outcomes**. The challenge is to demonstrate that static replay not only produces different trajectories but also leads to misjudgment of final results (e.g., code patch correctness). By comparing outcome flips in swap arms versus control arms, the paper shows that replay evaluators mispredict every success-relevant outcome call and generate patches with negligible similarity to reality, proving that replay benchmarks score the "wrong world."

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The authors developed an open-source harness supporting branching rollouts in the SWE-bench environment. The experiment involved ~900 rollouts across six paired runs. Trajectories were forked at specific steps, prefixes were re-executed in fresh containers, and continuation was performed with different models, paired with same-model controls to isolate noise.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - **Trajectory Divergence**: Swaps exceeded matched control floors by +0.25 to +0.66 normalized edit distance. 61-94% of post-fork actions were rewritten. 74-77% of early swaps diverged at the first post-fork action, compared to 6-35% for controls. Only 3% of replayed states remained valid.
    - **Outcome Flips**: All five observed outcome flips (e.g., upgrades rescuing unsolved instances) occurred in swap arms, with zero flips in 359 control forks.
    - **Replay Failure**: Log-stitching replay evaluators mispredicted every success-relevant outcome call, with predicted patches showing 0.00-0.11 similarity to reality.
    - **Noise Audit**: Temperature-0 "determinism" is configuration-dependent. FP8-served controls diverged in over 90% of forks, while AWQ-served ones remained nearly identical. Stronger models often exhausted steps without submitting under tight budgets.
- **Comparison**: Unlike traditional static replay evaluation, this method reveals that replay fails to capture dynamic changes in agent interactions, resulting in predictions that are severely detached from reality.

#### Summary
This paper highlights a fundamental flaw in current LLM agent routing evaluation: static replay evaluation incorrectly assumes an open-loop trajectory, ignoring the cascading effects of model switching on environmental states. Using a branching rollout method, the authors demonstrate that model switching causes rapid and significant trajectory divergence, and that replay evaluation completely fails to predict final outcomes. The study also reveals the significant impact of inference service configurations (e.g., FP8 vs. AWQ) on determinism. The authors release their harness and trajectories, calling for the community to adopt more realistic dynamic evaluation methods for agent routing research and deployment.
