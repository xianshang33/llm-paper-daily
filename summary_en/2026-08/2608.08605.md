## What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) built on Large Language Models (LLMs) are rapidly evolving, leveraging collaboration among planners, executors, and critics to solve complex tasks. However, the proliferation of new MAS frameworks has outpaced the development of robust evaluation methods.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Outcome-only benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K, HumanEval) discard the collaborative process, which is central to MAS value. Process-aware alternatives are flawed: native execution traces are tied to framework-specific schemas, preventing cross-method comparison, while LLM-as-Judge approaches require costly, model-dependent inference and suffer from inconsistency due to varying rubrics and judge models.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ForestBench**: A generalizable evaluation framework that maps heterogeneous native MAS traces into a unified space of collaboration graphs, enabling fair comparison across different methods using shared representations, reference sets, and metrics.
- **Challenge 1: Heterogeneity of Traces**. Different MAS frameworks use distinct role definitions and communication protocols, making direct comparison impossible. **Solution**: The paper introduces a method to transform native traces into unified "collaboration graphs," abstracting away framework-specific details to extract core interaction structures for standardized comparison.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of Diverse Success References**. Traditional evaluations often prescribe a single optimal path, ignoring the multiplicity of valid collaborative strategies. **Solution**: The concept of a "Reference Forest" is introduced, where multiple verified successful collaboration graphs are pre-computed for each query. Candidate traces are compared against this forest rather than a single gold standard, accommodating diverse successful processes.

## Implementation, Evaluation, and Comparison
- **Implementation**: The authors filtered 844 collaboration-necessary queries from seven public datasets. For each query, they pre-computed ten target-conditioned successful reference graphs to form the benchmark forests. Once built, ForestBench scores traces in milliseconds without further LLM inference.
- **Evaluation Results**: Six representative MAS frameworks were evaluated. Controlled studies on backbones, reference construction, and perturbations demonstrated the stability and scope of the evaluation.
- **Comparison**: Compared to LLM-as-Judge methods, ForestBench eliminates model-dependent bias and significantly reduces evaluation costs (milliseconds vs. seconds/minutes for inference). Unlike outcome-only benchmarks, it provides deep insights into collaboration efficiency and quality.

## Summary
ForestBench addresses the lack of unified standards and process neglect in MAS evaluation. By constructing unified collaboration graphs and diverse reference forests, it offers an efficient, stable, and inference-free evaluation mechanism. This provides a fair platform for comparing MAS frameworks and offers structured feedback for optimizing multi-agent interaction strategies.
