## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: As Large Language Model (LLM) agents improve in coding, research, and complex workflow execution, their potential as end-to-end business operators is growing. However, existing agent benchmarks rarely evaluate core business capabilities, such as capital allocation and long-term decision-making under market uncertainty.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current benchmarks typically focus on short-term, isolated tasks or environments with immediate feedback. They fail to simulate complex real-world business challenges, including noisy evidence, delayed and hard-to-attribute feedback, dynamic environmental changes, and persistent operational obligations. Consequently, they cannot effectively assess an agent's true intelligence in high-stakes, long-horizon business decisions.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Business Arena**: A controlled environment grounded in real Alibaba.com sourcing data and authoritative market conditions, designed to evaluate AI agents running a cross-border shop (buying from suppliers and selling to buyers) over a long horizon.
- **Challenge 1: Delayed Feedback and Attribution Difficulty**. Business consequences are often delayed and coupled, making it hard to judge individual decisions; final profit alone does not explain success or failure. **Solution**: The paper introduces skill-level metrics to reveal underlying strengths and weaknesses, and uses action-level attribution to trace realized gains and losses back to specific sourcing, pricing, and recovery decisions, enabling fine-grained performance analysis.
- **Challenge 2: Distinguishing Genuine Business Intelligence from Simulator Shortcuts**. Strong results might stem from ignoring constraints or exploiting simulator-specific loopholes rather than true business reasoning. **Solution**: The study compares agents against human-designed strategies to estimate available opportunity space and employs mechanism ablations to verify that results reflect genuine business intelligence, ruling out false highs from neglect or simulator exploits.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Data**: Business Arena is built on real Alibaba.com procurement data with market conditions calibrated from authoritative sources. It simulates the full cross-border trade lifecycle, including procurement, sales, inventory management, and customer service.
- **Evaluation Results**: Fifteen frontier models were evaluated, revealing a ninefold difference in mean final net worth. Even the best-performing model fell substantially behind human-designed strategies, indicating that business operation remains highly challenging for current LLM agents.
- **Comparison & Analysis**:
    - **Performance Gap**: The strongest models more than doubled their capital, a middle group earned modest returns, and the weakest finished with less than they started.
    - **Style Analysis**: Skill-level analysis identified distinct operating styles, such as margin-focused premium sellers, high-turnover wholesalers, and customer-service specialists.
    - **Attribution Analysis**: Action-level attribution pinpointed specific decisions in sourcing, pricing, and recovery that created or destroyed value.

## 4. Conclusion
Business Arena represents a first step toward a realistic and trustworthy testbed for evaluating end-to-end business agents. By combining real-world data, multi-dimensional evaluation metrics (profit, skill levels, action attribution), and rigorous ablation studies, the research not only quantifies the performance gap of current LLM agents in the business domain but also deeply analyzes their decision mechanisms and operating styles. The results show that while LLM agents have advanced in handling complex workflows, their ability to make business decisions amidst noisy evidence, delayed feedback, and dynamic environments still has significant room for improvement, currently failing to match the strategic level of human experts.
