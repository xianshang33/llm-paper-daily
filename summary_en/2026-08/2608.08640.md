## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents increasingly rely on reusable "skills" to extend capabilities beyond parametric knowledge. However, retrieving appropriate skills from large libraries is challenging because real-world user requests are often concise and underspecified, stating only goals while implicitly leaving out required capabilities and execution steps.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Existing benchmarks mostly cover queries with explicit procedural descriptions and domain-specific terminology, lacking coverage of concise, implicit real-world requests. Traditional retrievers rely on surface-level semantic matching, failing to infer underlying capability requirements, leading to inaccurate retrieval.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SkillReason Framework and SkillReason-Bench**: Introduced a large-scale cross-domain benchmark (3,729 queries, 61,228 skills) and a two-stage reasoning-enhanced skill retrieval framework.
- **Challenge 1: The Semantic Gap between Implicit Intent and Skills**. Difficulty: User requests are brief and lack specific execution steps, making it hard for traditional methods to capture deep capability needs. Solution: In Stage I, capability reasoning traces generated by a stronger teacher model provide explicit supervision via contrastive learning, retrieval distribution alignment, and language modeling, encouraging the retriever to internalize capability reasoning into its query representation.
- **Challenge 2: Balancing Reasoning Trajectory Adaptation and Efficiency**. Difficulty: Directly generating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) increases latency, and fixed reasoning paths may not suit the retriever's own characteristics. Solution: In Stage II, a retrieval-guided GRPO objective encourages the model to explore reasoning trajectories better suited to its capabilities and more effective for retrieval. At inference, it directly encodes the original query without autoregressive CoT generation, preserving efficient query-only retrieval.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Extensive experiments were conducted on the new SkillReason-Bench and existing benchmarks SkillRet and SRA-Bench.
- **Results**: SkillReason achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance across all three benchmarks. Results demonstrate that reasoning-enhanced training better bridges the semantic gap between high-level task goals and skill capabilities compared to traditional semantic matching methods.
- **Efficiency**: Although training involves complex reasoning supervision, the inference phase does not require generating explicit CoT, encoding raw queries directly to ensure low latency and high efficiency.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the challenge of skill retrieval for implicit user requests in LLM agents by proposing the SkillReason framework. By introducing the large-scale SkillReason-Bench and employing a two-stage training strategy (teacher-supervised reasoning internalization + GRPO-optimized reasoning trajectories), the method enables the retriever to "think like an expert" about potential capability needs while maintaining high inference efficiency. This approach significantly improves retrieval accuracy, providing crucial support for building more robust agent systems.
