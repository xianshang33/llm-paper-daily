## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents increasingly use external natural-language skills as reusable and editable guidance for complex tasks. However, effectiveness depends not only on skill quality but also on the policy's ability to translate guidance into actions.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current skill-based agents are typically trained with reinforcement learning (RL) objectives centered on sparse task-level rewards. This coarse supervision fails to capture subtle differences in how effectively the policy utilizes provided skills. Additionally, prior self-distillation methods rely on a single privileged context, which is insufficient for comprehensive evaluation.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed BCSD (Bidirectional Context Self-Distillation)**: A framework combining self-distillation with RL to train LLM agents to utilize external skills more effectively.
- **Challenge 1: Insufficient supervision from sparse rewards**. Traditional RL relies on final outcome rewards, making it hard to identify decisions genuinely informed by skills. **Solution**: BCSD generates fine-grained token-level signals from bidirectional contexts to rescale the RL advantage, providing richer supervision.
- **Challenge 2: Single-context perspective limits evaluation**. Prior self-distillation uses only one privileged context, ignoring different levels of skill abstraction. **Solution**: BCSD evaluates trajectories from two complementary views: an augmented view with high-level Meta-Skill guidance and a reduced view that prunes general guidance to highlight task-specific skills. These combined signals optimize the policy.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experiments**: Conducted on ALFWorld and WebShop benchmarks across various model scales.
- **Results**: BCSD achieved the strongest overall performance compared to baselines like OPSD + RL and GRPO. Ablation studies confirmed the complementary contributions of both augmented and reduced context views.
- **Reproducibility**: The authors promise to release code to ensure full reproducibility.

#### Summary
This paper addresses the issue of sparse supervision in skill utilization for LLM agents by proposing the BCSD framework. By leveraging bidirectional context self-distillation, BCSD extracts fine-grained signals from complementary augmented and reduced views, significantly enhancing the agent's ability to utilize external skills. Experimental results demonstrate state-of-the-art performance on multiple benchmarks, offering a new direction for improving skill efficiency in LLM agents.
