## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Predicting interventional "what if" questions requires mechanistic causal models rather than simple curve fitting. Learning such models necessitates experiments because passive data leaves mechanisms unidentified. Since experiments are expensive, the central challenge is data efficiency.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Traditional Bayesian experimental design selects informative interventions but is typically confined to fixed hypothesis spaces, lacking the open-ended hypothesis generation required for scientific discovery. Passive data is ambiguous and cannot distinguish between mechanisms that agree on observations but diverge under intervention. Furthermore, existing methods struggle when the true mechanism lies outside the current hypothesis class (M-open setting).

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Model Discovery Agent (MDA)**: A framework that couples a Large Language Model (LLM) as a proposer of candidate structures with standard Bayesian machinery—Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) for posteriors, Simulation-Based Inference (SBI) for intractable likelihoods, and Value-of-Information (VoI) for experiment design—to discover latent mechanistic world models from few interventions.
- **Challenge 1: Handling the M-open setting where the truth lies outside the current hypothesis class.**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional methods assume the truth is within the hypothesis space; they fail to discover new mechanisms when the current model is inadequate.
  - **Solution**: Introduces a predictive check that flags inadequacy, prompting the LLM proposer to expand the hypothesis space with new models, whose parameters are then identified via designed experiments.
- **Challenge 2: Achieving effective synergy between discovery and design for data efficiency.**
  - **Difficulty**: Isolated structure search or experiment design is often inefficient, struggling to converge to the correct mechanism with very few experiments.
  - **Solution**: Implements a "discovery and design reinforce" loop: the design step identifies the proposed mechanism, which improves predictions and enables further discoveries from remaining unexplained residuals.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Benchmarks**: Evaluated on three diverse benchmarks covering physics (FORCE BENCH), chemistry (CHEM BENCH), and biology (NEURON BENCH, a newly created partially observed single-neuron electrophysiology benchmark).
- **Results & Comparison**: MDA sets a new state-of-the-art (SOTA) in data-efficient model learning and reliable interventional forecasting. Compared to existing methods, MDA accurately identifies latent mechanisms with fewer experiments and demonstrates superior generalization and robustness across complex systems in physics, chemistry, and biology.

#### Summary
This paper presents the Model Discovery Agent (MDA), which combines the hypothesis generation capabilities of LLMs with the rigor of Bayesian experimental design to address the challenge of efficiently discovering mechanistic world models under costly experimental conditions. MDA is particularly suited for M-open scenarios, handling unknown mechanisms through predictive checks and hypothesis space expansion. Experimental results demonstrate that the method achieves state-of-the-art performance across multiple scientific domains, offering a powerful tool for automated scientific discovery.
