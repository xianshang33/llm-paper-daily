## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Advances in AI have accelerated robot autonomy research, but development has focused largely on execution rather than verifying the feasibility of actions proposed by planning models. LLM-driven robotics planning, while capable of zero-shot reasoning, introduces risks such as bias, ethical misalignment, inability to recall prior safety risks, and vulnerability to adversarial attacks.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current safety checks are often conducted independently and lack a comprehensive verification suite. Single-model judgments suffer from variance and bias, and existing autonomy systems lack a unified framework to minimize these risks and contain adversarial attacks targeting the autonomy ecosystem.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: An LLM-driven verification layer (Agentic Harnesses) acting as middleware between planning and execution to evaluate action permissibility.
- **Challenge 1: Unreliability and Bias of Single-Model Judgments**. The difficulty lies in individual LLMs prone to hallucinations or biases, leading to safety judgment errors. The paper addresses this by constructing an "LLM-as-a-Judge" ensemble that combines Chain-of-Thought reasoning across multiple models, synthesizing outputs to mimic a Mixture of Experts and Self-Consistency approach, thereby enhancing robustness and accuracy.
- **Challenge 2: Real-time Decision Making and Risk Control in Complex Threat Environments**. The challenge is making rapid, accurate decisions among accepting, rejecting, or escalating for human review while defending against adversarial attacks. The paper deploys this layer as middleware to gate plans before they reach low-level controls, achieving nearly 85% precision in classification and 97% containment of adversarial attacks, effectively balancing automation efficiency with safety.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation and Deployment**: The system is deployed as middleware between the server's planning module and the MCP server (which connects to the robot's low-level controls). It receives plans, processes them through the LLM ensemble, and outputs one of three decisions: approve, reject for reformulation, or escalate for human review.
- **Evaluation Results**: The system achieved nearly 85% precision across the "accept/escalate/reject" categories. In terms of security, it successfully contained 97% of adversarial attacks. Errors were mostly confined to the "escalate" boundary, with negligible errors between accepting and rejecting tasks.
- **Comparison**: Compared to previous independent and fragmented safety checks, this integrated verification layer provides more comprehensive risk coverage. It significantly outperforms traditional methods relying on single-model runs, particularly in reducing model variance and resisting adversarial attacks.

#### Summary
This paper proposes "Agentic Harnesses," an LLM-driven verification layer designed to address safety and ethical issues in robot autonomy planning actions. By employing a multi-model ensemble "judge" mechanism, the method effectively overcomes the limitations of single models, establishing a reliable safety gateway between planning and execution. Experimental results demonstrate that the system ensures high precision while significantly containing adversarial attacks, offering a new architectural paradigm for building safer and more trustworthy unsupervised robot autonomy systems.
