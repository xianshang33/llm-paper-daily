## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: The safety of Large Language Model (LLM) agents relies not only on model weights but also on the 'agent harness' that manages context, memory, tools, permissions, and runtime control. As agents are deployed for complex, long-horizon tasks, safety alignment at the harness level becomes critical.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Existing safety mechanisms treat the harness as a static deployment artifact, relying on human-designed rules or fixed guardrails that cannot automatically adapt to emerging risks from execution trajectories. Furthermore, coupled functions across harness components obscure safety responsibility attribution, making localized evolution difficult and hindering dynamic boundary refinement.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: Safety Harness Evolution (SHE), a framework that learns evolving safe boundaries from rollout trajectories.
- **Challenge 1: Coupled functions obscure harness refinement**. In current harnesses, unclear functional boundaries between components (e.g., context construction, memory retrieval) mean that refining one component can interfere with others. SHE addresses this by decomposing the harness into four artifacts with explicit safety responsibilities: System Prompt, Rule Bank, Safety Memory, and Tool Policy, defining clear boundaries for localized evolution.
- **Challenge 2: Trajectory feedback cannot directly contribute to harness evolution**. Distilling feasible evolution guidance from rich but complex execution trajectories is non-trivial. SHE introduces an attribution-guided evolution loop that converts trajectory failures into structured diagnoses, learns artifact-specific boundary refinements, and selects evolved harnesses through safety-utility validation.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Evaluation Setup**: Experiments were conducted on Agent-SafetyBench, comparing SHE against baselines like static SafeHarness.
- **Key Results**: SHE effectively enhances safety through harness evolution, achieving a 3.1x reduction in Attack Success Rate (ASR) compared to static SafeHarness, while also improving benign utility.
- **Generalization and Transfer**: The evolved harness generalizes well to unseen risks on the held-out AgentHarm benchmark and transfers across different agent models without requiring additional evolution.

## 4. Conclusion
SHE addresses the limitations of static and coupled LLM agent safety harnesses. By decoupling harness components and establishing an automated, trajectory-attribution-based evolution loop, SHE enables dynamic optimization of safety boundaries. Experiments demonstrate significant improvements in safety without compromising utility, along with strong generalization and cross-model transfer capabilities, offering a scalable solution for evolvable agent safety.
