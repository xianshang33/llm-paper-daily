## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large language model (LLM) agents are increasingly deployed as personal assistants, but existing evaluations mostly focus on short, self-contained requests in static environments (e.g., coding or office tasks). Everyday life assistance is different: tasks span weeks, the world changes continuously even when the agent is not prompted, and many constraints are implicit.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current benchmarks overlook three defining properties of life assistance: proactivity (agents must decide when to act or stay silent), dynamics (the environment state evolves spontaneously, e.g., weather, prices), and long-horizon consistency (maintaining a coherent plan over weeks). Existing benchmarks measure passive execution of clear instructions in static worlds, failing to assess an agent's ability to handle spontaneous changes and implicit constraints over time.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed VibeLifeBench**: A benchmark comprising 200 long-horizon tasks across ten everyday-life domains, set in a simulated world with 22 mock services where time advances independently.
- **Challenge 1: Proactive Perception and Decision-Making in Dynamic Environments**. The difficulty lies in "silent" changes that occur without notification; agents must actively re-inspect the world to detect changes and autonomously decide whether to act, ask, or remain silent. The paper addresses this by creating a self-evolving simulated environment where agents are evaluated on their ability to discover and react to unannounced changes.
- **Challenge 2: Long-Horizon Plan Consistency and Implicit Constraint Maintenance**. The challenge is maintaining self-consistency of an evolving plan over weeks while upholding initial implicit constraints (e.g., budget, health conditions). The paper solves this by using fine-grained, weighted checks that evaluate only the agent's actual outputs (final state, timeliness, and adherence to implicit constraints), measuring its persistence and coherence.

- **Comparison**: Unlike existing benchmarks focused on work/coding, static environments, and short tasks, VibeLifeBench specifically targets the long-cycle, dynamic, and implicit nature of daily life, revealing the inadequacy of current SOTA models in these realistic scenarios.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper highlights the gap in existing LLM agent evaluations for everyday life assistance and introduces VibeLifeBench to measure proactivity and persistence in dynamic, long-horizon environments. By simulating real-world spontaneous changes and implicit constraints, the benchmark reveals significant limitations in current frontier models, providing a clear evaluation standard and direction for developing more reliable life assistants.
