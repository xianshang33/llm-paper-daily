## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: In high-stakes domains like medical AI, numerical evidence from feature-attribution methods (e.g., SHAP) is often insufficient for audiences with varying expertise (patients, clinicians, data scientists). The same local explanation must be communicated in markedly different ways to different stakeholders.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Naive verbalization via Large Language Models (LLMs) or single-prompt role conditioning suffers from weak grounding, conflation of attribution with causal language, and outputs that are persuasive but unfaithful to the underlying model evidence. Existing narrative-XAI systems often treat generation as a monolithic step, lacking inspectable control over evidence selection and audience adaptation.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed XstrAI**, an audience-aware multi-agent framework that treats local explanations as fixed evidence and structures communication for specific target readers.
- **Challenge 1: Ensuring Faithfulness and Grounding**
  - **Difficulty**: LLMs are prone to hallucinations and misinterpreting statistical attribution as causality, leading to explanations that deviate from the original model evidence.
  - **Solution**: The framework encodes each prediction case as an immutable structured representation shared across all audiences. It employs specialized validation agents to check for grounding, attribution consistency, and communicative risk, triggering a bounded revision loop upon detecting inconsistencies.
- **Challenge 2: Achieving Audience-Specific Adaptation**
  - **Difficulty**: Diverse audiences (e.g., laypatients vs. expert clinicians) have vastly different needs, terminology understanding, and risk tolerances, which a single generation mode cannot adequately address.
  - **Solution**: Generation is factored into three specialized LLM agents responsible for audience-aware planning, linguistic realization, and validation, ensuring outputs are tailored to the specific audience while maintaining fixed underlying evidence.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on diabetes and stroke risk prediction tasks against 11 baselines, ranging from direct verbalization to re-implementations of state-of-the-art narrators.
- **Evaluation Methodology**: Combines an intra-narrative regime (measuring fidelity to SHAP evidence) and an extra-narrative regime (assessing audience appropriateness via reference corpora, multi-family LLM judges, and surveys with target readers).
- **Results & Comparison**:
  - Independent judges consistently assigned XstrAI's narratives to their intended audiences.
  - XstrAI was preferred over all baselines for Clinician and Patient audiences.
  - It showed competitive performance for the Data Scientist audience, where audience-conditioned single-prompt baselines led.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the challenge of conveying XAI explanations to diverse audiences by introducing XstrAI, a multi-agent framework. By fixing underlying evidence and separating planning, realization, and validation processes, it effectively mitigates faithfulness issues and enhances audience adaptation. Experiments demonstrate that the method significantly improves explanation quality and appropriateness for both non-expert and expert users while maintaining technical accuracy. The code is publicly available.
