## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Gist-based context compression, which summarizes older conversation history into compact representations, is a common approach in long-horizon language model agents. However, its impact on different types of memory retrieval is poorly understood.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: While recent structured memory systems (e.g., LightMem, Mem0) outperform simple truncation or sliding windows, their architectural complexity makes it difficult to isolate which components matter. Furthermore, generic summarization compresses history uniformly without regard for content type, leading to specific information loss that has not been systematically diagnosed.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Diagnostic Framework (SWC)**: Introduced Salience-Weighted Consolidation (SWC), a biologically-inspired framework motivated by sleep-based memory consolidation, to probe when gist compression helps and when it hurts.
- **Challenge 1: Systematic Loss of Temporal Information**. The difficulty lies in the fact that generic gist abstraction prompts preserve relational and event structures but discard dates and times, causing significant performance drops on temporal questions. The paper identifies this mechanism through preservation analysis and demonstrates that a single-sentence prompt modification can increase temporal expression preservation by approximately 20-fold without affecting other entities.
- **Challenge 2: Task-Type Sensitivity to Compression**. It is challenging to balance context length reduction with information retention. The study finds that while gist compression significantly outperforms truncation on multi-hop reasoning and single-hop factual questions, it fails on temporal questions. By explicitly protecting temporal anchors via prompt engineering, the paper recovers +0.314 judge accuracy on temporal questions, proving this is a correctable design omission rather than an inherent flaw.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on all 10 LoCoMo conversations, totaling 1,935 matched text-only questions (1,501 used in primary aggregate after excluding adversarial questions) at temperature 0.
- **Results**:
    - **Multi-hop & Single-hop**: Gist compression substantially outperforms truncation.
    - **Temporal Questions**: Unoptimized compression scores well below the full-context reference.
    - **Optimization Impact**: A simple prompt modification increased temporal expression preservation from 3.05% to 62.39% (a ~20x increase), while named entity and event preservation rates remained nearly unchanged (1.02x and 1.11x respectively). This fix recovered +0.314 accuracy on temporal questions.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Unlike complex systems like LightMem or Mem0, the SWC framework offers interpretability and highlights that explicit protection of temporal anchors is a critical design principle often missing in generic abstraction methods.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper uses the biologically-inspired SWC framework to dissect the trade-offs of gist-based context compression in long-horizon agents. It identifies the systematic neglect of temporal anchors as the primary cause of failure in generic compression. By demonstrating that a minimal prompt adjustment can significantly restore temporal reasoning capabilities without compromising other information, the work establishes a key design principle: temporal anchors must be explicitly protected during compression.
