## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Agent Skills are the de facto mechanism for extending LLM agents with reusable guidance, shaping task execution including planning, tool use, and validation.
- **Limitations of Prior Work**: Previous studies reported mixed results where some skills improved success rates while others increased costs or reduced performance. Existing evaluations lacked the ability to isolate and attribute specific failures or cost regressions to the loaded skills, making it difficult to understand the root causes of skill-induced harm.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Differential Analysis Framework and SkillTriage Tool**: This framework attributes failures by comparing a target skill-guided run against a no-skill or semantically matched reference run.
- **Challenge 1: How to accurately isolate and attribute functional failures caused by skills?**
  - **Difficulty**: Observing a failed run with a skill does not distinguish whether the failure was caused by the skill or other factors like environment mismatches.
  - **Solution**: By contrasting execution outcomes with and without skills, the study found that functional failures are rarely caused by irrelevant skills. Instead, seemingly relevant skills often cause the agent to incorrectly implement or omit required elements (Task-Implementation Faults accounted for 68.8% of functional failures).
- **Challenge 2: How to explain and quantify efficiency regressions (cost increases) induced by skills?**
  - **Difficulty**: Efficiency regressions cannot be explained solely by prompt length, and pinpointing the specific source of resource waste is challenging.
  - **Solution**: The differential analysis revealed that "Excessive Procedure" dominates efficiency regressions (62.6%), particularly excessive verification and heavy implementation pipelines. This indicates that skills often turn optional checklists into mandatory work, rather than just adding context overhead.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Datasets**: The framework was instantiated on SkillsBench and SWE-Skills-Bench. The authors built SkillTriage, a taxonomy-guided attribution tool that normalizes paired cases and generates triage reports.
- **Evaluation Results**: Identified 307 skill-induced failures, comprising 125 functional failures and 182 efficiency regressions.
  - **Functional Failures**: Primarily driven by Task-Implementation Faults (86 cases), wrong artifact locations (24 cases), and environment mismatches (13 cases).
  - **Efficiency Regressions**: Dominated by Excessive Procedure (114 cases), with excessive verification (67 cases) and heavy implementation pipelines (30 cases) being the largest contributors. Context overhead was a minor factor.
- **Comparison**: Unlike prior works that only reported average gains or overall pass rates, this paper provides fine-grained attribution, revealing how skills can impose mandatory workflows that degrade performance and increase costs.

## 4. Conclusion
This empirical study highlights the potential harms of "Skills" in LLM Agents. The proposed differential analysis framework and SkillTriage tool effectively isolate and attribute skill-induced failures. Key findings indicate that seemingly relevant skills often lead to implementation errors or omissions, while efficiency losses are primarily due to excessive verification and complex processes imposed by skills, not just prompt length. These insights offer directions for safer and more cost-aware skill reuse in future research and tooling.
