## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents increasingly rely on long-horizon reasoning for complex tasks involving planning, tool use, and memory. "Reflection" is a critical capability for assessing trajectory progress, identifying missing evidence, and deciding whether to continue, revise, or abandon current branches.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Outcome-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) struggles to train effective reflection because reflection is performed locally within a branch, while its utility is determined by the final global outcome. This "local-global mismatch" results in sparse, delayed, and local supervision signals for reflective decisions, making it difficult to learn optimal reflection policies.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed LoongReflect**: A training framework that formulates reflection as a memory-control policy, where agents operate on a reversible trajectory tree using explicit `<reflect>` and `<backtrack>` actions.
- **Challenge 1: Local-Global Perspective Mismatch**. The difficulty lies in the fact that local reflection cannot directly access its contribution to the final success, leading to sparse supervision. LoongReflect addresses this via a "Fast Channel" that distills globally informed reflective behavior from a privileged teacher, restricting supervision to reflection and backtracking tokens to provide dense, immediate guidance.
- **Challenge 2: Aligning Local Control with Global Success**. The challenge is ensuring local reflection/backtracking actions optimize overall trajectory success. LoongReflect employs a "Slow Channel" that optimizes complete trajectories using outcome-based GRPO, aligning local control decisions with final task success through a look-ahead, extragradient-style coordination mechanism.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The agent maintains working memory where reflection consolidates verified facts, missing evidence, and risks. Backtracking removes unreliable branches and preserves corrective lessons. Training combines two signals: global perspective distillation (fast) and outcome-based trajectory optimization (slow).
- **Evaluation Results**: Experiments were conducted on multi-hop Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and mathematical reasoning benchmarks.
- **Comparison**: LoongReflect demonstrates consistent improvements over baselines that use only outcome-based RL or self-distillation, proving its superiority in handling long-horizon tasks and leveraging reflection effectively.

#### Summary
LoongReflect addresses the core challenge of learning reflection in long-horizon agents: the mismatch between local reflective actions and global task outcomes. By modeling reflection as a memory-control policy and combining global perspective distillation with outcome-based trajectory optimization, the method significantly improves planning, error correction, and final success rates in complex reasoning tasks.
