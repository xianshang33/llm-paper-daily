## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) act as autonomous agents executing multi-step tasks (e.g., debugging, data analysis) using "Skills"—step-by-step natural language instructions. Current skill design relies on manual crafting or execution traces, lacking quantification of individual step contributions to overall performance.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Existing prompt optimization, compression, and workflow optimization methods operate at a holistic level. None assign value to individual steps within a fixed skill, leading to inefficient over-specification or risky blind pruning.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SkillShapley**: A step-level attribution framework for agent skills that models skill-step attribution as a Shapley value-based contribution estimation problem.
- **Challenge 1: Sharp performance cliffs caused by discretized benchmark rewards make traditional sampling inefficient**. Discrete rewards cause abrupt performance drops, making random sampling ineffective for capturing marginal contributions. SkillShapley addresses this by first identifying informative coalitional regions and then adaptively sampling new coalitions that yield reusable marginal evidence.
- **Challenge 2: Computational infeasibility due to combinatorial explosion of step interactions**. Exact Shapley calculation is intractable for many steps. Leveraging the empirical insight that step interactions are largely additive rather than synergistic, the method approximates exact Shapley rankings under a much smaller unique-configuration budget.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Setup**: Experiments conducted on skills from the widely adopted SkillsBench.
- **Baselines**: Compared against Individual scores (evaluating steps in isolation) and Leave-One-Out (LOO) scores.
- **Results**:
    - SkillShapley produced differentiated step rankings, whereas baselines resulted in many ties.
    - Removing top-ranked steps identified by SkillShapley caused clear performance degradation, validating its behavioral meaningfulness.
    - The framework effectively and efficiently identifies high- or low-value skill steps, offering key insights for skill creation.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the open problem of fine-grained step attribution in LLM agent skills. By proposing SkillShapley, a boundary-adaptive Shapley valuation framework, it overcomes sampling difficulties from discrete rewards and computational bottlenecks. Experiments demonstrate its ability to accurately quantify step contributions, guiding more efficient skill design and optimization.
