## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Models are evolving into autonomous agents capable of multi-turn, multi-step tool use. Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) is the dominant post-training paradigm, offering a performance ceiling determined by reward quality.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**:
    1. **RLVR Credit Assignment Issue**: Standard RLVR broadcasts a single trajectory-level reward to all tokens. In multi-turn sessions, independent turn outcomes share one signal, causing ill-posed credit assignment (inter-turn dilution), where successful and failed turns are indistinguishably reinforced.
    2. **Limitations of On-policy Distillation**: While providing dense token-level supervision, it is either bounded by the teacher's performance (teacher-bounded) or prone to gradient concentration collapse, where gradients overly focus on low-entropy format tokens rather than substantive reasoning.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed CrEST**: A hierarchical credit assignment framework that retains RL's verifier-bounded ceiling while incorporating dense token-level signals from a privileged self-teacher.
- **Challenge 1: Inter-turn Credit Dilution**
    - **Description & Difficulty**: In multi-turn sessions, each turn's outcome is independent, but traditional RL treats the session as a whole, making it difficult to attribute credit correctly, especially in long trajectories with high noise.
    - **Solution**: CrEST introduces "turn-segmented verified advantages," splitting reward signals by turn to calculate advantages precisely, thereby resolving inter-turn credit assignment and preventing erroneous reinforcement of failed turns.
- **Challenge 2: Intra-turn Gradient Collapse & Teacher Ceiling**
    - **Description & Difficulty**: Self-distillation provides fine-grained signals but often leads to gradient concentration on simple format tokens and is limited by the teacher model's quality.
    - **Solution**: CrEST employs "entropy-gated self-teacher modulation." Instead of using the teacher to determine update direction (imitation), it uses teacher signals only to modulate update magnitude. An entropy gate suppresses gradients from low-entropy tokens, focusing on high-entropy reasoning tokens, thus unlocking dense credit assignment without sacrificing the RL verifier-bounded ceiling.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Setup**: Evaluated on BFCL V3 and WildToolBench benchmarks across two model scales.
- **Baselines**: Compared against standard RL baselines (e.g., GRPO) and distillation baselines (e.g., OPSD).
- **Results**:
    - CrEST consistently outperforms both RL and distillation baselines.
    - Achieved the largest gains on long-trajectory tasks and strict session-level metrics.
    - Addressed the poor performance (<15% session accuracy) and sharp degradation with turn count observed in WildToolBench.
- **Key Insight**: Demonstrated that the teacher's role can be reduced from determining update directions to modulating update magnitudes.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the credit assignment challenge in training multi-turn LLM agents by proposing CrEST. The framework innovatively combines the verifier-bounded advantage of RL with the dense signal benefit of distillation. By solving inter-turn credit confusion via segmented advantages and intra-turn gradient issues via entropy-gated magnitude modulation, CrEST significantly enhances agent performance and robustness in complex tool-use scenarios, offering a new paradigm for efficient autonomous agent training.
