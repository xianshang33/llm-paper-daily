## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Authoritative scientific knowledge in geoscience is largely trapped in legacy monographs and historical literature, where unstructured text and complex layouts hinder computational access. As science shifts towards data-intensive discovery, systematically curating and reusing data from existing literature is critical.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current automated systems struggle with complex layouts (mixed text, tables, figures) in ultra-long documents and lack domain constraints and evidence tracing. This leads to low accuracy and reliability in extracted structured data, failing to meet FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) standards.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed HERMES**: A scalable multi-agent framework for extracting structured data from ultra-long geoscience documents.
- **Challenge 1: Unified understanding of ultra-long documents with complex multimodal layouts**. The difficulty lies in simultaneously processing text, tables, images, and captions across hundreds of pages without losing contextual links. HERMES addresses this by using a coordinating LLM to integrate parsed text, tables, figures, and captions into a unified document-level extraction process, leveraging multi-agent collaboration for global context awareness.
- **Challenge 2: Ensuring domain consistency and verifiability of extracted results**. General LLMs are prone to hallucinations and lack understanding of specific scientific rules. HERMES integrates domain constraints, validation rules, and evidence tracing mechanisms to ensure extracted data adheres to geoscience standards and can be traced back to original evidence, guaranteeing accuracy and trustworthiness.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: Applied to the 55-volume "Treatise on Invertebrate Paleontology," HERMES produced a structured database of 32,277 fossil taxonomic entities and 451,878 attributes, released online at https://treatise.geolex.org.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - **Accuracy**: Performance remained stable across fossil groups, with average F1 scores of approximately 0.90 for entities and 0.91 for attributes.
    - **Efficiency**: Achieved approximately sixfold improvement in per-volume efficiency compared to a fully manual baseline.
    - **Generalizability**: Demonstrated transferability across distinct geoscience domains (paleomagnetism and geochemistry) without additional model training.
- **Comparison**: Compared to traditional manual extraction or single-model approaches, HERMES significantly improves efficiency and accuracy by handling multimodal integration and enforcing domain rules, addressing context fragmentation and hallucinations in long-document processing.

## 4. Conclusion
HERMES provides a practical pathway to transform historical scientific literature into FAIR-oriented structured data. By leveraging multi-agent collaboration, domain constraint integration, and evidence tracing, it overcomes the challenges of processing ultra-long, multimodal scientific documents, offering sustainable infrastructure for data-intensive disciplines and large-scale knowledge integration.
