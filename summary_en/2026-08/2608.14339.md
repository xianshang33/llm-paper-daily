## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: As Large Language Models (LLMs) advance, LLM-based agents excel in multi-turn interactive tasks (e.g., online shopping). However, they lack "proactive exploration" capability—the ability to autonomously explore an environment to acquire information that improves future decision-making.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: The standard SFT-RL paradigm suffers from two fundamental bottlenecks:
    1. **Hindsight Bias in SFT Data**: Expert trajectories strip away essential exploratory steps, conditioning agents for reactive execution rather than proactive exploration.
    2. **Exploration Collapse during RL**: SFT-trained agents lack inherent exploratory tendencies, rarely sampling such behaviors during RL rollouts. Without guidance, RL fails to discover and reinforce the long-term benefits of exploration.

- **Challenge 1: How to overcome hindsight bias in SFT to teach exploration?**
    - **Difficulty**: Standard expert demonstrations are outcome-oriented and hide the trial-and-error process, preventing agents from learning when and how to explore.
    - **Solution**: Introduces "Exploratory Data Construction." It synthesizes exploration-rich trajectories directly from a powerful LLM expert using tree-structured context modeling, explicitly distilling diverse exploratory paths to mitigate the bias of standard demonstrations.
- **Challenge 2: How to distinguish "productive exploration" from "redundant wandering" in RL?**
    - **Difficulty**: In sparse reward settings, it is difficult for agents to determine which seemingly aimless exploration actions lead to long-term gains, leading to ineffective random walking or premature convergence.
    - **Solution**: Introduces "RL Optimization with Contrastive Signal Guidance." It leverages contrastive trajectory pairs to guide the agent in distinguishing productive exploration from redundant wandering, effectively reinforcing proactive exploration strategies during RL.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on multi-turn interactive task benchmarks (e.g., WebShop).
- **Baselines**: Compared against standard SFT, conventional RL fine-tuning, and other state-of-the-art Agent methods.
- **Results**: Extensive experiments demonstrate that SAFARI significantly outperforms baselines in tasks requiring long-horizon strategies and active information acquisition. Agents equipped with SAFARI execute strategic backtracking and pagination to find better solutions (e.g., lower-priced products), yielding higher overall rewards. The results also provide insights into the characteristics of proactive exploration and prove the method's effectiveness in mitigating exploration collapse.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the lack of proactive exploration in LLM agents by identifying defects in data bias and training mechanisms within the existing SFT-RL paradigm. By proposing the SAFARI framework, which combines SFT with synthesized exploration-rich data and RL guided by contrastive signals, it successfully instills proactive exploration capabilities. This approach not only improves performance in long-range decision-making tasks but also offers new perspectives on understanding and developing exploration behaviors in embodied agents.
