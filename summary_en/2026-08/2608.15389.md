## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Progress in LLM-based Text-to-SQL is reported across heterogeneous benchmarks (Spider, BIRD, Spider 2.0), backbones, and inference protocols, making cross-system comparisons fragile and inconsistent.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current surveys organize literature by training paradigms (ICL, fine-tuning, etc.), which fails to isolate inference-time structures that increasingly dominate benchmark performance. Re-running all systems for unified evaluation is prohibitively expensive and often infeasible due to changing closed APIs.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed an Autonomy-Based Taxonomy and Aggregated Leaderboard**: Reframes the field by treating the literature as the benchmark. It organizes reported metrics along an "inference-autonomy" axis (Constrained, In-context, Iterative, Agentic, Reasoning-internalized) with traceable provenance for every data point.
- **Challenge 1: Fair comparison amidst heterogeneous inference protocols**. Different systems externalize structured reasoning to varying degrees, making direct accuracy comparisons misleading regarding robustness and cost. **Solution**: Defined an "autonomy axis" to classify systems by how much structured reasoning is externalized between the question and final SQL, enabling a unified assessment of efficiency and effectiveness.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of controlled empirical anchors to validate the taxonomy**. Pure aggregation lacks deep insight into specific variables like Chain-of-Thought (CoT). **Solution**: Conducted a focused case study on Spider, comparing 8B open-source backbones with and without CoT against few-shot baselines, revealing that CoT gains concentrate on hard queries and autonomy trades off with cost.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Released a Python harness that mirrors the autonomy axis. New methods can be added via adapter and configuration files without re-running prior systems, ensuring scalability and maintainability.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    1. **Uneven Transfer**: Performance gains on Spider do not transfer uniformly to BIRD or Spider 2.0.
    2. **Cost of Autonomy**: Higher autonomy (e.g., Agentic) buys robustness but incurs significant costs in tokens, latency, and orchestration.
    3. **Position of Reasoning Internalization**: Reasoning internalization occupies a distinct point on the autonomy-cost frontier, sitting between answer-only decoding and externally orchestrated agents.
    4. **CoT Impact**: CoT supervision yields concentrated gains on Hard and Extra-Hard queries rather than uniform improvements across all difficulties.
- **Comparison**: Unlike traditional surveys based on training methods, this work provides a transparent, citation-linked leaderboard and empirically compares models like Qwen3-8B, LLaMA3.1-8B, DeepSeek V3, and GLM-4 under different autonomy settings.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the difficulty of comparing LLM Text-to-SQL systems by proposing an inference-autonomy taxonomy. Through an aggregated leaderboard and empirical case studies, it reveals the complex trade-offs between autonomy, robustness, and cost, identifying reasoning internalization as a viable middle ground. The released Python harness provides infrastructure for standardized future comparisons.
