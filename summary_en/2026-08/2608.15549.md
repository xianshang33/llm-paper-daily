## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Social robots (e.g., Misty) are increasingly used in assistance, education, and social interaction. However, enabling non-expert users to control these robots via natural language remains challenging. Existing platforms expose APIs but require developers to manually compose skills, configure parameters, bind sensor events, and manage runtime states.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current interfaces lack the ability to interpret diverse, open-ended user instructions in real-time. They fail to simultaneously handle "reactive physical interactions" (sensor-triggered actions) and "stateful social interactions" (context-dependent tasks), forcing non-experts to rely on pre-defined, hard-coded interaction patterns that are difficult to modify at runtime.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed MistyPilot**: A multi-agent LLM framework that interprets high-level natural language instructions and orchestrates corresponding skills on the Misty social robot.
- **Challenge 1: How to distinguish and handle reactive physical interactions versus stateful social interactions?**
  - **Difficulty**: Physical interactions require low-latency sensor-skill binding and direct invocation, while social interactions need dialogue context and task state management. A single model struggles to balance these disparate requirements.
  - **Solution**: Introduces a Task Router that dispatches instructions to two specialized agents: a Physically Interactive Agent for sensor-triggered control and direct skill invocation, and a Social Interaction Agent for dialogue-oriented state management and context-dependent multimodal response generation.
- **Challenge 2: How to improve system efficiency while maintaining response quality?**
  - **Difficulty**: Frequently generating full multimodal responses for social interactions leads to high latency and computational costs, especially for repetitive or similar requests.
  - **Solution**: The Social Interaction Agent employs a result reuse mechanism, leveraging previously generated results when applicable and only invoking full generation when necessary, thereby significantly enhancing efficiency.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: MistyPilot was deployed and tested on a physical Misty robot. Evaluation included five component-level suites (covering routing, sensor-skill binding, task state parsing, result reuse, and skill extension) and a preliminary user study with 12 participants.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - **Accuracy**: Achieved high accuracy in routing, sensor-skill binding, task state parsing, and result reuse.
  - **Scalability**: Supports skill extension up to 100 skills.
  - **Stability**: Demonstrated lower variance compared to an identical single-agent baseline, indicating greater stability with the multi-agent architecture.
  - **User Experience**: Participants reported positive perceptions of usability and interaction quality.
- **Comparison**: Compared to traditional methods requiring manual coding and pre-defined patterns, as well as single-agent LLM baselines with higher performance variance, MistyPilot shows significant improvements in automation, stability, and user friendliness.

## 4. Conclusion
MistyPilot successfully addresses key challenges in natural language programming for social robots through its multi-agent LLM architecture, enabling seamless coordination between reactive physical behaviors and stateful social behaviors. Its unique task routing and result reuse mechanisms not only improve execution accuracy and efficiency but also lower the barrier for non-expert users. Experiments demonstrate that the framework achieves high accuracy and positive user experience in real-world robot deployments, offering a new technical pathway for the widespread adoption of social robots.
