## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) can generate fluent text, but open-ended storytelling in evolving world simulations and AI-native games requires more than local fluency. Models must preserve facts, relationships, causal dependencies, and character states as the world changes.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current narrative evaluations focus on completed stories (e.g., overall quality or consistency bugs), failing to reveal when canon breaks first occur, why development stalls, or how different agent architectures trade off between capabilities. Additionally, differences in interactive system components (memory, planning) are often obscured by varying world settings and player policies, making it difficult to isolate specific capabilities.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed WSE-bench**: A process benchmark for dynamic LLM storytelling that extends evaluation from final stories to the creation process, separately assessing sustained generation, canonical coherence, and meaningful development.
- **Challenge 1: How to quantify multi-dimensional capabilities in open-ended narrative?**
  - **Difficulty**: Open-ended narratives lack a final draft, and traditional metrics struggle to distinguish between "generation length," "logical consistency," and "plot richness," which may compete with each other.
  - **Solution**: WSE-bench defines three independent metrics: Generation Coverage (proportion of planned steps produced), Consistency (tracking canon breaks), and Richness (measuring meaningful branching development), thereby clearly separating these capabilities.
- **Challenge 2: How to reveal complex trade-offs between architectures and capabilities?**
  - **Difficulty**: Simple linear weighting cannot capture non-linear relationships between capabilities, and increasing model scale does not necessarily improve all abilities.
  - **Solution**: Empirical analysis reveals a non-concave Pareto frontier between consistency and richness, showing multiple non-dominated intermediate configurations. It demonstrates that adding structure enriches trajectories but does not uniformly improve coherence, and model scale primarily boosts sustained generation rather than the other two capabilities.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Setup**: WSE-bench uses a fixed player policy to select attempted actions, tests different agent architectures, and employs independent evaluators to identify contradictions and meaningful developments. Sharing episode setups and selection schedules across conditions makes architectural differences easier to interpret.
- **Key Results**:
  1. **Capability Independence**: Sustained generation, canonical coherence, and meaningful development are distinct and sometimes competing capacities.
  2. **Non-Concave Pareto Frontier**: There is no smooth trade-off between consistency and richness in frontier models; simple positive linear weighting cannot select optimal configurations.
  3. **Impact of Structure**: Adding structured information can enrich narrative trajectories but does not uniformly improve coherence and may even shorten them.
  4. **Limitations of Model Scale**: Scaling up models primarily improves sustained generation, without reliable gains in canonical coherence or meaningful development.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Unlike benchmarks focusing only on final story quality (e.g., Yang and Jin, 2025) or just checking for consistency bugs (e.g., Li et al., 2026a), WSE-bench reveals dynamic failure modes during evolution, addressing the gap where compounding errors in long-horizon tasks are hidden by short-term evaluations.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper introduces WSE-bench, a process benchmark for LLM storytelling in open-world simulations. By shifting evaluation focus from final products to the creation process, the study reveals that sustained generation, canonical coherence, and meaningful development are distinct and often competing dimensions. The findings show that model scale mainly benefits generation length, with limited help for consistency and richness, and that a complex non-concave Pareto trade-off exists between the latter two. This benchmark provides new perspectives and tools for understanding and optimizing agent architectures in AI-native games and dynamic narrative systems.
