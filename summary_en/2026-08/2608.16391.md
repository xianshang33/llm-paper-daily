## 1. What the Paper Does

**Background**
As Large Language Models (LLMs) become widespread, third-party providers deploying open-weight models via APIs have become a key part of the ecosystem. However, users cannot directly verify if these hosted APIs truly run the claimed model versions, quantization configurations, or decoding strategies, creating a "verification gap."

**Why Existing Work Falls Short**
Existing audit methods often rely on log-probabilities (logprobs) returned by the target API, which many commercial or third-party APIs do not provide or may manipulate. Furthermore, traditional black-box testing struggles to distinguish output variations caused by stochastic sampling from systematic biases caused by model routing substitutions (e.g., using cheaper models or older checkpoints), lacking an effective means to quantify behavioral deviation without internal probability information.

## Core Contributions

**Proposed Ventor-QTest**
A composite black-box audit framework that formalizes hosted model routing as a stochastic process, verifying behavioral consistency without requiring probability information from the target API.

**Challenge 1: How to quantify distributional deviation without logprobs?**
*   **Difficulty**: Traditional KL divergence calculation requires full probability distributions, but black-box APIs only return text, and sampling randomness makes accurate distribution reconstruction difficult.
*   **Solution**: Proposes a "repeated-request component" that sends frozen constrained contexts multiple times, reconstructs categorical output distributions from returned text counts, and reports "Average Fidelity Loss" (AFL), a null-bias-corrected, within-window mean coarsened-KL statistic.

**Challenge 2: How to detect extreme anomalous behavior in long-sequence tasks?**
*   **Difficulty**: Long-horizon agentic tasks are sensitive to extreme fidelity losses, but average metrics may mask tail risks, and establishing reference baselines for long-sequence generation is challenging.
*   **Solution**: Proposes a "long-sequence component" that reports "Extreme Fidelity Loss" (EFL) through independent runs, utilizing the empirical upper tail of a run-level reference-centered-surprisal statistic to capture extreme deviations and reveal performance degradation under specific routings.

## Implementation and Evaluation

**Implementation and Deployment**
Ventor-QTest is open-sourced as part of Tencent's AI Infra Guard. The tool operates entirely on black-box queries without relying on internal model states.

**Evaluation Results**
1.  **AFL Validity**: Across three logprob-capable route conditions, AFL shows strong linear descriptive agreement with a logprob-derived coarsened-KL comparator, proving its proxy effectiveness in scenarios without logprobs.
2.  **EFL Variability Detection**: In seven route snapshots, 20-run sequence probes revealed route-specific EFL variations, indicating significant differences in extreme deviations across different deployment configurations.
3.  **Correlation with Downstream Performance**:
    *   AFL and EFL showed little detectable route-level association with GPQA-Diamond accuracy.
    *   In contrast, pronounced EFL coincided with a decline in Terminal-Bench pass rates as task exposure increased. This suggests that correctness in long-horizon tasks is more sensitive to extreme fidelity loss.

**Comparison**
Compared to traditional methods focusing only on average accuracy or relying on logprobs, Ventor-QTest jointly reports AFL and EFL. It is particularly effective for auditing long-horizon agentic tasks, revealing tail risks that average metrics fail to capture.

#### Summary

This paper addresses the verification challenge of third-party hosted LLM APIs by proposing Ventor-QTest, a black-box audit framework. By introducing Average Fidelity Loss (AFL) and Extreme Fidelity Loss (EFL), the method effectively quantifies the deviation between deployment behavior and trusted references without relying on target API probability information. Experiments demonstrate that EFL is closely correlated with performance degradation in long-horizon agentic tasks, highlighting the importance of jointly monitoring average and extreme deviations when auditing complex application scenarios.
