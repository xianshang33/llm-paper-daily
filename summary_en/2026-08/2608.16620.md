## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Historically, creating LLMs excelling in specific domains required full training pipelines. Recent trends suggest that post-training existing high-performing LLMs with high-quality data and carefully chosen objectives is sufficient.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Standard fine-tuning often leads to catastrophic forgetting of general capabilities when injecting new skills (like tool use). Additionally, training data for complex agentic tasks is scarce and noisy, making it difficult to achieve stable improvements through large-scale, uncontrolled training.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Palmyra x6**: An agentic model post-trained via Anchored Supervised Fine-Tuning (ASFT) on a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) base model, using a minimal corpus of verified synthetic trajectories.
- **Challenge 1: How to inject complex agentic skills without degrading base model capabilities?**
  - **Difficulty**: Standard SFT can disrupt the knowledge distribution of the base model, reducing general performance.
  - **Solution**: Introduced a KL-divergence anchor to constrain the model's distribution close to the frozen base model during fine-tuning. This allowed significant enhancement of tool-use capabilities using only 626 trajectories and a single epoch, while preserving the base model's original strengths.
- **Challenge 2: How to achieve optimal convergence and performance with extremely limited data?**
  - **Difficulty**: Small-sample training is highly sensitive to optimizer stability and learning rates; conventional optimizers may fail to fully exploit model potential or cause oscillation.
  - **Solution**: Employed a Muon + Adam hybrid optimizer, where Muon optimizes 2D weight matrices. Combined with a low learning rate and a conservative training recipe, this approach ensured efficient convergence and performance breakthroughs on the tiny dataset.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation Details**: Palmyra x6 is built on the GLM-5.2 MoE architecture (744B total parameters, ~40B active). It was trained on just 626 verified synthetic tool-use trajectories for a single epoch using a low learning rate and an ASFT objective (KL weight K=0.1).
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - Achieved a score of 0.785 on BFCL Core, ranking first.
  - Secured the highest mean score across six benchmarks among the cohort.
  - Showed substantial improvements over the previous default Writer Agent model, with notable gains in MCP-Atlas (+0.320), FinanceBench (+0.305), and IFBench (+0.304).
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Palmyra x6 performs competitively or leads against frontier models on public benchmarks. More importantly, it significantly outperforms the previous model on internal datasets simulating enterprise use cases, demonstrating its production readiness. It also shows competitive or leading results in bias and safety evaluations.

## 4. Conclusion
The Palmyra x6 technical report demonstrates a "less is more" post-training paradigm. By leveraging carefully designed Anchored Supervised Fine-Tuning (ASFT) and advanced optimization (Muon), the authors significantly enhanced the model's performance in enterprise agentic tasks (such as tool use and multi-step planning) using only a minimal amount of synthetic data (626 trajectories) and single-epoch training. This approach not only achieved state-of-the-art results on public benchmarks but also proved its efficiency and practicality in real-world enterprise scenarios, offering valuable insights for customizing high-performance models under resource constraints.
