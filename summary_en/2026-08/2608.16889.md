## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Long-horizon robot manipulation chains multiple contact-rich skills into multi-stage tasks. While Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models excel at individual skills, they fail in long chains due to compounding errors and implicit constraints where one subtask's execution silently restricts the next.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: The current "frozen VLA + LLM agent" recipe fails in two ways: (1) Whole-task exploration at test time has multiplicative cost ($T^K$) and failures cannot be attributed to specific stages; (2) It lacks representation of transitions, as VLA primitives have exit but no entry conditions, leading to states that are successful in isolation but unusable by successors.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed BATON**: A framework for long-horizon robot manipulation via Agentic Subtask Exploration and Transition-aware Memory.
- **Challenge 1: High Cost and Ambiguity of Whole-Task Exploration**. Exploring the entire task is exponentially expensive and makes failure attribution difficult. **Solution**: BATON makes the subtask the unit of exploration. Each subtask is explored in a cheap short-horizon regime and stored in memory. Long-horizon trajectories are composed from these solutions, making cost additive ($T \\cdot K$) and attributing every failure to a single stage.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of Transition Representation**. Missing entry conditions cause incompatibility between subtasks. **Solution**: BATON equips exploration with transition-aware memory. A verifier agent governs invocation transitions (calling VLA only when the wrist view confirms readiness). Across subtasks, handoff transitions restore entry states disturbed by predecessors, and lookahead transitions select strategies whose outcomes can be inherited by successors.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: No parameters are updated; the system relies on LLM planning, analytic primitives, and VLA calls.
- **Benchmark**: RoboMemArena for long-horizon manipulation.
- **Results**: BATON improves task success rate by 11.6% and cumulative success rate by 14.9% over the current State-of-the-Art (SoTA).

#### Summary
BATON addresses the compounding errors and state incompatibilities in long-horizon robot manipulation by decomposing tasks into independently explored subunits and managing inter-subtask transitions through a specialized memory mechanism. By introducing invocation, handoff, and lookahead transitions, it significantly reduces exploration costs and boosts success rates without requiring model fine-tuning, demonstrating the efficacy of modular exploration and state management in complex robotic tasks.
