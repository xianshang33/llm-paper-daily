## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: As Large Language Models (LLMs) and agents evolve from information retrieval to executing complex real-world tasks, evaluating their ability to reliably produce professionally usable deliverables in end-to-end (E2E) workflows has become a central challenge.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current benchmarks largely rely on researcher-defined tasks that may not reflect authentic user needs. Furthermore, their evaluation protocols are often too coarse to capture the nuanced functional, structural, and domain-specific requirements of real-world professional deliverables, limiting their ability to assess true practical capability.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed StartupBench**: An E2E agent benchmark grounded in market-validated AI startup products, covering six professional domains and focusing on real-world, deliverable-oriented tasks.
- **Challenge 1: Disconnect between task definition and real-market demand**. The difficulty lies in researcher biases leading to unrealistic tasks. The paper addresses this by systematically studying adopted AI startup products and their workflows to identify tasks with established practical demand, translating them into concrete deliverable-oriented evaluations.
- **Challenge 2: Insufficient evaluation granularity for professional standards**. Real-world outputs have complex requirements that holistic scores miss. The paper introduces fine-grained rubrics that capture specific functional, formatting, and domain constraints across multiple output formats (e.g., DOCX, XLSX, PPTX), enabling precise assessment of quality.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: StartupBench spans six domains (Healthcare, Finance, Legal, STEM/CS, Education/Social Science). Tasks involve long-horizon planning, search, interaction, and reasoning, resulting in multi-format deliverables. Models are evaluated under a unified agent harness.
- **Evaluation Results**: Even the strongest models successfully complete only approximately 30% of the tasks. While substantial partial progress is observed, full completion remains difficult.
- **Failure Analysis**: Major sources of failure include complex instruction following and lack of domain-specific expertise, indicating that current general-purpose agents struggle with the rigor of market-validated workflows.
- **Comparison**: Unlike previous benchmarks with preset tasks or coarse metrics, StartupBench reveals a significant gap in current models' ability to handle real-world E2E completions, providing a stricter empirical measure of progress.

#### Summary
StartupBench addresses the realism and granularity gaps in existing AI benchmarks by leveraging market-validated startup workflows. The results demonstrate that while LLMs have advanced, current general-purpose agents are not yet reliable for complex, market-driven real-world tasks, particularly those requiring deep domain knowledge and strict adherence to instructions. This benchmark serves as a critical tool for measuring progress toward truly useful autonomous agents.
