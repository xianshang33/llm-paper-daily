## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Combining Large Language Models (LLMs) with Reinforcement Learning (RL) is an active research area. Pure RL struggles with sparse rewards and long-horizon exploration, while pure LLM planners can decompose tasks but lack low-level control capabilities and calibrated uncertainty.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current hybrid architectures (e.g., SayCan, Eureka) often implicitly use LLM-generated signals as rewards without theoretical guarantees. Inaccurate LLM scores can mislead the RL agent into converging to suboptimal policies. Existing methods fail to theoretically prove that policy optimality is preserved when LLM scores are erroneous.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a**: A framework for hybrid LLM-planner and RL-controller agents based on Goal-Augmented Markov Decision Processes (GA-MDP), proving policy-invariant reward shaping from LLM feedback.
- **Challenge 1**: LLM state progress scores can be severely inaccurate; using them directly as rewards can alter the set of optimal policies, leading to incorrect agent behavior. **Solution**: The paper formalizes LLM per-state progress scores as bounded potential functions. Based on Ng et al. (1999), the resulting reward-shaping term does not alter the set of optimal policies of the augmented MDP, guaranteeing policy invariance even if LLM scores are completely wrong.
- **Challenge 2**: Theoretical soundness (fixed-point properties) is often conflated with empirical convergence rates, lacking independent auditability. **Solution**: The paper strictly distinguishes between soundness and empirical convergence. By formalizing the GA-MDP architecture, it provides theoretical guarantees for the fixed point independently of empirical convergence, making the hybrid training process auditable.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The authors released a reference implementation and served Qwen-2.5:14b locally. Evaluation included isolating the planner on 20 MiniGrid tasks (100% parse rate, 54.8% ground-truth coverage) and a small-scale pipeline validation on MiniGrid-DoorKey-6x6.
- **Evaluation Results**: Numerical verification was conducted on a small 3-state MDP under four potential configurations, including an adversarial one scaled to 20 times the base reward magnitude. Results confirmed that the set of optimal policies remained unchanged regardless of LLM score deviations.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Compared to general "LLM-as-reward" approaches like Text2Reward and Eureka, this work offers stronger theoretical guarantees by ensuring policy invariance rather than just heuristic performance improvements. It also diagnosed an integration failure (vocabulary mismatch between Done-oracle and LLM plans), consistent with theoretical predictions.

#### Summary
This paper primarily addresses the theoretical soundness of combining LLMs with RL, rather than just efficiency gains. By transforming LLM feedback into bounded potential functions for reward shaping, the paper theoretically proves that the method preserves optimal policy invariance even when LLM scores are inaccurate. This contribution provides a solid theoretical foundation for hybrid agent systems, making them more reliable and auditable than existing black-box LLM reward methods. Future work will focus on large-scale empirical convergence studies.
