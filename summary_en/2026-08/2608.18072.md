## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Radiology reports often lack consistent structure, making it difficult for clinicians to locate critical information and hindering secondary uses like research and quality monitoring. Additionally, internal inconsistencies (e.g., contradictions between 'Findings' and 'Impression', gender-anatomy conflicts) can lead to misdiagnosis or delayed treatment.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Structured reporting templates suffer from low adoption due to workflow burden and inflexibility. Existing NLP and LLM approaches typically focus on extracting specific labels or summarizing content, often failing to preserve the full semantic detail of the original report and rarely addressing both structuring and internal consistency quality assurance (QA) simultaneously.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a locally deployed multi-agent AI system**: This system integrates radiology report structuring and QA into a single workflow, using regex rules and local Large Language Models (LLMs) to restructure reports at the sentence level into standardized anatomical sections while detecting internal contradictions.
- **Challenge 1: How to achieve standardized structure while preserving original report integrity?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional extraction methods lose context, while generative methods may hallucinate.
  - **Solution**: The system employs a hybrid strategy, combining regex for initial segmentation with local LLMs to map sentences to predefined anatomical sections, ensuring no original content is omitted and no fabricated information is introduced.
- **Challenge 2: How to automatically detect subtle logical errors and inconsistencies?**
  - **Difficulty**: Inconsistencies may span different sections or involve medical common sense (e.g., male patients with uterus-related descriptions), requiring deep semantic understanding.
  - **Solution**: QA agents within the multi-agent system specifically detect section mismatches, gender-anatomy conflicts, and undocumented critical findings, flagging potential errors through an independent evaluation mechanism.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The system was deployed locally to ensure data privacy, processing 638 CT reports (chest, abdomen, pelvis) from 15 radiologists between 2023 and 2024. The pipeline includes sentence-level structuring via Regex and local LLMs, followed by multi-agent QA checks.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - **Structuring Performance**: Successfully restructured 22,270 sentences from all reports into the standard format.
  - **QA Performance**: Flagged 90 reports (14.1%), primarily for section mismatches.
  - **Expert Review**: Two board-certified radiologists independently evaluated a subset of 45 reports. They agreed that 69% were correctly restructured, 4% were incorrect, and disagreed on 27%. Crucially, both agreed that no clinically important information was omitted and no content was fabricated. 84% of reports were rated as "excellent" or "good" for QA.
- **Comparison**: Unlike existing NLP/LLM works that only extract labels or summarize, this approach preserves full text integrity and provides proactive quality control, addressing the practical limitations of previous methods in clinical workflows.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper presents and validates a locally deployed multi-agent AI system that effectively structures unstructured radiology reports into standardized anatomical formats while performing rigorous quality assurance. The system achieves high accuracy in structuring without compromising original information integrity and effectively identifies internal logical contradictions. Expert evaluation confirms its clinical safety (no omissions, no hallucinations) and effectiveness, suggesting that such systems can significantly support the standardization and quality improvement of radiology reporting.
