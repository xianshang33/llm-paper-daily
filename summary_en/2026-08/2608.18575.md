## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM)-based Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) often exhibit complex failure modes, leading to incorrect outcomes. This motivates the task of Agent Failure Attribution (AFA): identifying faulty agents and their error types within a failed multi-agent trajectory.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current approaches predominantly rely on LLMs via direct prompting, fine-tuning on synthetic data, or complex agentic pipelines. These methods suffer from substantial computational overhead due to long-context processing and expensive post-training. Furthermore, empirical evidence shows that even state-of-the-art LLMs achieve limited accuracy, indicating that scaling model size alone is insufficient for effective attribution.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed AFANet**: A lightweight graph-based framework that models interaction trajectories using step-level semantic signals and agent-level relationships, replacing heavy LLM reasoning.
- **Challenge 1: High Computational Cost and Latency**. Existing LLM methods incur high costs due to long-context inference and training. AFANet addresses this by utilizing lightweight Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) to model trajectory structures, achieving near-zero inference cost and significantly fewer parameters while maintaining high performance.
- **Challenge 2: Difficulty in Accurate Attribution amidst Complex Dependencies**. Error propagation in MAS is ambiguous and involves long-range dependencies, which LLMs struggle to pinpoint. AFANet explicitly captures agent interactions and semantic signals through graph structures, effectively tracing error propagation paths and matching or outperforming fine-tuned LLM baselines in accuracy.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: AFANet is a lightweight framework that avoids complex prompt engineering or expensive fine-tuning. It is robust across different GNN architectures and supports inexpensive test-time adaptation for Out-of-Distribution (OOD) scenarios.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - **Performance**: On in-domain benchmarks, AFANet matches or outperforms LLM-based baselines, including Qwen-2.5-7B/14B models (base, SFT, and GRPO-trained variants).
    - **Efficiency**: AFANet achieves up to 70x speedup compared to LLM baselines, with some scenarios showing up to 300x acceleration, alongside significantly reduced training and inference costs.
    - **Robustness**: The method maintains robust performance across various GNN architectures and shows improved results on OOD benchmarks through low-cost test-time adaptation.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper challenges the necessity of using heavy LLMs for agent failure attribution by introducing AFANet, a lightweight graph-based framework. By modeling multi-agent interaction trajectories as graphs, AFANet achieves attribution accuracy comparable to or better than expensive LLM methods at a fraction of the computational cost. The results suggest that effective failure attribution does not require heavy LLM reasoning, offering an efficient and structured alternative for debugging and explaining MAS failures.
