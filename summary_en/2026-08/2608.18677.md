## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: There are growing concerns that Generative AI and LLMs may standardize and homogenize art interpretation, flattening artistic meaning. Traditional art-historical narrative relies on the interplay between verifiable materials (images, letters) and scholarly perspectives.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current approaches fall short in three ways: 1) Multimodal models are limited to static image analysis without multi-work narrative design; 2) RAG systems supplement context but ignore the long-tail distribution of art materials and the role of user interaction in reshaping interpretive paths; 3) Historical chatbots have fixed personas, limiting open-ended biographical narratives. Thus, simulating the formation of art-historical narrative remains underexplored.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Sanyu Studio**: A multi-agent dialogue system that models 321 oil paintings by the artist Sanyu as agents with fact, interpretation, organization, and memory-filtering mechanisms to support plural art-historical narrative construction.
- **Challenge 1: How to avoid narrative homogenization given fragmented and limited historical evidence?**
  - **Difficulty**: Sanyu's biographical records are fragmented, and traditional AI tends to generate standardized interpretations based on statistical patterns.
  - **Solution**: The system employs a multi-agent "roundtable discussion" mechanism. User questions act as research prompts, painting agents serve as evidentiary standpoints for negotiation, and the Sanyu agent filters and synthesizes these exchanges to produce divergent yet coherent narratives.
- **Challenge 2: How to enable non-expert users to effectively participate in and reshape art-historical interpretation?**
  - **Difficulty**: The general public lacks the expertise to navigate complex art-historical materials.
  - **Solution**: The system integrates user prompts, evidence organization, and cognitive tendencies into the narrative generation loop. This allows AI to function as a narrative mechanism for non-experts, amplifying human agency and allowing users to shape the "Digital Sanyu" through interactive Q&A.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: Sanyu Studio is an LLM-based multi-agent system comprising user agents, painting agents (evidence), and a Sanyu agent (synthesis/memory). It was deployed in a seven-day workshop.
- **Evaluation Results**: The study involved eight art university participants. Results showed that user prompts, evidence organization, and cognitive tendencies shaped divergent but coherent versions of "Digital Sanyu." This demonstrates that AI can support plural narrative construction even with limited historical evidence.
- **Comparison**: Unlike static multimodal models, passive RAG systems, or fixed-persona chatbots, Sanyu Studio enables dynamic, user-driven, and evidence-negotiated art-historical narrative generation, offering a more open and interactive entry point for interpretation.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper presents Sanyu Studio, a multi-agent system for constructing art-historical narratives. By modeling Sanyu's paintings as agents with memory filtering, the system successfully simulates the formation of art-historical narratives. The study suggests that well-designed multi-agent LLM systems can avoid interpretive homogenization, amplify human agency under conditions of limited evidence, and provide the public with an interactive pathway to engage with pluralistic art-historical interpretations.
