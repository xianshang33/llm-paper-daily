## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: In agentic reinforcement learning, training LLM agents through long tool-use episodes often relies on step-level credit signals (e.g., LLM judge scores, outcome-conditioned log-prob ratios, policy confidence). This approach assumes these signals accurately reflect a step's actual causal contribution to the final outcome.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current evaluations compare credit signals against annotated step "correctness" rather than true "causal contribution." This mismatch fails to reveal whether signals capture decision points that actually change outcomes, leading to training based on spurious or irrelevant signals.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed an Executed Replay-based Causal Audit Framework**: Quantifies the actual change in outcome caused by re-sampling alternative actions at each decision point (causal contribution) to serve as ground truth for auditing credit assignment signals.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of Causal Identification in Credit Signals**. The difficulty lies in distinguishing step "fluency/plausibility" from "actual impact." The paper finds implicit credit signals mostly reflect policy fluency (median rank correlation +0.75), while outcome conditioning adds no causal information (partial correlation -0.004). All tested signals perform no better than chance in identifying causally pivotal steps.
- **Challenge 2: Sparsity and Model-Dependence of Causal Contribution**. Causal contribution is sparse (only 30.5% of decisions have measurable effect), and measurability varies significantly across models. The paper argues that comparing credit rules without matching effective sample size merely compares "training dose" (optimizer steps) rather than credit quality, explaining why some methods appear effective in training but are not.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Setup**: Experiments conducted in the single-agent tool environment ALFWorld using Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.1-8B. Causal ground truth generated via executed replay.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - **Audit Verdict**: For both model families, rank fidelity of all credit signals (judge, implicit, confidence) against causal ground truth overlaps with their shuffled controls, indicating failure to identify causally critical steps.
    - **Failure Mode Analysis**: Implicit credit correlates highly with fluency; a confidence-only router recovers pivotal steps at chance level but reduces judge cost by 13.1% per turn.
    - **Training Experiment**: In a seven-arm pre-registered training experiment, no credit signal reliably outperformed the untrained policy. Performance differences were fully explained by "training dose" (sample retention rates affecting optimizer steps), not credit content.
- **Comparison**: Unlike C3 which uses internal replay advantages, this work uses external executed causal ground truth. It reveals a significant divergence between annotated "correctness" and actual "causal contribution," challenging existing benchmarks.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper audits step-level credit assignment in LLM agents using executed replay-based causal ground truth. It finds that mainstream credit signals (judge scores, log-prob ratios, confidence) fail to identify causally impactful steps, performing no better than random chance. Apparent performance gains in training are artifacts of varying training doses (sample sizes) rather than signal quality. The study emphasizes that future comparisons of credit rules must match effective sample sizes, posing a significant challenge to the current paradigm of step-reward-based LLM agent training.
