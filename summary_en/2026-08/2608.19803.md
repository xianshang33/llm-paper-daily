## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are evolving into agents capable of long-horizon tasks (e.g., web navigation, embodied instruction following). Reinforcement Learning (RL) is a natural framework for optimizing these agents, but supervision is often limited to final task rewards.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Existing credit assignment methods (such as trajectory-level advantage estimation or graph-based methods like GraphGPO) have limitations. Trajectory-level methods assign the same final reward advantage to all actions, lacking granularity. Graph-based methods primarily rely on "shortest path distance to success" to propagate credit. This captures "reachability" rather than reliable "progress," leading to ambiguous credits where states at the same distance have vastly different success probabilities, and important intermediate milestones are undervalued simply because they are far from the final goal.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed MileGPO (Milestone Inference with Local Evidence for Graph-Based Policy Optimization)**: A method that derives process-level credit from grouped on-policy rollouts without requiring auxiliary models or additional environment interactions.
- **Challenge 1: How to identify truly valuable intermediate states (milestones) and traps from sparse final rewards?**
  - **Difficulty**: In long-horizon tasks, distinguishing key progress from ineffective wandering or error paths among numerous intermediate states is difficult.
  - **Solution**: **Milestone Discovery** identifies candidate milestones in successful rollouts and recurring traps in failed ones, preliminarily filtering nodes with potential value.
- **Challenge 2: How to eliminate ambiguity in intermediate credit assignment to ensure it reflects true local progress rather than just proximity to the goal?**
  - **Difficulty**: Distance to goal alone is an inaccurate measure of progress, as states at the same distance can have different success rates, and local branch choices create ambiguity.
  - **Solution**: Introduces **Reliability-Calibrated Shaping (RCS)** and **Progress-Contrastive Calibration (PCC)**. RCS weights candidates based on outcome confidence, strengthening reliable milestones/traps and down-weighting uncertain ones. PCC further tests if a candidate reflects local progress by comparing incoming transitions against alternative branches from the same state, resolving ambiguous intermediate credits.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: MileGPO operates solely on grouped on-policy rollout data to construct a local evidence graph, requiring no extra models or environment interactions.
- **Results**: Evaluated on ALFWorld and WebShop benchmarks.
- **Comparison**: MileGPO achieves State-of-the-Art (SOTA) performance on both datasets. Notably, it demonstrates a small in-distribution to out-of-distribution performance gap on ALFWorld, indicating strong generalization.
- **Ablation Studies**: Ablations and credit diagnostics confirm that reliability weighting, local progress assessment, and same-state branch evidence effectively complement milestone discovery and resolve ambiguous intermediate credit assignment.

#### Summary
MileGPO addresses the credit assignment challenge in long-horizon LLM agent RL by proposing a graph-based policy optimization method leveraging local evidence. Through three core designs—Milestone Discovery, Reliability-Calibrated Shaping, and Progress-Contrastive Calibration—it extracts fine-grained process-level credits from sparse final rewards. This approach overcomes the limitations of distance-based methods that ignore intermediate progress and suffer from credit ambiguity. Experiments demonstrate SOTA performance on complex benchmarks without additional computational or interaction costs, offering a new perspective for efficient RL optimization in long-horizon tasks.
