## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly used in financial decision systems, such as trading agents and surveillance assistants. Compliance in this context requires not just textual proficiency but the ability to identify rule applicability, translate rules into order decisions, pass deterministic execution checks, and support monitoring judgments with evidence.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current evaluation methods often rely on a single "compliance score," failing to distinguish whether a model truly follows rules or merely cites them while violating executable constraints (e.g., price bands, short-sale restrictions). Existing works do not separate four critical artifacts: stated reasoning, attempted action, execution enforcement, and monitor evidence.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ReguSim and ReguBench**: ReguSim is a controlled environment for studying LLM agent compliance under executable financial rules; ReguBench is a programmatically generated benchmark for evidence-based regulatory monitoring.
- **Challenge 1: How to separate and evaluate the "action-reasoning gap" in compliance?**
  - **Difficulty**: Models may correctly cite rules in reasoning but still attempt orders blocked by executable constraints, a nuance missed by traditional evaluations.
  - **Solution**: ReguSim routes trader actions through stylized market regimes and records execution outcomes separately from stated reasoning, clearly distinguishing between "stated reasoning" and "execution enforcement."
- **Challenge 2: How to prevent monitoring agents from being misled by incorrect trader rationales?**
  - **Difficulty**: Independent monitors can be swayed by a trader's confident but wrong compliance rationale if specific execution evidence is absent.
  - **Solution**: A bridge study and ReguBench demonstrate that monitors are less likely to be misled when provided with enforcement evidence. Furthermore, simple structured baselines match or exceed prompt-only LLMs in monitoring tasks.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The study used DeepSeek V4 Pro and Gemini 3.5 Flash as trading agents. ReguBench generates target-marked manipulation records with deterministic generator labels.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  1. **Rule Visibility**: Visible rules reduce but do not eliminate rejected actions.
  2. **Incentive/Persona Impact**: Incentives and persona framing significantly shift agent behavior and the frequency of rejected attempts.
  3. **Model Differences**: Gemini was generally more cautious than DeepSeek but still produced hard-blocked attempts under strong rule pressure.
  4. **Monitoring Performance**: LLMs did not clearly outperform simple structured baselines on the current synthetic samples.
- **Comparison**: Results show that simple structured baselines perform comparably to or better than prompt-only LLMs in monitoring tasks, challenging the assumption that LLMs inherently surpass traditional methods in all complex reasoning scenarios.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper argues that financial compliance evaluation should not be collapsed into a single score but treated as an audit of rule-grounded actions and evidence use. By introducing ReguSim and ReguBench, the authors reveal an "action gap" in LLM agents, where stated rule awareness does not guarantee executable compliance. The study emphasizes that effective compliance evaluation must separate reasoning, action, execution, and evidence, highlighting that structured methods remain competitive in monitoring tasks.
