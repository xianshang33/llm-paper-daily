## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: LLM agents are increasingly used for long-horizon tasks requiring reasoning and tool use. Runtime intervention is a key paradigm to improve reliability without retraining, but effective intervention requires not just failure detection but also providing a useful direction for recovery.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current approaches rely on expert solvers or critics that generate task-specific corrections. This introduces redundancy and high costs, as the intervention component must possess significant task-solving capabilities similar to the main actor. There is a lack of methods that provide constructive intervention using a lightweight auxiliary model that neither solves the task independently nor generates complex corrections.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: Comparison-Only Tiny Advisor (COTA), a framework for constructive runtime intervention based solely on comparison.
- **Challenge 1**: How to provide constructive recovery directions without requiring the tiny auxiliary model to solve the task or generate corrections?
  - **Difficulty & Solution**: Requiring the auxiliary model to generate correct actions demands high capacity. COTA addresses this by training a tiny comparator to judge whether sampled alternatives lead to better continuations than the actor's proposal. It uses pairwise supervision from same-prefix counterfactual branches, focusing on relative judgment rather than open-ended generation.
- **Challenge 2**: How to determine when to intervene to avoid disrupting potentially successful trajectories?
  - **Difficulty & Solution**: Simple failure prediction can harm performance by interrupting valid paths. COTA uses repeated comparisons to warrant intervention only when an alternative is deemed superior. The preferred alternative is returned as non-binding advice, allowing the original actor to replan, thus balancing guidance with autonomy.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: COTA trains a small comparator using pairwise data. At runtime, it samples alternative actions and compares them against the actor's current proposal. If an alternative is better, it is suggested to the actor for replanning.
- **Evaluation Results**: COTA was evaluated on WebShop, ALFWorld, and tau^3-Retail benchmarks with three different actors. It improved performance in all nine evaluation settings.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: COTA outperformed compared baselines. The results demonstrate that constructive runtime intervention remains effective even when the auxiliary model has substantially weaker task-solving capability than the actor, highlighting the efficiency of the "compare-only" approach.

#### Summary
This paper introduces COTA, addressing the cost and redundancy issues in LLM agent runtime intervention. By employing a tiny comparator that only evaluates relative quality of actions rather than generating solutions, COTA provides effective recovery directions with minimal computational overhead. Experiments across multiple benchmarks show that COTA outperforms existing methods, proving that lightweight, comparison-based intervention can significantly enhance agent reliability.
