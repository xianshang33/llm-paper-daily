## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly used as coding agents capable of building complete games from natural language requests. Game development is a demanding testbed as it requires the coherent integration of program logic, audiovisual assets, UI, and interactivity into a single executable artifact.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current benchmarks typically evaluate only the final game artifact or isolated stages of development. They fail to assess the complete development lifecycle, particularly overlooking the agent's ability to diagnose bugs, verify runtime behavior, and maintain functionality across iterative changes.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed GameXpert-Bench**: A comprehensive benchmark suite that operationalizes the game development lifecycle into three complementary tracks: GameGen (initial generation), GameFix (bug diagnosis and repair), and GameOpt (multi-turn optimization).
- **Challenge 1: How to comprehensively evaluate complete game generation from scratch?**
  - **Difficulty**: Games involve complex interactions between code, resources, and logic, making single-metric evaluations insufficient for assessing playability.
  - **Solution**: The GameGen track evaluates the creation of complete games from single natural language prompts in an empty workspace. It includes 97 tasks across 11 genres, assessed via live interaction and deterministic behavioral tests.
- **Challenge 2: How to quantify agent performance in debugging and multi-turn iteration?**
  - **Difficulty**: Lack of high-quality datasets with annotated defects and real-world multi-turn trajectories makes it hard to evaluate bug discovery and regression stability.
  - **Solution**: The GameFix track uses 50 human-verified "gold" games with 19-27 injected bugs each to test self-discovery and repair. The GameOpt track uses 17 optimization chains (102 requests) derived from real human-agent trajectories to evaluate cumulative optimization and functional preservation.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Scale**: The suite comprises 97 generation tasks, 100 repair tasks, and 17 multi-turn optimization chains. Evaluation combines live game interaction, deterministic tests, and product criteria with regression checks.
- **Results**: Current agents are reliable at producing playable foundations and implementing explicit requirements. However, they struggle significantly with discovering defects, verifying runtime behavior, and preserving functionality during code changes.
- **Comparison & Insights**: Unlike prior works focusing solely on final outputs, GameXpert-Bench reveals an asymmetry in agent capabilities: high initial generation quality does not equate to expert-level development skills. Agents show notable weaknesses in the GameFix and GameOpt tracks, highlighting gaps in robustness and engineering discipline.

## 4. Conclusion
GameXpert-Bench addresses the lack of full-lifecycle evaluation in game development by breaking down the process into generation, repair, and optimization. The study demonstrates that while LLM agents can rapidly prototype games, they fall short of expert-level proficiency, particularly in debugging and long-term maintenance. This benchmark provides a critical standard for guiding future improvements in the robustness and engineering capabilities of coding agents.
