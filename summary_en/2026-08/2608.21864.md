## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Clinical Vision Large Language Models (C-VLLMs) have improved digital diagnostics but still suffer from lesion noises, modality misalignment, hallucinations, and lack of contextual grounding in complex cases. Existing agent systems rely on static, non-adaptable pipelines lacking the versatility for complex medical reasoning.
- **Limitations of Previous Work**: Single backbone models struggle with reasoning across diverse clinical subfields and modalities. Traditional RLHF is computationally expensive and time-consuming. Current agents lack adaptability, leading to failures in handling multimodal misalignment and dynamic clinical contexts.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed BioMed-Agent-RL**: A unified medical agent framework incorporating adaptive orchestration, policy models, and reward-based reinforcement learning (RL). It utilizes a multimodal meta-learning approach to act as a field-specific expert and human judgment synthesizer.
- **Challenge 1: Multimodal Misalignment and Hallucinations**. Difficulty: Models often fail when visual cues are misleading or conflicting. Solution: The method employs Clinical Context-aware Preference Optimization (CPO), Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with dynamic entropy regulation. This allows the agent to synthesize conflicting cues and trust inherent reasoning even when specialist advice is faulty.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of Adaptability in Static Pipelines**. Difficulty: Existing systems cannot dynamically adjust reasoning paths for different modalities. Solution: The agent uses an iterative and adaptive RL approach to dynamically invoke model-level expertise (e.g., clinical grounding, lesion segmenter, reasoner) across various clinical modalities like X-ray, MRI, and CT.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The framework integrates CPO, DPO, and GRPO with dynamic entropy regulation. It operates by adaptively orchestrating specific mechanisms for multimodal grounding, reasoning, and report generation.
- **Evaluation Results**: Extensive ablation studies were conducted across multiple benchmarks. BioMed-Agent-RL significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art models, including baselines referred to as GPT-5.
- **Comparison**: The agent achieved approximately 73% accuracy, representing a ~5% gain over contemporary baselines. It demonstrates superior factual reliability, robustness, and expert-like reasoning capabilities compared to current methods.

## 4. Conclusion
BioMed-Agent-RL addresses the limitations of current C-VLLMs by introducing adaptive reinforcement learning and multimodal meta-learning. It overcomes the rigidity of static agent systems by dynamically integrating multimodal expert knowledge, ensuring reliable reasoning even amidst conflicting visual cues. Experimental results confirm its superiority in accuracy and robustness over SOTA models, setting a new standard for intelligent agents in independent clinical reasoning.
