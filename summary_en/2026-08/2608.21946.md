## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Reinforcement learning with outcome-based objectives (e.g., GRPO) enables LLM agents to solve complex, long-horizon tasks. However, reusable exploration patterns (e.g., task decomposition, recovery strategies) embedded in interaction trajectories are largely discarded after a single policy update, forcing agents to rediscover them from scratch, which is inefficient in sparse-reward environments.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current experience-augmented approaches retrieve historical guidance at inference time but fail to account for the policy's evolving capability. Guidance useful for an undertrained policy may become redundant or harmful once internalized. Additionally, these methods create persistent dependencies on external retrieval, incurring token overhead and sensitivity to retrieval noise at deployment.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed EDGE (Experience-Distillation for Guided Exploration)**: A framework that treats retrieved experiences as temporary training-time scaffolds, progressively internalizing their benefits into the parametric policy through a dynamic lifecycle.
- **Challenge 1: How to estimate the marginal gain of experiences without extra sampling and avoid negative guidance?**
  - **Difficulty**: Experience utility is policy-dependent; blind injection can degrade performance, and distinguishing positive vs. negative marginal contributions is difficult.
  - **Solution**: EDGE partitions each rollout group into experience-conditioned and experience-free trajectories to estimate and admit only positive marginal gains, filtering out ineffective or harmful guidance without additional sampling costs.
- **Challenge 2: How to internalize external experiences into the policy parameters to eliminate inference-time dependencies?**
  - **Difficulty**: Existing methods rely on inference-time retrieval, storing competence in the context window rather than model parameters, leading to high overhead and lack of true capability improvement.
  - **Solution**: It distills induced behavior into the base policy via a reverse-KL objective on its own empirical support. A co-evolutionary experience bank synthesizes guidance from emerging failure modes and prunes obsolete entries, enabling effective deployment without inference-time scaffolds.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: EDGE employs experience-guided exploration scaffolding during training and a co-evolutionary experience bank. At inference, it requires no external retrieval or proprietary reflectors, relying solely on the internalized policy parameters.
- **Evaluation Results**: Evaluated across embodied, web, and search-based QA tasks.
- **Comparison**: EDGE improves over strong RL baselines by up to 12.5 points. Unlike naive memory augmentation methods (e.g., EvolveR, GRPO+Mem0) which degrade performance below vanilla GRPO due to unfiltered injection, EDGE achieves significant gains while eliminating inference-time token overhead and retrieval sensitivity.

#### Summary
EDGE introduces a new paradigm for agentic RL by shifting experience reuse from "inference-time retrieval" to "training-time distillation." It addresses policy dependency and external reliance issues, significantly boosting performance on long-horizon tasks while enabling lighter, more robust deployment. This approach offers an effective path for continuous learning and capability internalization in LLM agents.
