## What the Paper Does
- **Background**: LLM-based self-evolving agents learn from past experiences by distilling interaction trajectories into reusable "skills" stored in skill banks. Existing research primarily focuses on operational aspects like skill acquisition, evolution, and retrieval.
- **Limitations of Prior Work**: Current methods overlook a fundamental reliability question: whether accumulated skills actually make positive mechanistic contributions. Isolated evaluation fails to capture the true utility of skills within specific combinations (coalitions) or after cross-domain transfer, leading to the retention or erroneous migration of unreliable skills.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A Skill Mechanistic Reliability Audit (SMRA) framework and two reliability interventions: Coalition-Aware Skill Selection (CASS) and Unsupervised Skill-Masked Coalition Optimizer (u-SMCO).
- **Challenge 1: Coalition Pollution**
    - **Description & Difficulty**: Overall performance gains at the skill bank level may conceal negative contributions of certain skills within specific coalitions, which are hard to detect via isolated evaluation.
    - **Solution**: CASS uses sampled Shapley marginals during skill accumulation to select candidate skills that provide more reliable positive contributions to the current bank, avoiding skills with negative synergistic effects.
- **Challenge 2: Cross-Domain Utility Reversal**
    - **Description & Difficulty**: Skills beneficial in the source domain may reverse their effects or become harmful when transferred to a target domain, where labeled data for direct evaluation is often unavailable.
    - **Solution**: u-SMCO masks transferred skills in an unsupervised manner post-transfer if excluding them improves retrieval quality on unlabeled target-domain data, automatically filtering out skills that fail or harm performance in the new domain.

## Implementation, Evaluation, and Comparison
- **Experimental Setup**: Agent experiments conducted on LoCoMo, LongMemEval, HotpotQA, and ALFWorld benchmarks.
- **Baselines**: Compared against strong existing skill-based self-evolving agent baselines.
- **Results**:
    - CASS and u-SMCO consistently outperform baselines in task performance and cross-domain generalization.
    - Coalition-conditioned reliability modeling reduces sensitivity to noisy outcome-reward fluctuations during reinforcement learning.
    - The study exposes the limitations of isolation-based skill evaluation, demonstrating the effectiveness of treating skill coalitions as the central unit for reliable skill evolution.

## Summary
This paper systematically identifies two key failure modes in self-evolving agents: coalition pollution and cross-domain utility reversal. By introducing Shapley value analysis and unsupervised mask optimization, the proposed methods significantly enhance agent robustness and generalization in complex tasks and cross-domain scenarios. It establishes that skill reliability is a joint property of skills, skill banks, and deployment domains.
