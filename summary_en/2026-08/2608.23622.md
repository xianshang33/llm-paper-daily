## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) excel in reasoning and planning, but scientific and engineering tasks (e.g., pharmaceutical process design) require understanding system responses to interventions, which relies on controlled experimentation rather than just plausible text generation.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current LLM-based systems, even with tools, typically retrieve information or execute isolated functions without structured experimental comparison. Consequently, their outputs are often suggestive rather than evidential, failing to provide reliable conclusions for tasks dependent on controlled intervention comparisons.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Multi-Agent Framework**: A framework enabling LLM agents to conduct controlled experiments using scientific simulation models, supporting reasoning through intervention, comparison, and observation.
- **Challenge 1: Translating natural language queries into executable controlled experimental designs.**
  - **Difficulty**: User queries are often vague, while scientific experiments require strict variable control and baseline comparisons, which LLMs struggle to map directly.
  - **Solution**: The system constructs a structured task representation, defining baseline configurations and parameters. Agents automatically design comparative experiments, ensuring only specific factors are varied while others remain fixed.
- **Challenge 2: Extracting causal insights from simulation data to generate actionable recommendations.**
  - **Difficulty**: High-fidelity simulations produce complex data that pure language models cannot accurately interpret in terms of physical/chemical causality.
  - **Solution**: Agents execute comparative simulations, interpret outcome differences, and synthesize evidence-based recommendations for process parameter optimization, shifting from "language reasoning" to "evidence-based reasoning."

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: Applied in an industrial pharmaceutical process design setting, coupling LLMs with high-fidelity simulation models. The workflow includes: receiving queries/baselines -> structuring tasks -> designing experiments -> executing comparative simulations -> interpreting results -> synthesizing recommendations.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - **Output Specificity**: The method produces more specific outputs compared to language-only reasoning baselines.
  - **User Ratings**: In industrial applications, user-rated correctness and helpfulness significantly improved.
  - **Ablation Studies & Case Analysis**: Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of simulation-integrated experimental reasoning; visualized case analyses demonstrated practical utility.
- **Comparison**: Unlike traditional LLMs acting merely as text generators or simple tool callers, this approach provides more evidential and actionable conclusions by introducing a controlled experimental loop.

#### Summary
This paper presents a multi-agent framework integrating LLMs with scientific simulation models to address scientific engineering problems requiring controlled experiments, such as pharmaceutical process design. By enabling LLM agents to test hypotheses, intervene, and compare observations within a simulation environment, the system overcomes the lack of evidential support in traditional LLM outputs. Experimental results show that the method provides more specific, correct, and helpful process optimization recommendations in industrial settings, validating the potential of simulation-integrated experimental reasoning in practical scientific discovery.
