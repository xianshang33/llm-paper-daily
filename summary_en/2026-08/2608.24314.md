## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Scaling the evaluation of conversational voice agents requires reliable methods that capture both observable interaction quality and contextual judgments typically provided by humans. Traditional human evaluation is expensive and unscalable, while existing automated metrics often fail to capture nuanced conversational quality.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Prior "LLM-as-a-Judge" research primarily focuses on text-based dialogues and suffers from issues like position bias, prompt sensitivity, and instability. Furthermore, voice agents involve end-to-end pipelines (ASR, reasoning, TTS) where errors compound; evaluating transcripts alone is insufficient. There is a lack of systematic benchmarking for LLM judge reliability, calibration, and the need for human oversight specifically in voice agent contexts.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a** benchmark framework for LLM judges in voice-agent evaluation, systematically comparing human judgments with GPT-4.1 and GPT-5 across telecom and retail scenarios, analyzing reliability and disagreement patterns under three different evaluation configurations (p0, p1, p2).
- **Challenge 1: Sensitivity to Evaluation Settings**. LLM judges can produce volatile scores depending on prompt structure or configuration. The paper addresses this by scoring the same interactions under three distinct configurations to quantify sensitivity and test whether agreement patterns generalize across setups and models, identifying stable evaluation dimensions.
- **Challenge 2: Complexity of Voice Pipelines and Metric Heterogeneity**. Voice agent quality is affected by ASR errors, latency, and synthesis, making some attributes harder to judge automatically than others. The paper addresses this by conducting metric-level correlation analysis and examining systematic human-LLM disagreements to distinguish which attributes are suitable for automation and which require human interpretation.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Data**: Used real-world voice agent conversations from telecom and retail domains, covering quality and safety dimensions. Evaluators included human annotators and LLMs (GPT-4.1, GPT-5).
- **Results**:
    - LLM-based evaluation is effective for large-scale assessment but its reliability is metric- and configuration-dependent, not uniform.
    - High consistency was found in some dimensions, but systematic disagreements persisted in areas requiring deep contextual interpretation.
    - Evaluation configurations significantly impacted scores, indicating that a single automated setup is insufficient for all quality dimensions.
- **Comparison**: Unlike prior work focusing on text-only or aggregate agreement, this study addresses end-to-end voice pipeline error propagation and provides granular metric-level insights, defining the boundaries of automated evaluation.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper establishes an empirical framework for LLM judge reliability in voice-agent evaluation. It concludes that while LLM judges are powerful for scaling, they should not blindly replace humans. A hybrid pipeline is recommended: LLMs handle scalable, objective metrics, while humans oversee complex, context-sensitive judgments. This provides a roadmap for robust voice-agent assessment standards.
