## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Clinical diagnosis is an active evidence-seeking process where clinicians iteratively acquire evidence, update hypotheses, and decide when to stop. However, many existing LLM-based medical diagnosis systems treat diagnosis as a static case-to-answer prediction, lacking support for dynamic evidence acquisition.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: While agentic LLMs offer dynamic alternatives via tool use, existing systems often under-specify how patient evidence is exposed, scaffolded, and controlled at runtime. They typically rely on fixed workflows or simple turn limits, failing to adequately model evidence sufficiency and termination control, which leads to issues like premature commitment.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed EviDx**, an evidence-aware active diagnosis framework that pairs patient-specific diagnostic environments with a clinical diagnostic scaffold and an observer-guided runtime harness.
- **Challenge 1: How to construct controllable interactive diagnostic environments from raw clinical data?**
  - **Difficulty**: Raw medical records are unstructured; giving LLMs full access simulates neither real-world step-by-step inquiry nor prevents "cheating".
  - **Solution**: The $\\mathcal{E}$-Synthesis module converts raw clinical cases into interactive environments, exposing evidence through standardized interfaces on demand to ensure authenticity and controllability.
- **Challenge 2: How to organize complex diagnostic processes and ensure timely termination based on evidence sufficiency?**
  - **Difficulty**: Managing multi-agent collaboration and dynamic evidence states is complex, and determining when enough evidence has been gathered is difficult, leading to infinite loops or hasty conclusions.
  - **Solution**: A Clinical Dx Scaffold organizes role-specialized agents, tools, and evolving evidence states. An observer-guided runtime harness regulates termination by tracking uncertainty and evidence coverage.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Evaluation Framework**: Introduces a 3-level evaluation pyramid assessing execution robustness, reasoning dynamics, and diagnostic outcomes.
- **Comparison**: Qualitatively compared with MDAgents, Agent Hospital, AgentClinic, MedAgentBench, and AutoMedic. Table 1 shows that while existing works lack in areas like interactive environments, evidence access, scaffolding, or runtime harnesses, EviDx supports all these dimensions.
- **Results**: Experiments demonstrate that EviDx improves diagnostic performance and process stability. It also reveals model-dependent capability boundaries in active diagnosis tasks.

## 4. Conclusion
EviDx addresses the limitations of existing LLM medical diagnosis systems in active evidence seeking by constructing interactive environments, structured diagnostic scaffolds, and intelligent runtime control mechanisms. It not only enhances diagnostic accuracy and process robustness but also provides a new framework and benchmark for evaluating and improving the dynamic reasoning capabilities of medical AI agents.
