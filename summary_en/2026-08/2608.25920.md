## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: As Large Language Model (LLM)-based Multi-Agent Systems (MASs) are increasingly applied to long-horizon complex tasks, reliability has become a core bottleneck. Existing debugging and repair methods typically rely on rerunning and resampling the entire execution trajectory.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current methods suffer from two main issues: First, complete reruns resample upstream model decisions instead of fixing the failure-producing execution, making it difficult to attribute terminal success to the repair strategy (causal vs. stochastic repair). Second, task-level verdicts and automatic categorization often fail to identify the specific trace-localized behaviors requiring intervention, leading to unstable diagnosis and low repair efficiency.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SymTrace Framework and SymFail Dataset**: SymTrace is a controlled evaluation framework that records MAS execution trajectories and establishes intervention anchors. It reconstructs pre-anchor execution using logs and only regenerates downstream trajectories, enabling reliable failure reproduction. SymFail is a dataset of 536 human-annotated failure trajectories with graph-linked locations, categories, and trace evidence.
- **Challenge 1: Existing rerun methods cannot distinguish causal repair from stochastic repair and have low failure reproduction rates.**
  - **Difficulty**: The randomness of LLMs means that complete reruns change upstream decisions, masking the true root cause and making it hard to judge repair effectiveness.
  - **Solution**: SymTrace fixes the upstream execution path and only resamples or intervenes downstream, isolating stochastic interference and achieving reliable failure reproduction (consistency improved from 41.42% to over 52.43%).
- **Challenge 2: Lack of precise local intervention mechanisms leads to extremely low repair success rates.**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional task-level regeneration is blind and cannot correct specific error nodes, resulting in a repair rate of only 6.90% within three attempts.
  - **Solution**: A symptom-driven intervention method (Suspicious-Node Intervention) is proposed, leveraging localization evidence from SymFail to precisely intervene on suspicious nodes. This raises the repair rate to 20.15%, a 191.89% improvement over state-of-the-art methods.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Large-scale empirical studies were conducted across three mainstream MAS frameworks using the SymTrace framework and SymFail dataset.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - **Failure Reproduction**: Existing unguided rerun methods had a failure reproduction rate of only 67.97%. SymTrace significantly improved reliability and consistency by fixing upstream paths.
  - **Repair Effectiveness Comparison**:
    - **Baseline**: Task-level regeneration achieved a maximum repair rate of 6.90% within three attempts.
    - **Proposed Method**: Suspicious-Node Intervention repaired 20.15% of failed cases with a single intervention.
    - **Improvement**: This represents a 2.92x increase (191.89% relative improvement) compared to the strongest task-level baseline.
  - **Diagnosis Stability**: Diagnostic consistency improved from 67.97% to 80.78%.

#### Summary
This paper investigates failure debugging in LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems, highlighting the flaws in causal attribution of existing resampling methods. By introducing the SymTrace framework and SymFail dataset, the authors demonstrate the inefficiency of current methods and validate the effectiveness of symptom-driven, trace-localized intervention. The results show that controlled execution and local evidence are superior to repeated stochastic regeneration, providing actionable insights for robust MAS deployment.
