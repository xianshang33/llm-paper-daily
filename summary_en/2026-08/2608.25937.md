## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Multi-agent systems (MAS) are increasingly used for complex reasoning and coordination. However, existing MAS research often simultaneously alters candidate generation, inter-agent communication, and final answer selection rules, making it difficult to attribute performance gains to specific components.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Due to the coupled changes in generation, communication, and selection, prior works fail to explain why systems sometimes report wrong answers even when correct ones were generated by the agent collective. This opacity masks "memetic drift," where consensus without quality control allows popular but incorrect answers to dominate.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a** conceptualization of multi-agent reasoning as an evolutionary pipeline comprising candidate generation, peer communication, and terminal selection, isolating these stages to diagnose the effective selection pressure of LLM judges.
- **Challenge 1**: Correct answers are generated but not selected. The difficulty lies in simple mechanisms like majority voting being overwhelmed by frequent incorrect answers. The paper addresses this by replaying fixed candidate pools and showing that combining answer frequency with judge evaluation rescues correct answers outnumbered by popular errors.
- **Challenge 2**: Quantifying the non-fixed reliability of LLM judges. The difficulty is that judge performance varies by task, generator, and the rarity of the correct answer. The paper maps judge reliability across 15,336 questions, demonstrating that a judge's value depends on making correct answers "recognizable" rather than just present.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Data**: Analyzed 15,336 questions from MMLU-Pro, GPQA, MedXpertQA, and MuSR, and replayed 81,390 fixed candidate pools from 16,278 questions.
- **Results**:
    1. Correct answers are often present in candidates but lost in final selection.
    2. Changing only the final answer selection rule (combining frequency and judge evaluation) improved accuracy from 63.82% to 70.82%-70.95%.
    3. The value of generating more candidates depends on whether they make correct answers present, frequent, or recognizable.
- **Comparison**: Aligns with Choi et al. and Wang et al. in finding that majority voting accounts for most gains attributed to debate. Echoes Smit et al. and Kim et al. in showing communication benefits are not universally reliable. Unlike end-to-end comparisons, this work provides a diagnostic basis by decoupling generation, recognition, and selection.

#### Summary
The paper reveals the paradox of "generated correctly but output incorrectly" in MAS, identifying the core issue as the answer selection mechanism rather than generation capability. By isolating analysis stages, the authors demonstrate that LLM judge effectiveness is context-dependent and that optimizing selection rules (combining frequency and judge scores) significantly boosts performance. This study provides a crucial diagnostic foundation for designing MAS architectures that protect generated correct answers from being lost.
