## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Multi-agent Large Language Model (LLM) workflows have emerged as a powerful paradigm for solving complex, open-ended tasks through collaborative reasoning. However, they incur substantial operating costs due to repeated LLM invocations and the accumulation of long-horizon contexts, leading to high token consumption, compute usage, and latency.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Existing cascade routing methods typically make one-shot, query-level decisions, assigning simple queries to lightweight models and complex ones to powerful models. These methods fail to adapt to the dynamic, state-dependent nature of multi-step workflows, where the optimal LLM at each step depends on evolving task progress, remaining difficulty, and cost-efficiency requirements, thus struggling to balance quality and cost over long horizons.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ProgRouter**: An online progress-guided routing framework that adaptively selects LLM agents across workflow steps to preserve task-solving quality while adhering to time and cost budgets.
- **Challenge 1: How to accurately quantify task progress in a dynamic environment?**
  - **Difficulty**: Multi-agent workflows are open-ended and stateful. Traditional static benchmarks or offline preference data cannot capture real-time status in online contexts, making it hard to assess the contribution of current steps to the final outcome.
  - **Solution**: Introduces a Multi-view Task Progress Scorer that combines coarse-grained workflow outcome regimes with fine-grained signals such as subtask completion, progress trends, and workflow state quality to comprehensively evaluate current progress.
- **Challenge 2: How to optimize long-term cost efficiency while ensuring progress?**
  - **Difficulty**: It is challenging to balance immediate progress gain, remaining time budgets, and long-term operating costs at each step; simple greedy strategies may lead to resource exhaustion later or waste earlier.
  - **Solution**: Designs a dual-path task progress predictor and an adaptive meta-gating mechanism to estimate the progress gain for each candidate LLM, enabling online step-wise routing decisions that balance progress gain with cost efficiency.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on benchmarks including HumanEval Plus, MBPP (code generation), MATH-500 (mathematical reasoning), and ASQA (retrieval-augmented long-form question answering).
- **Baselines**: Compared against key existing baseline methods, such as static routing and one-shot cascade routing.
- **Results**: Experiments demonstrate that ProgRouter significantly reduces operating costs relative to key baselines while maintaining strong task-solving performance (comparable to or better than the strongest baselines). It proves that fine-grained, progress-aware routing can achieve superior quality-cost trade-offs in complex agentic workflows.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the high operating costs of multi-agent LLM workflows by proposing ProgRouter. By introducing a multi-view task progress scorer and an adaptive routing mechanism, ProgRouter dynamically selects the most suitable LLM agents based on real-time task states. This method effectively overcomes the limitations of traditional one-shot routing in adapting to dynamic workflows, achieving significant cost reduction without compromising performance across multiple complex task benchmarks, offering new insights for sustainable LLM serving.
