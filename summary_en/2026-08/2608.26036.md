## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: LLM data agents are increasingly used as interfaces for operational data, handling tasks like SQL generation and result summarization. However, answer accuracy alone is an insufficient reliability signal, as benchmark-correct answers can stem from invalid computational traces.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current evaluations focus primarily on final answer matching, ignoring the underlying logic. Natural language reasoning suffers from a "Structure Gap," failing to reliably specify operator-level programs required by real-world systems. This leads to "silent failures" where incorrect filters, joins, or aggregations coincidentally produce correct answers, a risk masked by traditional metrics.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Trace Integrity**: A deployment reliability criterion evaluating whether the computation behind an answer is explicit, executable, schema-valid, operator-faithful, replayable, answer-consistent, and auditable.
- **Challenge 1: Unreliable Computation due to Structure Gap**. Natural language reasoning does not precisely map to database operators, leading to logically flawed but accidentally correct answers. The paper addresses this by introducing "Execution Contracts," structured artifacts that bind user intent to schema elements, operator plans, assumptions, and executable queries, ensuring transparency and auditability.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of Quantification for "Correct Answer/Invalid Trace" Risk**. Traditional metrics cannot distinguish genuine competence from accidental success. The paper introduces the CAIT (Correct Answer / Invalid Trace) Rate, which measures how often answer-only evaluation counts computationally unsupported outputs as successes, thereby exposing silent failure risks.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Empirical demonstration on the BIRD Mini-Dev dataset, comparing Direct SQL, Operation Summary + SQL, and Contract-First SQL.
- **Results**:
    - **Answer Accuracy**: Direct SQL (20%), Operation Summary + SQL (22%), Contract-First SQL (24%). Overall low and similar across methods.
    - **Trace Integrity Pass Rates**: 39%, 43%, and 40% respectively, indicating most generated traces fail integrity standards.
    - **CAIT Rates**: High at 55%, 59.1%, and 45.8%. This reveals that in over half the cases, seemingly correct answers are backed by invalid or unauditable computation.
- **Comparison Conclusion**: The results demonstrate that answer accuracy, trace validity, and silent failure risk are distinct evaluation signals. Relying solely on answer accuracy significantly overestimates actual system reliability.

#### Summary
The paper argues that real-world LLM data agents should be evaluated not just on output matching, but on whether outputs are supported by auditable computation. By proposing Trace Integrity and Execution Contracts, it offers a concrete solution to the Structure Gap in structured data tasks. It emphasizes the CAIT rate as a critical risk metric, advocating for a paradigm shift from "answer correctness" to "computational trustworthiness."
