## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are evolving from end-to-end Chain-of-Thought reasoning to integrating Agentic Intelligence, which involves dynamic planning, action execution, and self-reflection for complex problem-solving.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Most existing mathematical benchmarks evaluate only the final answer. This outcome-oriented approach lacks diagnostic value for identifying process-level logical failures or rigor, failing to guide the transformation of LLMs into robust agents.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Process-Level Benchmark Framework for Agentic Mathematical Capabilities**: The framework aligns problem-solving agentic behaviors with a structured taxonomy of reusable mathematical atomic capabilities to evaluate inherent agentic reasoning.
- **Challenge 1: How to quantify and evaluate complex agentic behaviors?**
  - **Difficulty**: Agentic behaviors involve multiple dynamic steps (planning, action, feedback), making them difficult to decompose into measurable units using traditional methods.
  - **Solution**: The paper establishes an "Agentic-to-Math-Atomic" mapping mechanism, decomposing high-level workflows into atomic capabilities like spatial reasoning, deductive reasoning, and computation, enabling fine-grained evaluation of each reasoning step.
- **Challenge 2: How to efficiently construct high-quality process-level annotated data?**
  - **Difficulty**: Manual annotation of multi-step reasoning trajectories is costly and error-prone, especially across textual and multimodal contexts.
  - **Solution**: An automated pipeline was designed to synthesize high-quality reasoning trajectories and generate fine-grained annotations via controlled LLM rewriting, supporting evaluation across planning, action, and feedback tasks.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The team developed an automated data synthesis pipeline using LLMs to generate and optimize reasoning trajectories. The benchmark covers textual and multimodal contexts, including tasks for planning, action, and feedback.
- **Evaluation Results**: Experiments revealed that models with similar end-to-end accuracy can exhibit markedly different agentic capability profiles. This indicates that relying solely on final answers masks defects in the reasoning process.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Unlike traditional benchmarks that focus only on final correctness, this method provides interpretable, process-level evaluation. It accurately reveals model strengths and weaknesses in specific atomic capabilities (e.g., spatial perception, modeling), offering more guiding metrics for developing next-generation mathematical agents.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the limitations of current mathematical benchmarks in evaluating LLM agentic capabilities by proposing a process-level evaluation framework based on atomic capability mapping. By decomposing agentic behaviors into reusable mathematical atomic units and using an automated pipeline for fine-grained annotation, the study demonstrates the importance of process-level evaluation for understanding the true potential of LLMs. The findings highlight that models with similar final accuracy may differ significantly in agentic capabilities, emphasizing the necessity of interpretable evaluation from "Atomic" to "Agentic".
