## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large language model (LLM) agents are increasingly deployed as autonomous loops, executing repeated iterations of planning, tool use, and state persistence from a single human goal. However, current safety safeguards are defined over single trajectories and reset their safety state at the start of each new trajectory.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Existing defenses (e.g., input sanitization, per-call classification) fail against attacks where evidence is fragmented across multiple iterations. Any trajectory-scoped monitor has a true-positive rate equal to its false-positive rate because it never sees the complete evidence window. Furthermore, simple fixes like geometrically decaying risk scores are insufficient, as the required "cooling-off" period for an adversary is a constant that does not grow with the horizon $N$.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed LoopHarness**: A framework that restores a persistent, non-decaying safety state at the loop level.
- **Challenge 1: Fragmentation of attack evidence across iterations makes traditional monitoring ineffective**. The difficulty lies in the fact that attack intent is split across independent trajectories, making single-trajectory monitors blind to the full context. The paper proves that any trajectory-scoped monitor performs no better than random guessing against such attacks, whereas a monitor retaining cross-iteration state can perfectly distinguish them.
- **Challenge 2: Simple risk decay mechanisms fail against patient adversaries**. The challenge is that if risk scores decay geometrically, attackers can simply wait for a fixed cooling-off period to reset risk, leaving long-running agents vulnerable. LoopHarness addresses this by introducing non-decaying persistent state and an arbiter detection floor $\\delta_M$, bounding the expected number of unauthorized irreversible actions to a constant independent of $N$.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation Protocol**: The authors present a comprehensive evaluation protocol including native Agent-SafetyBench tasks with paired clean and attacked episodes, an outer-state attack suite (where decisive evidence exists only across iterations), per-module ablations, and adaptive white-box red teaming.
- **Results & Comparison**: Under mediated commits and an arbiter detection floor $\\delta_M$, LoopHarness bounds the expected number of unauthorized irreversible actions by $B+m-1+m/\\delta_M$. The term $B+m-1$ is determined by a model-free rule, ensuring security even against fully colluding verifiers. In contrast, existing single-trajectory methods show negligible true-positive rates against cross-iteration attacks, and simple decay methods fail to provide security guarantees that scale with the horizon.

#### Summary
This paper reveals a fundamental issue in LLM agent safety: safety does not compose. Existing single-trajectory safety mechanisms completely fail against fragmented cross-iteration attacks. The authors propose LoopHarness, which maintains a persistent, non-decaying safety state at the loop level, providing theoretical safety bounds. Through rigorous evaluation, they demonstrate its effectiveness in resisting complex cross-iteration attacks, offering a new theoretical foundation and practical framework for the long-term safe operation of autonomous LLM agents.
