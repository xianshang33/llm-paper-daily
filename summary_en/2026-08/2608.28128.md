## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Fine-grained credit assignment is a central challenge in Reinforcement Learning (RL) for long-horizon LLM agents. Standard approaches train on programmatically verifiable terminal rewards, broadcasting sparse outcomes to every action in a trajectory.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Existing methods attempt to derive finer credit from the rollout side by constructing auxiliary signals or comparisons. However, they treat the verifier merely as a scalar reward source, discarding its internal task structure. State/trajectory similarity may miss critical facts, while hindsight or process feedback relies on auxiliary judgments and incurs high sampling costs.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed VICT (Verifier-Instrumented Credit Tracing)**: A training-time interface that exposes executable or evidence-backed atoms within the verifier and traces them back to actions via dependency-valid proof edges, shifting credit assignment from rollout-side inference to verifier-side tracing.
- **Challenge 1: How to leverage verifier internal structure without increasing inference cost?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional methods ignore verifier logic, leading to coarse credit assignment. Utilizing verifier details at inference time is computationally prohibitive.
  - **Solution**: VICT intervenes only during training. It redistributes group-relative advantage along valid "proof edges" derived from the verifier's logic, requiring no learned critic, branch rollouts, or inference-time verifier access.
- **Challenge 2: How to handle incomplete or ambiguous evidence to avoid misattribution?**
  - **Difficulty**: Forcing credit distribution based on temporal proximity or dense rewards introduces noise, especially in long sequences where many actions are irrelevant to success.
  - **Solution**: VICT abstains from assigning credit when evidence is incomplete or ambiguous. It modifies only the training-time advantage tensor while preserving the original terminal reward, avoiding biases from dense atom rewards or final-commit credit.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: VICT requires no learned critic, process labels, or inference-time verifier access. It integrates easily into existing group-based RL methods (e.g., GRPO) by modifying only the advantage calculation during training.
- **Evaluation Results**: On ALFWorld and WebShop benchmarks, VICT substantially outperforms outcome-only training and achieves strong performance comparable to or better than recent fine-grained credit methods.
- **Comparison**: Ablation studies rule out dense atom rewards, final-commit credit, temporal proximity, and sparsity as sufficient explanations for the improvement, validating that the gain comes from correctly utilizing verifier structure. Compared to baselines requiring extra sampling or complex auxiliary models, VICT is more efficient and direct.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper proposes VICT, a novel credit assignment method for long-horizon LLM agent RL. Its key insight is leveraging the structured check information already present in task verifiers. By tracing terminal rewards back to key actions via "proof edges," VICT significantly improves training efficiency and agent performance without adding inference overhead or relying on additional models, offering a new perspective on fine-grained credit assignment.
