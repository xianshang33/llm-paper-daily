## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: LLM agents are increasingly used in scientific and engineering workflows, interacting with external simulators (e.g., DWSIM) to obtain engineering evidence. When an agent makes substantive design modifications, previous verification evidence becomes stale, necessitating re-verification of the new state.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: While existing systems connect LLM agents with simulators for iterative processes, they often lack explicit protocol control over "when" to re-verify. Prior research focuses on spontaneous recognition of stale evidence, neglecting the impact of explicit "verification-cadence guidance" on agent behavior when verification-relevant states are held constant.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a controlled comparison framework**: Investigated the impact of retaining versus omitting explicit "verification-cadence guidance" on post-edit re-verification behavior in simulator-backed engineering agents.
- **Challenge 1: Difficulty in Evidence Freshness and Timing Decisions**. Agents struggle to spontaneously recognize that old evidence is invalid after design changes, leading to decisions based on stale data. The paper addresses this by comparing a "Cadence-Guided (CG)" condition (explicit instruction to request new simulation after modification) against a "Cadence-Omitted (CO)" condition, demonstrating that explicit guidance significantly improves adherence to verification policies.
- **Challenge 2: Ensuring Protocol Adherence Without Hard Gates**. Relying on model spontaneity often leads to missed verifications in the absence of hard constraints. The method shows that treating verification cadence as an explicit component of the interaction protocol, even without hard gating, significantly increases re-verification frequency and final task success rates.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Used DWSIM as the simulator backend for continuous valve-pressure adjustment tasks across 8 synthetic cases. Five Alibaba/Qwen models were evaluated. Each model-case-condition combination was executed three times via live API calls, yielding 120 evaluation slots per condition (CG and CO).
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - **Re-verification Rate**: 94/120 in CG vs. 32/120 in CO.
    - **Cadence Violations**: 26/120 in CG vs. 87/120 in CO.
    - **Final Success Rate**: Bounded final success achieved in 95/120 in CG vs. 35/120 in CO.
    - **Outlier Performance**: qwen3.5-35b-a3b performed poorly, with minimal re-verification (1/24 in CG, 0/24 in CO) and no final success in either condition.
- **Comparison Conclusion**: Compared to relying on spontaneous recognition of stale evidence, explicitly including verification cadence in the interaction protocol coordinates design modification with evidence acquisition more effectively, significantly enhancing reliability and success rates.

#### Summary
This paper demonstrates through controlled experiments that explicit "verification-cadence guidance" is critical in simulator-backed engineering agent workflows. Even without hard gating mechanisms, explicitly instructing agents to request new simulations after substantive modifications significantly reduces cadence violations, increases re-verification rates, and improves final task success. The study supports treating verification cadence as an explicit component of the engineering agent interaction protocol rather than relying on the model's spontaneous cognition.
