## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: LLM agents are increasingly becoming mutable systems that can autonomously modify prompts, tools, middleware, and execution environments at runtime. While this "self-evolution" enhances capabilities, successful mutations may leave persistent side effects (e.g., configuration overwrites, resource leaks) that are difficult to safely reverse in states different from where they were created.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current self-improvement procedures primarily optimize the forward effect of modifications (i.e., task objective improvement) while neglecting recoverability. Simple static inverse operations often fail to restore prior states because correct recovery depends on pre-mutation state evidence and must hold across various counterfactual states. Existing methods lack formalized verification and constraints for cross-state recoverability.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed EvoUndo Framework**: A framework for representing, synthesizing, diagnosing, and independently verifying the recoverability of model-generated self-modifications across counterfactual states.
- **Challenge 1: Precision of State Grounding**
    - **Difficulty**: Recovery operations require precise pointing to specific pre-mutation state information; inaccurate addressing or context localization leads to failure.
    - **Solution**: Introduces exact state-address diagnostics, which increased successful recovery from 0% to 79.2% in cases where the original recovery language was sufficient.
- **Challenge 2: Expressivity of Recovery Language**
    - **Difficulty**: The original recovery language (L0) had limited expressivity, unable to describe complex recovery logic, leading to unrecoverable natural failures.
    - **Solution**: Extends the recovery calculus to enhance language expressivity, increasing the deterministic oracle recovery rate from 48/197 to 191/197.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on 600 unseen one-shot self-evolution tasks, identifying 197 capability-improving mutations that failed recoverability verification.
- **Baseline Comparison**: Under the original recovery representation, conventional repair strategies achieved a 0/197 success rate on these natural failures.
- **Key Results**:
    - With the extended recovery language, empirical oracle recovery reached 191/197.
    - A 2x2 grounding-by-expressivity intervention isolated bottlenecks: Exact grounding improved recovery from 0/48 to 38/48 in the original language stratum; extended language enabled 142/143 (99.3%) recovery in the S1 stratum requiring higher expressivity.
    - **Model Dependency**: On the gpt-oss-120b backbone, combining exact address diagnostics with rich language reduced recovery to 93.0% due to negative interaction; this interaction was not observed in the Qwen3.8-27B replication, indicating model-dependent behavior.

## 4. Conclusion
EvoUndo demonstrates that reliable agent self-evolution cannot rely solely on iterative prompting but requires the co-design of verification, state grounding, witness semantics, and recovery-language expressivity. By enforcing robust recoverability across states as an invariant, the framework addresses safety and stability concerns in long-lived autonomous systems.
