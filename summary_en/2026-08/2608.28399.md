## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: In financial markets, sequential policies that react systematically to price movements can become predictable to other participants. Large Language Model (LLM) agents offer a controlled setting to study such behavior as they make sequential decisions conditioned on prior actions, memories, and self-generated interpretations.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Previous research on LLMs in finance largely focuses on final returns or task performance, lacking deep analysis of whether LLM agents exhibit stable, exploitable directional structures (i.e., systematic adverse timing) in their sequential decision-making processes. Traditional methods struggle to isolate systematic timing errors caused by internal model logic from random noise.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed RetailAgent Framework**: A controlled multimodal experimental framework for tracing sequential LLM decisions and self-authored memory under anonymized market conditions.
- **Challenge 1: How to quantify and prove the existence of systematic adverse timing in LLMs?**
  - **Difficulty**: Isolating negative returns caused by the strategy itself from overall market trends and random fluctuations is complex.
  - **Solution**: The paper designs an "exposure-matched" metric that compares returns during "long" vs. "flat" intervals along the same stock's intraday path, removing the overall fraction of long decisions. This reveals persistent negative timing across modalities, horizons, states, and model families.
- **Challenge 2: How to verify that this negative timing is driven by the alignment between actions and subsequent returns, rather than random noise?**
  - **Difficulty**: Proving that observed negative returns are not accidental but stem from a systematic mismatch between the agent's decision logic and subsequent market moves.
  - **Solution**: Shuffling saved action sequences substantially attenuates the negative effect, confirming that the alignment between actions and subsequent returns drives the score. Additionally, feeding self-authored memories increases policy persistence, further exacerbating adverse timing.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: RetailAgent inputs anonymized rolling intraday price histories and endogenous states into an LLM via multimodal pathways. The agent chooses "long" or "flat" at each interval, with the framework recording observations, states, actions, memories, and subsequent returns.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - LLM agents exhibited persistent and significant negative timing across all tested modalities, time horizons, states, and model families.
  - The adverse timing effect was more pronounced on stock-days where the agent used both actions (long and flat).
  - Shuffling action sequences significantly reduced the negative effect, confirming the structural and predictable nature of the strategy's flaws.
- **Comparison**: Unlike traditional studies on retail trader behavior, this work reveals in a controlled environment that LLM agents act as stable "contrarian indicators," providing behavioral signals for studying how other market participants might respond to predictable AI policies.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper reveals through the RetailAgent framework that LLM agents exhibit stable and recoverable directional structures in financial trading decisions, specifically manifesting as systematic adverse timing. This negative timing is not random noise but is driven by a systematic mismatch between the agent's actions and subsequent market returns, exacerbated by the inclusion of self-authored memories. These findings suggest that current LLM trading agents may become predictable counterparties in the market, offering new insights into AI agent behavior in market microstructure.
