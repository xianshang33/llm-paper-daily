## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Long-horizon agentic tasks (e.g., long-context QA and deep search) require LLMs to iteratively retrieve, integrate, and maintain dispersed information across multi-turn interactions. Preserving all interaction histories leads to a continuously growing working context, which hampers efficiency and performance.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Recent proactive context management methods allow models to edit their context but suffer from three key limitations: (1) A limited toolset restricted to search, deletion, and summarization, lacking support for global planning, long-term memory, and adaptive compression; (2) Inefficient exploration in RL that treats all context management actions uniformly despite their heterogeneous impacts; (3) Coarse-grained credit assignment that assigns final trajectory-level rewards to all intermediate editing actions, failing to accurately evaluate individual actions.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ContextPilot**: A proactive context management framework for long-horizon agentic reasoning, teaching agents via fine-grained Reinforcement Learning (RL).
- **Challenge 1: Insufficient Toolset for Long-Horizon Complexity**
  - **Difficulty**: Simple deletion or summarization cannot handle cross-fragment information integration, structured memory maintenance, or global action planning.
  - **Solution**: Systematically augmented the toolset with planning, long-term memory, and soft context offloading tools, enabling finer control over information flow.
- **Challenge 2: Inefficient Exploration and Coarse Credit Assignment in RL**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional RL struggles to identify critical context editing decisions and treats all actions equally, leading to noisy training signals.
  - **Solution**: Proposed an RL method tailored for context management. It uses context and entropy variation to identify critical editing decisions for branch sampling and estimates action-level advantages from all branched trajectories passing through the corresponding action, achieving fine-grained credit assignment.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experiments**: Evaluated on long-context QA and deep search tasks.
- **Results**: ContextPilot consistently outperforms existing baselines across various base models and benchmarks.
- **Key Advantage**: Achieves stronger task performance while maintaining a more compact working context, demonstrating a superior balance between information compression and retaining key evidence.
- **Resources**: Code, project page, and models are open-sourced on GitHub and Hugging Face.

#### Summary
ContextPilot addresses three major pain points in context management for long-horizon agentic tasks by enhancing the toolset and improving RL algorithms. It introduces planning, memory, and offloading mechanisms, and employs fine-grained credit assignment based on context and entropy variations to boost RL training efficiency. Experiments show that the method improves task performance while reducing context length, offering an efficient solution for context management in long-horizon agentic reasoning.
