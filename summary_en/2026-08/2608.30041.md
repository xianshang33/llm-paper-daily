## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents execute tasks by invoking external skills (e.g., APIs, databases) and incorporating outputs into their context. This architecture introduces risks of indirect prompt injection, where attacker-controlled data influences subsequent privileged actions.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current defenses primarily focus on classifying untrusted content or authorizing operations. They fail to address how an agent's future authority should dynamically change once untrusted data contaminates its state, often relying on model-level detection or static constraints that do not effectively disconnect contaminated states from forbidden outcomes.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SkillGuard**: A harness-level enforcement layer that treats the ingestion of untrusted data as "contamination" and restricts future capabilities to isolate the resulting state from deployer-defined forbidden states.
- **Challenge 1: How to precisely quantify and restrict agent capabilities post-contamination without additional LLM inference?**
  - **Difficulty**: Model-based defenses incur latency and cost, while binary restrictions are too coarse, severely impacting usability.
  - **Solution**: SkillGuard uses a "Skill Impact Graph" to represent security-relevant transitions and "steerability signatures" for parameter control. It computes weighted capability restrictions using binary, fractional, or fractional-flow strategies without any auxiliary LLM inference.
- **Challenge 2: How to defend against compositional attacks where individual observations are harmless but dangerous in combination?**
  - **Difficulty**: Single observations may not trigger violations, making them hard to detect, but their combination can achieve attack goals, bypassing single-layer defenses.
  - **Solution**: SkillGuard mediates invocations via an inline reference monitor and enforces capability restrictions based on reachability analysis. It effectively blocks attack chains even under compositional attack benchmarks by strictly isolating states and degrading capabilities.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Setup**: Evaluated on four AgentDojo suites with two backend LLMs (Gemini 2.5 Flash and Llama3.3-70B). Baselines included No Defense, Spotlighting, CaMeL, and AttriGuard.
- **Results**:
  - Under Tool Knowledge attacks, SkillGuard eliminated attack success in three out of four suites for both backends and reduced it to 4.8% and 14.3% on Slack.
  - Against compositional attacks, it outperformed all baselines on Llama and matched the strongest baseline on Gemini while maintaining higher benign utility.
  - Fractional-flow restriction preserved significantly more capabilities than binary restriction at the same attack success rate.
- **Performance**: SkillGuard adds zero model calls or token overhead, offering a significant efficiency advantage over baselines requiring extra inference or complex processing.

## 4. Conclusion
SkillGuard introduces a reachability-based capability confinement framework that effectively addresses permission management in LLM agents facing indirect prompt injection. By leveraging skill impact graphs and dynamic, inference-free capability restrictions, it ensures high security while maximizing normal agent functionality, with negligible performance overhead. This approach provides a new system-level defense paradigm for building safer LLM agent systems.
