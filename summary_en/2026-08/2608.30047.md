## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Recent AI systems claim to enable autonomous scientific discovery, including algorithm invention and paper writing. However, it remains unclear whether these systems possess true "creativity"—the ability to produce solutions that are both novel and useful.
- **Limitations of Prior Work**: Existing evaluation methods are often limited to single-turn outputs or measure only the breadth of ideation. They fail to distinguish between novelty relative to the agent's own experience (internal) and novelty relative to the human solution space (historical) in multi-turn settings. Furthermore, reliance on human judgment is not scalable, and prior automated metrics often treat novelty as a static scalar rather than a dynamic process.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Framework for Evaluating LLM Agent Creativity**: The framework uses ML engineering tasks as a testbed to evaluate multi-turn agents across three dimensions: P-Creativity (psychological novelty relative to the agent's own trajectory), H-Creativity (historical novelty relative to a human corpus), and Usefulness (task performance).
- **Challenge 1: How to automate reliable creativity evaluation at scale?**
  - **Difficulty**: Human evaluation is the gold standard but does not scale; existing automated metrics lack granularity in distinguishing internal vs. external novelty.
  - **Solution**: Developed an "LLM-as-a-Judge" pipeline and verified its strong correlation with human judgments, providing a reliable automated metric for large-scale P-Creativity evaluation.
- **Challenge 2: How to quantify the gap between exploring novel solutions and achieving utility?**
  - **Difficulty**: Agents may generate novel but ineffective code, making it hard to determine if novelty translates to usefulness.
  - **Solution**: By analyzing agent trajectories and separately measuring P-Creativity, H-Creativity, and performance, the study reveals that agents' P-Creativity declines as they shift from exploration to exploitation, and high H-Creativity does not correlate with high performance.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Setup**: Evaluated two agent frameworks, AIDE and AIRA-Dojo, on 10 Kaggle-style ML tasks from MLE-Bench. Agent logs were segmented into episodes (Plan → Code → Execute → Score) for analysis.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  1. **P-Creativity Trend**: All agents exhibited declining psychological novelty as they transitioned from exploration to exploitation phases.
  2. **H-Creativity vs. Performance Paradox**: LLM agents demonstrated higher historical novelty (H-Creativity) than medal-winning humans, meaning their solutions were more unique compared to the human corpus. However, they achieved lower task performance.
- **Comparison**: Unlike related work that treats novelty as a single scalar against final submissions, this study shows that while current agents can explore novel regions of the solution space distant from human conventions, they lack the capacity to convert this novelty into improved performance.

#### Summary
This paper introduces a multi-dimensional framework for evaluating LLM agent creativity, addressing the gap in distinguishing internal from historical novelty. The key insight is that while current LLM agents possess the ability to explore non-traditional solution spaces (high H-Creativity), they significantly struggle to translate this novelty into useful, high-performing engineering outcomes. This suggests future research should focus not just on encouraging novelty, but on enhancing the agent's ability to make novel ideas practically effective.
