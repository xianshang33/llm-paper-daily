## What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Post-training pipelines for LLM agents increasingly adopt a two-stage formula: world-model training (next-state prediction to understand environment dynamics) followed by policy training (reward maximization to master tasks). While this sequential pipeline empirically outperforms policy-only training, the underlying mechanism remains opaque.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Prior studies either evaluate the composed pipeline as a black box or characterize post-training updates in isolation, without directly contrasting the world-model and policy stages. Consequently, it is unclear whether world models merely provide better initialization or leave behind representational structures that are meaningfully complementary to policy learning.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a**: Joint spectral and behavioral framework to dissect the geometric complementarity and interaction mechanisms between world models and policies in LLM agents.
- **Challenge 1**: How do world model knowledge and policy updates interact in parameter space? This is difficult because it requires distinguishing whether both stages learn the same solution or complementary structures. The paper analyzes additive parameter updates geometrically, finding that effective world-model updates are low-rank and share an input-feature subspace with policy updates while writing to nearly orthogonal output directions, demonstrating geometric complementarity.
- **Challenge 2**: Does policy training preserve and utilize aligned world knowledge effectively? This is challenging because standard RL may overwrite or ignore useful world-model representations. Through projection interventions, the paper finds sequential training induces greater robustness (learning alternative input pathways). It proposes training-free merging based on geometric input bases and an online world-model loss during policy RL, showing that explicitly preserving world knowledge improves performance.

## Implementation, Evaluation, and Comparison
- **Implementation & Evaluation**: The study conducts controlled experiments combining world-model and policy training, employing spectral decomposition (SVD) and behavioral analysis. Metrics include the rank of parameter updates, overlap of input/output subspaces, robustness under projection interventions, and the range of explored states/actions.
- **Results & Comparison**:
    1. **Geometric Findings**: World-model and policy updates share input subspaces but have nearly orthogonal output directions. Unlike works like Zhu et al. (2025) that focus on isolated update properties, this paper reveals structural differences between the two stages.
    2. **Behavioral Findings**: Sequentially trained agents explore a wider range of states and actions compared to separate policy RL.
    3. **Improvements**: Two proposed methods—training-free merging based on geometric input bases and adding an online world-model loss during policy RL—both outperform the untreated baseline, indicating room for optimization in preserving world knowledge in current pipelines.

## Summary
This paper reveals that world knowledge (world models) and task-directed ability (policies) in LLM agents are learned in geometrically complementary forms. World models provide low-rank updates with shared inputs but orthogonal outputs, upon which policies build. The study shows that while sequential training is effective, it does not fully preserve world knowledge. Engineering the interface between these stages (e.g., via improved merging strategies or auxiliary losses) can further enhance agent performance. Future post-training pipelines should prioritize optimizing this geometric interface.
