## 1. What the Paper Does

**Background**
Long-horizon agentic tasks go beyond chaining short interactions. They require Large Language Models (LLMs) to continuously explore, learn from experience, and adapt policies over thousands of steps in evolving dynamic environments with long-range dependencies.

**Why Existing Work Falls Short**
Current benchmarks often focus on short-term interactions or static environments, lacking simulation of complex dynamic events (e.g., promotions, disasters, supply chain shocks) and multi-round counterpart negotiations found in real business scenarios. They fail to effectively evaluate an agent's ability to make autonomous decisions, manage resources, and adapt strategies over a year-long horizon amidst fluctuating market demands and supply chain volatility.

## Core Contributions

**Proposed E-Commerce Bench**
The first open-source benchmark integrating multi-round counterpart negotiation and dynamic events into a year-long business operation. Agents concurrently run multiple online stores, performing market research, supplier negotiation, inventory sourcing, sales optimization, order fulfillment, return handling, and cash flow management to maximize year-end total assets.

**Challenge 1: Long-term Strategic Adaptation and Learning in Dynamic Environments**
*   **Challenge**: Over a 365-day simulation, market demand fluctuates wildly due to external events. Agents must learn from history and adjust procurement/sales strategies over time, requiring robust long-term memory and policy evolution capabilities.
*   **Solution**: The environment uses real e-commerce data for products/suppliers and deterministic demand/negotiation kernels for reproducibility. Results show models like Qwen3.8-Max-Preview demonstrate strategic learning by progressively lowering procurement prices across repeated orders.

**Challenge 2: Balancing Multi-dimensional Complex Business Objectives**
*   **Challenge**: Agents must balance profit maximization with fraud avoidance, operational efficiency, and solvency. Optimizing for one dimension can lead to failure in others (e.g., high profit but high fraud risk), making global optimization difficult.
*   **Solution**: Seven evaluation dimensions were designed. Experiments reveal no single model dominates all aspects; for instance, GPT-5.6 Sol has the highest assets but ranks low in fraud avoidance, highlighting trade-offs in complex business decision-making.

## Implementation and Evaluation

**Implementation**
*   **Environment**: Product and supplier data are derived from a real e-commerce platform. For reproducibility, both market sides are deterministic: customer behavior follows a fixed demand model, and supplier decisions are driven by a negotiation kernel (LLMs only handle verbalization).
*   **Task**: Agents operate multiple stores over 365 days, involving thousands of interaction steps.

**Evaluation Results**
*   **Overall**: 18 frontier models were evaluated, with no single model dominating all metrics.
*   **Top Performer**: GPT-5.6 Sol achieved the highest returns, growing a ¥100,000 stake to ¥1,431,425, but ranked 16th in fraud avoidance and trailed Fable5 in operational efficiency.
*   **Open-Weight Comparison**: Qwen3.8-Max-Preview led open-weight models with ¥416,252, outperforming GLM 5.2 (high) by 38%. It demonstrated the strongest long-horizon learning, progressively bargaining down prices.
*   **Multi-dimensional Analysis**: Radar charts normalized scores across seven dimensions (Profit, Learning, Negotiation, Fraud Avoidance, Execution, Efficiency, Solvency), revealing distinct strengths and weaknesses for each model family.

## 4. Conclusion

E-Commerce Bench fills the gap in evaluating long-horizon autonomous business operations. By simulating a realistic and dynamic e-commerce environment, it reveals disparities in current LLM agents' abilities to handle long-term dependencies, dynamic adaptation, and multi-objective trade-offs. While top closed-source models excel in asset growth, they show weaknesses in robustness and specific business capabilities, whereas some open-source models demonstrate strong long-term learning and adaptability.
