## What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) can closely mimic human writing, raising concerns about impersonation, trust, and detection in social settings. Adolescents frequently use generative AI but may struggle to recognize it and lack the experience to critically evaluate its source and reliability.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Prior research primarily focuses on static text detection in isolated environments (binary classification tasks), ignoring complex factors in real-time social interactions such as asynchronous communication and social relevance. Furthermore, most studies target adult populations, overlooking how adolescents' unique social cues and group identities influence their detection reasoning, leaving a gap in understanding detection in dynamic collaborative settings.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed DoppelBot**: A cooperative social deduction game designed to study how young people detect and respond to AI impersonation in live, collaborative environments.
- **Challenge 1: How to accurately detect AI in dynamic social interactions?**
  - **Difficulty**: High LLM fluency makes linguistic cues insufficient for distinguishing AI from humans in real-time conversations, which also involve non-verbal social signals.
  - **Solution**: Through repeated exposure in the DoppelBot game, middle schoolers improved their detection accuracy over time by shifting their strategy from relying on linguistic cues to leveraging shared social and contextual signals (e.g., timing, group rapport).
- **Challenge 2: What are adolescents' cognitive and reflective capabilities regarding AI impersonation?**
  - **Difficulty**: Adolescents may lack the critical thinking skills to understand AI limitations (e.g., lack of embodiment/intent) and privacy risks.
  - **Solution**: The study found that students not only improved detection skills but also demonstrated an understanding of AI limitations and reflected on broader issues like data privacy through the gaming experience.

## Implementation, Evaluation, and Comparison
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The team developed DoppelBot, a text-based cooperative game where AI agents impersonate players. Studies were conducted with middle schoolers, collecting game transcripts and voting data. An anonymized dataset was released to support future research.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - Students' AI detection accuracy significantly improved over time.
  - Detection strategies evolved from relying on linguistic features to utilizing social and contextual cues.
  - Students showed deep understanding of AI limitations and reflected on privacy concerns.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Unlike prior studies focusing on adults and static text with low/variable accuracy, this work focuses on adolescents in dynamic social scenarios, highlighting the critical role of social cues in AI detection and filling the gap in multi-user real-time interaction research.

## Summary
This paper systematically investigates middle schoolers' ability to identify AI impostors in real-time collaborative settings through the DoppelBot game. Results indicate that adolescents can improve detection accuracy by learning to leverage social contextual signals through repeated exposure, while also enhancing their awareness of AI ethics and privacy. This work provides new insights into young users' behavior in social AI environments and releases a dataset to facilitate further research.
