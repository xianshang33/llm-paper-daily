## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Autonomous scientific research agents are increasingly applied to end-to-end workflows (literature review, data analysis, experimentation, report generation). However, open-ended research tasks often lack clear specifications for analyses, methods, and success criteria.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current evaluation methods rely on expert-authored rubrics or generic LLM judgments. Expert rubrics are hard to scale, while generic judgments lack task-specific granularity, leading agents to miss key analyses, use inappropriate methods, or draw unsupported conclusions.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed AutoSciRub**: An "evaluation-first" framework that induces a task-specific executable rubric before research execution, using it to guide execution, criterion-level verification, and iterative revision.
- **Challenge 1: Making Implicit Requirements Explicit**. Open-ended instructions contain implicit scientific goals and evidence standards that are hard for agents to capture. **Solution**: AutoSciRub decomposes underspecified instructions into atomic scientific goals, grounds them in literature and data, and synthesizes specific, actionable, and verifiable criteria.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of Basis for Self-Correction**. Traditional agents lack specific standards to guide optimization after initial generation. **Solution**: The induced rubric enables criterion-level verification to identify unmet criteria, guiding targeted refinement of the research report and artifacts.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: Code is open-sourced. The core mechanism involves inducing the Rubric first, then guiding execution and revision.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - On **ResearchClawBench**, AutoSciRub consistently improved all tested configurations. It achieved an average gain of 2.08 points across three backbone LLMs (fixed Codex harness) and 2.95 points across three agent harnesses (fixed DeepSeek-V4-Flash backbone).
  - On a 20-task subset of **AstaBench E2E Discovery**, it achieved an average improvement of 16.8 points across three agent harnesses, while maintaining or increasing the number of successfully completed tasks.
- **Comparison**: Compared to baselines relying on manual expert rubrics or generic LLM judges, AutoSciRub provides a more effective and generalizable control mechanism by automatically inducing task-specific executable criteria, significantly reducing errors caused by ambiguous requirements.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the execution deviations in autonomous scientific agents caused by ambiguous open-ended tasks by proposing AutoSciRub. By adopting an "evaluation-first" strategy, it automatically generates rubrics with atomic goals and verifiable criteria before execution, effectively making implicit scientific requirements explicit. Experiments demonstrate that this method significantly improves performance across multiple benchmarks, offering a robust guidance mechanism for automated scientific research.
