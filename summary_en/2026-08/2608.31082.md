## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: The major bet in enterprise AI is deploying LLM agents to reason over unstructured data (web pages, reports, contracts, PDFs) to answer complex questions. However, current agents operate at prohibitive costs because each question requires repeatedly opening large documents to retrieve scattered evidence, consuming up to a million tokens per query.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: If data were pre-structured, the same questions would reduce to cheap database lookups (28x cheaper on FanOutQA). However, pre-structuring everything is not viable because documents contain vastly more potential structures than any workload will use, and useful structures are unknown until queries arrive. Traditional RAG is cheap but lacks accuracy, while full agentic reasoning is accurate but expensive.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed "Agentic Data Cracking"**: A method that adaptively and speculatively structures unstructured data as a byproduct of the reasoning process itself.
- **Challenge 1: How to extract reusable structure from expensive reasoning without significant overhead?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional methods either pre-process all data (wasteful) or re-parse documents for every query (expensive).
  - **Solution**: Whenever an agent opens a document to answer a question, a "cracking sub-agent" forks from the already-loaded context at marginal cost to extract grounded structure likely to serve related future queries.
- **Challenge 2: How to handle structural adaptivity under unknown query patterns?**
  - **Difficulty**: Useful structures depend on specific query loads; static structures cannot cover dynamic needs.
  - **Solution**: The structuring is adaptive (observed queries decide when and what matters) and speculative (goes beyond the current question). Over time, an increasing share of queries is fully covered by structured data, eliminating the need to open original documents, thus maintaining high agent accuracy at near-RAG costs.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on an extended FanOutQA benchmark, where each test question is accompanied by one additional related question to simulate repeated access.
- **Results**:
  - **Cost Reduction**: Agentic Data Cracking reduces costs by 53% compared to standard agentic reasoning while preserving nearly all accuracy.
  - **Efficiency Gain**: At the 10th percentile of per-question savings, the method runs 9x cheaper than traditional approaches.
  - **Comparison with Ideal**: While not reaching the 28x cost advantage of ideal pre-structured storage, it significantly narrows the gap between high-performance agentic reasoning and low-cost RAG.
  - **Comparison with RAG**: It retains most of the accuracy advantage of agentic reasoning over fixed top-k RAG while reducing costs to levels comparable to RAG.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper introduces "Agentic Data Cracking" to address the high cost of LLM agent reasoning over unstructured data. By adaptively extracting and accumulating structured knowledge during inference, the system improves with use, creating a shared knowledge substrate beneath the model. This approach significantly reduces query costs while maintaining high accuracy, marking a crucial step toward next-generation data infrastructure for agentic reasoning.
