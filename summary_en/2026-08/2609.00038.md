## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: The production default for evaluating LLM agents is "outcome-only" judgment, where a judge model assesses performance based solely on the user request and the final reply. This approach is structurally blind to agents that arrive at the correct answer via incorrect processes.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current evaluation methods rely on human labels or final outcomes, failing to distinguish between genuinely correct execution and lucky guesses with severe intermediate errors (e.g., skipping eligibility checks, ignoring tool outputs). These "silent faults" (where the customer-visible outcome remains correct) evade detection, allowing flawed agents to reach production.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A correctness-by-construction testbed for judges of tool-using agents, featuring a scripted Oracle policy and a fault injector that generates trajectories with precise ground truth labels (fault type, step, outcome survival) at zero annotation cost.
- **Challenge 1: How to quantify the blind spot of outcome-only evaluation for silent faults?**
    - **Difficulty**: In real-world scenarios, it is difficult to identify process errors behind correct results due to the lack of process-level Ground Truth.
    - **Solution**: The authors inject single faults at known steps and stratify them into "loud" (affecting outcome) and "silent" (not affecting outcome). Results show outcome-only judges catch 84% of loud faults but only 45% of silent ones, while falsely flagging 33% of correct trajectories.
- **Challenge 2: How to comprehensively evaluate different judge models on detection, localization, and cost?**
    - **Difficulty**: Simultaneously measuring detection rate, step localization accuracy, fault typing, and computational cost while excluding model uncertainty is complex.
    - **Solution**: Five judge designs were compared (programmatic rules, outcome-only, step-rubric at two sizes, self-consistency ensemble). The step-rubric judge achieved 77% recall on silent faults with zero false alarms at 3x the cost, whereas self-consistency tripled costs with no performance gain.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: A deterministic tool-using support-desk environment was built, along with a single-fault injector covering six fault types. All raw verdicts and an analysis pipeline are released, allowing offline reproduction of all results without further model calls.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - **Outcome-only Judge**: Appears reasonable overall (0.61 recall) but decomposes to poor performance on silent faults (0.45 recall) with high false positive rates (33%).
    - **Step-rubric Judge**: Achieves 77% recall on silent faults with zero false alarms, at 3x the cost.
    - **Specific Fault Type**: For "unsupported_claim" faults (appending a fictional promise to a perfect trajectory), all judges (including step-judges seeing the full trajectory) missed 82% of cases, and programmatic rules failed entirely.
    - **Self-Consistency**: Tripled costs without improving any metric.
    - **Small Model Pitfall**: An 8B parameter judge was found to behave as an "always-say-faulty" baseline in disguise.
- **Comparison**: Compared to traditional human-label-dependent or outcome-only evaluations, this method provides process-level granular assessment, exposing structural blind spots in current production defaults.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper highlights the severe limitations of "outcome-only" methods in LLM agent evaluation, particularly their low detection rate for "silent faults" where process errors yield correct results. By constructing a deterministic test environment with precise Ground Truth, the authors quantify the limitations of various judge models, demonstrating the superiority of step-level evaluation (Step-rubric) for detecting silent faults despite higher costs. The paper argues that future judge evaluations must stratify recall by outcome survival and releases fully reproducible artifacts to advance the field.
