## 1. What the Paper Did

### Background
Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are strong perceivers of visual data, but their capability to act within embodied systems remains unclear. This study investigates the extent to which MLLMs can function as generalist vision-language-action agents by embedding them directly into a drone's control loop, with the entire action space defined solely via prompts.

### Why Existing Work Falls Short
Recent systems (e.g., TypeFly, PIVOT, See Point Fly) achieve closed-loop control by narrowing the model's decision-making scope, often relying on external detectors, candidate point selection, or LiDAR planners. These approaches offload responsibility from the MLLM, failing to test its ability to autonomously follow action protocols and make end-to-end decisions without fine-tuning or specific function-calling schemas.

## Core Contributions

### Proposed DroneCATS Benchmark and Agent Architecture
Introduced DroneCATS-Agent, an architecture where the MLLM is a swappable component, and DroneCATS, a benchmark that treats the model as the independent variable. This framework enables the model to perform yaw searches, deliberate when unsure, and self-declare arrival without fine-tuning.

### Challenge 1: Action Protocol Adherence and Termination in Edge Models
**Challenge Description**: On resource-constrained edge devices, small models (e.g., 2B parameters) often navigate successfully but fail episodes by declaring arrival prematurely or not at all, struggling to maintain strict action protocols.
**Solution Approach**: The DroneCATS benchmark quantifies this gap, revealing that the bottleneck is not navigation but the model's lack of discipline in sustaining planning and emitting correct terminating actions, providing a basis for optimizing lightweight models' action logic.

### Challenge 2: View Confusion and Instruction Copying in Multi-Drone Coordination
**Challenge Description**: In multi-drone commanding tasks, small models tend to blindly copy single coordinates across distinct views, leading to coordination failures and widening the performance gap with frontier models.
**Solution Approach**: By including multi-drone tasks in the benchmark, the study exposes defects in spatial reasoning across multiple views, highlighting the need for improved consistency in protocol execution and spatial understanding.

## Implementation and Evaluation

### Implementation and Deployment
The study evaluated a range of models from frontier closed-source ones to open-source small models (down to 2B parameters). The DroneCATS-Agent architecture supports plug-and-play model replacement, running directly in the drone control loop with prompt-defined action spaces.

### Evaluation Results and Comparison
- **Key Paradox**: Small open-source models often navigate into the success radius more reliably than frontier models but fail due to incorrect termination declarations. This indicates that spatial perception is intact, but action protocol adherence is weak.
- **Multi-Drone Tasks**: Small models performed poorly in multi-drone scenarios, mechanically copying coordinates without understanding multi-view spatial relationships.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Unlike PIVOT or SPF which reduce control to pixel pointing, or Fly0 which relies on external LiDAR, this study shows that even simple embodied settings are unsolved, with the primary bottleneck being the model's "action discipline" rather than perception or basic navigation.

## 4. Conclusion

This paper reveals through the DroneCATS benchmark that while MLLMs possess sufficient spatial perception for drone navigation, they lack the "discipline" to persistently plan and accurately terminate tasks in low-cost edge computing environments. The key future challenge is closing this gap by developing lightweight models that are both fast and strictly adherent to action protocols.
