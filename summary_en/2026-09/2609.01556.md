## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Existing embedding retrieval benchmarks typically validate cases where surface form (wording) and semantic meaning align. However, in structural retrieval, surface form and underlying structure are deliberately decoupled (e.g., math problems requiring the same technique but using different vocabulary; agent trajectories following the same procedure on different objects).
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current evaluation methods cannot distinguish whether models truly understand task structure or merely rely on literal token similarity. Single-domain studies conflate embedding model properties with benchmark construction biases, failing to accurately assess retrieval capabilities in "same structure, different form" scenarios.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a cross-domain structural retrieval evaluation protocol**: Evaluated embedding retrieval under a unified protocol across two unrelated domains: competition mathematics (MathNet-Retrieve) and embodied-agent trajectories (ALFWorld-derived), featuring tiered disguises, exact chance baselines, Rank-1 failure taxonomy, and bootstrap confidence intervals.
- **Challenge 1: Embedding models heavily anchor on surface form rather than deep structure**. In mathematics, Hit@1 accuracy drops to 0.0% under heavy disguise, with models preferring lexically similar incorrect answers despite the correct answer being in the Top 10. In agent trajectories, performance falls to or below random chance when target objects differ. **Solution**: Introduced a lexical reranker as a control variable, which hurt performance in mathematics but helped in trajectories (closing 26-36% of the gap), diagnosing whether surface variation is adversarial or incidental and revealing the literal token anchoring bias.
- **Challenge 2: LLM reranker recovery is domain-dependent and influenced by memorization**. While LLM rerankers recovered 5-63% of the gap in mathematics and 43-76% in trajectories, effect sizes varied by domain, and gains in mathematics were concentrated in well-known competitions, suggesting memorization. **Solution**: Downstream paired experiments showed no statistical difference between Oracle and adversarially bad retrieval (McNemar p = 0.678), indicating that solver accuracy is largely a truncation proxy, leaving little headroom for retrieval improvements.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Datasets & Setup**:
    - Math: MathNet-Retrieve (500 queries, 117,088 items).
    - Trajectories: ALFWorld-derived (118 queries, 336 trajectories).
- **Key Results**:
    - **Embedding Failure**: Hit@1 was 0.0% in heavily disguised math tasks; below chance in trajectory tasks involving different objects/receptacles.
    - **Lexical Reranker Diagnostic**: Hurt performance in math, improved it in trajectories, proving the differing nature of surface variation.
    - **LLM Reranker Performance**: Positive direction across all judges, but magnitudes were not transferable. Math gains (+19.8 points in specific cells) indicated memorization.
    - **Downstream Impact**: No significant difference between Oracle and poor retrieval in downstream solving tasks, as solver bottlenecks were related to generation length/truncation rather than retrieval quality.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper reveals severe surface-form bias in current embedding retrieval models, showing they prefer literal token matching over underlying task structure. Through cross-domain comparison and rigorous controls, it demonstrates that single-benchmark evaluations are misleading. Furthermore, it highlights that in current downstream applications (like math solving), retrieval improvements yield diminishing returns because the bottleneck lies in the generation phase, not retrieval. This work provides critical insights for building more robust structural retrieval benchmarks and evaluation frameworks.
