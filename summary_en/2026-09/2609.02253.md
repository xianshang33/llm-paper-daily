## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Deep Research Agents (DRAs) augment LLMs with external tools to answer complex, long-horizon questions via multi-turn reasoning. Learning from prior experience is critical for continual improvement.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current memory mechanisms are limited. Episodic memory retrieves verbose, task-specific traces that burden decision-making. Reflective or procedural memory methods distill skills into static text summaries or fixed prompts, which remain decoupled from downstream policy adaptation, failing to directly optimize the agent's strategy during inference.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed APEx Framework**: A hierarchical experience utilization framework featuring a closed-loop architecture of Executor, Distiller, and Planner, organizing history into instance-level trajectory memories and category-level procedural skills.
- **Challenge 1: How to extract generalizable procedural skills from lengthy, noisy histories?**
  - **Difficulty**: Raw interaction trajectories contain significant noise and are task-specific, making direct retrieval inefficient and hard to generalize.
  - **Solution**: APEx employs a three-stage alternating GRPO training paradigm. It enables reward-guided skill distillation (instead of fixed-prompt generation) to compress instance-level trajectories into abstract, category-level procedural skills.
- **Challenge 2: How to achieve test-time policy adaptation using distilled skills without ground-truth labels?**
  - **Difficulty**: In open-domain testing, the lack of ground truth makes direct fine-tuning prone to policy drift, and traditional methods cannot dynamically adjust strategies for new queries.
  - **Solution**: Introduces Skill-guided Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (TTRL). Distilled skills serve as procedural priors to regularize the online Planner's adaptation, enabling ground-truth-free self-improvement while preventing policy drift via skill-alignment regularization.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Training**: APEx optimizes the Executor, Distiller, and Planner modules through a closed-loop interaction using a three-stage alternating Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm.
- **Evaluation Results**: Experiments on 7 benchmarks demonstrate state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance.
- **Comparison with Related Work**:
  - Outperforms GPT-5.4 by 14.7 points.
  - Surpasses the strongest memory-augmented baseline by 3.0 points.
  - Demonstrates superior capability in handling complex long-horizon tasks by reducing decision burden and enhancing policy adaptability compared to traditional episodic and static procedural memory methods.

#### Summary
APEx addresses the core bottleneck in experience utilization for DRAs: transforming historical interactions into generalizable procedural skills and adapting to new tasks unsupervised at test time. By leveraging a hierarchical memory structure and reward-guided distillation, APEx shifts the paradigm from "retrieving the past" to "adapting to the future," significantly improving performance and robustness in complex question-answering tasks.
