## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Retail supply chain operations rely on coupled decision modules (e.g., assignment, batching, picking, packing) that must adapt to evolving business requirements. Large Language Models (LLMs) offer a natural-language interface for adjusting these systems.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current methods primarily focus on generating or modifying individual optimization models. They struggle to extend to heterogeneous decision pipelines because a single requirement may admit multiple intervention paths with varying downstream effects, making simple point-wise optimization insufficient for handling complex couplings.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Graph-Constrained Agentic Framework**: Formulates requirement-driven adaptation as the joint selection of an intervention route and admissible module-level changes.
- **Challenge 1: Complexity of Multiple Intervention Paths and Downstream Effects in Heterogeneous Pipelines**. The difficulty lies in the large, structured search space where upstream changes alter downstream inputs and feasible decisions. The method addresses this by having domain agents expose admissible reformulation interfaces, while a central processor searches over bounded intervention paths, thereby constraining the search space and ensuring validity.
- **Challenge 2: Selection Among Locally Valid but Globally Suboptimal Reformulations**. The challenge is that locally valid module changes may lead to different system-level outcomes after downstream re-execution. The method resolves this by validating and comparing candidates using downstream Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) to ensure end-to-end effectiveness.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation**: Collaborated with a large retail partner to evaluate 100 warehouse requirements elicited from practitioner interviews. Used GPT, Qwen, and DeepSeek as base LLMs.
- **Results vs. Related Work**: Compared to direct LLM reformulation, the proposed framework improved correctness and end-to-end success rates across all three models. Specifically, end-to-end success increased from 72–76% to 79–83%, demonstrating the framework's superiority in handling complex supply chain adaptation tasks.

#### Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of adapting coupled decision modules in retail supply chains to evolving requirements by proposing a graph-constrained agentic framework. By bounding the intervention path search space and validating with downstream KPIs, the method effectively handles the complexities of multiple intervention paths and downstream effects in heterogeneous pipelines. Experiments show significant improvements in adaptation capability and success rates in real-world retail scenarios.
