## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: The rise of Agentic AI and LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) offers unprecedented automation capabilities but raises critical concerns regarding the preservation of human-centered values like privacy, fairness, and safety.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Traditional software engineering focuses on functional correctness, while current MAS research often prioritizes performance over value alignment. Existing approaches lack architectural design guidelines that proactively embed ethical values into coordination mechanisms and system topologies, failing to prevent bias propagation and privacy violations in autonomous interactions.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a** framework for Value-Preserving Architectures that links architectural design decisions directly to human-centered values to build trustworthy MAS.
- **Challenge 1: Privacy Violations in Data Flow**. Personal data traversing agent boundaries can violate data minimization principles. **Solution**: The paper proposes a privacy-aware architecture with a federated topology, keeping data localized and limiting sharing to necessary contexts to preserve privacy.
- **Challenge 2: Systemic Bias and Lack of Pluralism**. Central orchestrators may filter out minority perspectives, and historical biases can be amplified. **Solution**: It introduces a distributed architecture to promote pluralism and diversity, along with a "Guard-Agent" architecture specifically designed to detect and mitigate unfairness through decentralized oversight.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The paper primarily presents theoretical architectural patterns and illustrates them through representative real-world use cases rather than providing a specific codebase or large-scale deployment metrics.
- **Evaluation Results**: As a conceptual and architectural paper, the evaluation is qualitative, demonstrating how the proposed patterns structurally mitigate ethical risks in specific scenarios, rather than relying on quantitative benchmarks.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Unlike traditional MAS designs focused solely on functionality, this work emphasizes "Responsible Software Engineering," filling the gap in system-level value alignment and offering foundational architectural guidelines superior to post-hoc filtering methods.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper argues that architectural choices in MAS significantly influence value-oriented behavior. By defining patterns such as federated privacy-aware structures, distributed pluralistic systems, and guard-agent mechanisms, it lays the foundation for a unified set of guidelines for designing trustworthy Agentic AI systems, shifting the engineering paradigm from mere functional correctness to robust value alignment.
