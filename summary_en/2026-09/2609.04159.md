## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents are increasingly proposed as autonomous analysts in Security Operations Centers (SOCs) to handle massive volumes of security alerts.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current approaches are unreliable at enterprise scale due to two structural limitations: finite context windows cannot hold multi-thousand-host authentication graphs, and free-form text generation offers no guarantee that recommended containment actions are consistent with the underlying network topology.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Sentinel-RL Architecture**: An enterprise-grade agentic-SOC architecture that decouples topological reasoning from semantic reasoning. It uses a heterogeneous graph attention encoder to summarize live authentication subgraphs into fixed-dimensional states, a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) policy to map states to constrained investigative actions, and restricts the LLM to consuming policy recommendations and generating analyst-readable narratives gated by a critic.
- **Challenge 1: Network Topology Exceeds LLM Context Limits**
  - **Difficulty**: Mid-sized corporate networks involve tens of thousands to millions of dynamic connections, far exceeding current LLM context windows, causing models to "guess" when tracking lateral movement.
  - **Solution**: Introduces a heterogeneous graph attention encoder that compresses complex real-time authentication subgraphs into fixed-dimensional state vectors, allowing the RL policy to understand global topology without relying on LLM context.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of Consistency and Safety Guarantees in LLM-Generated Actions**
  - **Difficulty**: Actions like host isolation or IP blocking directly impact business continuity; probabilistic text generation by LLMs cannot provide hard rule constraints, auditability, or consistency.
  - **Solution**: Employs a PPO reinforcement learning policy to output actions from a constrained space, ensuring decisions adhere to topological logic and safety rules, while the LLM is restricted to explanation and report generation.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Data Ingestion Efficiency**: Using a two-phase CREATE ingestion pattern, a 24M-edge authentication subgraph was loaded into Neo4j in 14.2 minutes on a single 32-core node, approximately 24x faster than the canonical MERGE-based pipeline.
- **Alert Engine Performance**: The sliding-window alert engine reliably triggered a threshold of "25 events per 10 seconds" in ≤2.5 seconds wall time across 50 trials.
- **Detection Accuracy and Convergence**: PPO training converged over 200 iterations with a mean episodic return of 8.74±0.31. On labeled red-team events, it achieved a held-out precision of 0.91 and recall of 0.87.
- **End-to-End Latency**: The integrated containment loop completed the full "detect→investigate→recommend→human-approve" cycle with a median time of 6.3 seconds.
- **Engineering Contributions**: Provided reusable engineering patterns (hot-node deadlock workaround) and portable HPC deployment patterns (anchor-node co-location), along with an enterprise-readiness analysis covering false-positive economics, reversibility, and audit compliance.

## 4. Conclusion
Sentinel-RL addresses the core bottlenecks of LLMs in SOC automation through a neuro-symbolic hybrid approach. By offloading topological reasoning to a graph-based reinforcement learning policy while retaining LLMs for semantic understanding and reporting, the system achieves enterprise-grade throughput and low-latency response while ensuring action consistency and topology awareness, providing a reliable engineering paradigm for automated security operations.
