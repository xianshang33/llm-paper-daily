## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Autonomous coding agents (AI Scientists) are increasingly proposed for conducting analyses and writing research reports. However, executing a prescribed analysis is fundamentally different from making a scientific discovery.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current benchmarks are designed for "reproduction," where tasks, data, and rubrics are built around hidden target studies, rewarding the recovery of known results. This setup fails to distinguish engineering execution capabilities from open-ended scientific inquiry and often relies on human grading, which hinders automated iterative improvement of agents.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed TruthInsightBench**: An evidence-grounded benchmark designed for "discovery" rather than "reproduction," aiming to automatically evaluate open-ended scientific discovery agents.
- **Challenge 1: How to quantify evidentiary maturity in open-ended scientific discovery?**
  - **Difficulty**: Scientific discovery lacks a single ground-truth answer, rendering traditional matching-based scoring ineffective. Human evaluation is costly and non-scalable.
  - **Solution**: Designed a fixed LLM-based judge that operationalizes evidentiary maturity into 29 artifact-grounded items across six dimensions. The scoring is fully automated and deterministically aggregated, requiring no human intervention, thus supporting self-improving loops.
- **Challenge 2: How to distinguish "execution capability" from "scientific judgment"?**
  - **Difficulty**: Existing agents may excel at coding and running analyses but lack the scientific rigor (e.g., controls, robustness checks) needed to establish trustworthy claims. Existing benchmarks fail to capture this gap.
  - **Solution**: Utilized 40 blind tasks (providing only neutral objectives and frozen data, withholding conclusions) to force agents to determine supported claims. Results showed that while agents perform well in execution, they lack key discriminating acts for trustworthy claims, clearly quantifying the deficit in scientific judgment.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The benchmark comprises 40 blind tasks derived from peer-reviewed studies across 10 scientific domains. Data and scoring code are open-sourced. Evaluation uses a fixed LLM judge to score agent-generated claims based on evidence.
- **Evaluation Results**: Four coding agents evaluated on a single frozen base model formed a narrow performance plateau (58.4–60.3/100), with no statistically significant pairwise separation.
- **Comparison & Analysis**:
  - **Strengths**: Agents performed competently in evidence auditability and novelty, handling analysis and reporting well.
  - **Weaknesses**: Agents largely lacked discriminating acts required for trustworthy claims, such as controls, robustness checks, falsifiability, and cross-dataset generalization.
  - **Conclusion**: The bottleneck is scientific judgment, not coding ability. Unlike existing works focusing on reproducing known results, TruthInsightBench reveals the current limitations of AI Scientists in genuine scientific discovery.

#### Summary
TruthInsightBench fills the gap in evaluating open-ended scientific discovery agents by providing an automated, evidence-based scoring mechanism that clearly distinguishes analysis execution from scientific discovery. The study highlights that the primary bottleneck for current agents is a lack of scientific judgment rather than coding proficiency. This benchmark provides a measurable target for assessing and improving the true discovery capabilities of AI Scientists.
