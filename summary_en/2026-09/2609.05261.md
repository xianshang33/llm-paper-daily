## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents increasingly rely on execution traces to master complex interactive tasks. Historical trajectories preserve decision processes and provide behavioral knowledge beyond pre-trained models, such as effective action patterns and long-term strategies.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current paradigms are bottlenecked by shallow trajectory retrieval and flat skill summarization, fundamentally ignoring temporal dependencies and outcome-conditioned topology. Existing methods focus on what information to retain but ignore behavioral relations among units, failing to distinguish reliable transitions from failure-prone shortcuts or explain how local behaviors compose into successful strategies.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Trace2Tower**: A transition-aware EigenTrace framework that distills raw trajectories into a robust skill hierarchy.
- **Challenge 1: How to extract general and reliable behavioral patterns from noisy, task-specific raw trajectories?**
    - **Difficulty**: Raw trajectories are lengthy, redundant, and contain unnecessary exploration and failure branches, leading to noise and error propagation if reused directly.
    - **Solution**: Trace2Tower abstracts step-level interactions into canonical events, constructing a unified graph governed by semantic compatibility, transition dynamics, and outcome evidence. It uses novel contrastive spectral decomposition to isolate stable, success-aligned behavioral modes while rigorously suppressing failure-prone shortcuts.
- **Challenge 2: How to organize extracted patterns into a reusable, hierarchical skill system?**
    - **Difficulty**: Existing skill libraries are often flat, lacking modeling of hierarchical relationships between action templates, procedural routines, and overarching strategies, hindering context-efficient reuse.
    - **Solution**: The extracted modes organically populate a dynamic "skill tower" ranging from low-level action templates to high-level task strategies. This tower is continuously refined via verifier-guided feedback, forming a multi-level skill hierarchy.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on ALFWorld and WebShop benchmarks.
- **Key Results**:
    - On ALFWorld, Trace2Tower achieved an 87.31% success rate with only 10.35 steps and 0.26 invalid actions.
    - On WebShop, it reached a 50.67% exact success rate.
- **Comparison**: Trace2Tower significantly outperforms existing baselines (such as experience reuse or skill construction methods like ExpeL) in both task mastery and context-efficient experience reuse across both benchmarks.

## 4. Conclusion
Trace2Tower addresses the limitations of shallow retrieval and flat summarization in LLM agent trajectory utilization by introducing a transition-aware EigenTrace induction mechanism. By constructing a unified behavior graph and applying contrastive spectral decomposition, it effectively removes failure shortcuts, extracts stable success-aligned patterns, and organizes them into a multi-level dynamic skill tower. The method demonstrates superior performance and efficiency in complex interactive tasks, offering a new paradigm for continuous learning and experience reuse in agents.
