## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: AI Agents are increasingly deployed in real-world settings, interacting with external tools and making sequential decisions with limited human oversight. This creates an urgent need for reliable and auditable explanations of agent actions and rationales.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Traditional Explainable AI (XAI) methods are designed for static, single-step predictive models and fail to provide the process-level transparency required for interactive, multi-step systems. They struggle to capture agent trajectories, reasoning processes, and tool interactions in dynamic environments.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a post-hoc XAI framework based on execution traces**: This framework transforms lengthy agent execution traces into structured reports and faithful natural-language explanations explicitly grounded in observable behavior.
- **Challenge 1: How to ensure explanation faithfulness and avoid hallucinations?**
  - **Difficulty**: Naive LLM-generated explanations often contain unsupported claims or hallucinations, failing to reflect the agent's actual behavioral logic.
  - **Solution**: The method relies solely on execution traces, strictly grounding explanations in observable behaviors. This generates high-fidelity explanations and reliably identifies unsupported claims, unjustified actions, and evidence gaps.
- **Challenge 2: How to achieve generality across different architectures and tasks?**
  - **Difficulty**: Significant variations exist among different agent architectures, environments, and tasks, making model-specific explanation methods hard to generalize.
  - **Solution**: By relying only on standardized execution traces rather than internal model parameters or specific architectural details, the framework is applicable across diverse agent architectures, environments, and tasks.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation**: The study conducted both human and automated evaluations across multiple benchmarks and different agent architectures.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Results demonstrate that the framework produces high-quality, trace-faithful explanations. It outperforms naive LLM-generated baselines by more reliably identifying unsupported claims, unjustified actions, and evidence gaps, thereby enhancing the reliability and auditability of explanations.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the lack of process-level transparency in AI Agents by proposing a general-purpose post-hoc XAI framework. By leveraging only execution traces, it generates structured and faithful natural-language explanations, effectively overcoming the limitations of traditional XAI methods in dynamic, multi-step interaction scenarios. Evaluations confirm that the method surpasses simple LLM-generated baselines in explanation quality and error detection, supporting the trustworthy deployment of AI Agents.
