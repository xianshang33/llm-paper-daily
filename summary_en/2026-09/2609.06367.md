## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: LLM-as-a-Judge has become a popular paradigm for evaluating natural language generation due to its flexibility and scalability. However, the inherent randomness of LLM outputs introduces uncertainty, limiting reliability in high-stakes domains like healthcare and finance.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current uncertainty quantification methods, primarily based on Conformal Prediction, are typically applied to a single LLM judge. These approaches overlook the variability introduced by different LLM evaluators (due to differences in training data, architecture, etc.), leading to model-dependent uncertainty estimates that may be overconfident and lack robustness.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A Robust Conformal Consensus framework for multi-agent LLM-as-a-Judge interval evaluation.
- **Challenge 1: Single-Model Bias and Ignored Variability**. The difficulty lies in the fact that a single LLM's score cannot reflect the true variability of the evaluation process, and significant disagreements exist between different models. The paper addresses this by incorporating multiple independent LLM judges to construct conformal prediction intervals, explicitly capturing and quantifying inter-model discrepancies for more stable uncertainty estimates.
- **Challenge 2: Effective Aggregation of Multi-Source Uncertainty**. The challenge is how to reasonably integrate prediction intervals from multiple LLMs with different distributions and biases while maintaining statistical coverage guarantees. The paper proposes an interval adaptation framework that recalibrates and aggregates intervals from multiple judges, ensuring the final results have valid coverage and reduced extreme biases through consensus.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The framework is compatible with existing conformal prediction methods and requires no fine-tuning of the underlying LLMs, only a small calibration set. Experiments involve nine different LLMs as judges.
- **Evaluation Results**: Extensive experiments demonstrate that the method produces valid prediction intervals with guaranteed coverage. Compared to traditional conformal prediction using a single LLM judge, the multi-judge interval aggregation yields more stable and reliable evaluation outcomes.
- **Comparison**: Unlike existing single-model conformal prediction methods, this approach better handles inter-LLM variability, avoiding robustness issues caused by the choice of a specific LLM, and provides more trustworthy uncertainty quantification.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the under-explored uncertainty in LLM-as-a-Judge evaluations by proposing a robust framework based on multi-agent conformal prediction. By integrating scores from multiple LLM judges and constructing prediction intervals, the method effectively overcomes the limitations and variability of single-model evaluations, providing statistically guaranteed, stable, and reliable uncertainty quantification for automated assessments in high-stakes applications.
