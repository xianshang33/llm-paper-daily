## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Social Deduction Games (SDGs), such as Werewolf and Avalon, require agents to reason under partial observability by maintaining "relational beliefs" about hidden roles and team alignments. LLM agents are increasingly used as testbeds for long-horizon social reasoning.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current LLM-agent approaches primarily optimize actions and in-game speech through prompting or preference optimization without explicitly grounding them in relational beliefs. This leads to strategically inconsistent behaviors, especially in compact LLMs, such as making accusations based on surface-level cues rather than latent player relations, or voting inconsistently with their own reasoning, as existing methods reinforce behaviors even when the underlying social reasoning is flawed.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: Multi-Agent Relational Belief Optimization (MARBO), a belief-grounded preference optimization framework that leverages relational beliefs to guide strategic decisions and in-game speech.
- **Challenge 1: Strategic Inconsistency due to Lack of Relational Grounding**. The difficulty lies in the disconnect between an agent's internal reasoning about player relationships and its external actions. MARBO addresses this by providing preference feedback only when behaviors are supported by reliable relational beliefs and lead to strategically favorable outcomes, ensuring consistent learning under uncertainty.
- **Challenge 2: Fragility of Compact LLMs in Long-Horizon Reasoning**. Small models struggle to maintain coherent context and logic over long games. MARBO mitigates this by constraining optimization to belief-supported actions, filtering out noisy or contradictory behaviors, thus enabling compact models to learn robust strategic patterns.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: MARBO is a training framework with open-source code available. It was evaluated in representative SDG environments.
- **Evaluation Results**: Experiments demonstrate that MARBO enables compact LLM agents to consistently outperform existing baselines. Improvements are seen in reduced false accusations, higher consistency between voting decisions and internal reasoning, and more strategically effective speech.
- **Comparison**: Compared to standard prompting, reflection mechanisms, and generic preference optimization, MARBO shows superior performance in tasks requiring deep social reasoning, effectively handling partial observability and long-term dependencies by avoiding strategic inconsistencies.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the issue of strategic inconsistency in LLM agents within social deduction games caused by the lack of relational belief grounding. By introducing MARBO, which ties preference optimization to reliable relational beliefs, the study significantly enhances the reasoning consistency and gameplay performance of compact LLMs in complex social environments, offering a new paradigm for multi-agent optimization under partial observability.
