## 1. What the Paper Did

**Background**
In an open Agentic Web, agents running frozen models from different vendors need to share knowledge about "which tool to call and when" while keeping their interaction histories private.

**Why Existing Work Falls Short**
Current methods for knowledge sharing are inadequate:
1. **Weights and adapters cannot transfer knowledge across heterogeneous, frozen architectures.**
2. **Flat text (prompts, example pools) lacks structure:** Protocols cannot distinguish between noise statistics, mergeable rules, and documentation, making privacy preservation (e.g., sensitivity estimation for differential privacy) and conflict resolution ill-defined.

## Core Contributions

**Proposed Typed Federated Artifacts framework and its instantiation, SYNAPSE**
This framework exchanges tool-routing knowledge as schema-validated objects, where each field has a defined role, type, and validation rule.

**Challenge 1: How to define clear privacy protection and conflict resolution mechanisms across heterogeneous frozen models?**
*   **Difficulty:** Flat text makes sensitivity estimation difficult, and string-level merging can lose nuanced rule differences or introduce noise.
*   **Solution:** By defining type signatures for each field, per-field differential privacy (based on declared rather than estimated sensitivity) and per-field conflict resolution (detecting and preserving dissent rather than voting on whole strings) become well-defined operations.

**Challenge 2: How to effectively merge experiences from different clients into actionable routing knowledge?**
*   **Difficulty:** Experiences regarding the same tool may contradict across clients, and分散ed experiences need to be integrated into a unified knowledge base for retrieval.
*   **Solution:** Construct a structured compendium containing tool metadata, success scenarios, precautions, and call templates. Edge aggregators use typed operators to merge contributions, keeping per-field majority votes and recording dissent as typed conflict logs, which routers read as "do not use" conditions during inference.

## Implementation and Evaluation

**Experimental Setup**
*   **Datasets:** StableToolBench (cleaned, removing junk and duplicates, covering 3,180 tools) and τ-bench retail.
*   **Baselines:** Centralized knowledge base, flat text merging methods, TF-IDF classifier.

**Key Results**
1.  **Near-Centralized Performance:** On StableToolBench, the federated compendium's routing performance is within 1.1 points of the centralized version, with 20 MB of JSON transmitted per client per round.
2.  **Advantage of Structured Data:** Presenting experience as typed fields rather than flat strings improves performance by 8.5 points on clean data and 7.4 points under 60% injected contradiction. The study shows that "typed merging" and "structured rendering" are inseparable; typed merging shown as flat text performs worst.
3.  **τ-bench Improvement:** On τ-bench retail, all compendium arms improved GPT-4o agent's per-step tool-call accuracy by at least 6.7 points, attributed mainly to format optimization rather than federated experience itself.
4.  **Cautionary Findings:** On a topic-labeled math proxy and the original StableToolBench, a simple TF-IDF classifier outperformed all LLM routing arms (by 48 and 26 points, respectively) due to label leakage and verbatim duplication of test queries in the benchmark pool. This indicates current benchmarks fail to measure routing capabilities for unlabeled tools.

## 4. Conclusion

This paper introduces the concept of Typed Federated Artifacts to address the challenge of sharing tool-routing knowledge among heterogeneous, frozen LLM agents. Through the SYNAPSE instantiation, it demonstrates the advantages of structured, schema-validated knowledge exchange in privacy, conflict resolution, and cross-model transfer. Despite identifying evaluation biases due to data leakage in specific benchmarks, the method shows efficiency close to centralized learning in standardized environments and significantly outperforms flat text exchange. The study highlights the importance of establishing standardized units of knowledge exchange in the Agentic Web and points out limitations in current benchmarks for assessing true routing capabilities.
