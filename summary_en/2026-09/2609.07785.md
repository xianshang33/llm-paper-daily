## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: LLM-agent leaderboards are often interpreted as direct indicators of agent quality, where a higher rank implies a better agent. However, an agent's performance is a composite of the base model, scaffold (prompts, tools, control logic), label source (tests, expert annotations, LLM judges), and resource budgets.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current public evaluation logs and leaderboard scores often ignore these configurational differences. When systems differ in task mixture, label sources, release details, or cost rules, simple rank comparisons do not support claims of pairwise superiority. For instance, different label sources can yield contradictory rankings, and existing methods lack explicit definitions of estimands and statistical uncertainty, leading to fragile conclusions based on noisy score differences.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed an estimand-aware pairwise decision procedure**: This framework clarifies what leaderboard scores estimate and determines under what conditions one agent can be statistically deemed superior to another.
- **Challenge 1: Ambiguity of the Estimand leads to inconsistent comparison baselines.**
  - **Difficulty**: Different evaluation designs (e.g., task weighting, label sources, pass@k rules) correspond to different estimands. Comparing raw scores directly conflates these distinct metrics.
  - **Solution**: The procedure mandates stating the comparison target and measurement source, checks for common support, and evaluates the supported difference using a stated uncertainty rule and practical margin, ensuring comparisons are made within the same estimand context.
- **Challenge 2: Statistical uncertainty in finite samples is often ignored, leading to spurious superiority claims.**
  - **Difficulty**: Small score differences in finite samples may stem from random noise rather than true performance gaps, and results can be sensitive to target reweighting.
  - **Solution**: By employing controlled checks and confidence intervals (including max-deviation simultaneous intervals), the method only reports a statistically supported winner when evidence permits; otherwise, it identifies limiting conditions such as lack of support or label disagreement.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Datasets**: Empirical analysis was conducted on SWE-bench, AgentRewardBench, and tau2-bench. DataAgentBench and Open Agent were used to demonstrate estimability from coarser public records.
- **Key Results**:
  - On SWE-bench Verified, among 45 pairwise comparisons of the top 10 agents, only 5 were stable, 1 was target-sensitive, and 39 were underpowered using descriptive pointwise 95% intervals; no pairs were resolved using max-deviation simultaneous intervals.
  - On AgentRewardBench, expert labels ranked Claude 3.7 Sonnet first, while GPT-4o-mini judge labels ranked GPT-4o first, highlighting the impact of label sources.
  - On tau2-bench, using pass@4 instead of pass@1 increased scores by 17.7–20.4 points, and cost-aware utility rules altered the selected best system.
- **Comparison Conclusion**: Unlike traditional approaches that treat leaderboard ranks as definitive orderings, this study reveals that most close rank differences are statistically unresolved. It demonstrates that the choice of proxy labels and utility rules fundamentally changes system selection, emphasizing that fine-grained superiority claims depend heavily on the specified estimand and uncertainty rules.

#### Summary
The paper argues that leaderboard scores are merely summaries of specific released evaluations and should not be equated with general agent superiority. The proposed estimand-aware pairwise decision procedure highlights that valid superiority claims require explicit definition of the estimand, measurement source, and uncertainty rules. The findings indicate that many closely ranked systems in current public benchmarks lack statistically significant differences, warning against blind reliance on leaderboard ranks for model selection.
