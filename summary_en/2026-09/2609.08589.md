## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly used in agent frameworks where they self-report task progress to decide whether to continue or stop execution. This "model as progress bar" mechanism relies on the model's accurate self-perception of its state.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current benchmarks primarily focus on whether an agent successfully completes a task, lacking systematic study on the reliability of progress reporting throughout the execution lifecycle. It remains unclear at which stages models fail and how these failures manifest, leading developers to blindly rely on model self-reports for flow control.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A systematic evaluation protocol for LLM agent task-progress reporting, including adding reporting obligations to the public $\tau^2$-bench and creating StageIF, a controlled testbed with checkpoints across the task lifecycle.
- **Challenge 1: Accuracy drop in mid-task reporting**. The difficulty lies in models losing context or becoming overconfident during complex multi-step tasks, leading to premature completion claims. The paper uses StageIF's multi-stage checkpoints to reveal that most deployed models lose accuracy while work is underway but recover once the task is done, exposing a "mid-task failure" pattern.
- **Challenge 2: Conservative bias in new-generation models and stage-dependent variability**. The challenge is that failure modes differ across model generations; newer models fix the mid-task drop but become overly conservative at the finish line. The paper demonstrates that no model is reliable at all stages, and failure points shift with model generation, proving the unreliability of relying solely on model reports.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: Evaluation was conducted in two settings: a modified $\tau^2$-bench requiring lifecycle-stage tags in replies, and the new StageIF testbed allowing progress checks at predefined stages. Multiple mainstream deployed LLMs were evaluated.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - Almost every tested model is reliable at some stages but unreliable at others.
    - Most conventional models lose accuracy during the middle of task execution (prematurely declaring completion) but recover accuracy after task completion.
    - The newest generation of models closes the mid-task accuracy gap but exhibits conservatism at the finish line (hesitating to declare completion even when done).
- **Comparison**: Unlike traditional benchmarks (e.g., WebArena, AgentBench) that only assess final task success, this study decouples "task execution success" from "progress reporting accuracy," revealing that state reports can severely mislead control flow even if the task is eventually successful.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper systematically investigates the reliability of LLM agents in reporting progress throughout task execution. It finds that models are unreliable as "progress bars," with error patterns showing distinct stage-specific characteristics (mid-task failure or end-task conservatism). The proposed evaluation protocol fills a critical gap in the field, strongly suggesting that agent frameworks should not control task flow based solely on model-generated state signals, but should incorporate external state verification or other mechanisms for robustness.
