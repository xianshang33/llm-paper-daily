## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed as autonomous agents capable of long-horizon planning and tool use. However, most agents rely on unconstrained generation over accumulating histories, leaving procedural knowledge (what to do, in what order, under which conditions) implicit and unstructured.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current approaches rely on textual memory, conditional guidelines, or explicit workflows. Memory-based methods preserve experience but require the agent to reconstruct applicability from free text; state-conditioned guidelines retrieve rules without explicitly linking sequential steps; and workflow/state-machine approaches often require costly manual design and lack dynamic responsiveness to the agent's current progress.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Procedural Graph**: A self-evolving execution structure that organizes procedural knowledge into (procedure, relation, procedure) triplets, analogous to how Knowledge Graphs organize factual knowledge, specifically addressing "what-to-do" questions.
- **Challenge 1: Maintaining procedural coherence and avoiding invalid repetitions in long-horizon tasks.**
  - **Difficulty**: As trajectories lengthen, agents lose track of objectives, invoke tools out of order, or repeat unproductive actions because free-form generation struggles to implicitly maintain complex dependencies.
  - **Solution**: The framework localizes the agent's active node at each step and uses a guidance model to translate the surrounding subgraph into step-level situational guidance. This biases the next action without dictating it, balancing structured constraints with reasoning freedom.
- **Challenge 2: Automating the construction and optimization of procedural structures to reduce manual engineering.**
  - **Difficulty**: Manual workflow design is labor-intensive and brittle, while existing automated search methods are typically offline and lack online self-correction capabilities.
  - **Solution**: A self-evolution mechanism where an LLM refiner contrasts failed and successful trajectories to edit the graph's topology and attributes. It commits edits that improve validation performance and retains rejected ones to discourage repetition, allowing the graph to evolve from a minimal skeleton to match or surpass hand-designed structures.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The framework starts with a minimal procedural graph skeleton and iterates through a closed loop. It includes a localization module, a guidance model that converts subgraphs into natural language instructions, and an LLM refiner for graph updates based on contrastive learning.
- **Evaluation Results**: Tested across multiple datasets, task types, and LLM backbones. The Procedural Graph delivers consistent performance gains over memory-based baselines.
- **Comparison with Related Work**:
  - Outperforms traditional memory-augmented methods by providing structured procedural constraints rather than unstructured text retrieval.
  - Surpasses hand-designed workflows by automatically building graphs that match or exceed human expertise and by repairing flawed expert priors.
  - Achieves further performance improvements through self-evolution without requiring extensive manual engineering.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper introduces the Procedural Graph, a self-evolving execution structure for LLM agents. By explicitly organizing procedural knowledge into a graph structure and combining it with situational guidance and self-evolution mechanisms, the method effectively addresses issues of goal loss and error repetition in long-horizon tasks. Experiments demonstrate that it consistently outperforms existing memory baselines and hand-crafted workflows, enabling efficient, automated procedural optimization with minimal human intervention.
