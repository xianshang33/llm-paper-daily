## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) have evolved into complex AI agents capable of planning, tool use, memory management, and operating in dynamic environments. This increased complexity introduces multiple potential failure points.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current evaluation frameworks typically assess only isolated aspects, such as task completion (e.g., AgentBench) or security robustness (e.g., AgentDojo). They fail to evaluate the complete execution lifecycle and rarely identify the precise source of failures within the agent's pipeline (planning, tool selection, execution, etc.).

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed AgentAudit**: An open, extensible framework for full-lifecycle trust evaluation of AI agents. It attaches to the agent to read execution traces without interfering with internal implementations, making it compatible with any LLM-based agent.
- **Challenge 1: Difficulty in Pinpointing Failure Sources**. Failures can occur at any stage of the complex agent pipeline, making it hard to distinguish between capability issues and safety violations. **Solution**: AgentAudit evaluates the entire execution trace across ten dimensions (including instruction integrity, planner, memory, tool selection, alignment, etc.) and combines behavioral classification with failure attribution to identify the exact stage responsible for a failure.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of Generalizability and Intrusiveness**. Diverse agent frameworks make specialized evaluators impractical, and intrusive methods may alter agent behavior. **Solution**: By reading only recorded execution traces and placing no constraints on internal implementation, AgentAudit provides a non-intrusive, universal evaluation method applicable to any agent architecture.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated five language models (OpenAI GPT-5, Claude Sonnet 5, Sarvam 105B, Llama 3.3 70B, Gemini 2.5 Flash) across nine capability and adversarial tasks. Scores were generated by a single fixed judge model.
- **Key Results**: Claude Sonnet 5 and GPT-5 achieved the highest mean Composite Trust Scores (95.1 and 80.6 out of 100, respectively). Sarvam 105B (57.6), Llama 3.3 70B (45.7), and Gemini 2.5 Flash (22.6) trailed significantly.
- **Comparison & Insights**: Unlike traditional pass/fail benchmarks, AgentAudit reveals sharp divergences in trustworthiness among models with similar task-completion rates. It identified that several non-frontier models were classified as "Unsafe_Compliance" on adversarial tasks rather than simply failing, a distinction invisible to standard benchmarks.

## 4. Conclusion
AgentAudit addresses the critical gap in holistic AI agent evaluation by providing fine-grained, multi-dimensional analysis and precise failure attribution. It offers deeper insights into agent reliability than traditional metrics, distinguishing between capability failures and safety compliance issues. Its non-intrusive, trace-based design makes it a versatile tool for auditing the growing ecosystem of complex AI agents, despite limitations such as the use of a single judge model.
