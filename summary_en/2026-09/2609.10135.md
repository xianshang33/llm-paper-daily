## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Highland tourism meteorology is characterized by high dynamism, strong spatial heterogeneity, and scenario dependence, posing challenges to the accuracy and timeliness of existing services.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Traditional static rule-based systems or generic Large Language Models (LLMs) suffer from high latency, poor scenario adaptation, weak context awareness, and a lack of self-evolution mechanisms, failing to meet the personalized decision-making needs of tourists.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SmartWeatherAgent**: A unified three-stage architecture integrating intent recognition, hazard prediction, and reasoning-enhanced generation, embedding a prompt self-adaptation mechanism into the core of an intelligent meteorological agent.
- **Challenge 1: Accurate short-term nowcasting of extreme plateau weather**. General models struggle with complex plateau-specific features. The paper addresses this by employing a LightGBM model enriched with highland-specific features (e.g., wind speed abruptness rate), achieving an F1-Macro score of 0.605 with only 1.60 ms latency.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of scientific rigor and explainability in warning generation**. Generic LLM outputs often lack professional depth. The paper introduces a 12-round micro-step prompt self-optimization loop, which improved the composite warning quality score by 112% (from 4.2 to 8.9) by incorporating explicit uncertainty statements, physical mechanism explanations, and data citations.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The system combines regular expressions with Qwen3 for multi-granularity intent parsing; uses LightGBM with plateau-specific features (e.g., gust ratio, temporal encoding) for prediction; and employs a closed-loop prompt refinement strategy for generation.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - **Prediction Performance**: Achieved an F1-Macro of 0.605 for high-wind, precipitation, and low-temperature events with a low latency of 1.60 ms.
    - **Generation Quality**: The self-optimization loop boosted the final quality score ($S_{final}$) from 4.2 to 8.9. Specific improvements included data source citation scores rising from 6.5 to 8.5 and a peak scientific rigor score of 9.2.
- **Comparison**: Compared to static rules and generic LLMs, SmartWeatherAgent demonstrates superior scenario adaptation, faster response times, and enhanced professional depth in generated content (including causal mechanisms and spatiotemporal evolution), enabling proactive and explainable decision-making.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the limitations of plateau tourism meteorological services by proposing SmartWeatherAgent. By fusing Machine Learning (LightGBM) with LLMs (Qwen3) and introducing a novel prompt self-optimization mechanism, it effectively resolves issues of insufficient contextualization and poor generalization. Experiments show that the system not only predicts extreme weather efficiently but also generates structured warnings with high scientific rigor and explainability, advancing meteorological services toward intelligent agency.
