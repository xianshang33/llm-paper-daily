## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Multi-agent systems based Large Language Models (LLMs) coordinate multiple agents to solve complex tasks, aiming to leverage cognitive diversity for improved performance. However, when agents produce conflicting answers, the aggregation mechanism determines whether complementary insights are synthesized or shared errors are compounded.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Existing collective decision-making methods (e.g., voting, electoral rules, LLM judges) rely on "forward reasoning" (mapping evidence to labels). These methods aggregate estimates that share the same "evidence-to-label" factorization, inheriting correlated errors from the forward pool. Additionally, LLM judges suffer from systematic biases (position, verbosity), and multi-round debates are computationally expensive and prone to conformity.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A label-free anchor framework for multi-agent collective decision-making using Bayesian Backward Reasoning.
- **Challenge 1: How to break correlated errors in forward reasoning?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional methods aggregate only from forward paths, leading to highly correlated errors and lacking an objective reference when agents disagree.
  - **Solution**: Construct a "reverse posterior" for each instance via explicit likelihood. This provides a differently factorized approximation compared to the forward posterior. Since estimates from different factorizations share errors less often, Jensen-Shannon divergence is used to measure cross-path consistency.
- **Challenge 2: How to effectively aggregate conflicting answers using consistency signals?**
  - **Difficulty**: Simple voting or single judges cannot quantify confidence differences between diverse reasoning paths.
  - **Solution**: Three strategies are proposed based on cross-path consistency: Hard Selection (MinJS), Soft Reweighting (FwdJS), and Log-Linear Fusion (LogLin), optimizing final decisions without requiring labels.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Setup**: Evaluated on the DDXPlus dataset across five different LLM backbones.
- **Results**:
  - **MinJS**: Outperforms random selection across all backbones.
  - **FwdJS**: Generally improves over the strongest baseline.
  - **LogLin**: Achieves the best performance among all evaluated methods, with the largest gains observed in subsets where agents disagree.
- **Comparison**: Although the reverse posterior has weaker standalone accuracy, it serves as a more useful anchor than forward-only alternatives by providing complementary information. With available labeled data, a lightweight two-stage calibration can further refine the reverse anchor and improve aggregation.

#### Summary
This paper addresses the issue of error correlation caused by forward reasoning in multi-agent systems by innovatively introducing Bayesian backward reasoning to construct a label-free anchor. By calculating the Jensen-Shannon divergence between forward and reverse posteriors, it measures cross-path consistency and designs various aggregation strategies. Experiments demonstrate that this method significantly improves the performance of multi-agent collective decision-making on the DDXPlus dataset, especially in handling agent disagreements, offering a new paradigm for robust aggregation without additional training data.
