## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: LLM-based multi-agent systems distribute complex tasks via role specialization but frequently suffer from coordination failures (specifically FC2 in MAST-Data) where agents fail to track peers' roles, knowledge, or intentions, even when communication succeeds.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current research is split between failure taxonomies (diagnosing why coordination breaks) and Theory of Mind (ToM)/RL training (using artificial, authored tasks). There is a gap: diagnostic work rarely leads to direct training signals, and existing ToM benchmarks do not utilize real-world execution failures as supervision. No prior work uses diagnosed FC2 failures as the joint source for both benchmark items and RL reward signals.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ToMAS**: A preliminary rubric and pipeline for converting diagnosed multi-agent coordination failures into trainable partner-state reasoning items.
- **Challenge 1: Converting raw failure traces into valid reasoning items.**
  - **Difficulty**: Not all coordination failures involve misaligned mental states; many are protocol or tool errors unsuitable for ToM reasoning.
  - **Solution**: ToMAS applies four explicit convertibility criteria to filter execution traces. From 242 eligible traces, it produced 39 "CLEAN" items. A reliability pilot showed high inter-annotator agreement (94.4% raw, Cohen's kappa = 0.92).
- **Challenge 2: Validating converted items as effective RL rewards.**
  - **Difficulty**: Demonstrating that these binary rewards derived from failures can effectively guide model learning without overfitting or numerical instability.
  - **Solution/Finding**: A small-scale GRPO feasibility experiment was conducted with Qwen2.5-1.5B. Post-hoc analysis revealed that LoRA updates were numerically negligible (max |ΔW| ≈ 7e-6), meaning the model did not actually learn. Thus, while the pipeline is executable, the experiment did not establish a training effect, highlighting limitations in source provenance and lexical-overlap scoring.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The ToMAS pipeline processes MAST-Data execution traces, filtering them into partner-state reasoning items. These items were used as binary rewards in a GRPO fine-tuning experiment on Qwen2.5-1.5B.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - **Data Quality**: High reliability in conversion (Kappa = 0.92).
  - **Training Outcome**: On a held-out 28-item Magentic GAIA diagnostic, performance gains were minimal (exceeding ROUGE-L thresholds on only 2 items). Crucially, the lack of improvement was attributed to negligible weight updates, not necessarily the quality of the data itself.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Unlike previous ToM benchmarks that rely on synthetic games or abstract scenarios, ToMAS grounds its data in real multi-agent failures. However, compared to successful RL alignment studies, this pilot failed to demonstrate convergence due to technical constraints, offering a critical critique of the "diagnosis-to-training" workflow rather than a SOTA performance claim.

## 4. Conclusion
ToMAS introduces a novel approach to leverage real-world multi-agent coordination failures as supervised signals for Theory of Mind training. While the initial GRPO experiment did not yield positive training results due to negligible LoRA updates, the work successfully validates a high-consistency pipeline for converting failure traces into reasoning items. It critically identifies the requirements for future conclusive studies, specifically addressing the provenance gap between training and evaluation data and the need for robust evaluation metrics beyond lexical overlap.
