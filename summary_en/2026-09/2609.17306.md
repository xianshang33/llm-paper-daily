## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: As the number of open-source Large Language Models (LLMs) explodes, Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) combine multiple model outputs to solve complex reasoning tasks. However, there is limited research on how to optimally select candidate models from this massive pool.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current research often assumes that adding more models or diversity inherently improves performance. It lacks systematic evaluation of "model selection strategies" across different MAS architectures (pre-generation routing vs. post-generation voting/judging), ignoring the potential instability introduced by arbitrary heterogeneous model integration.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a**: Systematic evaluation framework for model pool selection in MAS, comparing 8 selection strategies (including model size, accuracy, and answer diversity) across different MAS architectures.
- **Challenge 1: The gap between theoretical oracle potential and actual performance**. Difficulty: High oracle performance does not necessarily translate to actual MAS improvements; blindly expanding the candidate pool often degrades performance below that of the best single model. Solution: Empirical analysis shows that selecting candidates within a single model family yields the best relative performance improvement over standalone models, avoiding instability from heterogeneity.
- **Challenge 2: Complexity of heterogeneous model integration**. Difficulty: Different architectural families exhibit varying levels of similarity, and domain specializations are not always realized as expected, making combination effects hard to predict. Solution: The paper demonstrates that pre-evaluating candidates and sticking to single-family selection is more robust than random or purely diversity-based selection, providing critical design guidelines for MAS.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Systematically evaluated 8 model selection strategies on challenging scientific reasoning benchmarks. Covered two basic MAS architectures: Before-generation (routing) and After-generation (majority voting, LLM-as-a-judge).
- **Key Results**:
    1. Expanding candidate pool sizes often degrades performance, falling below the top-performing base model.
    2. A significant gap exists between theoretical oracle potential and actual system performance.
    3. **Best Strategy**: Selecting candidates within a single model family yields the best relative performance improvement compared to standalone models.
- **Comparison**: Contrary to the intuition that "more models are better" or "more diversity is better," the study shows that arbitrarily adding heterogeneous models introduces system instability. It highlights model selection as a critical design choice rather than simple resource accumulation.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper reveals the phenomenon of "Mo' Models, Mo' Problems" in MAS design. Through systematic evaluation, the authors prove that blindly expanding the model candidate pool is often counterproductive. The core insight is that careful selection within a single model family outperforms building large heterogeneous pools. This finding provides important guidance for the efficient and stable design of future MAS, emphasizing the critical importance of model selection strategies.
