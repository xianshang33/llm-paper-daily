## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents excel in language generation and problem-solving but face structural limitations when simulating an individualized, developing "self." Existing generative agent simulations (e.g., Stanford Smallville) rely on static memory and fixed prompts.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current methods fail to maintain long-term behavioral inertia, leading to personality drift; their reflection mechanisms merely store summaries in context without altering underlying behavioral dispositions (non-evolutionary reflection); and they lack an endogenous "self-other" boundary, preventing agents from distinguishing themselves from others through social comparison.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed the Self-Emergence Agent Architecture (SEAA)**: This architecture integrates a Behavior-Inertia HMM, Reflexive Metacognition, and Social-Contrastive Self-Modeling, forming a closed loop: "social action → feedback → self-reflection → inertia update → differentiated action."
- **Challenge 1: Lack of stable individual behavioral inertia**. The difficulty lies in base LLMs reflecting global corpus distributions rather than individual trajectories, causing personality drift. SEAA addresses this by introducing a Hidden Markov Model (HMM) that encodes long-term behavioral and cognitive inertia as an editable state-transition matrix, thereby stabilizing individual traits.
- **Challenge 2: Reflection fails to drive self-evolution and lacks self-other boundaries**. The challenge is that traditional reflection stores text without modifying the agent's essence, and agents cannot endogenously distinguish self from others. SEAA solves this by using the reflection loop to directly update HMM parameters for genuine behavioral reshaping and employing continuous social behavior comparison in a multi-agent environment to spontaneously form "self-other" boundaries.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Protocol**: The paper provides a reproducible experimental protocol with operational metrics, including a language-model-free mechanism prototype and experiments with hosted LLMs.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - **Mechanism Prototype**: Demonstrated that the closed loop spontaneously breaks symmetry, causing initially identical agents to consolidate distinct, stable personalities, unlike matched controls.
    - **LLM Experiments**: Internal differences surfaced as distinct first-person self-narratives. In a five-agent deliberation, a social structure spontaneously emerged (a consensus hub and a unanimously rejected outlier), which never appeared in the undifferentiated control group.
- **Comparison**: Compared to existing generative agents relying on static memory and fixed prompts, SEAA achieves endogenous self-evolution and stable personality differentiation, effectively addressing personality drift and ineffective reflection.

#### Summary
This paper presents a unified theoretical framework and concrete architecture (SEAA) for studying the emergence of artificial self. Inspired by Zhuangzi's epistemologically agnostic stance, SEAA studies only observable behavioral emergence without claiming subjective qualia. By combining HMM-based behavioral inertia, parameter-level reflection updates, and social contrast mechanisms, this work provides a microscope-style experimental sandbox and mechanistic evidence for understanding the spontaneous formation of artificial consciousness and social structures.
