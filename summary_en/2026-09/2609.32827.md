## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: As Large Language Models (LLMs) evolve into autonomous agents, Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) are increasingly used for complex tasks. Existing Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) approaches typically rely on hand-crafted reward functions to optimize collaboration.
- **Limitations of Prior Work**: Constructing reliable and comprehensive reward metrics is difficult in practice. While Preference Learning avoids complex reward engineering by using comparative feedback, its extension to multi-agent systems is underexplored. Key challenges include the vast policy space making preference data coverage difficult and high variance in learned rewards when data is insufficient.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a General Multi-Agent Preference Learning Framework (MAPL)**: This framework formulates preference-based MAS from both decentralized and centralized perspectives, enabling iterative updates by comparing current solutions with those generated by various agents.
- **Challenge 1: Vast policy space in MAS makes it difficult to collect preference data covering diverse cooperation schemes.**
  - **Difficulty**: The joint policy space grows exponentially, making static offline datasets inadequate for covering all possible collaboration paths, leading to poor out-of-distribution generalization.
  - **Solution**: MAPL employs an online iterative update mechanism. It actively explores the policy space by dynamically generating decentralized or centralized solutions during training and performing pairwise comparisons, thereby collecting more representative preference data.
- **Challenge 2: Inaccurate and high-variance reward models due to sparse or insufficient preference data undermine policy optimization.**
  - **Difficulty**: When training data fails to cover high-quality collaborative behaviors, the resulting reward signals are noisy and can mislead agent learning.
  - **Solution**: The paper instantiates MAPL with two algorithms: MARLHF (with a learned reward model) and MADPO (Multi-Agent Direct Preference Optimization). MARLHF mitigates variance through robust reward modeling, while MADPO bypasses explicit reward modeling to reduce error propagation, enhancing stability within the framework.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on four tasks: collaborative writing, coding, tool use, and travel planning.
- **Baselines**: Compared against traditional MARL methods with fixed, well-defined rewards, and compared MARLHF vs. MADPO within the MAPL framework.
- **Results**:
  - MAPL significantly improved collaboration quality and efficiency, achieving performance close to MARL baselines with perfect hand-crafted rewards, demonstrating its effectiveness in the absence of explicit reward metrics.
  - Within MAPL, MARLHF generally outperformed MADPO on most tasks, showing greater robustness.
  - However, MARLHF was found to be sensitive to data coverage, agent/comparator models, and underlying MARL algorithms, indicating that its performance relies heavily on the quality of the preference data distribution.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the difficulty of designing reward functions in LLM multi-agent collaboration by proposing MAPL, a general multi-agent preference learning framework. By introducing an online iterative preference comparison mechanism, MAPL effectively tackles the data coverage challenge posed by the vast policy space. Through MARLHF and MADPO instantiations, it mitigates reward modeling instability. Experiments show that the method achieves collaborative performance comparable to traditional RL with explicit rewards, offering a new optimization paradigm for efficient LLM agent collaboration.
