## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Multi-agent systems built on Large Language Models (LLMs) are largely homogeneous, with agents behaving similarly even across different models. Without communication mechanisms and distinguished only by public roles, these homogeneous agents face coordination dilemmas during concurrent actions.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: The widely used "Theory of Mind" (ToM) approach cannot escape this dilemma. Since homogeneous agents form identical predictions about each other and respond in the same way, they collide on targets that need to be split and diverge on targets that require joint effort. This double failure is termed the "Symmetry Trap."

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a** training-free reasoning framework called "Theory of Scene" (ToS), which transforms homogeneity from the cause of the trap into the solution by having agents read their public roles and shared task context.
- **Challenge 1: How to break symmetric behavior in homogeneous agents without communication?**
  - **Difficulty**: Homogeneous agents generate identical reasoning paths without additional differentiation, leading to action conflicts or ineffective dispersion.
  - **Solution**: ToS introduces "Role Gating," which combines public roles with scene information to determine if ownership overlaps or is divided, thereby deriving a unique division of labor where each agent takes the part fixed by its role.
- **Challenge 2: How to dynamically adapt to complex scenarios mixing competitive and cooperative targets?**
  - **Difficulty**: Tasks may simultaneously contain competitive targets requiring division and cooperative targets requiring convergence, making static strategies ineffective.
  - **Solution**: ToS uses "Task Coupling" to analyze task context, inferring whether the team must converge on targets, divide them, or execute stages sequentially, thus flexibly adjusting coordination strategies.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Evaluation Environment**: Introduced DivvyBench, covering tabletop picking, airspace survey, and household assistance scenarios with competitive, cooperative, and mixed task types; also evaluated on GovSim and Overcooked benchmarks.
- **Results**: ToS outperforms all six baselines across every benchmark.
  - **DivvyBench**: Success rate increased from 71.1% (ToM) to 99.6%.
  - **GovSim**: Total gain increased from 207 to 400.
  - **Overcooked**: Level-normalized throughput increased from 1.41 to 1.67.
- **Conclusion**: Each baseline falls far behind ToS in at least one setting, demonstrating ToS's significant advantage in handling the symmetry trap.

#### Summary
This paper identifies the "Symmetry Trap" in multi-agent LLM systems, where homogeneous agents fail to coordinate due to identical behaviors in concurrent, non-communicative settings. The proposed ToS framework leverages role gating and task coupling to dynamically derive labor division using public roles and scene context. It significantly improves coordination performance in competitive, cooperative, and mixed tasks without training, achieving state-of-the-art results across multiple benchmarks.
