## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Long-horizon LLM agents must convert accumulated experience into durable memory, deciding what to retain, compress, abstract, or forget. Existing approaches treat memory primarily as a storage problem (e.g., larger context windows, RAG) rather than a state-management problem.
- **Limitations of Prior Work**: Current methods focus on "how much" is remembered, lacking effective metrics for the quality of consolidation decisions and failing to ensure the trustworthiness and safety of these decisions. Traditional retrieval systems retain information but fail in cross-level transfer and lack governance mechanisms.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Four-Phase Research Framework**: Shifts the focus from memory quantity to the quality and trustworthiness of consolidation decisions.
- **Challenge 1: How to accurately define episodic boundaries and prevent information leakage?**
  - **Difficulty**: Learning episodic boundaries from agent traces is noisy and prone to leakage.
  - **Solution**: Developed a "three-gate anti-leakage protocol" based on downstream utility, effectively limiting leakage despite not achieving perfect oracle correlation.
- **Challenge 2: How to optimize abstraction levels under token budgets while avoiding degenerate forgetting?**
  - **Difficulty**: Models often fall into "degenerate forgetting" (inaction for high precision) or suffer from distribution-shift failures named "λ-prevalence coupling."
  - **Solution**: Introduced controlled consolidation mechanisms, achieving a 22.7% task success improvement with ~7x compression, while identifying and analyzing these failure modes.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **ConsolidationBench**: Introduced an oracle-by-construction benchmark scoring decisions on three non-circular axes. Results showed production retrieval systems score zero on cross-level transfer, whereas the proposed method effectively distinguishes strategy quality.
- **Governance Verification**: Introduced "governed consolidation" with guarantees for poison-resistance, reversibility, and auditability. Statistical analysis confirmed governance is distinct from quality scores ($r^2 = 0.43$), with identical-quality policies showing threefold differences in governance.
- **Negative Result on External Validity**: After redesigning the metric to remove structural ceilings, a large-scale study (2,532 answer cells) found that quality scores do not predict real transfer accuracy (Spearman $ρ= -0.24$). This honest negative result clarifies that the quality score is an oracle-normalized measure of decision-making, not a predictor of downstream performance.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper redefines the paradigm for long-horizon agent memory, shifting from capacity to the quality and trustworthiness of consolidation. It reveals limitations in existing retrieval systems, proposes anti-leakage protocols and governed consolidation, and rigorously reports the weak correlation between quality scores and downstream performance. The core contribution is an epistemic framework for measurable, auditable, and reliable agent memory.
