## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) coordinate LLM-based agents to solve complex tasks. In practice, they must handle continuous streams of heterogeneous and complex tasks, requiring agents to decompose tasks, self-organize, and adapt while sharing resources.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Early MAS approaches relied on centralized controllers or fixed coordination patterns, limiting scalability and adaptability. Existing decentralized and dynamic MAS methods often require training dedicated routers or invoking LLMs for agent selection, resulting in substantial computational costs and coordination overhead. Most prior work focuses on single-task collaboration, lacking effective support for self-organization and load balancing under concurrent multi-task loads.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed DEALS (Decentralized Expertise-Aware Load Serving)**: A decentralized, low-complexity framework enabling agent-level self-organization and dynamic routing of concurrent tasks.
- **Challenge 1: How to achieve efficient load balancing and task routing in a decentralized environment without centralized bottlenecks or high computational costs?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional methods suffer from single points of failure (centralized) or high latency/cost due to LLM-based routing decisions.
  - **Solution**: DEALS equips each agent with a local task queue. Its router decides whether to process locally or forward to a neighbor based solely on differences in backlog and success rates. This avoids training dedicated routers or frequent LLM calls for routing, achieving low-complexity dynamic load balancing.
- **Challenge 2: How to achieve task-level self-organization and self-evolution to handle heterogeneous task streams and optimize overall system performance?**
  - **Difficulty**: Concurrent processing of heterogeneous tasks can lead to workload imbalance, and partially solved tasks are difficult to seamlessly transfer between agents, affecting throughput and accuracy.
  - **Solution**: DEALS allows executors to process independent tasks concurrently within and across agents. It supports the resumption of partially solved tasks by other agents. This mechanism enables the system to automatically adjust task distribution based on real-time load and expertise, facilitating self-organized collaborative evolution.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Extensive experiments were conducted in both homogeneous and heterogeneous agent pools to evaluate DEALS across multiple dimensions.
- **Key Results**:
  - **Performance Improvement**: DEALS outperformed baseline methods in answer accuracy and task throughput.
  - **Load Balancing**: The system self-organized to balance agent expertise and workload, effectively preventing scenarios where some agents were overloaded while others were idle.
  - **Comparison Advantage**: Compared to centralized control or expensive decentralized methods requiring training/inference overhead, DEALS achieved superior coordination efficiency and system performance with significantly lower computational complexity.

## 4. Conclusion
DEALS proposes a novel decentralized, expertise-aware load serving framework that addresses the challenges of self-organization and load balancing in multi-agent LLM systems handling concurrent heterogeneous task streams. By employing a lightweight routing mechanism based on local backlog and success rates, along with a concurrent execution model supporting task resumption, DEALS significantly reduces coordination overhead without compromising performance. It enhances system scalability, accuracy, and throughput, offering a new paradigm for efficient multi-agent collaboration.
