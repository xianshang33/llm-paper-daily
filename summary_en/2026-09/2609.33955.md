## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Current LLM-as-a-Judge evaluations typically score fixed outputs under fixed task definitions. However, production multi-turn business agents require a maintained measurement system where correctness depends on business-specific facts and procedures, outcomes emerge across turns, and failures must be attributed to either agent capability or missing business knowledge to be actionable.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Existing multi-turn benchmarks measure conversational capability but lack methods for building and maintaining continuously operated measurement systems. Standard judges cannot handle instance-specific correctness (e.g., specific merchant catalogs, prices, procedures) or effectively distinguish between model failure and data gaps. Direct user feedback is sparse and unreliable, while human review alone lacks scalability and automatic reliability.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed an integrated methodology**: Spanning evaluation specification, modular LLM judges, intent-preserving user simulation, and human-in-the-loop governance to make multi-turn agent measurement reliable, actionable, and maintainable.
- **Challenge 1: Difficulty in state consistency and failure attribution in multi-turn interactions**. Task completion and state consistency emerge dynamically, making it hard to distinguish model errors from knowledge base gaps. **Solution**: The paper defines conversation-level end states and actionable failure ownership, using atomic judges that share versioned evidence and feed into an explicit aggregation graph to precisely track and attribute failures.
- **Challenge 2: Difficulty in continuous calibration and maintenance of the evaluation system**. Evaluation standards must evolve with business guidelines, products, and traffic, and human reference standards themselves require revision. **Solution**: Independent human audits estimate measurement fidelity, renew tiered reference sets, and route disagreements to label correction, guideline revision, or judge improvement, creating a shared feedback loop to ensure the system remains calibrated as it evolves.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The system includes evaluation specification modules, modular LLM judges, user simulators released only after task-preservation and stability checks, and a human-in-the-loop governance process.
- **Evaluation Results**: Production studies show that system-level fidelity improved across repeated audits. Under the shared feedback loop, both human reviewers and automated judges improved together. Their combined workflow demonstrated the strongest descriptive performance in reported task-completion settings.
- **Comparison & Conclusion**: As the studies are observational and the human reference required revision, the findings demonstrate operational usefulness rather than causal or universal superiority. Compared to traditional fixed-output LLM-as-a-Judge approaches, this framework offers greater reliability and actionability for dynamic, instance-specific business scenarios.

#### Summary
This paper addresses the evaluation challenges of production multi-turn business agents by proposing a comprehensive LLM-as-a-Judge measurement system design methodology. By integrating evaluation specifications, modular judges, user simulation, and human-in-the-loop governance, it solves core challenges such as state tracking, failure attribution, and continuous system calibration in multi-turn interactions. Empirical results indicate that the framework effectively improves measurement fidelity and enables the co-evolution of human and automated evaluation, providing a practical guide for building reliable and maintainable business agent assessment systems.
