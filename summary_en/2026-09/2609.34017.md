## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large-language-model multi-agent systems (LLM-MAS) decompose complex tasks among specialized agents, introducing a reliability issue where errors from one agent are accepted as context by downstream agents, propagating and amplifying across the workflow.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Many current safeguards rely on learned or LLM-based judges. However, these judges produce probabilistic verdicts that can inherit the very failure modes they aim to catch. There is a lack of deterministic layers capable of stopping contract-detectable handoff defects without relying on probabilistic LLM judgments.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Maat**: A runtime governance layer that validates agent-to-agent handoffs against a versioned workflow contract (anchor) without using any language model in the validation or scoring path.
- **Challenge 1: Uncertainty and Error Inheritance in Probabilistic Verification**. Traditional LLM-based judges suffer from hallucinations and judgment errors, making reliable verification difficult. Maat addresses this by employing a deterministic, rule-based seven-check rubric to structurally validate handoff data without invoking an LLM, thereby eliminating probabilistic noise in the verification step.
- **Challenge 2: Balancing Error Propagation Prevention with Cost Efficiency**. Early errors in multi-agent workflows waste computational resources and skew final results if not intercepted. Maat halts the workflow immediately upon detecting contract violations, preventing error cascades and reducing model call costs by 17-53% in scenarios with early, attributable halts.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated across six controlled domain workflows (6-15 agents, 522 trials) with injected data-level defects, scored using a deterministic seven-check rubric.
- **Initial vs. Corrected Results**: Initial reports claimed performance gains of 2.9-26.5% across all workflows. A post-publication audit revealed that benchmark scorers incorrectly credited any early halt as a prevented defect. Manual review of 94 governed halts found 35 false alarms (37%) caused by validator configuration defects rather than model behavior.
- **Corrected Outcomes**: In paired trials where governed runs completed or halted due to verified defects, rubric scores improved by +7.7% to +29.1% in five workflows and remained flat (+0.8%) in software development. When false-alarm halts were counted as failed work, the governed arm underperformed the ungoverned arm in four of six workflows.
- **Comparison Conclusion**: The results support deterministic handoff validation for contract-expressible defects (e.g., tax, identity, price drift) and highlight significant cost savings. However, effectiveness is highly dependent on correct contract and validator configuration. Maat does not establish universal correctness or hallucination detection but serves as a complementary layer to post-hoc attribution and learned guardrails.

#### Summary
Maat introduces a deterministic runtime governance mechanism independent of LLMs to address error propagation in multi-agent systems. While initial results suggested performance gains and cost savings, rigorous auditing exposed high false-positive rates due to validator misconfigurations. Corrected data indicates efficacy for specific contract-expressible defects but underscores the critical need for testing the validation logic itself. Maat is not a universal solution but an important complementary component for deterministic rule-checking within multi-agent safety architectures.
