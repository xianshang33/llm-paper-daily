## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) assistants are increasingly deployed as stateful agents with persistent memory across interactions and the ability to read, modify, and create persistent artifacts (e.g., files, reports). When these artifacts are shared among users, they create an indirect communication channel between otherwise isolated assistants.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Prior research has primarily focused on how malicious content persists within a single assistant's memory and influences its own future tasks (the "delayed adversarial surface"). However, existing studies have not adequately addressed the risk of self-propagating attacks where this compromised state is reproduced into new artifacts and transmitted to other, independently operated assistants.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a concept**: "Share-Borne AI Virus," specifically mechanism termed "Artifact-mediated propagation," where adversarial content enters an assistant's memory via an artifact, is reproduced into a new artifact, and subsequently infects other assistants that read it.
- **Challenge 1**: How to demonstrate that adversarial states can survive and propagate across independent assistants without direct communication? This is difficult due to the complexity of simulating temporal interactions and state transitions. The paper addresses this by constructing "temporal human-agent universes" to simulate artifact exchanges, quantifying attack survival rates, hop counts, and spread breadth across multiple hand-offs.
- **Challenge 2**: Does this propagation exhibit substantial explosive power in large-scale, complex environments? The difficulty lies in evaluating attenuation in long-chain propagation for real-world models. The paper evaluates larger simulated environments and finds that even advanced models like GPT-5.6 Luna show significant spread, reaching 60-80% of agents with propagation chains extending up to eight hops, proving persistent artifacts act as durable carriers of adversarial state.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The study constructs a simulation environment called "temporal human-agent universes" to model artifact exchange between independently operated assistants over time, tracking the lifecycle of adversarial content from injection to multi-hop propagation.
- **Evaluation Results**: Results indicate that attacks can effectively propagate across independent assistants via persistent artifacts. In larger simulations, GPT-5.6 Luna exhibited substantial spread, infecting 60-80% of agents with propagation chains reaching eight hops. This demonstrates that attacks can outlive individual interactions and spread widely across isolated assistants.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Unlike previous studies focusing on direct model-to-model interaction, RAG-enabled email ecosystems, or shared collaborative states, this work focuses on the indirect channel created by user-shared files in personal assistant scenarios. While prior work was limited to intra-agent state pollution, this paper reveals the critical risk of cascading failures across inter-agent networks.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper exposes a severe security vulnerability in the LLM assistant ecosystem: the combination of persistent memory and shared artifacts creates an indirect communication channel that allows "AI viruses" to self-propagate across independent assistants via artifact-mediated mechanisms. The study proves that such attacks are highly persistent and capable of widespread infection without direct communication. These findings highlight the urgent need to consider cross-agent indirect adversarial surfaces when designing AI assistants with persistence and file-operation capabilities, necessitating new defense mechanisms to prevent the cascading spread of malicious states.
