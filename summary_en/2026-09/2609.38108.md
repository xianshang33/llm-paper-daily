## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents solve long-horizon tasks by generating plans and executing them in environments. Successful planning requires two distinct capabilities: selecting an appropriate planning mode for the task and executing it faithfully.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current planner-executor systems can fail at either stage: agents may deviate from structured plans during execution, or they may faithfully execute a plan poorly matched to the task. Final task success metrics alone cannot distinguish between selection failures and execution failures. Generic methods like Plan+ReAct often fail to preserve the declared planning structure.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed "Planning-as-Routing" Framework**: The paper introduces a framework where the LLM declares one of four planning modes (Predefined, Sequential, Hierarchical, or Search), and a deterministic router dispatches the task to a corresponding pattern-specific executor to bridge the Plan Declaration-Execution Gap.
- **Challenge 1: Generic executors fail to maintain declared plan structures**. It is difficult for LLMs to adhere to complex logical structures (like hierarchical or search logic) over long action sequences. The paper addresses this by using "pattern-specific executors" that enforce structural constraints via deterministic code, ensuring execution trajectories align with the declared logic.
- **Challenge 2: Effectiveness of planning modes varies across environments and models**. There is no single best planning mode for all tasks (e.g., Search works best for ALFWorld, Hierarchical for SWE-bench). The paper empirically demonstrates this variability and highlights that while routing solves execution consistency, automatically selecting the optimal mode for specific tasks remains an open challenge.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated across four benchmarks (including ALFWorld and SWE-bench Verified) and three different LLMs.
- **Key Results**:
    1. **Execution Consistency**: Generic Plan+ReAct preserved the declared plan structure in only 22–45% of trajectories, whereas pattern-specific executors enforced the intended structure.
    2. **Performance Gains**: Pattern-specific executors significantly improved task success rates compared to Plan+ReAct. Success on ALFWorld rose from 0.48 to 0.92, and on SWE-bench Verified from 0.36 to 0.44.
    3. **Mode Selection Variability**: Search mode performed best on ALFWorld, while Hierarchical mode excelled on SWE-bench. Current LLMs do not yet reliably select the strongest mode for each task, though few-shot examples showed some improvement.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper investigates the "Plan Declaration-Execution Gap" in LLM agents. By proposing the Planning-as-Routing framework, it demonstrates that decoupling planning mode selection from specific deterministic executors significantly improves execution fidelity and task success. While routing effectively closes the execution gap, the ability to dynamically select the optimal planning mode for specific tasks requires further advancement. Reliable agent planning necessitates both effective mode selection and faithful execution.
