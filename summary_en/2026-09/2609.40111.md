## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: As Large Language Models (LLMs) advance in complex reasoning and agentic tasks, agents are increasingly used in interactive software and web environments. Failed agent rollouts contain rich information (observations, actions, environment responses) beyond final rewards, presenting an opportunity for learning.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current agent post-training methods (e.g., AgentTuning, RAGEN) primarily rely on reward-filtered trajectories or policy optimization, often overlooking fine-grained error diagnosis and correction details. They typically focus on successful trajectories or simple retries, failing to fully leverage the learning signals embedded in failure analysis and evidence-based verification.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed the Agent Error Dataset (AED) and Agentic Error-to-Training (AET) Pipeline**: Created a large-scale dataset with 50,228 error-diagnosis pairs across 33 environments and 23 policy models. Designed a five-stage pipeline to collect natural failures, generate diagnoses/corrections, and verify them against recorded evidence.
- **Challenge 1: How to extract learnable error diagnoses and corrections from unstructured failure trajectories?**
  - **Difficulty**: Failure trajectories are noisy, and automatically generated diagnoses may be inaccurate or hallucinated, making them unsuitable for direct training.
  - **Solution**: The AET pipeline generates diagnoses and proposed corrections, verifying them against recorded execution evidence. In replay-supported environments, it compares corrections with original action retries from the same checkpoint under matched settings, ensuring correction validity and constructing high-quality training views.
- **Challenge 2: How to effectively integrate diagnostic knowledge and repair capabilities into model training?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional fine-tuning may conflate diagnostic logic with execution logic or ignore intermediate step correctness by focusing only on final success.
  - **Solution**: The paper constructs separate training views for diagnosis and actor recovery. Full-diagnosis fine-tuning improves step-level agreement with internal teacher labels, while action-only repair training specifically enhances the model's ability to correct failures, significantly outperforming training on successful trajectories alone.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Dataset & Pipeline**: AED comprises 50,228 error-diagnosis pairs from 9,961 source tasks. The AET pipeline retains source traces and metadata, enabling cross-setting failure analysis without re-running rollouts.
- **Correction Verification**: In 3,062 matched replay pairs, first-proposal corrections increased verifier pass rates from 18.4% to 51.1% (a 32.7 percentage point gain), significantly outperforming original action retries.
- **Model Fine-tuning Performance**: Using a frozen diagnosis release, full-diagnosis fine-tuning on 1,656 source tasks improved Qwen3-8B's exact-step agreement with internal teacher labels from 47.2% to 63.6% on a 943-case holdout. This surpasses the strongest prompted reference (Claude Opus 5 at 54.7%) and the Qwen3-8B baseline (47.2%). Performance improves consistently with larger training sets.
- **Actor Training Comparison**: In a single-seed comparison, action-only repair training scored 6.67 percentage points higher on WebShop-lite than success-only training, demonstrating the value of training on failure corrections.

#### Summary
This paper introduces the Agent Error Dataset (AED) and the Agentic Error-to-Training (AET) pipeline, systematically addressing how to leverage agent failure experiences for post-training. By scaling up error-diagnosis pairs and verifying corrections, the study demonstrates that fine-tuning based on error diagnosis and repair significantly enhances model performance and step-level consistency, outperforming traditional success-only training and strong prompting baselines. This work provides new data resources and methodologies for failure analysis and error-aware post-training in agent systems.
