## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: As language agents face increasingly complex tasks, there is a growing need for continual improvement. A promising approach is evolving the "agent harness" (the software governing tool use, memory, and execution) while keeping the underlying LLM fixed.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current methods automate this using a meta-coding agent that modifies the harness based on execution feedback. However, relying solely on the agent's existing knowledge and observed failures restricts exploration and makes adaptation reactive rather than proactive.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ScholarEvolve**: A framework inspired by how human experts learn from research literature, automatically leveraging state-of-the-art research to guide harness evolution.
- **Challenge 1: Limited Exploration and Reactive Adaptation**. Existing methods are confined to fixing known errors, lacking proactive exploration of new strategies, which leads to performance plateaus. **Solution**: ScholarEvolve uses topic modeling to identify distinct improvement strategies from recent research papers and organizes harness evolution into functional modules, actively introducing external knowledge to broaden the search space.
- **Challenge 2: Effective Evaluation of Strategy Combinations**. With numerous strategies extracted from literature, determining which combinations truly enhance performance is difficult. **Solution**: The framework implements these strategies and automatically evaluates their combinations to identify optimal configurations, supporting the incorporation of new publications over time for lifelong proactive evolution.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on AppWorld and Tau2-Bench benchmarks.
- **Key Results**:
  - On AppWorld Challenge, ScholarEvolve improved Qwen3.5-27B task goal completion from 49.6% to 63.6% (+14.0 pp).
  - On Tau2-Bench Telecom, it improved GPT-5.4-mini pass@1 from 72.7% to 81.9% (+9.2 pp).
- **Comparison**: Compared to traditional meta-agent methods relying only on execution feedback, ScholarEvolve significantly breaks through local optimization limits by integrating external research knowledge, achieving substantial performance gains and demonstrating lifelong learning capabilities.

## 4. Conclusion
ScholarEvolve introduces a novel paradigm for agent evolution: "Learning from Research." It overcomes the limitations of exploration and proactivity in traditional feedback-based methods by automatically absorbing the latest academic findings to guide code evolution of the agent harness. Experiments demonstrate that this approach significantly boosts agent performance on complex tasks without modifying the underlying model, offering an effective path toward building intelligent agents with lifelong learning capabilities.
