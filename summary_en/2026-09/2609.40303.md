## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Autonomous Machine Learning Engineering (MLE) agents have made significant progress on public leaderboards. To address stagnation in long-horizon tasks and limited LLM primitives, modern MLE agents are deployed on increasingly elaborate "harnesses," including multi-agent orchestrators and dedicated retrieval sub-agents.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: It is difficult to disentangle whether performance gains stem from harness design improvements or enhancements in the underlying LLM backbone. High evaluation costs lead to unfair comparisons across different backbones and hardware with insufficient seeds. Furthermore, as LLMs gain direct code execution capabilities and larger context windows, many traditional harness mechanisms designed to compensate for model limitations may have become redundant.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a minimal-harness coding agent baseline**: Through large-scale systematic ablation studies, the paper argues that complex machinery layers are redundant in the coding agent setting when powered by strong backbones.
- **Challenge 1: How to decouple backbone capability from harness mechanism contributions?**
  - **Difficulty**: Performance improvements in prior work often coincided with backbone iterations, and resource constraints prevented controlled comparisons, making attribution difficult.
  - **Solution**: The authors compared open-source state-of-the-art complex harnesses against a minimal-harness baseline (with only basic read/write/execute primitives) under identical time budgets and using the same frontier LLM backbones, finding no advantage for the complex harnesses.
- **Challenge 2: Validating the necessity of complex mechanisms for long-horizon tasks.**
  - **Difficulty**: MLE is traditionally viewed as a long-horizon problem requiring specialized memory management and multi-agent collaboration.
  - **Solution**: Leveraging modern LLMs with million-token context windows, the study demonstrates that a single session with direct environment access is sufficient for current MLE benchmarks, rendering complex search trees and memory hierarchies low-return investments.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The study used the MLE-bench standard testbed with a fixed set of tasks (fixed30 set) and a 24-hour time budget. It compared various mainstream harnesses (e.g., Malena, Arbor, AiScientist, MLEvolve, ScienceFlow) against the minimal baseline.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - With the same backbone models (e.g., Kimi K3, GLM 5.2, DeepSeek V4 Flash, Gemma 4 31B), complex open-source SOTA harnesses showed no statistically significant advantage over the minimal-harness baseline in terms of medal rate and mean percentile.
  - Performance is primarily driven by the backbone model's capability rather than the complexity of the external harness.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Consistent with findings in software engineering (SWE-bench), the paper shows that harness improvements yield diminishing returns as backbones strengthen. It concludes that the effort spent hand-crafting complex harnesses for current MLE benchmarks yields poor returns.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper questions the necessity of increasingly complex harnesses in autonomous MLE agent design. Through rigorous controlled experiments, the authors find that simple, minimal-harness agents with direct environment access perform comparably to or better than complex multi-agent orchestration systems when powered by strong frontier LLMs. The conclusion suggests that the current bottleneck lies in model capability rather than auxiliary mechanisms, recommending a shift in focus from over-engineering external harnesses to improving backbone models.
