## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents are increasingly combining reasoning, tool use, and action, but most evidence comes from episodic tasks with immediate feedback and resettable failures. Long-running physical control (e.g., irrigation) operates in a different regime where actions alter future states, errors compound, and agents must improve from experience without rewriting the physical rules that ensure safety.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Traditional methods like Model Predictive Control (MPC) or Reinforcement Learning work well when models are fully specified but lack semantic reasoning. Existing LLM agent research focuses on short-term tasks, failing to address state dependency, delayed consequences, and the accumulation of locally plausible but globally harmful errors in long-horizon settings, while struggling to guarantee self-improvement without compromising physical safety constraints.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Mimir**, a physics-grounded LLM agent framework for long-horizon irrigation control, organized around two repair timescales.
- **Challenge 1: Physical Grounding and Execution Safety**. LLMs operate on semantic representations, whereas irrigation is governed by numerical dynamics (units, conservation laws). Direct LLM outputs can violate physical constraints. **Solution**: At the fast timescale, a structured physical interface and deterministic simulator convert LLM outputs into proposals, which are numerically checked, revised, and subjected to bounded deterministic action selection before execution, ensuring physical truth remains in explicit numerical mechanisms.
- **Challenge 2: Long-Horizon Error Accumulation and Experiential Learning**. Consequences of decisions may be delayed, and agents must learn from recurring failures without eroding safety. **Solution**: At the slow timescale, recurrent failure patterns are consolidated into persistent contextual principles that condition future proposals, while keeping the physical model, evaluator, and execution constraints immutable, enabling bounded, evidence-driven self-improvement.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Retrospective evaluation across multiple sites, crops, and years using a common evaluator.
- **Key Results**: Mimir achieved the lowest reported aggregate control cost among all evaluated references. It used approximately 51% less irrigation water compared to historical schedule replays.
- **Ablation Studies**: Removing forward simulation, verified revision, or persistent context resulted in higher control costs, validating the necessity of each component.
- **Model Scale Analysis**: No monotonic gain was observed from increasing LLM size or changing model families, indicating that performance gains stem from the architectural design rather than model scale.
- **Comparison**: Mimir outperformed traditional physical baselines and historical benchmarks in both water efficiency and cost control, demonstrating the advantage of combining semantic reasoning with numerical constraints.

## 4. Conclusion
Mimir demonstrates that persistent physical agents can combine semantic reasoning with bounded, evidence-driven self-improvement while reserving physical truth and actuator authority for explicit numerical mechanisms. The study emphasizes that in long-horizon physical control, LLMs should not directly rewrite physical rules; instead, a dual-timescale repair mechanism (fast-scale numerical verification and slow-scale principle refinement) ensures safety and efficiency. This paradigm offers significant insights for applying LLMs in physical systems requiring long-term state dependency and strict safety constraints.
