## 1. What the Paper Does

### Background
Building reliable robot capabilities across diverse tasks requires substantial human effort to develop skills, design rewards, and integrate perception with control. While Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) enable agents to perceive and reason, deploying them efficiently remains challenging.

### Why Existing Work Falls Short
Current approaches face a trade-off: "Code-as-Policy" offers efficient execution but struggles with complex symbolic checks (e.g., grasp success), while "Agent-as-Policy" provides flexible reasoning but suffers from slow decision-making due to frequent model calls. Furthermore, existing methods often rely on updating model weights or extensive human engineering, lacking the ability to autonomously improve execution systems without parameter updates.

## Core Contributions

### Proposed RPG (Reconstruct, Practice, Go Real) Framework
A framework for autonomous improvement of robot execution systems without updating model weights. It identifies manipulation capabilities from offline datasets, constructs simulation practice tasks, diagnoses failures using feedback, and refines reusable symbolic skills and system prompts.

### Challenge 1: How to achieve autonomous skill evolution and generalization without updating model weights?
**Difficulty**: Traditional RL or fine-tuning requires significant data/compute for weight updates and lacks interpretable, modular skill reuse.
**Solution**: RPG uses a "Reconstruct-Practice-Go Real" pipeline. In the "Practice" phase, it diagnoses failures using execution feedback and privileged simulator states to generate or correct reusable symbolic skills and system prompts. Cross-task evaluation ensures these changes are valid before retention, enabling knowledge accumulation rather than parameter tuning.

### Challenge 2: How to bridge the Sim-to-Real gap and ensure skill transfer across tasks?
**Difficulty**: Policies optimized in simulation often fail in reality due to physics discrepancies, and single-task optimization can cause negative transfer to other tasks.
**Solution**: RPG introduces cross-task evaluation to test individual and merged revisions before retaining them, ensuring robustness and generality. In the "Go Real" phase, a unified calibration and hardware adaptation procedure allows the frozen system to deploy successfully on physical robots, achieving zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Implementation and Evaluation

### Implementation and Deployment
RPG consists of three stages:
1. **Reconstruct**: Identifies capabilities from offline data and builds simulation practice tasks.
2. **Practice**: Agents diagnose failures, revise shared skills and prompts, and validate changes across tasks.
3. **Go Real**: The improved system is calibrated, adapted to hardware, frozen, and deployed on physical robots.

### Evaluation Results
- **Simulation**: On 22 manipulation tasks, RPG improved success rates from 28.6% (initial) to 95.0% after 15 practice rounds.
- **Real-world**: After calibration, the frozen system succeeded in all 30 physical trials (10/10 for each of 3 tasks).

### Comparison with Related Work
RPG significantly outperforms baselines:
- **ASPIRE**: 75.5% success rate.
- **CaP-Agent0 (GPT-6 Astra Pro)**: 60.0% success rate.
RPG demonstrates superior success rates and positive skill transfer across tasks.

#### Summary

This paper presents RPG, a framework that enables embodied agents to autonomously improve execution systems without updating model weights. By combining offline data reconstruction, closed-loop simulation practice with diagnosis, and rigorous cross-task validation, RPG automatically generates and optimizes reusable symbolic skills. Experiments show that RPG surpasses state-of-the-art baselines (ASPIRE, CaP-Agent0) in both simulation and real-world deployments, offering a new paradigm for efficient, robust, and generalizable robot control with reduced human engineering effort.
