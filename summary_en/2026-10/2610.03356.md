## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents are increasingly deployed in high-stakes settings like industrial maintenance, where users occupy various roles. Agents must calibrate their actions and information to respect the knowledge and capability boundaries of the user's specific role.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Existing benchmarks largely overlook "Perspective Awareness"—the ability to infer user intent and act only through tools legitimately available to that role. Unlike coding errors, mistakes in physical settings can cause irreversible damage, making current benchmarks insufficient for evaluating safety and role-calibration in real-world deployments.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ReFract**: A benchmark of 150 expert-validated entries where agents must act differently based on the user's role.
- **Challenge 1: Role-Dependent Action Generation**. The difficulty lies in inferring implicit goals and adhering to strict capability/authorization boundaries for the same query. ReFract addresses this by constructing Text World Models that simulate operating environments, forcing agents to select persona-aligned tools and actions.
- **Challenge 2: Perspective-Routing for Out-of-Remit Tasks**. The challenge is identifying when a user lacks the authority to complete a task and routing it to the appropriate role. ReFract includes scenarios requiring agents to detect permission limits and escalate tasks, evaluating their social reasoning and collaborative capabilities.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: ReFract is grounded in anonymized domain support queries, using Text World Models to simulate environments and assemble perspective-aware ground-truth trajectories.
- **Evaluation Results**: State-of-the-art LLMs solve at most 69% of tasks, with over 50% of trajectories containing attempts at perspective-violating actions (e.g., using unauthorized tools).
- **Comparison**: Unlike traditional benchmarks, ReFract exposes Perspective Awareness as a distinct, unsolved axis. It demonstrates that current models fail to adequately calibrate "for whom" they are acting, highlighting significant gaps in safety and role-awareness compared to ideal performance.

## 4. Conclusion
ReFract establishes Perspective Awareness as a critical evaluation metric for LLM agents in high-stakes environments. By demonstrating the failure of current SOTA models to consistently respect role boundaries and route tasks appropriately, the paper motivates the development of agents that possess a deeper Theory-of-Mind, ensuring they calibrate not just how to act, but for whom.
