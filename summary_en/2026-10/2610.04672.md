## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) have evolved into the core of autonomous agents. LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) coordinate multiple agents to accomplish complex tasks. Real-world collaboration is typically "partially observable," where agents only access local information due to physical or privacy constraints.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Most existing benchmarks assume global observability, trivializing real-world challenges and lacking systematic support for evaluating collaboration mechanisms. Recent attempts at partial observability focus on end-to-end performance rather than mechanism-level analysis and often rely on subjective LLM-as-a-judge evaluations.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed MASBench**: A benchmark designed specifically for benchmarking LLM-based multi-agent collaboration under partially observable constraints.
- **Challenge 1: How to systematically evaluate diverse collaboration mechanisms?**
  - **Difficulty**: Collaboration mechanisms (protocol, memory, routing) are intertwined, making it hard to isolate and quantify their specific contributions under information constraints.
  - **Solution**: MASBench structures tasks into three progressive categories (Reasoning, Scheduling, Game) to specifically evaluate three representative mechanisms: Protocol, Memory, and Routing.
- **Challenge 2: How to provide objective, multi-dimensional evaluation under partial observability?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional evaluations often ignore communication overhead and rely on subjective judgments, failing to reflect true collaboration efficiency.
  - **Solution**: Introduces deterministic metrics including performance score, communication cost, and cost-effectiveness to characterize both outcomes and overhead.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: MASBench supports various LLM backbones and mechanism configurations. The code is open-source. It forces agents to collaborate under strict information constraints.
- **Evaluation Results**: Experiments demonstrate that simply increasing the number of agents does not guarantee performance gains. Effective mechanism design is critical. The benchmark reveals trade-offs between communication costs and task success rates.
- **Comparison**: Unlike benchmarks assuming global observability, MASBench highlights the decisive impact of communication efficiency and information sharing strategies in partially observable settings, providing more realistic empirical guidance.

## 4. Conclusion
MASBench fills the gap in evaluating multi-agent collaboration under partial observability. By decoupling collaboration mechanisms and introducing deterministic metrics, it provides a standardized platform and empirical guidance for designing efficient LLM-based MAS, emphasizing the importance of mechanism design in information-constrained scenarios.
