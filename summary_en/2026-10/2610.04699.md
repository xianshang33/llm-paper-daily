## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: In regulated domains like finance, healthcare, and law, AI Agents must enforce external rules. A verifier must distinguish between rules settleable by fixed code checks (decidable) and those requiring a judge (undecidable). This decision must be made at runtime for every predicate of every action.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current escalation schemes assume "self-decidability," expecting models to determine if a rule is checkable. However, models exhibit opposite error directions on regulatory boundaries and collectively err on deployed rule sets by over-claiming decidability, making simple consensus or single-model judgments untrustworthy.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed CoVer (corroborate-then-verify)**: An algorithm that treats unanimity as a nomination, admitting a predicate as decidable only if the synthesized check survives intervention (reading fields the agent cannot write) and holds under deterministic rewording.
- **Challenge 1: Unreliability and Divergent Errors at Boundaries**. The difficulty lies in models agreeing on easy cases but failing divergently on regulatory text, preventing the selection of a uniformly conservative model. CoVer addresses this by applying a strict validation gate (intervention test) that rejects most falsely admitted cases, constructing a reliable boundary.
- **Challenge 2: Collusive Blind Spots of Reference Judges**. Using a reference judge from the same distribution merely ratifies shared blind spots; agreement rates increase without improving the share of genuinely decidable items. CoVer bypasses this by constructing decidability through verifiable instruments (e.g., reading read-only fields) rather than relying on consensus certification.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Setup**: Collected ~22,000 labels across six corpora (including EU AI Act, FINRA guidance, and a deployed credit agent) using four models from three labs.
- **Results**:
    - Models agree on obvious cases but collapse on regulatory text.
    - On deployed agent rules, models err together by over-claiming decidability, a dangerous failure mode that prevents escalation.
    - Agreement with a reference judge is shown to certify nothing: agreement climbs from 30% to 77% across calibration bands, but the truly decidable share remains static due to shared blind spots.
- **CoVer Performance**: The CoVer gate rejects the majority of wrongly admitted predicates from corroboration. It reports the resulting cost in coverage rather than tuning it away, ensuring high precision in defining the decidability boundary.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper argues that self-decidability is not a capability to elicit from a model but a boundary the verifier must construct. It exposes systemic failures in LLMs' ability to distinguish code-checkable rules from those requiring judgment, particularly highlighting collusive errors. The proposed CoVer algorithm offers a robust method for defining this boundary via "corroborate-then-verify" strategies, providing a safer foundation for agent verification in high-stakes environments.
