## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are shifting from monolithic generalists to modular agentic systems. These systems use a shared frozen backbone and dynamically swap lightweight Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) adapters for specialized tasks like logical reasoning, code generation, and safety compliance.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current adaptation methods fail to address "Sequential Subspace Interference." The high-variance internal states required for Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning geometrically collide with latent representations of safety constraints. Standard fine-tuning on logical tasks leads to a 23.3% interference penalty on alignment benchmarks, a phenomenon termed "Reasoning Drift." This occurs because gradients for logical tasks are rarely orthogonal to safety objectives, causing high-entropy features from prior tasks to linger and interfere with subsequent safety-critical tasks ("Shadow Mimicry").

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed AURA (Adaptive Unique Residual Allocation)**: A spectral regularization framework that enforces Spectral Independence between reasoning and safety.
- **Challenge 1: Sequential Subspace Interference and Reasoning Drift**. The difficulty lies in the geometric collision of activation manifolds in a shared residual stream, where standard fine-tuning fails to ensure gradient orthogonality. AURA addresses this by explicitly estimating the null space of the alignment manifold and constraining reasoning updates to its orthogonal complement, allowing improved logical reasoning without compromising safety priors.
- **Challenge 2: Activation Hygiene in Modular Agents**. The challenge is that while adapter weights are modular, the residual stream is a shared resource, leading to feature entanglement. AURA solves this by enforcing geometric decoupling, maintaining greater than 0.98 cosine fidelity to the safe state, thus preventing the contamination of safety contexts by previous high-entropy tasks.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: AURA is implemented as a spectral regularization framework during fine-tuning, suitable for high-frequency agentic environments requiring rapid context switching. It operates without modifying the backbone weights, focusing instead on constraining update directions.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - **Performance Recovery**: AURA recovers 23.0% of the performance lost due to logical fine-tuning interference.
    - **Safety Preservation**: It maintains greater than 0.98 cosine fidelity to the original safe state.
    - **Comparison**: Unlike standard fine-tuning which incurs a 23.3% interference penalty, AURA effectively mitigates this drop, demonstrating that reasoning and alignment can be optimized simultaneously through geometric decoupling.

#### Summary
This paper identifies "Sequential Subspace Interference" in modular agentic systems, where standard fine-tuning causes significant reasoning drift and safety vulnerabilities. The authors propose AURA, a framework that uses spectral regularization and orthogonal adaptation to constrain reasoning updates to the orthogonal complement of the safety alignment manifold. Empirical results show that AURA significantly improves logical reasoning capabilities while preserving safety alignment with high fidelity, offering a robust solution for building efficient and secure modular agents.
