## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Small Language Model (SLM) agents face risks of compositional harm during multi-step tool use, such as data leakage where a private read is followed by an external send. Safety controls are needed to track consequences across steps with minimal monitoring overhead.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Traditional per-action labels miss compositional harms as individual steps appear benign. Baselines based on executed-step rates suffer from "control-unit mismatch," diluting harm signals by dividing by the total number of actions, thus underestimating episode-level risk. Additionally, full-prefix scoring is computationally expensive and includes irrelevant context.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed G-CARB (Graph-Localized Conformal Agent Risk Budget)**: A framework that calibrates when to stop an agent to control compositional harm using graph-localized evidence.
- **Challenge 1: How to effectively track cross-step dependencies to identify compositional harm without adding LLM inference overhead?**
  - **Difficulty**: Scoring full history is costly and noisy, while ignoring history misses chain-like leaks (e.g., read-carry-send).
  - **Solution**: G-CARB selects scorer evidence along observable dependency paths from private sources to outgoing actions. It retains relevant dependency chains without requiring additional language model inference for gate score computation.
- **Challenge 2: How to address the underestimation of harm due to control-unit mismatch in traditional conformal risk control?**
  - **Difficulty**: Standard methods may underestimate single-step harm due to large denominators (action counts) or fail to bound episode-level risk properly.
  - **Solution**: Introduces CARB with a global harm ledger that records declared harmful events across all executed actions before stopping. Under exchangeable episodes, standard Conformal Risk Control (CRC) calibrates the stopping threshold to bound the expected declared loss.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Evaluated on the AgentDojo replay dataset with two 14B backbone models. G-CARB acts as an external gate where a lightweight scorer evaluates evidence, and a calibrated threshold determines execution continuation, avoiding LLM calls at every decision.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - **Efficiency**: G-CARB roughly halves the number of scorer-input records at intermediate risk budgets compared to full-prefix scoring.
  - **Task Completion**: Improves autonomous task completion rates relative to full-prefix scoring.
  - **Comparisons**: While random contexts of the same size yield similar gains, controlled examples show that retaining relevant dependencies further avoids stopping benign work, validating the effectiveness of graph-localized evidence selection.
  - **Vs. Related Work**: Outperforms CORA and executed-step-rate baselines by accurately controlling compositional harm through the global harm ledger and dependency-aware evidence selection, while maintaining low computational overhead.

#### Summary
This paper addresses compositional harm in SLM agents by proposing G-CARB. By introducing a global harm ledger and graph-dependent evidence selection, G-CARB ensures theoretical conformal risk bounds while significantly reducing monitoring overhead and improving task completion. It effectively resolves limitations in cross-step harm tracking and control-unit matching, offering a new paradigm for cost-effective and efficient agent safety control.
