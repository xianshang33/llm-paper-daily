## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Multi-agent debate (MAD) has emerged as an effective paradigm for enhancing Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning and is increasingly applied to multimodal settings. However, existing multimodal MAD frameworks typically expose all agents to the same fixed visual input.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current methods ignore the substantial variation in visual scale requirements across different samples and agents. Furthermore, they frequently suffer from "groupthink," where agents prematurely abandon correct deductions to conform with confident but hallucinated peer responses.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed DREAM Framework**: Introduces Dynamic Resolution Assignment For Multimodal Multi-Agent Debate to address rigid visual scaling and groupthink.
- **Challenge 1: Performance bottlenecks caused by static visual resolution**. The difficulty lies in the fact that optimal resolution is sample-dependent; high resolution introduces noise in simple tasks, while low resolution obscures details in complex ones. Solution: The paper proposes a Dynamic Resolution Assignment mechanism via a zero-shot "probe round." Agents test multiple resolutions, quantify uncertainty using Average Normalized Log-Likelihood (ANLL), and use an adaptive threshold to assign each agent to its empirically optimal resolution.
- **Challenge 2: Groupthink in multi-agent interactions**. The difficulty is that agents often override their own correct early judgments due to pressure from confident but incorrect peer responses. Solution: The paper introduces Uncertainty-Guided Rollback Aggregation, which tracks uncertainty over debate rounds and restores early low-uncertainty answers that were overridden by group pressure.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on six multimodal datasets without dataset-specific tuning.
- **Results**: DREAM improves the accuracy-token trade-off by 1.5% to 3.2% in accuracy compared to standard multi-agent debate baselines.
- **Advantages**: The method not only enhances final answer accuracy but also optimizes token efficiency by dynamically adjusting resolution, avoiding the noise from fixed high resolutions or information loss from fixed low resolutions.

## 4. Conclusion
The DREAM framework effectively addresses the bottlenecks of rigid visual scaling and groupthink in multimodal multi-agent debate through two core components: dynamic resolution assignment and uncertainty-guided rollback aggregation. Experiments demonstrate significant improvements in reasoning accuracy and computational resource optimization across multiple benchmarks, offering a new perspective for collaborative reasoning in multimodal large models.
