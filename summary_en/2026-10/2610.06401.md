## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Tool-using LLM agents are vulnerable to indirect prompt injection attacks because they process untrusted external content from sources like webpages and emails, which can hijack agent tasks.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current training-time defenses reduce attack success rates but often degrade general capabilities. The authors show these defenses cause significant drift in the model's output distribution, altering behavior even in benign settings. A specific failure mode is identified where models refuse to execute necessary steps indicated by legitimate tool outputs, leaving authorized tasks unfinished.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed RAISED (Robust Attack Invariance through Self-Distillation)**: A training framework combining self-generation and self-distillation to address the robustness-utility trade-off.
- **Challenge 1: Distribution Drift and Utility Degradation**. Existing defenses shift the model away from its original behavior distribution. RAISED addresses this by using self-distillation to train the student model to match the teacher's clean-context behavior on both clean and injected variants, preserving the original distribution while enhancing robustness.
- **Challenge 2: Over-rejection of Benign Tool Guidance**. Current defenses inadvertently suppress the model's ability to follow legitimate actionable information from tools. RAISED mitigates this by self-generating scenarios where task completion relies on tool outputs, ensuring the model learns to ignore malicious injections while retaining responsiveness to legitimate tool feedback.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: RAISED first self-generates tool-use scenarios, emphasizing cases requiring action based on tool outputs. It then employs self-distillation, training the student to mimic the teacher's behavior on clean contexts across both clean and injected trajectory variants.
- **Evaluation Results**: RAISED substantially reduces the attack success rate of prompt injections in tool responses.
- **Comparison**: Unlike prior training-based defenses, RAISED preserves utility on both agentic and general-purpose benchmarks, avoiding the performance degradation typical of other methods.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper identifies critical flaws in existing defenses against indirect prompt injection, specifically distribution drift and the failure to act on legitimate tool guidance. The proposed RAISED framework effectively counters these issues by leveraging self-generation and self-distillation, achieving strong robustness against attacks while maintaining high model utility in complex and general tasks.
