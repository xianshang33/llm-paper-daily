## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: As LLM agents evolve towards persistent, long-horizon interactions, memory systems are crucial for retaining and reusing past experiences. However, most existing agent memory systems construct memory in a "query-agnostic" manner, leading to unnecessary preprocessing costs and the potential loss of details that may prove essential later.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: While recent studies have shifted towards runtime adaptive memory processing, they are typically limited to specific operations or fixed processing pipelines. Existing methods suffer from structural rigidity, lacking flexible control over performance, cost, and latency. They often optimize only a subset of efficiency objectives (e.g., token usage) while ignoring user-perceived latency, and fail to leverage the varying capabilities, costs, and latencies of heterogeneous models (LLMs/VLMs) based on query difficulty and multimodal evidence.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed MemPilot Framework**: A flexible framework that orchestrates on-demand multimodal memory curation under different performance-cost-latency preferences.
- **Challenge 1: Structural Rigidity in Runtime Memory Control and Difficulty in Fine-grained Trade-off Optimization**. Existing methods rely on hand-designed operations or fixed pipelines, making query-specific adaptation difficult. MemPilot addresses this by optimizing a multi-step LLM policy via reinforcement learning. This policy iteratively chooses between retrieving from query-agnostic memory and delegating query-specific curation of raw multimodal history to heterogeneous LLMs and VLMs, enabling joint control over evidence amount, curation instructions, model selection, and visual access.
- **Challenge 2: Policy Optimization under Competing Objectives and Credit Assignment**. Balancing competing objectives like performance, cost, and latency is challenging with traditional aggregation methods. MemPilot adapts objective-wise advantage decoupling by separately estimating each objective's advantage before aggregation. Furthermore, it introduces prefix-based marginal utility estimation for fine-grained credit assignment across multi-step rollouts.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Experiments were conducted on five multimodal agent-memory benchmarks.
- **Results**: MemPilot demonstrated favorable performance-cost-latency trade-offs across various optimization preferences.
- **Comparison**: Preference sweeps showed that MemPilot yields broader Pareto frontiers compared to existing trade-off-aware baselines, demonstrating its superiority in flexibility and efficiency optimization.

## 4. Conclusion
MemPilot addresses the limitations of existing agent memory systems regarding runtime adaptability and multi-objective trade-offs. By introducing a reinforcement learning-based multi-step policy to dynamically orchestrate on-demand memory curation using heterogeneous models, and employing objective-wise advantage decoupling along with prefix-based marginal utility estimation, MemPilot achieves fine-grained computational resource allocation. Experiments confirm that the method provides superior and more flexible trade-offs between performance, cost, and latency compared to existing baselines.
