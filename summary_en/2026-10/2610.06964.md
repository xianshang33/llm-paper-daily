## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents show strong capabilities in interactive environments but lack mechanisms for continual evolution from experience. Fine-tuning is computationally expensive and requires parameter access, limiting its applicability to closed-source or large-scale models.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: While external memory allows experience accumulation without parameter updates, existing methods focus on representation and organization, resulting in knowledge tightly coupled with specific contexts. This limits generalization. The core challenge is transforming concrete interactions into abstract, reusable knowledge for future decision-making.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SAGA Framework**: Self-evolving Agents through Experience-Grounded Abstraction.
- **Challenge 1: Difficulty in abstracting concrete experiences**. It is hard to extract generalizable principles from specific trajectories while retaining applicability conditions. **Solution**: SAGA progressively transforms interaction trajectories into episodic descriptions, reusable procedures, and principles with explicit conditions, maintaining links to execution evidence in a hierarchical memory.
- **Challenge 2: Difficulty in grounding abstract knowledge into actions**. It is challenging to translate high-level principles into executable steps. **Solution**: SAGA instantiates retrieved principles into task-specific operational guidance and refines candidate actions through corrective feedback and resampling based on principle-derived checks, creating an execution-abstraction feedback loop.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Evaluation Setup**: Experiments conducted on ScienceWorld and ALFWorld benchmarks.
- **Results**: SAGA demonstrates improved task performance compared to baseline methods. Ablation studies highlight the critical role of contextual instantiation and action regulation in leveraging principle-level knowledge.
- **Comparison**: Unlike methods that store raw experiences, SAGA's abstraction mechanism enables better generalization to unseen scenarios by providing high-level principled guidance.

#### Summary
This paper addresses the limitation of LLM agents in continual learning by proposing SAGA. By abstracting concrete interactions into hierarchical knowledge (episodes, procedures, principles) and employing an execution-abstraction feedback loop, SAGA effectively bridges the gap between abstract knowledge and concrete action. Experimental results confirm significant performance improvements in complex interactive environments.
