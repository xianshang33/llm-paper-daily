## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: The ability of LLM agents to learn from experience in unfamiliar and dynamic environments is central to Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). Evaluating this capability is crucial for understanding how effectively agents acquire and utilize new knowledge.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current benchmarks primarily evaluate tasks where rules are explicitly provided or already familiar to pretrained models. This makes it difficult to distinguish whether performance improvements stem from genuine learning through interaction or merely from reasoning with existing pretrained knowledge.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Learn2Play Bench**: A benchmark featuring newly designed text-based games with novel or counterintuitive rules, forcing agents to acquire knowledge through interaction rather than relying on pretrained data.
- **Challenge 1: Distinguishing Learning from Retrieval**
  - **Description & Difficulty**: In standard tasks, performance gains may result from retrieving pretrained knowledge rather than learning new rules, leading to inaccurate assessments of learning ability.
  - **Solution**: The paper designs game environments with hidden, novel, or counterintuitive rules, ensuring agents cannot rely on pretraining and must learn via trial-and-error and feedback.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of Standardized and Controlled Evaluation Protocols**
  - **Description & Difficulty**: Existing studies use disparate models, tasks, and protocols, making it hard to directly compare the learning capabilities of different self-evolving methods.
  - **Solution**: It provides reproducible feedback and automatic scoring for controlled evaluation across repeated attempts, and varies game instances to test the generalization of learned knowledge to new situations.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Setup**: The study evaluates the impact of different backbone models, self-evolving methods, and agent harnesses on learning performance.
- **Key Findings & Comparisons**:
  1. **Experience Retention**: Retaining complete records of actions and feedback supports more effective learning than summarizing experiences into abstract rules or strategies.
  2. **Human-Agent Gap**: Top-performing human players achieve higher peak scores than evaluated agents. Humans explore more diverse strategies and repeat actions less frequently.
  3. **Harness Matters**: With a fixed backbone, changing the agent harness can improve performance while reducing estimated inference costs.

## 4. Conclusion
Learn2Play Bench addresses the gap in evaluating LLM agents' ability to learn from experience in unfamiliar environments. By introducing text-based games with novel rules, the benchmark reveals limitations in current agents regarding experience retention, strategy exploration, and framework dependency. The findings suggest that simple memory retention outperforms complex rule extraction, and optimizing the agent harness is an effective way to enhance learning efficiency and reduce costs, offering directions for future improvements in adaptive agent capabilities.
