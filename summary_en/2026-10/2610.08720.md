## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: LLM-based agents are advancing in scientific and engineering problem-solving. Physics simulation is a critical yet challenging testbed for reproducing complex physical phenomena, with applications in embodied AI, games, and films. Solvers are the core component, computing how dynamic system states evolve over time.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Building solvers requires integrating physical understanding (model identification), mathematical reasoning (formulating dynamics), and software engineering (coding). Previous works mostly focus on static scene construction or simple dynamic generation, lacking a comprehensive benchmark to evaluate LLM agents' ability to generate complete, correct, and executable physical solvers from scratch.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed WorldSolver Benchmark**: A benchmark comprising 168 simulation tasks derived from 61 classic computer graphics papers across 7 physical domains. Each task provides a fixed code scaffold, requiring the agent to complete the solver implementation.
- **Challenge 1: Integration of Multimodal Capabilities and Code Accuracy**. The difficulty lies in the agent's need to simultaneously possess physical intuition, mathematical modeling skills, and coding proficiency; failure in any step leads to overall failure. The paper addresses this by providing fixed environment scaffolds to focus evaluation on solver logic and using execution checks to verify runnability.
- **Challenge 2: Dual Verification of Physical Realism and Visual Fidelity**. The challenge is that executable code does not guarantee physical correctness. The paper introduces Visual Fidelity (reproducing intended dynamic behavior) and Physical Plausibility (physics-grounded verification) to rigorously assess the correctness of the generated dynamics beyond mere execution.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Metrics**: Evaluation is conducted along three dimensions: Execution Checks (successful run), Visual Fidelity (visual match to expected behavior), and Physical Plausibility (adherence to physical laws).
- **Results**: Frontier agents struggle to produce executable solvers, and achieving visual and physical correctness is even harder. GPT-5.6-Sol and Claude-Opus-5 performed comparatively better but achieved overall scores of only 48.7% and 46.7%, respectively.
- **Comparison**: While GPT-5.6-Sol and Claude-Opus-5 outperformed other evaluated agents, the low absolute scores indicate that current LLMs are far from mastering complex physics simulation generation, highlighting significant room for improvement.

## 4. Conclusion
WorldSolver represents an early but crucial step toward agentic solver generation. It exposes the limitations of current LLM agents in integrating physical understanding, mathematical reasoning, and code implementation. The benchmark aims to drive progress toward agents capable of faithfully simulating the dynamic physical world, providing a standard for evaluation and future research in embodied AI and related fields.
