## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: While Large Language Models (LLMs) can solve many narrow tasks, querying them individually for millions of related instances is prohibitively expensive. Existing agents typically invoke general-purpose LLMs for each instance, leading to linear cost growth.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current approaches rely on human-engineered solutions, such as distilling smaller task-specific models or writing reusable programs. There is a lack of frameworks to evaluate or enable LLM agents to autonomously convert general capabilities into cheap, task-specific solutions that balance quality and amortized cost.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed BOTTLED Benchmark**: A new benchmark where agents receive an entire unlabeled workload and must complete it under fixed time, compute, and API budgets by creating reusable artifacts (e.g., small models or programs).
- **Challenge 1: Discrepancy between Zero-Shot and Bottling Capabilities**. The difficulty lies in the fact that strong zero-shot performance does not reliably translate to efficient bottling, as agents must complexly trade off resources between exploration (building artifacts) and exploitation (applying them). The paper reveals this gap, showing that many models perform significantly below their zero-shot confidence intervals after bottling, indicating that base model strength alone is insufficient for cost efficiency.
- **Challenge 2: Balancing Quality and Cost under Limited Budgets**. The challenge is for the agent to judge whether upfront investment yields sufficient savings across the batch while maintaining answer quality. The paper demonstrates that top models (e.g., Opus 5) can retain most performance while drastically reducing costs by autonomously choosing optimal strategies, validating the approach's effectiveness under extreme cost constraints.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The BOTTLED benchmark requires agents to process full workloads within strict budgets, choosing methods like distillation or code generation. Experiments involved 10 LLMs across 3 tasks.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - 48 out of 60 bottling runs scored below the lower bound of the 95% confidence interval of their model's zero-shot performance.
    - 31 runs underperformed compared to small-model distillation baselines with the same token budget.
    - However, successful bottling yields substantial savings: Opus 5 retained ~82% of its zero-shot Macro-F1 on query-product relevance classification at ~657x lower cost.
- **Comparison with Related Work**:
    - Compared to Jev, a specialized "System 1" model for cheap inference, Opus 5 via bottling recovered ~94% of Jev's Macro-F1 at one-quarter of Jev's projected full-workload cost.
    - Results indicate that while current agent bottling capabilities are unstable, they offer significant potential, outperforming repeated queries and competing favorably with dedicated lightweight models in specific scenarios.

#### Summary
This paper introduces the concept of "Bottling" and the BOTTLED benchmark to evaluate and improve LLM agents' ability to transform general capabilities into low-cost, scalable artifacts for repetitive workloads. The study finds that current strong zero-shot models do not necessarily possess robust bottling capabilities, revealing a significant performance gap. Nevertheless, successful bottling strategies can achieve hundreds-fold cost reductions while maintaining high accuracy, even outperforming specialized lightweight models in cost-efficiency. This research provides a crucial foundation for developing more efficient autonomous agents.
