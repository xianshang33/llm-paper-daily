## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Search agents enable Large Language Models (LLMs) to iteratively retrieve and use information for complex multi-hop questions. Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) is a promising approach for post-training these agents.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current RLVR methods rely heavily on sparse, outcome-based supervision. This sparsity makes credit assignment difficult; even if intermediate steps retrieve crucial evidence, the entire trajectory may receive zero reward if the final answer is incorrect. This limits learning efficiency by failing to leverage signals from intermediate retrieval progress.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a training framework combining intermediate signals with final outcome rewards**: The framework systematically investigates how reward shaping and credit assignment strategies can utilize learning signals from intermediate retrieval steps to improve RL for search agents.
- **Challenge 1: Constructing and selecting intermediate supervision signals**. The difficulty lies in determining which retrieved information warrants a reward (e.g., immediate reward for acquired facts vs. delayed reward pending prerequisites). The paper addresses this by studying various reward shaping strategies and analyzing their impact on policy updates.
- **Challenge 2: Mechanisms for credit assignment of intermediate rewards**. The challenge is deciding how to incorporate intermediate rewards into policy updates (either as a modification to the global trajectory advantage or locally attributed to specific search actions). The paper compares global credit assignment (altering shared advantage for the whole trajectory) against local credit assignment (updating only specific search actions), revealing the critical impact of assignment location on training behavior.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Experiments were conducted across multiple benchmarks under matched training conditions to ensure fairness.
- **Results**: The results demonstrate that incorporating intermediate supervision signals with appropriate credit assignment strategies significantly improves the aggregate performance of search agents.
- **Comparison**: Compared to traditional RLVR methods relying solely on final outcome rewards, the proposed approach more effectively utilizes information from multi-step search trajectories. The study highlights that both the choice of intermediate signal and the method of credit assignment significantly affect training outcomes, establishing reward design and credit assignment as key design dimensions for effective search agents.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper investigates the role of intermediate supervision in reinforcement learning for search agents, highlighting the inefficiencies of sparse, outcome-only rewards. By systematically studying reward shaping and credit assignment, the authors propose a framework that integrates intermediate signals with final rewards. Experiments confirm that carefully designed intermediate rewards and their assignment mechanisms significantly enhance agent performance, offering valuable design guidelines for future retrieval-augmented generation agents.
