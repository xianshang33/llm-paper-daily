## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: With the rise of AI coding agents, questions have emerged about whether Software Engineering (SE) is becoming obsolete and if software engineers are redundant. Empirical data suggests that while AI boosts efficiency in simple tasks, it often leads to "hallucination loops" in complex, high-stakes professional development, producing plausible but unverifiable code.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current practices like "Vibe Coding" often mistake interaction fluency for correctness. Existing research indicates that no amount of model training can eliminate the structural deficits of Large Language Models (LLMs), meaning AI-assisted development without rigorous SE foundations results in untrustworthy software.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Core Argument**: The advent of AI coding agents makes SE and software engineers essential, not obsolete. SE serves as the missing foundation to fill the "structural vacuum" created by LLMs, with engineers acting as methodologists, mediators, and custodians.
- **Challenge 1: Structural Deficits of LLMs Lead to Untrustworthy Outputs**. LLMs suffer from probabilistic generation, agnosticism, and semantic statelessness, creating a structural vacuum that training cannot fix. The paper argues this vacuum must be filled by four knowledge levers—methodological, domain, design, and process knowledge—which must be reified as persistent artifacts managed by software engineers.
- **Challenge 2: Bridging the Gap from "Probable" to "Provable"**. Professional software requires correctness, whereas AI offers only probability. The paper demonstrates that software engineers are necessary to mediate this gap by applying formal specifications and verification, thereby preventing developers from wasting time correcting plausible but incorrect AI-generated code.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Evaluation and Comparison**: This is a position paper based on a synthesis of existing empirical studies rather than a new system implementation. The author contrasts several key findings:
    - Cui et al. [1] found a 26% increase in task completion with AI, primarily benefiting less experienced developers.
    - Becker et al. [2] showed in a randomized controlled trial that experienced developers took 19% longer when using AI, mistakenly believing they were helped.
    - Xu et al. [10] reported that while total code output increased, core contributors' original code output dropped by 19%, with time shifted to code review and rework.
- **Conclusion Contrast**: Contrasting with optimistic views that AI will replace programmers, the paper aligns with Meyer [5] in arguing that while "Vibe Coding" may suffice for casual apps, professional software demands SE rigor. Empirical evidence shows that time saved on delimited tasks is recaptured in supervising complex AI outputs, as the reference for correctness resides with the developer, not the tool.

#### Summary
This paper strongly refutes the notion that AI will render Software Engineering obsolete. It argues that the inherent probabilistic and stateless nature of LLMs prevents them from independently handling professional software development. SE methodologies, domain knowledge, and process controls are indispensable for ensuring software trustworthiness. Future software engineers will evolve from coders to managers and verifiers of AI agents, with their core value lying in transforming AI's "probabilistic generation" into engineering's "provable correctness."
