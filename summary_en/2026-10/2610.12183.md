## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Black-box optimization (BBO) is critical in scientific and engineering domains where objective evaluations are expensive and gradients are unavailable. Traditional methods (e.g., Bayesian Optimization, Evolutionary Algorithms) rely solely on numerical observations, ignoring rich non-numerical information like task descriptions and domain semantics. LLM agents offer a new paradigm by integrating semantic understanding, computational tools, and feedback-driven decision-making.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current studies on agentic BBO utilize heterogeneous task domains and system configurations, making cross-study comparisons difficult and isolating the impact of specific design choices nearly impossible. There is a lack of a unified standard to evaluate the true capabilities of different LLM agents in BBO.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed AgenticBBO-Bench**: A cross-domain benchmark for agentic BBO covering synthetic functions, hyperparameter optimization, database tuning, chip design, and molecular design, all under a unified finite-budget evaluation protocol.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of unified evaluation standards hinders performance comparison.** Difficulty arises from heterogeneous tasks and configurations in prior work. **Solution**: The paper constructs a standardized benchmark across five diverse domains with a unified evaluation protocol, enabling direct comparison of different agent systems and LLMs.
- **Challenge 2: Unclear mechanisms of how specific agent design elements affect performance.** It is difficult to quantify the contribution of tools, priors, or the LLM's role. **Solution**: Through ablation studies, the paper investigates three key factors: optimization tools, task information/priors, and the LLM's role during search, revealing that numerical tools do not always help, while task semantics are broadly useful.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: Code is open-sourced. The benchmark integrates various task environments and supports evaluating different LLMs via the Codex agent harness.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - Agentic BBO achieves higher family-averaged scores than direct LLM-based methods in all five domains.
    - It outperforms the best numerical optimizers in four out of five domains.
    - In the frontier challenge evaluating seven LLMs, GPT-6 Astra and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash lie on the Pareto frontier of performance and cost.
- **Comparison with Related Work**:
    - Compared to traditional numerical optimizers, agentic methods better leverage semantic information, showing superior performance in most complex tasks.
    - Compared to direct LLM prompting, the agent framework (integrating tools and feedback loops) significantly improves optimization outcomes.
    - Key findings indicate that additional numerical tools do not consistently improve performance, specific priors can be less reliable than general task semantics, and numerical optimizers can effectively absorb gains from agent-established search trajectories.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the lack of unified benchmarks in agentic black-box optimization by proposing AgenticBBO-Bench. Extensive experiments demonstrate the superiority of agentic methods in cross-domain BBO tasks and provide deep insights into the factors influencing agent performance. This benchmark provides a standardized platform for comparing future general-purpose models and agent systems, advancing the field towards universal BBO via agentic operations.
