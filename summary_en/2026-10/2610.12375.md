## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: LLM Agents are increasingly deployed in autonomous applications (e.g., trip planning, IT triage), but their lack of supervision leads to high costs (due to hallucinations, loops) and safety risks (e.g., irreversible actions, sandbox escapes).
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current solutions are either post-hoc evaluations (too late to save costs/prevent damage) or online safeguard agents (add significant latency/cost, struggle with small models detecting large model errors, and ignore structural dependencies like topology).

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed OnTrack**: A real-time streaming monitoring mechanism that models agent execution as a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) and uses Streaming Structure-Aware Optimal Transport to compare current steps against successful references in ~1ms per step.
- **Challenge 1: How to achieve high-precision real-time structural matching with low latency?**
  - **Difficulty**: Semantic similarity misses topological dependencies, while complex graph matching is too slow for real-time use.
  - **Solution**: OnTrack maps steps and dependencies to DAG nodes/edges and uses optimal transport theory to efficiently compute structural distance between partial and reference trajectories.
- **Challenge 2: How to maintain monitoring effectiveness under varying data availability?**
  - **Difficulty**: Historical traces or tool schemas may be unavailable, limiting reference data.
  - **Solution**: The paper defines three access regimes (Full, Schema-only, No Prior). It adapts monitoring goals from plan violation detection (full access) to identifying loops/stalls/redundant calls (limited access), ensuring utility across constraints.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Setup**: Evaluated on SWE-bench trajectories, focusing on the first 8 steps.
- **Results**:
  - **Ranking Performance**: OnTrack outperforms content-similarity baselines in ranking failing trajectories below successful ones, with a +0.057 improvement in AUROC.
  - **Cost Savings**: An abort policy saved ~18% of compute resources wasted on failing runs.
  - **Precision**: 83% of interrupted runs were indeed heading toward failure (5 out of 6 aborts were correct).
- **Comparison**: Unlike post-hoc methods, OnTrack intervenes pre-failure. Unlike safeguard agents, it avoids extra LLM calls, reducing latency/cost while capturing structural errors.

#### Summary
OnTrack introduces a lightweight, real-time monitoring solution for LLM Agents using structure-aware optimal transport. It addresses cost and safety concerns by detecting anomalies in milliseconds. Experiments show it surpasses semantic baselines in early failure detection and significantly reduces wasted compute, offering a practical engineering solution for safe Agent deployment.
