## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Reinforcement Learning (RL) is a key paradigm for enhancing Large Language Model (LLM) agents in reasoning, planning, and tool use. However, existing agent RL methods suffer from an "on-policy reasoning bottleneck": when tasks exceed the model's current capability, successful trajectories become extremely sparse, causing training instability or collapse.
- **Limitations of Prior Work**: Standard on-policy RL mainly amplifies behaviors already supported by the current policy, failing to induce new reasoning patterns. While teacher-guided methods (e.g., LUFFY, OPD) offer partial mitigation, their performance is capped by the teacher's capabilities and cannot help the model transcend its own boundaries.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Rollout-Time Intervention (RTI)**: A technique that leverages reward-side evidence (evaluation rubrics) during rollout to convert erroneous trajectories into successful ones, eliciting paths beyond the policy's intrinsic exploration capacity.
- **Challenge 1: Sparse Effective Trajectories Leading to Training Collapse**. When tasks are too difficult, the model rarely generates successful trajectories, starving the learning process. RTI addresses this by intervening during rollout to correct early critical errors using evaluation rules, transforming failed attempts into successful demonstrations and providing high-quality learning signals for hard tasks.
- **Challenge 2: Distributional Shift from Off-Policy Interventions**. Intervention-generated trajectories are inherently off-policy, risking performance degradation due to large distributional shifts. RTI mitigates this by filtering out trajectories with excessive distributional shifts, ensuring that only moderately shifted, high-value data guides policy optimization safely.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Extensive experiments were conducted across five benchmarks.
- **Results**: RTI consistently outperforms existing RL baselines.
- **Performance**: The post-trained policy not only approaches the performance of significantly stronger contemporary LLMs but, more importantly, transcends its original capability boundary, demonstrating the method's effectiveness in unlocking agent potential.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the training collapse in agent RL caused by tasks exceeding model capabilities by proposing Rollout-Time Intervention (RTI). By correcting erroneous trajectories during rollout using evaluation rules and filtering for distributional shift, RTI effectively tackles the challenges of trajectory sparsity and off-policy data usage. Experiments show that RTI significantly enhances agent performance, allowing it to surpass original capability limits, offering a new pathway for improving LLM agents in complex tasks.
