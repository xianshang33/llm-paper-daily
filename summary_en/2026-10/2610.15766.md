## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have enhanced robotic long-horizon planning. However, applying them directly to surgical robotics faces significant hurdles.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current end-to-end VLA models suffer from three main issues in surgical contexts: insufficient precision in predicting low-level continuous actions compared to strict surgical tolerances; opaque decision-making processes that lack interpretability for clinicians; and scarcity of high-quality, expert-level surgical manipulation data.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SurgiCLAW**: A hierarchical atomic-skill framework for precision surgical manipulation. It decouples semantic decision-making from high-precision control by confining the MLLM to visual understanding, structured reasoning, and atomic skill selection, while delegating physical execution to verifiable deterministic control programs.
- **Challenge 1: Ensuring High Precision and Safety in Surgical Operations.**
  - **Difficulty**: Direct output of continuous control signals by LLMs can lead to micro-deviations causing tissue damage, lacking explicit safety boundaries.
  - **Solution**: SurgiCLAW restricts the MLLM to selecting from a predefined library of "atomic skills." Each skill has explicit inputs, prerequisites, motion boundaries, and safety constraints, executed by deterministic controllers (e.g., geometric optimization, visual servoing), ensuring precise and verifiable physical execution.
- **Challenge 2: Addressing Data Scarcity and Lack of Interpretability.**
  - **Difficulty**: Acquiring expert surgical trajectories is costly, and black-box models hinder clinical trust.
  - **Solution**: The authors constructed a state-to-skill invocation dataset, augmented with visual and state perturbations. The model undergoes supervised fine-tuning for structured skill invocation, followed by optimization using three rewards (action correctness, operational prerequisites, format validity). This enhances data efficiency and provides interpretable, structured rationales for decisions.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on brain-machine-interface (BMI) implantation, a representative high-precision task. Metrics included subtask success, offline model comparison, real-robot performance, and ablation studies.
- **Results**:
  - On a fixed test set, SurgiCLAW achieved **94.6%** skill-selection accuracy and a **93.0%** Macro-F1 score.
  - In 50 closed-loop real-robot implantation trials, it achieved an **88.0%** task success rate.
- **Comparison**: Compared to traditional end-to-end imitation learning, reinforcement learning, and general VLA models, SurgiCLAW significantly improves execution precision and safety while maintaining high-level semantic understanding, effectively addressing the precision and transparency gaps in generic models.

## 4. Conclusion
SurgiCLAW introduces a novel hierarchical architecture that successfully integrates the high-level cognitive capabilities of MLLMs with the precise execution of deterministic control systems. By introducing atomic skills and structured reasoning, it effectively overcomes core challenges in surgical robotics: data scarcity, extreme precision requirements, and the need for interpretable decisions. Experimental results demonstrate high accuracy and success rates in BMI implantation tasks, offering a trustworthy pathway for intelligent high-precision surgical robots.
