## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large language model (LLM) agents are increasingly deployed in teams for tasks like debating, evidence pooling, and negotiation. However, these teams often fail in characteristically social ways, such as information holders remaining silent, dominant speakers anchoring groups to wrong answers, and unsanctioned norm violations.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current agent systems typically encode social context via static role instructions or unstructured memory. They lack an explicit, validated account of how directed relationships evolve over time, treating teams as collections of fixed personas rather than dynamic social systems. This fails to capture the development of trust, avoidance, alignment, or opposition, leading to insufficient social fidelity.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Agent Sociodrama Framework**: A longitudinal role-playing framework for modeling and validating evolving social dynamics in LLM agent teams, introducing Trajectory-Guided Persona Recasting (TPR).
- **Challenge 1: Ensuring Fidelity in Evolving Social Dynamics**
  - **Difficulty**: It is not enough to merely simulate interactions; the changes in emotional states and asymmetric relationships between agents must align with classical social theory patterns and human judgments to avoid socially illogical interactions.
  - **Solution**: Introduces a unified external LLM judge that updates agents' emotional states and directed, asymmetric relationships every round. These social trajectories are validated against classical social theory patterns and human agreement.
- **Challenge 2: Repairing Team Failures Caused by Social Dynamic Issues**
  - **Difficulty**: When teams fail due to social dynamics (e.g., poor communication, power imbalances), static personas cannot adaptively adjust, making it hard to extract actionable signals for repair from failed interactions.
  - **Solution**: Proposes Trajectory-Guided Persona Recasting (TPR), which diagnoses group-dynamic failures from interaction transcripts and social trajectories, then recasts agent personas before rerunning the task to optimize collaboration.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Setup**: Evaluated across 6 LLM backbones and 5 social scenarios. Social fidelity was measured using classical social theory patterns and consistency with human judgments. TPR performance was assessed on four collaborative benchmarks.
- **Key Results**:
  - **Social Fidelity**: Generated social trajectories exhibited structured social patterns and model-specific differences, validating the framework's effectiveness.
  - **Performance Improvement**: TPR significantly improved performance for both heterogeneous and randomly initialized agent persona compositions, yielding up to an 83.3 percentage point increase in accuracy over initial casting.
  - **Comparison with Related Work**: In distributed information cooperation and mixed-motive bargaining tasks, Agent Sociodrama outperformed SOTOPIA and AgentVerse in three out of four comparisons, with up to a 37.5 percentage point higher Pareto rate.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper presents Agent Sociodrama, addressing the lack of dynamic social relationship modeling in long-term LLM agent team interactions. By maintaining evolving emotional and relational states via an external judge and validating their social fidelity, the work demonstrates that validated social trajectories provide actionable signals for repairing agent teams. The proposed TPR method significantly enhances team performance in complex collaborative tasks by diagnosing and recasting personas, surpassing existing baselines like SOTOPIA and AgentVerse.
