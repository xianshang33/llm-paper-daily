## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Reinforcement learning (RL) for Large Language Model (LLM) agents faces significant challenges in long-horizon tasks with sparse outcome rewards, making it difficult to assign credit to individual actions.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Methods like GRPO assign uniform trajectory-level advantage to all actions, failing to distinguish individual contributions (e.g., rewarding mistakes in successful trajectories). Critic-based methods (e.g., PPO) require training an additional value function, which is computationally expensive and unstable when learning from sparse rewards over long histories.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed LLM Value Function RL (LVRL)**: A method that leverages the pretrained knowledge and in-context learning abilities of an LLM to estimate policy-aware state values without fine-tuning a separate critic.
- **Challenge 1: How to achieve fine-grained step-level credit assignment without expensive training?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional critics are costly and unstable; trajectory-level methods are too coarse.
  - **Solution**: LVRL uses a frozen LLM as a value estimator, conditioning it on group context and calibrating predictions using known initial and terminal state values to derive step-level credit from value differences.
- **Challenge 2: How to effectively leverage expert policies for distillation?**
  - **Difficulty**: Standard distillation treats all steps equally, missing critical errors that degrade performance.
  - **Solution**: Introduced LLM Value Function OPD (LVOPD), which focuses teacher supervision specifically on actions associated with decreases in estimated value, thereby correcting errors more efficiently.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Setup**: Evaluated across diverse agentic benchmarks: AppWorld, BabyAI, and SWE-bench Verified, with policy scales ranging from 4B to 32B parameters.
- **Results vs. Baselines**:
  - LVRL consistently and substantially outperforms both GRPO and PPO across all scales.
  - On AppWorld, LVRL yields up to 13.6 percentage points absolute improvement over GRPO.
  - On SWE-bench Verified, LVRL yields up to 8.6 percentage points absolute improvement over GRPO.
  - On BabyAI (requiring extended exploration), LVRL bridges the gap from near-total failure (0%) to 75.0% success.
  - LVOPD shows consistent improvements over standard distillation baselines on AppWorld.
- **Analysis**: Monte-Carlo analysis confirms that the frozen critic accurately identifies semantic pitfalls and recoveries, demonstrating that LVRL extracts robust, policy-aware step-level credit from sparse terminal outcomes.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper introduces LVRL and LVOPD to address the credit assignment problem in long-horizon LLM agent tasks with sparse rewards. By utilizing the in-context learning capabilities of frozen LLMs, the approach avoids the high cost and instability of training separate critics while providing finer-grained feedback than trajectory-level methods. Extensive experiments show that LVRL significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods like GRPO and PPO, particularly in complex exploration tasks, offering a new efficient paradigm for RL training of LLM agents.
