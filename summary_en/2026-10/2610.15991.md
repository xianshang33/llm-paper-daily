## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: In distributed agent workflows, delegated authority is cached at tool gateways. Revocation status is often reported via issuer commit, transport acknowledgment, or receiver persistence.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current observation points (e.g., successful transport or receiver storage) do not guarantee that all gateways have enforced the change or that guarded effects have stopped. Even if a receiver acknowledges and persists a revocation event, downstream gateways may retain stale authority due to network partitions or selective non-delivery, leading to unauthorized operations and committed effects (e.g., file system writes or database commits).

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a C0-C5 Bounded Outcome Contract**: This contract spans from issuer commit to effect prevention, implemented as a persistent monitor over a frozen gateway set, call set, and observation window.
- **Challenge 1: Misjudgment of Revocation Completion**. Traditional methods treat transport acknowledgment or receiver persistence as completion, leading to false negatives in distributed failure scenarios (i.e., believing revocation is complete when it is not). The paper defines six semantic states (C0-C5) and uses a 14-case fault matrix for conservative classification. It demonstrates that relying solely on C3 or C4 states results in 7 or 5 false positives, respectively, whereas the full C0-C5 verdict eliminates these errors.
- **Challenge 2: Verification of Evidence Immutability and Persistence in Distributed Environments**. Ensuring that evidence of revocation execution is not tampered with or lost during gateway restarts, network jitter, or partial failures is difficult. The paper implements a persistent monitor based on a fixed observation window, auditing 8 semantic cases to ensure evidence immutability and detection of sticky violations, providing a deterministic verdict for revocation completion under complex fault conditions.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation and Deployment**: Experiments were conducted using a maintained Keycloak 26.7 identity server, involving two durable receivers, eight physical agent-tool gateways, Filesystem and PostgreSQL effect layers, and 16 AWS hosts across three availability zones. Reproduction was also performed using OPA/Toxiproxy and a live three-role LLM workflow with networked MCP tools.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - In normal delivery and recovery scenarios, the C0-C5 contract confirmed no effects were committed.
    - In a receiver-selective non-delivery attack scenario, despite successful transport acknowledgments and receiver persistence, 112 out of 225 calls (49.78%) were admitted by gateways, and all 112 effects were committed. This proves that transport success is evidence of progress, not a verdict of revocation completion.
    - In an unmodified external MCPMark agent experiment, normal C4 state denied all 31 exposed post-barrier calls with no effects committed; whereas selective C3 state allowed 344 calls and committed 145 effects.
- **Comparison**: Compared to existing works like OAuth token revocation, CAEP/SSF frameworks, and "Proof of Execution," which focus on the issuer side or transport layer, this paper highlights the lack of end-to-end verification of actual enforcement at distributed gateways. By introducing an observation window at the effect layer, the proposed method fills this gap and eliminates false positives inherent in traditional approaches.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper exposes the danger of assuming "receipt equals revocation completion" in distributed agent systems. By proposing the C0-C5 Bounded Outcome Contract, the authors demonstrate that monitoring must extend to the prevention of actual effects to accurately determine if revocation is truly complete. Experiments show that under network anomalies or malicious configurations, traditional transport confirmation mechanisms mask serious permission vulnerabilities, while the proposed contract mechanism provides zero-false-positive audit results, establishing a new verification standard for distributed agent security.
