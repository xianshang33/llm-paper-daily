## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Agentic workflows combine Large Language Models (LLMs) with planning, memory, tool use, execution environments, and iterative feedback. Diagnosing failures is difficult because identical user-visible symptoms can stem from model behavior, workflow orchestration, external tools, infrastructure, or their interactions.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current taxonomies focus on foundation model risks (e.g., hallucinations, bias) or traditional software defects. They typically identify "what" went wrong but fail to consistently localize "where" in the workflow the failure occurred, which agent capability was implicated, or whether the responsibility lies with the model, the system, or their interaction. This lack of intermediate process state localization hinders effective diagnosis for complex agentic systems.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Source-Aware Taxonomy of Agentic Workflow Failures**: This taxonomy integrates source attribution with three empirical dimensions: a concrete failure-type dictionary, workflow-stage localization, and agent-capability localization. Its core structure is a two-axis matrix mapping workflow stages against agent capabilities.
- **Challenge 1: Multi-source Nature and Interaction Complexity of Failures**. The difficulty lies in disentangling whether a symptom arises from model reasoning, tool API failures, or infrastructure issues. The paper addresses this by using a "stage-capability" matrix to localize failures to specific intermediate process states and functional capabilities, distinguishing internal model mechanisms from external system interactions.
- **Challenge 2: Cognitive Bias between Academic Research and Engineering Practice**. There is a significant gap between reported failures in literature and real-world deployments. By analyzing 397 literature-derived and 1,196 GitHub-derived failure records, the paper reveals that academia focuses on planning and reasoning, while industry struggles with execution and infrastructure, highlighting the need to treat reliability as a systems problem.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Data and Analysis**: The study analyzed 397 failure records from academic literature and 1,196 from GitHub, including a temporal hold-out validation.
- **Evaluation Results and Comparison**:
    - **Literature-derived Failures**: Emphasize mechanisms related to planning, reasoning, learning, perception, and knowledge.
    - **GitHub-derived Failures**: Concentrate heavily on the execution stage, specifically actuation, tool usage, API interactions, and infrastructure issues. Iterative execution and actuation are the dominant hotspots in real-world data.
- **Comparison Conclusion**: Unlike traditional taxonomies that classify only final failure manifestations, this approach reveals that agentic workflow reliability is not just a model-output problem but a systems engineering issue involving toolchains, API stability, and execution environments. The disparity also reflects differences in reporting mechanisms and observability.

#### Summary
This paper presents a novel source-aware taxonomy for agentic workflow failures, enabling fine-grained diagnosis through a two-axis localization matrix. Empirical analysis demonstrates a significant divergence between academic and industrial failure distributions, with the former focusing on cognitive capabilities and the latter on execution and system integration. The work underscores the importance of treating agentic workflow reliability as a systemic challenge, providing a foundation for structured evaluation, root cause analysis, failure injection, and engineering triage.
