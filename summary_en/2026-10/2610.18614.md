## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Controlling LLM agents in long-horizon, partially observable environments requires a per-step confidence signal to decide whether to trust an action, abstain, or hand off to a human. This signal must predict the eventual success of the current rollout.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current reinforcement learning calibration methods focus on the correctness of individual steps. This greedy optimization fails to capture long-term dependencies, resulting in confidence scores that are locally predictive but poor at forecasting the final outcome of multi-step episodes.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Outcome-Grounded Confidence Calibration**: A method that calibrates step-wise confidence against the eventual outcome of a multi-step episode using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and proper scoring rules.
- **Challenge 1: Linking Local Confidence to Global Outcomes**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional methods optimize for single-step accuracy, ignoring cumulative uncertainty, making them ineffective for predicting final task success.
  - **Solution**: The method introduces a reward function grounded in the final episode outcome, directly optimizing the consistency between step confidence and final success, enabling the agent to predict overall feasibility from the first step.
- **Challenge 2: Disentangling Sources of Confidence Improvement**
  - **Difficulty**: Aggregate metrics mix task difficulty, episode depth, and attempt-level foresight, obscuring whether improvements come from identifying hard tasks or distinguishing successful attempts.
  - **Solution**: A new evaluation protocol separates these factors, revealing that gains primarily stem from early recognition of inevitably failing tasks, while distinguishing success/failure within the same task remains harder.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Offline and online RL training was conducted on Qwen2.5-7B agents in ALFWorld, TextWorld, and WebShop environments. Online training applied four calibration rewards in live ALFWorld settings.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - **Offline RL**: AUROC of step confidence against episode outcome improved from near-chance to 0.68–0.85. The top-10% most confident steps succeeded ~4.3x more often than average.
  - **Online RL**: Early-step AUROC improved, and Expected Calibration Error (ECE) dropped from 0.42 to below 0.12. Selective execution outperformed both untrained agents and format-only RL controls without degrading task success.
- **Comparison**: Unlike prior work focused on single-step correctness, this approach significantly enhances long-horizon outcome prediction reliability, with gains driven by early failure detection.

#### Summary
This paper introduces outcome-grounded confidence calibration for LLM agents, addressing the limitation of traditional single-step calibration in predicting long-horizon task outcomes. By leveraging GRPO and proper scoring rules, the method enables agents to generate reliable per-step confidence signals. Experiments demonstrate significant improvements in calibration quality (AUROC and ECE) across multiple environments, allowing agents to identify potentially failing tasks early, thereby facilitating effective runtime control and human intervention.
