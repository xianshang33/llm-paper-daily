## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Reinforcement Learning (RL) is the primary paradigm for optimizing Large Language Model (LLM) agents. A core challenge in long-horizon multi-turn interactions is accurately attributing trajectory-level rewards to individual actions.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Traditional Actor-Critic algorithms (e.g., PPO) incur high computational overhead and instability due to separate critic networks. Efficient critic-free algorithms (e.g., GRPO) broadcast trajectory-level advantages uniformly to all actions. This "uniform credit assignment dilemma" ignores differences in local validity and downstream influence, leading to the erroneous reinforcement of invalid actions in successful trajectories and unfair penalization in failed ones.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a** Progress-Aware Advantage Shaping (ProAS) framework, a plug-and-play method that leverages a dynamically evolving Agentic Reference Graph (ARG) for fine-grained credit assignment without critic overhead.
- **Challenge 1: Reinforcement of invalid actions in successful trajectories**. Uniform positive advantages inadvertently reinforce structurally unreasonable actions that pollute context. **Solution**: The "Masking Unreasonable Incentives" (MUI) mechanism uses ARG to identify and mask rewards for actions that do not contribute substantively to task progress, ensuring only reasonable actions are reinforced.
- **Challenge 2: Unfair attribution in failed trajectories**. Early critical deviations cause cascading failures, but uniform penalization treats all subsequent actions equally, ignoring causal dependencies. **Solution**: The "Asymmetric Penalty Redistribution" (APR) mechanism analyzes downstream influence via ARG, concentrating penalties on the key early actions that caused the deviation rather than distributing them evenly.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: ProAS is a plugin framework compatible with mainstream group-based baselines. It constructs dynamic ARGs to track task progress and action dependencies, demonstrating robustness even with imperfect or distilled ARGs.
- **Evaluation Results**: Extensive experiments on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct models showed peak success rates of 91.9% and 94.3% on ALFWorld, and 66.7% and 70.8% on WebShop, respectively.
- **Comparison**: ProAS elevates mainstream baselines to new state-of-the-art performance. It accelerates training convergence by up to 2.44x compared to baselines, while maintaining superior stability and final performance.

#### Summary
ProAS effectively addresses the uniform credit assignment dilemma in critic-free RL by introducing progress-aware advantage shaping. Without the computational burden of a critic network, it significantly enhances LLM agent performance and training efficiency in multi-turn tasks through fine-grained action-level credit correction, offering a new direction for efficient RL alignment.
