## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) multi-agent systems enhance individual capabilities through collaboration across specialized roles, tools, and workflows. However, this collaboration complicates failure diagnosis, as errors can propagate across multiple agents before affecting the final outcome.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current methods primarily trace information dependencies (i.e., where information came from). However, knowing the source does not equate to identifying where things went wrong. For instance, an agent might faithfully propagate incorrect information or misinterpret correct data. Mere dependency tracing cannot distinguish between error introduction, propagation, and repair, making it difficult to pinpoint responsibility and decisive error steps.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: BLADE (Blame Localization via Audited Dependency Evidence), a training-free framework that moves beyond tracing information to auditing its use for failure attribution.
- **Challenge 1: Identifying relevant interactions is difficult**. Critical decisions may depend on information from distant steps or multiple agents, making it hard to determine which dependencies warrant attention. **Solution**: BLADE uses Jev for structured probabilistic judgments and leverages dependency probabilities to select relevant "source-decision" handoffs for auditing, focusing on the most likely points of failure.
- **Challenge 2: Tracing dependencies alone is insufficient to establish responsibility**. Tracing shows flow but not whether the receiving agent used the source faithfully and within task requirements (e.g., misreading correct results). **Solution**: At selected handoffs, BLADE audits whether receiving decisions use sources faithfully, distinguishing between error introduction, propagation, and repair, and forms a global diagnosis considering all execution steps.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: BLADE is a training-free framework that operates without reference answers, integrated within a shared Jev decision interface for structured probabilistic judgments.
- **Evaluation Results**: Evaluated on 1,228 failed runs across five Who&When and Who&When Pro subsets.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: BLADE achieved the best or tied-best step accuracy among eight strategies. It outperformed the "All-at-once" strategy by 2.2 to 11.1 percentage points, demonstrating superior accuracy in locating faulty steps by making responsibility evidence explicit.

#### Summary
BLADE addresses the challenge of failure attribution in multi-agent systems by auditing dependency evidence rather than just tracing dependencies. By probabilistically selecting key handoffs and auditing the faithfulness of information usage, it effectively distinguishes between error introduction and propagation. Experiments show that BLADE significantly outperforms existing baselines without needing reference answers, offering a path toward more interpretable and reliable agentic systems.
