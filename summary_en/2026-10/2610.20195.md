## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Effective social interaction requires agents to reach plans that fit participants' needs and circumstances, not just generate fluent responses. Existing evaluations of social agents primarily rely on LLM judges to rate conversations without verifying the resulting plans.
- **Limitations of Prior Work**: Current methods (e.g., SOTOPIA) use LLM judges to assess goal completion and believability but fail to verify if the agreed-upon plan satisfies private conditions (e.g., budget, availability). While negotiation benchmarks verify outcomes, they do not cover everyday coordination where decisive facts must be elicited through dialogue. The paper shows that 36% of conversations rated highly for goal completion fail actual verification.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed HICO-Bench and PaCPO**: Introduced a benchmark of 180 everyday coordination scenarios and a novel learning algorithm called Paired-Credit Policy Optimization (PaCPO).
- **Challenge 1: Lack of objective verification for plan feasibility**. Difficulty: Everyday coordination depends on private information revealed during conversation, and LLM judges often overestimate social abilities without checking logical consistency. Solution: HICO-Bench uses "Paired Worlds" where two versions of a scenario share public context but differ in one private condition. It defines "Verified Success" by programmatically checking Feasibility (matching records satisfying private conditions) and using an LLM to check Integrity (conversation supports the plan).
- **Challenge 2: Credit assignment with sparse rewards**. Difficulty: Outcomes are only known at the end of the conversation, making it hard to attribute success to specific turns. Solution: PaCPO asks the agent for a tentative plan after each turn and checks it against both scenario versions. It rewards turns where the tentative plans fit their respective versions, enabling effective learning from final outcomes.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Benchmark Findings**: Tests across 13 LLMs revealed that a significant portion of highly-rated conversations failed to achieve verified success, highlighting the gap in current evaluation metrics.
- **Algorithm Performance**: Applying PaCPO to Qwen3-8B increased its Verified Success rate from 20.4% to 28.3%.
- **Comparison**: Unlike traditional methods that rely solely on conversational fluency or judge ratings, this approach significantly improves actual performance in coordination tasks by leveraging verifiable outcome feedback.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper addresses the flaw in current social agent evaluations that prioritize dialogue over results. By introducing the HICO-Bench benchmark and the PaCPO algorithm, it demonstrates that learning from verifiable outcomes rather than just mimicking fluent conversation significantly enhances agent effectiveness in real-world coordination tasks involving private information.
