## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: As LLM-driven agents are deployed in long-horizon tasks, their performance depends heavily on the "Harness"—the infrastructure managing interaction loops, tool execution, context, permissions, state, recovery, and verification. Recursive self-improvement motivates agents to modify this infrastructure, making the ability to engineer Harnesses critical.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current benchmarks (e.g., SWE-bench Pro) measure downstream task success under a fixed harness, leaving harness engineering unexplored. Traditional software engineering benchmarks rely on mature repositories prone to training data leakage and do not capture the generalization required for rapidly evolving harness architectures.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Doc2Harness**: A two-stage benchmark that directly evaluates long-horizon harness engineering, providing the first deterministic benchmark for this capability.
- **Challenge 1: How to isolate and standardize the harness construction process?**
  - **Difficulty**: Harnesses involve complex system interactions and dependencies, making fair comparison difficult and risking interference from the build environment.
  - **Solution**: A two-stage design where Stage 1 has the build model operate within a fixed builder harness, receiving an interface contract and consolidated development documentation to construct a candidate harness in an isolated container.
- **Challenge 2: How to objectively and deterministically evaluate the quality of the constructed harness?**
  - **Difficulty**: End-to-end evaluations can be stochastic and struggle to distinguish between model limitations and harness design flaws, requiring mechanisms to rule out vacuous passes.
  - **Solution**: In Stage 2, the frozen candidate harness runs in separate containers, driven by a scripted mock model. Scoring uses four observable channels (model requests, disk files, exit codes, command output) with positive controls to ensure validity.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Doc2Harness comprises 328 test cases. Nineteen frontier models were evaluated, each with three independent builds. The environment uses container isolation for determinism and reproducibility.
- **Results**:
  - GPT-6 Astra achieved the highest pass rate at 53.05%.
  - The weakest model reached 27.44%.
  - A ~26 percentage point gap exists between the strongest and weakest models.
  - Even the strongest model failed nearly half the cases, with failures clustering around cross-cutting functionality splits across modules.
- **Comparison**: Unlike benchmarks focusing on code generation or fixed-environment tasks, Doc2Harness specifically targets harness engineering, highlighting generalization and infrastructure building capabilities, revealing significant disparities and deficiencies in current models' system-level engineering skills.

## 4. Conclusion
Doc2Harness fills the gap in evaluating LLM agents' harness engineering capabilities. Through isolated two-stage construction and deterministic testing, it effectively quantifies generalization in long-horizon infrastructure building. Results indicate that while frontier models show promise, they struggle with complex cross-module functional splits, identifying harness engineering as a key bottleneck and a crucial direction for future model improvement.
