## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Behavioral consistency across repeated executions is a key signal for assessing the reliability and quantifying the uncertainty of Large Language Model (LLM) agents. Existing methods typically focus on final outcomes or complete action sequences.
- **Limitations of Prior Work**: Current approaches usually summarize consistency with a single task-level score, failing to identify where behavioral divergences occur in intermediate steps. Furthermore, they often fail to ensure actions are compared under equivalent decision contexts; for instance, position-based alignment may mismatch actions from different semantic states, and sequence-based methods are overly sensitive to minor ordering shifts.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: GraphACM, a graph-based framework for fine-grained behavioral consistency measurement and uncertainty quantification.
- **Challenge 1: How to accurately align and compare actions in non-linear execution paths?**
  - **Difficulty**: Agent trajectories vary due to environmental feedback, causing equivalent states to appear at different timesteps. Traditional sequence alignment methods (e.g., edit distance or positional correspondence) fail to capture semantically equivalent decision points.
  - **Solution**: GraphACM merges equivalent states from multiple rollouts into a directed, action-labeled graph. This enables action comparisons at shared decision state nodes, ensuring evaluations occur within identical contextual frameworks.
- **Challenge 2: How to extract global uncertainty signals from fine-grained local consistency to predict task success?**
  - **Difficulty**: Local consistency fluctuations need to be aggregated into a discriminative global metric that effectively distinguishes between successful and failed trajectories while maintaining interpretability.
  - **Solution**: The paper introduces node-level and global action consistency measures. The global consistency score serves as a proxy for uncertainty, demonstrating a positive correlation with task success and effective prediction capabilities.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Extensive experiments were conducted on three benchmarks (ALFWorld, AppWorld, and WebShop) using three different LLMs.
- **Key Results**: GraphACM's global consistency score is positively correlated with task success. It achieves an average AUROC of 0.811 for task-success prediction.
- **Comparison with Baselines**:
  - Outperforms the strongest behavioral-consistency baseline (AUROC 0.729).
  - Outperforms verbalized confidence methods (AUROC 0.769).
- **Advantages**: Beyond providing more accurate uncertainty quantification, GraphACM pinpoints specific state nodes where agent actions agree or diverge, offering richer interpretability than single-score metrics.

## 4. Conclusion
GraphACM addresses the issues of context misalignment and coarse granularity in existing behavioral consistency measurements by constructing state-merged graphs. It provides a fine-grained, state-conditioned consistency measure that not only more accurately quantifies LLM agent uncertainty and predicts task success but also reveals specific points of agreement and divergence in the decision-making process, offering a new tool for enhancing agent reliability and interpretability.
