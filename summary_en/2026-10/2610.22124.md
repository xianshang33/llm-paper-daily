## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Modern agentic systems extend language models with external tools to complete complex tasks through multi-turn interactions. In consequential settings, these agents are constrained by policies regarding pricing, privacy, confirmation, and safety.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current evaluation methods have a critical gap. Capability benchmarks measure task completion, and content-safety red-teaming measures harmful generations, but neither tests whether agents comply with predefined policies during otherwise valid tool use. Violations are hard to audit as they may emerge only after several turns, span multiple tool calls, or appear in downstream systems.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed PROBE (Policy Rule Observation via Behavioral Exploration)**: An algorithm for training LLM-based auditors that interact with deployed agents as realistic users to probe for policy violations through multi-turn, tool-mediated dialogue.
- **Challenge 1: Detecting hidden policy violations in multi-turn interactions**. The difficulty lies in violations being distributed across dialogue turns and tool state changes rather than single-turn outputs. PROBE formalizes auditing as a two-player Markov game and uses a composite reward balancing violation discovery, plausibility, progress, and diversity to guide effective multi-turn exploration.
- **Challenge 2: Training efficiency and generalization of auditors**. The challenge is efficiently training auditors to cover diverse policy scenarios. PROBE compares GRPO, DPO, and GFlowNet-based optimization and employs curriculum learning (increasing turn complexity), enabling auditors significantly smaller than the target agent to uncover broader and higher-yield violations.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Auditors are fine-tuned using QLoRA (r64) while the target agent is frozen. The environment includes 20 policies (Privacy, Safety, Business, Operational) and 8 tools (4 mutating). A verifier suite uses regex, state machines, and schema validation.
- **Results**: Extensive experiments show that PROBE-trained auditors uncover substantially broader and higher-yield policy violations than same-architecture zero-shot adversaries.
- **Comparison**: PROBE outperforms baseline methods even when the auditor is significantly smaller than the target agent. Results demonstrate that multi-turn behavioral auditing is a distinct and necessary axis of evaluation, complementing existing capability and safety benchmarks.

## 4. Conclusion
PROBE addresses the gap in policy compliance auditing for tool-using agents. By introducing a reinforcement learning-based multi-turn interactive auditing framework, it effectively reveals deep-seated policy violations missed by traditional benchmarks. This approach offers a scalable path for automated policy auditing in real-world agentic systems and suggests a co-evolutionary loop where auditors expose failures and agents improve from feedback.
