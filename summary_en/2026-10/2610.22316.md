## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: LLM-based agents and Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) increasingly handle complex tasks via long-horizon tool use and collaboration. However, they are prone to "goal drift," deviating from the overall goal during execution, which wastes resources and leads to undesired outcomes.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current systems rely on "planning" (decomposing goals, but execution may still diverge) and "verification" (post-hoc checks, leading to costly retries and irreversible errors). Neither provides effective, low-cost, real-time correction during execution.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Steering Agent**: An agent that supervises execution on the fly and steers the system back on track when drift is detected.
- **Challenge 1: Balancing Intervention Timing and Cost**
  - **Difficulty**: Determining when to intervene is crucial; frequent intervention is costly, while insufficient intervention fails to correct drift.
  - **Solution**: Introduced a dual-path mechanism: a low-cost "fast path" to decide when to intervene and a high-cost "slow path" to generate specific intervention actions, balancing efficiency and effectiveness.
- **Challenge 2: Endowing the Agent with Steering Intelligence**
  - **Difficulty**: The LLM needs metacognitive abilities to assess execution against the global goal and generate effective corrective actions.
  - **Solution**: Jointly trained both paths using Reinforcement Learning, combining a pairwise ranking objective with Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to unlock steering intelligence.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experiments**: Evaluated on information-seeking search tasks for both single-agent and multi-agent systems.
- **Key Results**:
  - **Performance**: The Steering Agent significantly improved task success rates and efficiency.
  - **Generalization**: The learned steering intelligence generalizes to out-of-domain tasks and transfers to unseen executors.
  - **Controllability**: Supports controllable intervention frequency without retraining.
- **Comparison**: Unlike planning (prone to deviation) and verification (high-cost retries), this method offers real-time online supervision, achieving more stable goal attainment with lower resource consumption.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the goal drift problem in LLM agents by proposing a Reinforcement Learning-based Steering Agent. By introducing a dual-path intervention mechanism and training with GRPO, the agent can monitor and correct execution deviations in real-time. Experiments demonstrate that this approach enhances the performance and efficiency of single and multi-agent systems, offering strong generalization and controllability, thus providing a new paradigm for building robust LLM agent systems.
