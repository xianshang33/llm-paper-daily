## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: While Large Language Model (LLM) agents excel in complex tasks, they typically operate in isolated sessions, lacking the ability to continuously learn from historical interactions or adapt to user-specific environments. As foundational LLM reasoning improves, the bottleneck for agentic evolution shifts from accumulating generic knowledge to effectively managing situational experience.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current self-evolution paradigms often indiscriminately inject raw interaction trajectories into memory or skill modules. This leads to obscured behavioral patterns, compromised update controllability, and a failure to transform stable user preferences into reusable experience for autonomous evolution.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Darwin-Claw Framework**: An environment-adaptive self-evolving framework that systematically distills episodic experiences to drive the comprehensive evolution of operational skills and durable memory.
- **Challenge 1: How to extract valuable signals from heterogeneous and noisy historical interactions?**
  - **Difficulty**: Directly injecting raw trajectories introduces noise, obscuring true behavioral patterns and making evolution uncontrollable.
  - **Solution**: Darwin-Claw introduces a Hierarchical Experience Consolidation System that extracts fine-grained error attributions and generalizes them into dynamically updated prototype vectors, thereby structurally representing situational experience.
- **Challenge 2: How to achieve co-evolution of skills and memory with environmental adaptation?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional methods struggle to balance generic knowledge with specific user environment adaptation and lack explicit optimization signals for skill refinement.
  - **Solution**: Grounded in an iterative "agent-actor" and "evolver-critic" co-adaptation theory, it uses structured situational experience as interaction patches for context-sensitive perception and provides explicit optimization signals for skill refinement, ensuring controllable and stable continuous evolution.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: Darwin-Claw is a readily deployable, automated framework compatible with mainstream agent systems, requiring no manual curation.
- **Evaluation Results**: Extensive experiments on WildClawBench demonstrate substantial performance improvements.
- **Comparison**: Compared to existing methods that directly use contrasted trajectories, Darwin-Claw establishes a controllable and stable pathway for continual, user-centric adaptation, effectively addressing issues of uncontrollable updates and obscured behavioral patterns found in prior works.

## 4. Conclusion
Darwin-Claw addresses the lack of continuous learning and environmental adaptation in LLM agents by proposing a self-evolving framework based on hierarchical experience consolidation. By leveraging fine-grained error attribution and prototype vector generalization, it effectively transforms situational experience into signals for skill optimization and memory updates. Experiments prove its superior performance over existing paradigms, offering a new solution for building lifelong learning agents capable of adapting to specific user environments.
