## 1. What the paper did
- **Background**: Multi-criteria decision-making in healthcare operations (e.g., outpatient scheduling, ED configuration) requires balancing patient waiting time, provider workload, and operating costs. These trade-offs are represented by weights in multi-objective optimization, but quantifying stakeholder preferences (patients, providers, managers) is difficult and context-dependent.
- **Limitations of existing work**: Traditional methods rely on informal or fixed weights that fail to capture dynamic preferences. While recent studies use LLM agents to simulate stakeholders and generate weights, they lack a framework to assess the reliability of these LLM-generated preferences and quantify how their uncertainty impacts final optimization decisions.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a framework**: To assess the reliability of LLM-generated preferences as inputs for multi-objective optimization and to quantify the impact of their uncertainty on decisions.
- **Challenge 1: Stability and consistency of explicit preferences**. The difficulty lies in the fact that deliberation among LLM agents can erase inherent differences between roles, leading to homogenized preferences. The paper finds that simple averaging preserves role-specific differences, whereas agent deliberation eliminates them, suggesting that "discussion" among LLMs may not effectively integrate diverse viewpoints.
- **Challenge 2: Order bias and prediction difficulties in implicit preferences**. The challenge is that LLM pairwise choices are heavily influenced by presentation order (primacy effect). The paper finds that statistical models incorporating contextual information do not significantly outperform models using only presentation order in predicting new choices, revealing high instability in LLM implicit preferences.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: The study examines explicit preferences by asking role-playing LLM agents to rate criteria importance in outpatient scheduling, and implicit preferences by inferring trade-offs from pairwise choices between ED configurations.
- **Key Results**:
    1. Explicit preferences are sensitive to operating conditions and vary across roles, but deliberation among agents erases these differences, while simple averaging preserves them.
    2. Implicit preferences are strongly biased by presentation order, with limited predictive gain from adding contextual information.
    3. Robust weighted-sum optimization models were formulated to account for preference uncertainty. In most cases, these models select different schedules or configurations compared to those obtained using average preference weights.
- **Comparison & Significance**: Compared to traditional approaches that directly use average weights or unaudited LLM outputs, this paper demonstrates that LLM-generated preferences require rigorous auditing before being used as optimization weights in healthcare decision support, as ignoring uncertainty can lead to suboptimal decisions.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper systematically analyzes the possibilities and limitations of LLM-based multi-agent systems in healthcare multi-criteria decision-making. It highlights that while LLMs can generate plausible preferences, explicit preferences are susceptible to homogenization during deliberation, and implicit preferences are prone to order bias. Therefore, directly using LLM outputs as optimization weights is risky. The study advocates for handling preference uncertainty through robust optimization and conducting strict reliability audits before deployment.
