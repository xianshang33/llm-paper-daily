## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: LLM agents are widely used in service chatbots for routine support, but emotionally demanding complaint handling still relies heavily on human agents due to its complexity.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current customer service benchmarks focus on operational capabilities like domain knowledge and tool use, failing to evaluate how agents handle complaints after service failures, particularly under conditions of information asymmetry and emotional stress.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ServiceBench Benchmark and APCS Framework**: Introduced ServiceBench, the first benchmark dedicated to complaint handling with 2,500 scenarios across 184 categories and 5 industries. Proposed Asymmetric Progressive Complaint Simulation (APCS) to mimic realistic information asymmetry.
- **Challenge 1: Difficulty in Proactive Information Gathering under Information Asymmetry**. In real scenarios, agents start with limited information and must probe for details. Existing models often lack this proactive ability, resorting to premature compensation. The paper addresses this by using APCS to control information exposure and a justice-based evaluation framework (interactional, procedural, distributive justice) to penalize shortcuts and reward strategic dialogue guidance.
- **Challenge 2: Strategic Dialogue Guidance and Emotional Recovery**. Handling complaints requires not just solving issues but restoring relationships, demanding high-level emotional intelligence and long-term planning. The paper demonstrates that targeted post-training using Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) significantly enhances these capabilities, allowing smaller models to match or surpass larger general-purpose models.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated multiple LLMs (including DeepSeek-V4-Pro, GPT-5, Qwen3-8B) on ServiceBench. Compared performance under standard asymmetric settings versus full-information settings.
- **Key Findings**:
    1. Even state-of-the-art LLMs struggle with effective complaint handling, often failing to proactively gather information and instead offering premature compensation.
    2. Performance improves substantially when full customer information is provided upfront, highlighting the bottleneck in information gathering.
    3. DeepSeek-V4-Pro and GPT-5 achieved the strongest overall performance among base models.
- **Optimization Results**: Targeted SFT and DPO training on Qwen3-8B enabled it to match or exceed larger models on several metrics, proving the efficacy of specialized training.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Unlike previous benchmarks focusing on task completion, this study uses a justice-theory-based evaluation to reveal trade-offs between resolution quality and goodwill expenditure, providing deeper insights into agent behavior.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper highlights the limitations of current LLMs in complex complaint handling, specifically their passive strategies under information asymmetry. By introducing the ServiceBench benchmark and APCS framework, it systematically evaluates and identifies deficiencies in proactive information gathering and strategic dialogue management. The study proves that combining justice-based evaluation with targeted post-training (SFT/DPO) can significantly improve smaller models' performance in emotionally intensive service recovery tasks, paving the way for more intelligent and empathetic customer service agents.
