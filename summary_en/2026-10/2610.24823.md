## What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents are vulnerable to adversarial inputs that induce malicious behaviors, known as jailbreaking. Existing optimization-based attacks primarily use First-Order (FO) methods leveraging gradients or Zeroth-Order (ZO) methods relying on function-value feedback.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: The complementarity and optimization ordering of FO and ZO methods remain underexplored. Most attacks focus on single greedy outputs, ignoring the diverse reasoning trajectories and actions induced by stochastic decoding from the same adversarial input. There is a lack of systematic study on how to effectively combine and order these two optimization paradigms.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Pair-Aware Optimization Framework**: This framework combines ZO exploration and FO refinement, coordinating them under a stochastic generation perspective for more effective agent jailbreaking.
- **Challenge 1: How to effectively combine discrete space exploration with continuous space refinement?**
  - **Difficulty**: ZO methods excel at global exploration in discrete prompt spaces but lack fine-tuning capabilities. FO methods excel at local refinement but are prone to local optima and depend on gradient information. Direct combination can lead to inefficient optimization trajectories.
  - **Solution**: The paper systematically investigates four strategies (Pure ZO, Pure FO, ZO->FO, FO->ZO) and identifies ZO->FO as optimal. It proposes a ZO-FO framework that explores adversarial prefixes via ZO optimization and refines corresponding suffixes via FO optimization, leveraging their complementarity.
- **Challenge 2: How to coordinate prefix and suffix optimization in a stochastic decoding environment?**
  - **Difficulty**: In stochastic settings, the same input produces diverse behavior distributions. Single-output evaluation is inaccurate, and the interaction between prefixes and suffixes is complex, making it hard to determine the best pairs.
  - **Solution**: Introduces "Pair-aware optimization," which retains multiple prefix candidates, conditionally refines their suffixes, and selects from the resulting prefix-suffix pairs. It also adopts consistent stochastic decoding during both optimization and evaluation to accurately estimate attack quality under distributional objectives.

## Implementation, Deployment, Evaluation Results, and Comparison
- **Implementation & Deployment**: Code is open-sourced. Experiments are conducted on malicious-instruction and malicious-environment benchmarks using consistent stochastic decoding for optimization and evaluation.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - Hybrid optimization significantly outperforms single-optimizer methods (pure ZO or FO) in attack effectiveness.
  - Optimization order substantially influences performance, with ZO->FO showing the best results, validating the "explore then refine" strategy.
  - Under the stochastic generation perspective, the method captures diverse malicious reasoning trajectories and actions missed by greedy decoding, improving jailbreak success rates and robustness.
- **Comparison**: Compared to existing pure gradient-based or search-based methods, and simple hybrid attempts, the proposed Pair-Aware framework achieves higher attack success rates through systematic order study and pair selection mechanisms.

## Summary
This paper addresses LLM agent jailbreaking by proposing the Pair-Aware Optimization framework. By systematically studying the complementarity and ordering of first-order and second-order optimization methods, it establishes the best practice of ZO for prefix exploration and FO for suffix refinement. The introduction of pair-aware mechanisms and consistent stochastic decoding solves optimization challenges in stochastic environments. Experiments demonstrate that this method outperforms existing single-optimizer approaches across various benchmarks, offering new insights into understanding and defending against LLM agent jailbreaks.
