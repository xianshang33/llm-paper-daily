## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: In bounded agentic workflows (e.g., telecom support, inventory management), the decision space of an LLM saturates when decisions are separated from text generation. Agents repeatedly encounter the same states, yet existing LLM agents do not treat these decisions as optimization problems, instead re-deriving them from context at every turn.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Traditional methods invoke the LLM for reasoning at every step, leading to high computational costs, latency, and suboptimal global decisions (e.g., premature handoffs or inefficient check orders). This results in redundant resource usage and performance bottlenecks.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Bellman Language Models (BLM)**: A framework based on the "Discretize, Then Decide" paradigm, which models the workflow as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) and solves Bellman's equation to optimize and reuse decisions.
- **Challenge 1: How to transform unstructured conversations into an optimizable discrete state space?**
  - **Difficulty**: Natural language conversations are highly continuous and diverse, making direct optimization difficult.
  - **Solution**: The paper proposes extracting key facts from workflow logs or LLM runs to map conversations into finite discrete states. It estimates the probability and cost of each action until the state space and probability distributions converge, thereby constructing an accurate MDP.
- **Challenge 2: How to significantly reduce LLM invocation costs while ensuring reliability?**
  - **Difficulty**: Relying solely on rules lacks flexibility, while relying entirely on LLMs is expensive and unstable.
  - **Solution**: BLM acts as a controller around the model. For seen states, it executes pre-computed optimal policies (e.g., fixed tool calls or template replies) without calling the LLM. It only falls back to the LLM for unseen states. This hybrid mechanism leverages the efficiency of optimized solutions while retaining the generalization capability of LLMs.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on $\tau^2$-bench telecom and InventoryBench.
- **Key Results**:
  - On $\tau^2$-bench telecom, the solved controller alone (no model) solves 79% of tasks.
  - **Qwen3.8-27B**: As a BLM, task success rate increased from 29% to 96%, with a 93% reduction in model calls.
  - **Claude Opus 5.5**: Originally achieving 96% success, running as a BLM solved 2 additional tasks and reduced model calls and cost per task by approximately 98%.
  - **InventoryBench**: The solved policy (no model) outperformed all published methods.
- **Comparison/Limitations**: In tasks requiring significant creative content generation (e.g., resolving SymPy code issues), BLM offers little benefit because decisions cannot be easily pre-solved, indicating the method is best suited for scenarios with limited and repetitive decision spaces.

#### Summary
This paper introduces Bellman Language Models (BLM), addressing the saturation of decision spaces in bounded workflows by transforming LLM agent decision-making into an MDP optimization problem. Through the "Discretize, Then Decide" approach, BLM solves for optimal policies once and reuses them, significantly improving agent efficiency, reliability, and cost-effectiveness. Experiments demonstrate that this method drastically reduces LLM invocation costs (up to 98%) while maintaining or improving top-tier task success rates in benchmarks like telecom and inventory management, offering a new paradigm for building efficient and economical LLM agents.
