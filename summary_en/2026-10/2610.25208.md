## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: In product crises (e.g., safety, quality, service issues), a company's public response significantly impacts public judgment of responsibility and trust. As opinions spread and evolve rapidly on social media, crisis communication is not just about generating reasonable text but involves a dynamic process of long-term trust reconstruction.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current LLM benchmarks are largely static and single-turn, focusing only on the quality or logical coherence of isolated responses. They lack the capability to evaluate long-horizon, risk-sensitive communication, failing to simulate dynamic trajectories involving public feedback, competitor actions, and evolving trust, thus unable to reflect real-world performance in complex crisis scenarios.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed BrandEval**: A two-track multi-agent benchmark comprising BrandEval-Static for strategic analysis and BrandEval-Dynamic (BrandPolis) for stress-testing response policies. It also introduces BrandSRD, a Chinese crisis-response dataset, and a reference agent based on Strategic Rationale (SR).
- **Challenge 1: Static evaluation fails to predict long-term trust volatility and recovery**. The difficulty lies in the lagged and non-linear nature of crisis consequences, where single-response scores cannot capture tail risks after public opinion发酵. The paper addresses this by introducing the BrandPolis multi-agent sandbox, which simulates consumer feedback and competitor reactions to track trust-risk trajectories, exposing strategies that appear reasonable statically but lead to trust collapse dynamically.
- **Challenge 2: Traditional Chain-of-Thought (CoT) lacks goal-directed future simulation**. CoT is often linear and myopic, failing to weigh long-term impacts on diverse stakeholders. The paper proposes "Strategic Rationale" (SR), requiring models to set goals, map stakeholders, and simulate futures, thereby formulating proactive interventions (e.g., releasing data, inviting third-party tests) rather than just passive apologies.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The study constructs the BrandSRD dataset and develops the BrandPolis sandbox with consumer agents, competitor agents, and a trust evolution model. The reference agent is instantiated using the Strategic Rationale (SR) framework.
- **Evaluation Results**: Experiments across proprietary and open-source models show that static strategic quality is informative but incomplete; it aligns with some end-state outcomes but does not reliably predict volatility or delayed recovery. Dynamic stress tests further reveal tail-risk failures under manipulative or weakly grounded policies.
- **Comparison**: Compared to traditional CoT methods, SR guides models from "myopic mitigation" to "strategic intervention," demonstrating greater resilience and recovery in simulated long-term reputation trajectories. Existing benchmarks fail to capture these differences due to the lack of dynamic interaction mechanisms.

## 4. Conclusion
BrandEval fills the gap in LLM evaluation for crisis communication by combining static analysis with dynamic sandbox simulation to comprehensively assess strategic thinking and long-term risk control. The study highlights that static text generation evaluation is insufficient for measuring crisis communication effectiveness, necessitating dynamic frameworks that incorporate multi-party game theory and temporal dimensions. The proposed Strategic Rationale method offers a new direction for enhancing LLM decision-making quality in high-stakes scenarios.
