## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Agentic LLMs are increasingly used in scientific prediction (e.g., biology) by grounding decisions in external evidence from tools, aiming for accurate and interpretable predictions.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current evaluation methods often overlook "hidden label shortcuts" (e.g., dataset identifiers, template artifacts) within serialized tool results. These shortcuts create spurious correlations with labels, leading to apparent success without genuine scientific reasoning. Analyzing the concatenated tool trace directly causes signal dilution due to heterogeneity, making it difficult to pinpoint the specific source of cheating.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Two-Stage Audit Framework**: Designed to detect and verify whether Agentic LLMs truly rely on scientific evidence or exploit data shortcuts.
- **Challenge 1: Signal Dilution Hinders Source Localization**. In long traces with multiple tool outputs, shortcut signals in specific tools are diluted by irrelevant content, preventing precise localization by traditional methods. **Solution**: Introduced "Per-Tool TF-IDF Decomposition" (PT2D), which localizes label-predictive patterns to individual tool results, avoiding signal dilution in the concatenated trace and accurately identifying the tool carrying the strongest signal.
- **Challenge 2: Distinguishing Genuine Reasoning from Shortcut Dependence**. Even if suspicious signals are located, it is hard to prove the model relies on them rather than implicit knowledge. **Solution**: Implemented "Token-Class Redaction" for counterfactual testing. By removing the specific token classes (e.g., identifiers) identified by PT2D, the method tests if the frozen LLM's performance drops. A drop to chance level confirms dependence on the shortcut rather than scientific reasoning.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Scenario**: Applied to TCR-pMHC binding prediction, a biomedical task requiring integration of complex experimental and computational evidence.
- **Baseline Comparison**: The tool-augmented model significantly outperformed the no-evidence baseline, superficially suggesting the LLM learned to use scientific evidence.
- **Audit Results**:
    - PT2D analysis revealed that the strongest predictive signal was concentrated in identifiers within one specific tool result, not biological features.
    - Counterfactual testing showed that redacting these identifiers reduced model performance to chance levels.
    - Synthetic noise injection experiments confirmed that adding extra tools dilutes the signal of the key tool, validating PT2D's effectiveness in signal localization.
- **Conclusion vs. Related Work**: Unlike traditional methods focusing solely on final accuracy, this audit exposed the falsity behind benchmark gains, proving that seemingly rigorous performance improvements were actually due to exploitation of data leaks or formatting artifacts.

#### Summary
This paper reveals a critical evaluation failure mode in Agentic LLMs for scientific workflows: hidden label shortcuts can masquerade as scientific reasoning. Using the proposed two-stage audit (PT2D and Token-Class Redaction), the authors demonstrated that high performance in TCR-pMHC prediction was primarily driven by dependence on identifiers in tool outputs, not genuine biological reasoning. These findings emphasize the necessity of source-level audits before trusting benchmark improvements, ensuring models truly utilize external evidence.
