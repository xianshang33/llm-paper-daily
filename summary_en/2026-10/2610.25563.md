## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents interact with Internet of Things (IoT) systems via natural language, issuing tool calls to gather physical observations. However, a requested capability is often provided by multiple devices, and the agent's plan does not specify which physical endpoint to invoke.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Prior approaches focus on retrieving relevant tool descriptions or verifying plan feasibility but fail to learn which eligible device yields the most useful observation. They overlook dynamic factors such as occlusion, sensor faults, battery levels, and latency that affect utility, and lack mechanisms to balance task success with service costs while preserving privacy.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed an Online Tool Grounding Framework**: This framework binds specified tool calls to specific physical endpoints by formulating the selection as a Contextual Bandit problem. It combines adaptive context partitions with hierarchical endpoint trees to optimize selection under service cost and device participation constraints.
- **Challenge 1: Balancing Utility and Feasibility in Dynamic Environments**. The difficulty lies in the fact that device utility is context-dependent (e.g., viewing angle, battery), and feedback is only available for the selected device, creating an exploration-exploitation dilemma. The proposed method addresses this by modeling selection as a contextual bandit, using adaptive context partitions and hierarchical endpoint trees to share execution feedback across related requests and devices, optimizing for success rate, latency, and energy.
- **Challenge 2: Privacy Protection of Sensitive Execution Histories**. Execution logs reveal activity patterns in monitored spaces, and releasing individual rewards or unprotected aggregates compromises privacy. The paper introduces an optional Gaussian mechanism to perturb temporal-tree reward aggregates, providing differential privacy guarantees for released statistics under fixed, reward-independent assignments.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: Evaluated in a simulated loading-dock workload environment. The system architecture separates concerns: the LLM selects the operation and target, while the proposed router selects the specific device.
- **Evaluation Results**: The proposed router achieved higher step success rates and better cost-adjusted service compared to baselines. It also demonstrated lower simulated device energy consumption and latency.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Outperformed nearest-device, advertised-quality, UCB1, and LinUCB selection strategies in terms of success rate, cost-efficiency, energy usage, and latency.

#### Summary
This paper addresses the multi-device selection challenge for LLM agents in IoT environments by proposing an online tool grounding framework. By formulating the problem as a contextual bandit and incorporating adaptive partitioning, hierarchical trees, and differential privacy, it effectively optimizes device selection under dynamic conditions while protecting user privacy. Experiments demonstrate significant improvements over existing baselines in success rate, cost, energy, and latency.
