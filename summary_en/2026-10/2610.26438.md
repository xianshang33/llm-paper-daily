## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents now trade real capital using news as evidence. However, they cannot reliably distinguish fabricated headlines from real news. Since thousands of agents consume the same feeds under near-identical policies, a single fabricated post can trigger synchronized trades across many portfolios.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Prior LLM security research focuses on the "control plane" (e.g., system prompts, role definitions, tool routing), such as prompt injection and jailbreaking. These defenses do not protect the "data plane"—the information evidence that agents are instructed to act upon. Consequently, agents remain vulnerable to attacks targeting the trusted evidence itself.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a systematic benchmark for information injection attacks against LLM trading agents**: The benchmark spans five injection vectors (fact poisoning, sentiment narrative, selective evidence, structured signal, coordinated multi-source) and seven event categories, evaluated over ~10,000 decisions on 15 U.S. stocks.
- **Challenge 1: Inversion of traditional security intuition where fabricated news is more dangerous than prompt injection**. The difficulty lies in the assumption that instruction-targeted attacks are primary threats. The paper demonstrates that while prompt injection has a 14% Attack Success Rate (ASR), fabricated news reaches 62%, with specific 30-word templates achieving 100% ASR across all tested stocks.
- **Challenge 2: Attack success depends heavily on surface form and agent state, evading standard defenses**. The challenge is that effectiveness is driven by credibility cues (e.g., named sources, "BREAKING" tags) rather than just content, and varies by agent position. The paper shows removing credibility cues drops ASR from 100% to 0%. Furthermore, standard defenses like model scaling and peer debate fail, with only hierarchical aggregation reducing ASR.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Setup**: Evaluated on 15 real U.S. stocks with ~10,000 trading decisions. The study tested five injection vectors and seven event categories, with ablations on trading mode, position state, phrasing, cross-ticker transfer, model scale/vendor, and injection realism.
- **Key Results**:
    1. **High ASR**: Fabricated news significantly outperforms prompt injection (62% vs 14% ASR), with specific templates hitting 100%.
    2. **Surface Form Over Content**: For fact-poisoning, removing credibility cues (like "BREAKING" tags) reduces ASR from 100% to 0%, indicating agents rely on superficial signals.
    3. **State Dependence**: Success varies by agent state; e.g., a fake indicator panel scores 91% ASR against an agent about to hold, but only 12% against one about to buy.
    4. **Generalization & Stealth**: Attacks generalize across model vendors and remain effective when blended unlabeled into real news feeds.
    5. **Defense Failure**: Model scaling stops shallow vectors but not fact poisoning; peer debate fails; only hierarchical aggregation effectively reduces ASR.
- **Comparison**: Unlike prior work focusing on control plane vulnerabilities, this study highlights the critical vulnerability of the data plane, showing that existing mainstream defenses are largely ineffective against information injection.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper exposes severe security threats to LLM financial trading agents via "data plane" information injection attacks. Through a systematic benchmark, it proves that fabricated news is more harmful than traditional prompt injection, with success rates heavily dependent on surface credibility cues and agent state. Most existing defenses, such as model scaling and peer debate, are ineffective, highlighting the urgent need for robust evidence verification mechanisms in autonomous financial agents.
