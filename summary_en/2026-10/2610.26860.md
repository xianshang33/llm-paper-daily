## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have advanced in visual reasoning, but many real-world tasks require evidence (grounded perception, external knowledge, numerical measurements) that cannot be reliably inferred internally.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Controlled studies show that simply increasing the inference budget of base models yields diminishing returns. Current methods lack mechanisms to effectively integrate and retain outputs from heterogeneous tools across multi-step reasoning processes.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed OpenVLHarness**: A multimodal reasoning harness structured around a "capability layer" and "persistent multimodal memory" to support iterative evidence acquisition and composition.
- **Challenge 1: Incompatibility of Heterogeneous Tool Outputs**. Tools produce diverse, unstructured outputs difficult for LLMs to interpret. The capability layer combines task-level interfaces with evidence rendering to convert heterogeneous backend outputs into consistent, model-readable observations.
- **Challenge 2: Context Loss in Multi-step Reasoning**. Intermediate evidence (images, data, references) is often lost during long reasoning chains. Persistent multimodal memory retains these as addressable artifacts, while a coding interface executes programs over stored data to combine results from multiple tools.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Evaluation Setup**: Tested on 22 challenging multimodal benchmarks using 8 open-source and proprietary backbones (e.g., Qwen3-VL, Kimi K3, GPT-6 series).
- **Key Results**:
    - OpenVLHarness improves average scores by 9.7–12.6 points over no-tool baselines. Specific gains: +11.2 for Qwen3-VL-8B, +9.7 for Kimi K3, +9.9 for GPT-6 Astra.
    - **Cost Efficiency**: With GPT-6 Luna at medium reasoning effort, OpenVLHarness outperforms the base model, Codex, and Visual Sketchpad at maximum effort by 7.7–8.5 points, while incurring at most 37% of their model-token cost.
- **Comparison**: Analysis shows that while base models plateau with increased budget, OpenVLHarness significantly boosts performance, particularly in spatial grounding, counting, and complex VQA tasks.

## 4. Conclusion
OpenVLHarness addresses the limitations of MLLMs in tasks requiring external evidence by integrating a capability layer and persistent memory. It effectively incorporates heterogeneous tools into the reasoning loop, significantly improving performance across 22 benchmarks while drastically reducing computational costs compared to high-effort baseline strategies, demonstrating the efficacy of tool-augmented reasoning with memory retention.
