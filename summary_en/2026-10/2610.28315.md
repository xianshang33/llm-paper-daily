## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: LLM agents increasingly use persistent memory for personalization, posing privacy risks as stored personal attributes may be exposed.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Prior studies mostly focus on recovering information from static records or use adversarial agents to elicit attributes. They overlook how an agent's routine consultation of memory dynamically influences user responses in non-adversarial settings, leading to self-reinforcing privacy leakage.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a "Disclosure Begets Disclosure" Self-Reinforcing Privacy Loop**: Identified a mechanism where memory-conditioned agent responses (often containing presuppositions) lead users to affirm or leave uncorrected guesses, solidifying cues and inferred values into the record, thereby increasing leakage over sessions.
- **Challenge 1: Quantifying and explaining the dynamic accumulation of privacy leakage**. The difficulty lies in distinguishing single-turn leakage from feedback loop effects. Simulations showed that removing agent mentions of attributes reduces subsequent leakage by about twice the amount predicted by single-turn effects, confirming the loop.
- **Challenge 2: Breaking the privacy loop without sacrificing personalization quality**. The challenge is that users cannot easily escape the loop (correcting reveals attributes, avoiding leads to entrenched errors). The paper demonstrates that instructing the agent not to state or presuppose attributes reduces leakage to near no-memory levels while retaining most quality gains, outperforming input filtering or anonymization.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation**: Multi-session simulations were conducted using simulated users with 24 personal attributes interacting with four different support agents. Metrics included response quality and empirical leakage (share of correctly inferred attributes from the full record).
- **Results & Comparison**:
    - Across four models, memory improved response quality but significantly increased empirical leakage, with the gap widening over sessions.
    - Compared to filtering or anonymizing user inputs/storage, constraining agent responses (no stating/presupposing attributes) was more effective at reducing leakage while maintaining high quality.
    - Unlike existing work focusing on static record analysis or adversarial attacks, this study addresses endogenous privacy risks arising from normal interactions.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper reveals a self-reinforcing privacy leakage loop in LLM agents with persistent memory, where agent presuppositions prompt user confirmation, solidifying sensitive information. It concludes that user-side protection is insufficient; the most effective mitigation is suppressing attribute statements and presuppositions at the agent level, significantly reducing privacy risk while preserving personalization benefits.
