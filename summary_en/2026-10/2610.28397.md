## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Agentic AI extends Large Language Models (LLMs) from text generation to planning and executing complex workflows, introducing risks such as unsafe actions, information leakage, and adversarial manipulation. Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) further expand capabilities through specialization and collaboration, but distributed authority, heterogeneous roles, and opaque or potentially compromised participants make safeguarding significantly more challenging.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current defenses address individual threats in isolation (e.g., prompt injection, tool safety), resulting in fragmented coverage across local execution, system-level interactions, and enforcement mechanisms. They lack a unified approach for hybrid systems containing both instrumentable and opaque agents, failing to effectively handle cross-agent attack propagation and system-level trust boundaries.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed MAS-Guard**: A unified framework designed to safeguard single- and multi-agent AI applications, accommodating both instrumentable and opaque agents.
- **Challenge 1: The disconnect between local and global perspectives creates defense blind spots**. The difficulty lies in the fact that individual agents cannot observe the full context, while unsafe behaviors often emerge at shared boundaries or during cross-agent interactions. MAS-Guard addresses this by combining a local Agent Guardrail (A-Guard) layer, which uses task-specific experts within instrumentable agents, with a system-level control plane that monitors overall routing and interactions.
- **Challenge 2: Difficulty in unified enforcement across heterogeneous agent environments**. The challenge stems from mixing fully controllable "instrumentable agents" with "opaque agents" that only expose interfaces, making consistent policy application hard. MAS-Guard introduces developer-owned "Agent Charters" and a Control Plane that combine deterministic structural checks (routes, capabilities, formats) with semantic assessments, enforcing security through a shared set of interventions: allow, reflection, confirmation, and block.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The MAS-Guard architecture operates on two levels. The lower level is the local A-Guard integrated into instrumentable agents. The upper level is the system-wide Control Plane, which manages Agent Charters and performs structural and semantic checks. It supports four intervention actions: allow, reflection, confirmation, and block.
- **Evaluation Results**: Evaluated across six benchmarks covering diverse single- and multi-agent scenarios using three agent LLMs from different providers. MAS-Guard reduced the overall attack success rate by 71.5% to 86.3% compared to unguarded agents.
- **Comparison and Utility**: While significantly enhancing security, MAS-Guard met utility reference criteria on benign tasks, demonstrating its ability to maintain system usability while defending against various attacks, including indirect prompt injection, tool misuse, and information leakage. In contrast, existing works typically address only specific threat categories and lack coordinated system-level defense.

## 4. Conclusion
MAS-Guard fills a critical gap in LLM agent safety by proposing a unified protection scheme from individual agents to multi-agent systems. By combining local fine-grained checks with system-level macro control, it effectively resolves the fragmentation of defenses in hybrid agent environments, significantly reducing attack success rates and providing a viable infrastructure for building secure agentic AI applications.
