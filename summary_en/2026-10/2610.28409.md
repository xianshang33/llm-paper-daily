## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large language model (LLM) agents are often assigned an inference budget (e.g., tokens, API calls, or environment steps) and must maximize task success over multiple turns. Current multi-turn agents typically rely on external controllers (such as planners or routers) to enforce budgets at inference time, rather than learning resource allocation internally.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Prior budget-aware training mostly focuses on controlling the cost of single-generation outputs, failing to address dynamic resource allocation across multi-turn interactions. Reliance on external mechanisms adds inference complexity, and it remains unclear if policies can learn to adapt resource usage based on dynamically specified budgets and task difficulty.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a** budget-aware reward framework for multi-turn agents, systematically comparing 11 different reward objectives to enable agents to learn cross-turn resource allocation and adapt to arbitrary test-time constraints without external controllers.
- **Challenge 1: Dynamic resource allocation in multi-turn interactions**. The difficulty lies in deciding "when" and "how much" to spend at each turn while considering remaining budget and task state, a complex sequential decision problem. The paper addresses this by introducing rewards that penalize overspending (e.g., Sigmoid penalty or fixed negative reward), training Qwen3.5 models in a shared pipeline, enabling the policy to internalize budget constraints and adapt behavior accordingly.
- **Challenge 2: Generalization across resource types and budget values**. The challenge is adapting to various resource types (tokens, API calls, steps) and unseen budget limits. By training policies on AppWorld and BabyAI environments across diverse budget values, the study demonstrates that training with overspending penalties not only improves in-budget success but also generalizes zero-shot to unseen budgets, shifted task distributions, and new benchmarks.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Experiments were conducted on AppWorld (budgets on tokens, API calls, environment steps) and BabyAI (turn budgets). Qwen3.5 4B and 9B models were used, training separate policies for each environment and resource type across multiple budget values. Eleven reward objectives, including adapted length penalties and new formulations, were compared.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - Training with rewards that penalize overspending significantly improved in-budget success and natural compliance compared to untrained models and training on task success alone.
    - **Sigmoid overspend penalty** most often achieved the highest in-budget success at individual budgets.
    - **Fixed negative reward** for overspending generally yielded the strongest natural compliance.
    - **Combining penalties for overspend frequency and magnitude** resulted in the smallest average gap to the best method at each budget.
- **Comparison**: The approach outperforms traditional methods relying on external budget controllers, demonstrating that a single agent can effectively learn resource allocation via budget feedback, showing robustness in zero-shot settings against distribution shifts.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper demonstrates that multi-turn LLM agents can internalize budget constraints through carefully designed reward functions, particularly those penalizing overspending. This enables autonomous optimization of resource allocation under dynamically specified budgets, eliminating the need for complex external control mechanisms. The method enhances adaptability and compliance across different resource types, unseen budgets, and new task distributions, offering a new training paradigm for building more efficient and controllable autonomous agents.
