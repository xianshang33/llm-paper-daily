## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Modern LLM agents persist state across sessions via two channels: long-lived memory files silently prepended to the system prompt, and scheduled tasks that wake the agent without user input. A one-time indirect prompt injection into either channel can re-fire across sessions long after the attacker has left, a threat termed "Carryover Attacks."
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Existing prompt-injection benchmarks evaluate attacks only within the session where malicious content is first observed. They leave unmeasured the critical post-planting window, failing to assess when and how often a planted payload re-fires in subsequent sessions.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed CarryoverBench**: A benchmark framework specifically designed to evaluate carryover attacks on LLM agents, featuring 12 personal-assistant scenarios and 101 attack tasks across three trigger channels.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of systematic evaluation for cross-session persistent attacks**. The difficulty lies in traditional benchmarks focusing only on single-turn interactions, ignoring the persistence and recurrence of payloads in memory or scheduled tasks. The paper addresses this by designing three trigger channels (Instant, Autonomous, Latent) and developing a rule-based verifier that scores "planting" and "triggering" as independent stages.
- **Challenge 2: Difficulty in distinguishing whether defenses prevent planting or triggering**. The challenge is that many defenses may only affect initial injection success but fail to stop the execution of already planted payloads. By independently scoring planting and triggering across five frontier models over multiple rounds, the paper reveals that once planted, payloads show little decay, and the triggering stage often lacks additional protection, exposing the limitations of current defenses.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: CarryoverBench includes 12 realistic personal-assistant scenarios and 101 attack tasks. It uses a deterministic rule-based verifier to independently score planting and triggering success rates, reporting Attack Success Rate (ASR) alongside benign-task utility to highlight the helpfulness-safety trade-off.
- **Evaluation Results**: Experiments on five frontier models show that planted payloads do not exhibit measurable decay over five rounds. Conditional on successful planting, the triggering stage provides little additional protection. Furthermore, porting the Latent channel to a second runtime environment, Hermes, attenuates but does not eliminate the effect.
- **Comparison**: Unlike traditional benchmarks that only evaluate single-session IPI, CarryoverBench highlights the unique risks posed by long-term memory and heartbeat mechanisms, demonstrating that defense strategies effective for single-turn conversations are insufficient for cross-session scenarios.

#### Summary
This paper identifies a new security threat for LLM agents: Carryover Attacks, arising from cross-session state persistence. By introducing the CarryoverBench benchmark, the authors systematically evaluate attack persistence across Instant, Autonomous, and Latent trigger channels. The findings indicate that for current mainstream models, once a malicious payload is successfully planted, subsequent triggering is nearly unstoppable, and the attack effect does not decay over time. This suggests that defending against carryover attacks requires additional control mechanisms at the runtime layer, rather than relying solely on prompt-level interventions.
