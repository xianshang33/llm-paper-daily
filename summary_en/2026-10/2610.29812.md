## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Literature-searching language agents are becoming standard in scientific assistants for life sciences, yet there is little controlled evidence on which parts of their harness design actually matter.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Previous studies often focus on model capabilities or retrieval over fixed corpora, lacking systematic ablation of different agent architectures (e.g., closed-book, single-shot, multi-step) in open-world settings (like live PubMed APIs). They also often overlook deep audits of citation faithfulness, failing to distinguish between retrieval failures and reasoning errors.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a** reproducible harness-ablation protocol for literature agents, comparing four architectures (Closed-book, Single-shot Retrieval, ReAct-style Agent, Oracle) on the SciFact benchmark using the live PubMed API.
- **Challenge 1: Poor retrieval quality leading to verification failure**. Naive single-shot retrieval recalls only 6% of gold papers and turns overconfident errors into abstentions without improving accuracy. The paper addresses this by allowing the model to formulate and refine its own queries (ReAct style), which triples gold recall and yields the best verdicts.
- **Challenge 2: Hallucinated relevance and limitations of existence checks**. Closed-book models cite real but irrelevant PMIDs, a failure mode that simple "existence checks" cannot catch. Through a citation audit using a cross-family LLM judge, the paper shows that only 3/327 citations from closed-book models are relevant, versus 67% for search agents, proving existence checks are insufficient safeguards.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The study uses the SciFact dataset with the live PubMed E-utilities API as the sole tool. It fixes the backbone model and output format while varying the harness across two efficient frontier backbones. Code, prompts, and cached tool calls are released.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - **Retrieval Bottleneck**: With gold abstracts, models achieve 79-86% accuracy, a significant 17-24 point gap over the best realistic harness (62%), indicating retrieval, not reasoning, is the main bottleneck.
    - **Architecture Comparison**: The ReAct-style agent achieved the best realistic accuracy (62%), outperforming closed-book and single-shot retrieval (49-59%), although differences were not statistically significant at the current sample size. Agents used less than half their search budget.
    - **Citation Audit**: Closed-book models cited existing PMIDs (327/335) that were almost never relevant; search agent citations were 67% relevant.
- **Comparison**: Unlike systems like MultiVerS operating on fixed corpora, this work validates dynamic retrieval agents in an open world. Compared to benchmarks like MedRAG, it focuses more on component ablation and citation faithfulness.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper demonstrates through controlled experiments that enhancing an agent's ability to autonomously construct queries is more effective than relying on parametric knowledge for biomedical claim verification. It identifies retrieval as the primary bottleneck and warns that checking for PMID existence alone is insufficient to prevent the hallucination of irrelevant citations, providing empirical guidance and safety warnings for future scientific agent design.
