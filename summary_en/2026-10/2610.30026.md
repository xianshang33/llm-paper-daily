## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Enterprise agents rely on an ingestion layer to convert Word or PDF documents into tokens before executing tasks like policy compliance or ticketing. While mature converters faithfully recover text and structure, creating an appearance of a healthy pipeline, they often discard semantic layers encoded in visual formatting.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current mainstream converters and parsing benchmarks ignore the meaning authors encode through visual cues (e.g., color, highlights). This loss is "silent," causing agents to be confidently wrong rather than visibly incomplete, a failure mode that is difficult to detect and monitor in production deployments.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A systematic analysis and evaluation framework for visual cue loss in enterprise document ingestion, featuring a task built from 76 real policy documents to quantify the recovery of author-emphasized requirements using a deterministic matcher without LLM judges.
- **Challenge 1: Silent loss of visual semantics leads to agent hallucinations**. The difficulty lies in traditional parsers discarding format while keeping text, triggering no error alerts, and causing a fourfold increase in false positives on unflagged requirements. The paper addresses this by localizing the loss to highlights and colors via span-level analysis and demonstrating that preserving these cues significantly reduces error rates.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of standardized evaluation for visual cues**. It is difficult to distinguish which formats carry semantic weight as existing benchmarks do not score them. The paper uses leave-one-out analysis to statistically prove that only highlight (+0.302 F1) and color (+0.262 F1) are critical, while others (bold, italic, etc.) have negligible impact, thereby pinpointing the exact fix needed.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Data**: The study uses 76 real enterprise policy documents, validated against rendered page images (Kappa=0.72). An analysis of 6,460 documents revealed that 32.8% contain deliberate color or highlights.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - **Performance Collapse**: Formatting-blind ingestion causes micro F1 to drop from 0.690 to 0.150 (Docling) and 0.075 (plain text).
    - **Model Generalization**: This gap replicates across six answering models from two vendors, including Claude (Opus, Sonnet, Haiku) and OpenAI's GPT-5.6.
    - **Error Rate Comparison**: Docling's false-positive rate on requirements not flagged by the author is four times higher than the proposed method.
    - **Additional Gain**: A pre-registered test shows that explicitly typing out the cue for the agent adds +0.085 performance beyond merely preserving it.
- **Comparison**: Compared to mainstream converters (like Docling) and plain text parsing, the proposed approach significantly improves requirement extraction accuracy and reliability by preserving key visual cues, specifically color and highlights.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper reveals a critical, overlooked issue in enterprise document ingestion: the silent loss of semantic information encoded in visual formats (especially color and highlights), which leads to high-risk agent errors. The study demonstrates that the root cause lies in the ingestion layer, not the reasoning model, and that the fix is cheap and measurable. For agent builders, preserving and explicitly transmitting these visual cues is a key step toward enhancing system reliability.
