## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Harness optimization, which improves an agent's prompts, tools, and workflows, is an effective way to enhance agents built on frozen or closed-source LLMs. Leading approaches like the Darwin-Godel Machine (DGM) and Huxley-Godel Machine (HGM) rely on recursive self-improvement and were primarily developed for coding tasks.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: In coding, the competence needed to improve the agent aligns with the competence being improved. However, in long-horizon planning tasks, agents must produce sequences of tool calls satisfying interacting constraints, breaking this alignment. Existing open-ended self-modification methods repeatedly explore ineffective changes, consuming substantial evaluation budgets due to a lack of structured history regarding past modifications and their outcomes.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed HGM-Dual**: A diagnostic-guided framework for budget-efficient harness evolution specifically designed for planning agents.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of structured search history context leading to redundant ineffective exploration**. In planning, different branches often encounter similar constraint failures. Without knowing if a fix was previously attempted and its outcome, editors repeat ineffective edits (e.g., 41.8% repetition rate).
    - **Solution**: HGM-Dual represents agent evolution as "lineage-aware context." It explicitly records what each ancestor changed, which behaviors improved or regressed, and which modifications were ineffective, providing clear edit-outcome attribution instead of an unstructured archive.
- **Challenge 2: Untargeted improvements failing to address specific constraint violations**. Planning success depends on interdependent decisions; broad modifications often fail to pinpoint the specific harness components responsible for high-priority constraint violations.
    - **Solution**: HGM-Dual introduces "error-conditioned dual-path child generation." For each parent, it generates a "general child" for open-ended exploration and "specialized children" that target the harness components most likely responsible for the parent's highest-ranked constraint violations.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Results**: HGM-Dual outperforms the strongest baseline by up to 14.45% relative improvement in case accuracy.
- **Efficiency**: It matches the final performance of HGM variants while using 2x-4x fewer evaluation budgets.
- **Comparison**: Compared to DGM and HGM, HGM-Dual significantly reduces the rate of redundant invalid edits and improves sample efficiency through targeted optimization.

#### Summary
HGM-Dual effectively addresses the low search efficiency and lack of targeting in existing recursive self-improvement methods for long-horizon planning. By introducing lineage-aware context and error-guided dual-path generation, it not only improves planning accuracy under complex constraints but also drastically reduces the computational and evaluation costs of optimization, offering a new paradigm for efficient harness optimization of closed-source LLM agents.
