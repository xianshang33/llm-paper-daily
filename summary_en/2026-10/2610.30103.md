## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Many NP-complete (NPC) problems have polynomial-time (P) "twins" that differ by only a single structural detail (e.g., 2-coloring vs. 3-coloring graphs). A model capable of true reasoning should identify this complexity boundary rather than relying on memorization.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current benchmarks (e.g., GraphArena, NPPC, NPHardEval) either fix task phrasing or ignore complexity classes, failing to distinguish between "familiarity with surface forms" and "true complexity reasoning." They often use textbook-style formulations, allowing models to cheat by recalling solutions from training data rather than analyzing structural complexity.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed P2NPCBench Benchmark and AgentGAN Framework**: A benchmark comprising 12 problem families where each NPC problem is paired with a P twin, constructed using adversarial rewriting to remove textbook cues.
- **Challenge 1: How to remove "textbook cues" that allow models to rely on memory rather than reasoning?**
  - **Difficulty**: Models easily recognize specific keywords or standard sentence structures to directly invoke solutions from training data, bypassing structural analysis.
  - **Solution**: Used AgentGAN, an adversarial multi-agent framework. A generator rewrites problem statements until a panel of frozen code models fails, while an executable validity gate ensures each rewrite preserves both the answer correctness and the complexity class, creating "hardened" statements.
- **Challenge 2: How to accurately assess whether models are limited by computational resources or reasoning/coding abilities?**
  - **Difficulty**: Low accuracy on NPC problems in traditional evaluations might be misattributed to timeouts due to large search spaces rather than logical errors.
  - **Solution**: Compared performance on P and NPC twins and conducted stress tests with a 10x time limit. Results showed minimal accuracy improvement for NPC (28.5% to 31.9%), proving failures stem mainly from incorrect program generation or runtime crashes, not insufficient computation time.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Dataset**: Constructed a dataset of 2,114 hardened statements, all with human-verified reference solutions.
- **Evaluation Subjects**: Evaluated 12 proprietary and open-source models.
- **Key Results**:
  - All models scored lower on NPC statements than their P twins (pass@1: 35.4% vs. 63.8%).
  - 95.1% of model-family pairs failed more often on the NPC side.
  - Hardening narrowed the performance gap between classes (from 24.8 to 14.6 points), indicating part of the original gap was due to familiarity with textbook phrasing.
  - Failure taxonomy showed runtime errors as the largest NPC failure type (44.1%), with nearly half (46.5%) due to malformed or truncated code.
- **Comparison**: Unlike existing benchmarks, P2NPCBench reveals that models struggle significantly to distinguish P from NPC problems when surface cues are removed, demonstrating that previous performance gaps were partially due to data leakage or memorization effects.

#### Summary
This paper highlights flaws in current LLM algorithmic reasoning evaluations, specifically the over-reliance on memorization of standard formulations. By introducing the AgentGAN adversarial rewriting framework and the P2NPCBench benchmark, the authors demonstrate that even without surface cues, models perform significantly worse on NPC problems than P problems. This gap is primarily driven by code generation correctness rather than computational resource limits, emphasizing the need for LLMs that possess true structural reasoning capabilities rather than just pattern matching.
