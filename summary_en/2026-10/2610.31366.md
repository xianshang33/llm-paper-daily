## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Many LLM agents maintain external persistent memory and derive additional state from past interactions, such as summaries. Deleting an original memory record does not necessarily remove information carried by its descendants.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current deletion operations typically focus only on storage-level record removal ("exact source-record deletion"). This approach fails to account for behavioral effects or derived states, making it difficult to assess whether information can still be behaviorally recovered from fixed-weight LLM agents after source deletion.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed AfterDeleteBench**: A controlled behavioral audit benchmark for assessing post-deletion information persistence in fixed-weight LLM agents.
- **Challenge 1: How to accurately attribute recovered information to the deleted record rather than model guessing or background knowledge?**
  - **Difficulty**: Models might hallucinate correct secrets or use common sense, leading to false positives in deletion failure assessment.
  - **Solution**: Introduces a matched control group design. For each synthetic secret, it compares an "exposed-then-deleted" agent with a "matched control" agent never exposed to the secret. Recovery is attributed to original exposure only if the exposed group succeeds while the control group fails.
- **Challenge 2: How to evaluate information recovery without a complete verbatim copy?**
  - **Difficulty**: Even if the full secret string is deleted, its components may remain stored separately and reconstructible via public composition rules, evading traditional exact-match detection.
  - **Solution**: Designs non-verbatim recovery scenarios where only separate components exist. Experiments show high exact reconstruction rates (62-99%) across Qwen, Mistral, and Phi-3 models, demonstrating the risk of information leakage from derived states.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation**: AfterDeleteBench was tested on models like Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Mistral, and Phi-3. In the primary setting, a single query recovered ~66% of deleted secrets from Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, with zero recovery in control groups.
- **Key Findings**:
  1. **Non-Verbatim Recovery**: High probability of exact reconstruction even without the full secret string, using only dispersed components and public composition rules.
  2. **Measurement Sensitivity**: The response interface is part of the measurement system. Mismatches between requested format and parser can lead to false negatives. Thus, exact reconstruction is treated as the primary privacy event, separate from write-back and fresh-session recall.
  3. **Mitigation Comparison**: "Provenance-aware closure" eliminated observed recovery, whereas "exact-literal write filters" only blocked reinsertion but could not erase existing information.
- **Comparison**: Unlike traditional methods focusing solely on storage operations, this work emphasizes evaluating source deletion at the behavioral level of the agent and its memory system.

#### Summary
This paper reveals the risk that information persists in LLM agents through derived states (e.g., summaries or dispersed components) even after original memory records are deleted. By introducing AfterDeleteBench, the authors demonstrate that simple source record deletion is insufficient for true forgetting, especially with non-verbatim derived states. The study highlights the importance of behavioral auditing and suggests that effective privacy protection requires comprehensive provenance-aware management strategies beyond simple storage deletion.
