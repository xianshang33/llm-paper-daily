## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM)-powered computer-use agents are increasingly handling private information and executing tasks on behalf of users in applications like email. Phishing poses significant privacy and security risks, depending not just on message detection but on how agents respond to malicious requests.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Existing phishing datasets are designed primarily for conventional detection tasks and fail to adequately characterize agent susceptibility to persuasive malicious requests. Traditional evaluations focus solely on detection rates, ignoring whether agents execute malicious actions when detection fails. Furthermore, current attack methods often rely on predefined strategies rather than adaptive optimization against specific agent behaviors.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed AgentPhishing Benchmark and PhishOpt Algorithm**: A framework to evaluate the safety of LLM email agents under adaptive attacks, focusing on their compliance with malicious requests.
- **Challenge 1: Static datasets do not reflect agent vulnerability to adaptive social engineering attacks**. The difficulty lies in generating attacks that are both realistic and effective against specific agent defenses. The paper addresses this by developing PhishOpt, which optimizes reusable email-generation skills against individual agents under fixed benchmark and realism constraints.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of systematic analysis and generalization of persuasion strategies leading to agent failure**. The challenge is identifying which psychological manipulation principles cause failures. The paper analyzes generated emails using Cialdini's principles of persuasion to identify strategies associated with agent failures and constructs a universal generation skill to enhance attack effectiveness.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation**: The study evaluated nine mainstream LLM email agents using the AgentPhishing benchmark. PhishOpt iteratively optimizes phishing emails tailored to specific agents.
- **Results**: While agents handled traditional phishing emails from existing datasets safely, they remained highly susceptible to attacks adapted to their behavior. Four out of the nine evaluated agents exhibited phishing detection rates below 10% and malicious request compliance rates above 45%.
- **Comparison**: Unlike traditional phishing evaluations that focus only on classification, AgentPhishing reveals the limitations of inferring agent safety from detection performance alone. Compared to works like AgentBait that use predefined strategies, this adaptive optimization method more effectively exposes deep vulnerabilities in agents under social engineering attacks.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper highlights severe security vulnerabilities in current LLM email agents against adaptive social engineering attacks. By introducing the AgentPhishing benchmark and PhishOpt, the study demonstrates that traditional detection metrics are insufficient for measuring true agent safety. The results show that a significant proportion of advanced agents can be deceived into executing malicious operations by carefully crafted persuasive attacks, emphasizing the need for comprehensive security testing standards that include behavioral response evaluation.
