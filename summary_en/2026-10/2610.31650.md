## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Tool-using LLM agents rely on environmental facts to determine if an action is permitted. When a tool response omits a safety-relevant fact, an agent may execute a prohibited action even if the user request is benign and all returned statements are true.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current benchmarks (e.g., AgentDojo) evaluate tool-use capability, action safety, and robustness to adversarial observations. However, they fail to address "missing evidence," where no attacker instruction is present and all observations are true. They do not adequately assess how agents reason from available facts to consequential actions or whether they can actively recover missing critical facts to distinguish permission from prohibition.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed EpiSEM-Bench**: An executable benchmark for evaluating unsafe execution, safety boundaries, and evidence recovery in LLM agents under missing evidence.
- **Challenge 1: Decoupling Safety Conditions from Visible Evidence**. The difficulty lies in traditional evaluations conflating task completion with safety, allowing agents to pass by blindly refusing or executing. EpiSEM-Bench addresses this by constructing 300 matched unsafe/safe task pairs (600 worlds), systematically suppressing critical facts to separate the world's safety state from the agent's visible evidence, thereby precisely measuring unsafe execution under evidence omission.
- **Challenge 2: Evaluating Active Evidence Recovery**. The challenge is that resistance to missing evidence does not equate to useful recovery; agents must actively seek missing facts and adjust behavior. The benchmark introduces an independent audit tool to test if agents withhold prohibited actions while completing permissible ones, determining the minimum observed failure cardinality to quantify performance differences in evidence acquisition strategies.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: EpiSEM-Bench contains 300 matched task pairs across six domains, with 2-7 critical facts per task. The suppression inventory includes 1,363 facts (944 critical, 419 irrelevant). It simulates evidence missingness by systematically suppressing critical facts and uses an executable Oracle to judge action safety against the complete state.
- **Evaluation Results**: Testing on ten mainstream models revealed substantial variation in omission robustness, evidence acquisition, and appropriate action. Resistance to missing evidence does not necessarily translate into useful recovery. While enabling recovery access reduced unsafe execution for all ten models, they differed significantly in their ability to actively obtain missing facts and act appropriately in both safe and unsafe contexts.
- **Comparison**: Unlike existing benchmarks, EpiSEM-Bench not only focuses on final action safety but also analyzes the cognitive process (i.e., attempts to recover evidence) under incomplete information, filling the gap in evaluating "non-adversarial evidence missingness."

## 4. Conclusion
This paper presents EpiSEM-Bench, the first benchmark focused on evaluating the epistemic safety of LLM agents under missing evidence. By decoupling world states from visible evidence, it reveals the vulnerabilities of current models in handling incomplete information and their insufficient evidence recovery capabilities, providing crucial evaluation tools and insights for enhancing agent safety in real-world complex environments.
