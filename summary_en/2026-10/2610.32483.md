## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: LLM Agents consist of a foundation model and an execution harness (the layer connecting the model to the environment). Benchmark performance reflects the joint behavior of this "Model-Harness" system, not just model capability.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current evaluations are task-centric, focusing only on final success rates or aggregate rewards while treating the harness as a fixed setup. This masks diagnostic information, making it impossible to distinguish whether failures stem from model limitations or harness design flaws, and lacks fine-grained analysis of model-harness interactions.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a** comprehensive evaluation framework for LLM Agent "Model-Harness" interactions, featuring five complementary metrics: Valid Completion Rate (VCR), Model-Harness Fit, Cross-Model Fit, Interaction Residuals, and Token Load.
- **Challenge 1: How to decouple and quantify the synergy efficiency between models and harnesses?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional metrics cannot distinguish whether a failure is due to the model generating incorrect instructions or the harness failing to parse/execute them, leading to attribution errors.
  - **Solution**: Introduced "Valid Completion Rate (VCR)" and "Model-Harness Fit" to measure how reliably an agent finishes and delivers evaluable results, independent of task correctness, thereby quantifying how well a specific harness supports a specific model.
- **Challenge 2: How to evaluate harness generality and hidden execution characteristics?**
  - **Difficulty**: Performance on a single model does not represent overall harness quality, and component-level summaries often obscure specific pairing strengths/weaknesses and resource consumption differences.
  - **Solution**: Proposed "Cross-Model Fit" to assess harness suitability across different models; used "Interaction Residuals" to reveal pairing-specific execution profiles and "Token Load" to analyze resource consumption (including failed attempts), finding that higher token usage does not necessarily yield better outcomes.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Setup**: Evaluated 4 general-purpose harnesses (OpenCode, Codex CLI, Pi CLI, DeepAgents) with 4 core models, plus 2 fixed native systems. Tested across 6 benchmarks covering context management, tool use, long-context planning, multimodal interaction, and artifact handoff. The study included 111 evaluation settings and generated 52,168 trajectories.
- **Key Results**:
  - **Ranking Variability**: Harness rankings vary significantly across models and benchmarks. Task performance and VCR may favor different pairings.
  - **Top Performers**: OpenCode ranked first in Cross-Model Fit, showing strong generality. DeepSeek V4 Flash Preview maintained consistently high Mean VCR across harnesses on the shared four-benchmark panel.
  - **Efficiency**: Higher token consumption does not guarantee better results; distinct execution profiles can accompany similar task scores.
- **Comparison**: Unlike traditional methods that only look at final success rates, this framework reveals distinct execution profiles behind similar task scores, demonstrating the necessity of evaluating model-harness interactions.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper emphasizes the importance of evaluating LLM Agents as joint Model-Harness systems. Through the proposed five-dimensional framework, it reveals the high dependency of harness rankings and the complex relationship between task success and execution reliability. The findings suggest future directions should include training models with transferable harness-use capabilities and designing harnesses adapted to specific models and tasks, rather than optimizing models or frameworks in isolation.
