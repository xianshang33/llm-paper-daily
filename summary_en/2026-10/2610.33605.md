## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: As AI evolves from static data processing to Generative and Agentic AI, systems now retrieve, generate, infer, remember, and act autonomously. Traditional data governance models, focused on access control for stored assets, are insufficient for these dynamic information states and autonomous decision loops.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Classical data governance treats data as static assets and focuses on IT housekeeping rather than strategic oversight. It fails to address the shift from "access control" to "action control," cannot effectively govern dynamically generated information and multi-agent interactions, and lacks a unified approach to cross-jurisdictional legal compliance (e.g., India's DPDP Act, EU AI Act).

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Unified Data Governance Framework**: Integrates three disciplinary lenses—technology, law, and economics—covering the full lifecycle from LLMs and RAG pipelines to autonomous multi-agent systems.
- **Challenge 1: Governing Dynamic Information States and Generated Content**. The difficulty lies in the fluid nature of AI-generated information involving inference and memory, which static governance cannot track. The paper addresses this by defining "Seven Dynamic Information States" and the "Data-Model-Agent-Action Loop," shifting governance focus to the entire lifecycle of produced information to ensure traceability and auditability.
- **Challenge 2: Action Control for Autonomous Agents and Cross-Domain Compliance Complexity**. The challenge is managing autonomous agent actions while satisfying technical security (Zero Trust), legal compliance (varying jurisdictions), and economic incentives (liability allocation). The paper proposes the "CRAFT Framework" and a "Governance Control Plane" design for 2030, combining Zero Trust architecture with comparative legal perspectives (anchored in Indian law) to technically constrain agent behavior and clarify legal liabilities.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: As a monograph/preprint, it does not provide a specific codebase but offers a theoretical framework and design blueprint. Key deployment concepts include the "Governance Control Plane" integrating digital trust, cybersecurity, and Zero Trust architecture.
- **Evaluation Results**: Evaluation is conducted through case studies (e.g., "SmartCity Surveillance"), comparative legal analysis (across six jurisdictions including India, EU, US), and economic reasoning. It demonstrates how abstract legal rights (like privacy) translate into concrete technical governance requirements.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Unlike traditional IT data governance limited to metadata and access control, this work governs agent behavior and generated content. Compared to single-discipline studies, it innovatively fuses technology, law, and economics, offering a comprehensive interdisciplinary solution.

## 4. Conclusion
This book constructs a unified governance framework for Generative and Agentic AI, addressing the limitations of traditional static data governance in dynamic, autonomous systems. By integrating technology (Zero Trust, lineage), law (global data protection, IP), and economics (liability, incentives), it provides a full-lifecycle governance scheme covering data quality, ethics, security, and compliance. Its core contribution is defining a governance paradigm for dynamic information states and designing a future-oriented Governance Control Plane, serving as a practical guide for policymakers and developers across jurisdictions.
