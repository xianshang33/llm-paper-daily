## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) requires agentic systems to design, train, and evaluate their successors. Data-driven post-training is a central component of this loop, aiming to enhance model capabilities through automated data curation.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current evaluations typically focus on single benchmarks, where agents select from static pools or distill stronger models to boost a single score in one run. However, real-world post-training is multi-domain, where gains in one area often come at the cost of regression in another. Existing works fail to effectively address this Pareto optimization problem across multiple domains.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ParetoRSI-Gym**: A framework for evaluating multi-domain RSI systems, where an agent iteratively searches for data, curates it, trains, and reads per-domain results, keeping the initial LLM, five domains (Math, Code, Finance, Medicine, Science), toolset, and SFT pipeline fixed.
- **Challenge 1: Trade-offs and Synergistic Optimization Across Multiple Domains**. The difficulty lies in the fact that improving performance in one domain can lead to degradation in others (negative transfer), and it is hard to distinguish true capability gains from overfitting to proxy benchmarks. ParetoRSI-Gym addresses this by introducing generalization benchmarks to separate capability gains from overfitting and requiring the agent to update principles based on per-domain feedback in each round, aiming for holistic expansion of the capability vector rather than single-point breakthroughs.
- **Challenge 2: Effectiveness and Variability of Automated Data Curation Strategies**. The challenge is that different agent systems may adopt vastly different data selection strategies for the same task, and the "harness" significantly impacts final performance. The framework reveals these unique strategies and their significant impact on final model performance through multi-round iterations, multi-seed experiments, and detailed analysis, demonstrating its effectiveness in capturing these nuances.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The framework fixes the initial LLM, five specific domains, tools, and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) pipeline. In each round, the agent searches and pulls data from HuggingFace based on current principles, cleans, repairs, and justifies entries, submits them for training, and updates principles for the next round based on per-domain benchmark scores.
- **Evaluation Results**: Tested across three agent systems, three base models, five rounds, and multiple seeds. The strongest system (Claude Code with Opus-5) raised the generalization scores of its proxy-best checkpoint by 9.2% relative to the starting model (+2.3 absolute points, averaged over three base models and five domains). Single-domain relative gains reached up to 63% (Medicine on Ministral-3-3B).
- **Comparison and Analysis**: Unlike traditional single-benchmark evaluations, ParetoRSI-Gym demonstrates how agents balance trade-offs across multiple domains. Analysis shows that the agent's "harness" is as important as the model it drives, and each agent maintains a unique data selection strategy, providing a more comprehensive perspective for evaluating RSI systems in real-world ML engineering.

## 4. Conclusion
ParetoRSI-Gym fills the gap in evaluating RSI systems in realistic multi-domain scenarios. By fixing all variables except data, the framework effectively isolates and quantifies the agent's contribution to data curation. Experimental results prove that advanced agent systems can significantly improve multi-domain generalization performance, while revealing differences in agent strategies. This work marks an important step towards evaluating complex RSI systems in the real world, emphasizing the importance of assessing AI self-improvement capabilities in a multi-objective optimization context.
