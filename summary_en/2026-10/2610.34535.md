## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large language model (LLM) agents are increasingly integrated into operational loops with physical consequences, such as sending public alerts, dispatching responders, and querying citizen records. In public-safety deployments, a single erroneous tool call incurs real-world costs.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current agent-safety benchmarks measure generic harm or prompt-injection success and implicitly treat "refusal" as the safe default. This assumption breaks in public safety: an agent that is too cautious to dispatch help fails just as critically as one that triggers a false alert. Existing benchmarks lack a bidirectional evaluation that jointly assesses "over-refusal" (missing real emergencies) and "over-reaction" (unwarranted actions).

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SentryBench**: A cost-sensitive benchmark for evaluating the adversarial robustness of tool-using LLM agents in public-safety deployments, grounded in real emergency call transcripts, disaster messages, and injection/jailbreak payloads.
- **Challenge 1: Quantifying Bidirectional Risks and Asymmetric Costs**. The difficulty lies in traditional safety evaluations focusing only on preventing harmful outputs (unidirectional), ignoring that "inaction" (over-refusal) in public safety has high, potentially lethal costs, with asymmetric penalties for different error types. The paper addresses this by formalizing deployment harm as an asymmetric cost functional and introducing a two-sided benign control arm (urgent vs. ambiguous scenarios) to simultaneously measure attack success, over-refusal, and over-reaction.
- **Challenge 2: The Over-Refusal Trade-off in Defense**. The challenge is that traditional "caution-first" defenses slightly reduce attack success but cause extremely high over-refusal rates (missing genuine help requests). The paper proves that naive caution is harm-dominated and proposes a "targeted trust gate" wrapper. It theoretically proves this gate adds zero over-refusal on clean inputs and experimentally demonstrates it dominates the Pareto frontier with the lowest harm across all models.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: SentryBench comprises 840 scored episodes across three frontier models and three defense mechanisms. It covers four sub-domains, five attack vectors (injection, poisoning, goal-hijack, over-trust, privilege escalation), and three outcome classes. Provider content filter effects are reported separately.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - **Baseline Comparison**: The "refuse-more" strategy drove over-refusal to 100% for two out of three models, with at most a 3.7-point change in attack success, resulting in the worst overall harm.
    - **Proposed Method Performance**: The proposed trust-gating wrapper achieved the lowest harm on every model, with over-refusal at or below baseline levels and higher attack containment, thus dominating the performance frontier.
    - **Platform Effects**: Provider content filters were found to silently perform part of the defense, necessitating separate reporting to avoid confounding model comparisons.

#### Summary
SentryBench fills a critical gap in LLM agent safety evaluation for public safety, highlighting the failure of the traditional "refusal equals safety" assumption in high-stakes environments. By introducing a cost-sensitive, asymmetric harm evaluation framework, the study demonstrates that simple caution strategies lead to catastrophic over-refusal. The proposed target trust gating method effectively mitigates adversarial attacks without sacrificing responsiveness to genuine emergencies. The research underscores the need to jointly optimize attack defense and usability in critical deployments and to transparently account for platform-level filtering effects.
