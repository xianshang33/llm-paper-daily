## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: The continuous biomanufacturing industry urgently needs high-fidelity digital twins for real-time process analytical technology (PAT) and control, which rely on accurate kinetic models. Bioreactors (e.g., CSTRs) exhibit highly nonlinear, time-varying behaviors (such as substrate inhibition and product toxicity). Traditional first-principles modeling requires exhaustive a priori knowledge, while purely data-driven methods suffer from poor generalization and lack mechanistic interpretability.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current state estimation techniques (e.g., EKF, MHE) assume the model structure is known and cannot handle unmodeled dynamics. Symbolic regression and sparse identification algorithms (e.g., SINDy) often yield high-dimensional polynomial approximations lacking biological meaning. Methods like PINNs impose rigid physical assumptions, making them unsuitable for discovering complex, unknown biological kinetic structures.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A hybrid closed-loop architecture for autonomous kinetic state-space discovery driven by a Large Language Model (LLM) agent.
- **Challenge 1: Decoupling entangled terms in complex biological rate laws**. Biological growth kinetics often involve intricate entanglement of multiplicative (inhibitory) and additive (catalytic/regulatory) terms, making direct identification difficult. The paper employs an "isolation-peeling" methodology, using Moving Horizon Estimation (MHE) and logarithmic decomposition to mathematically decouple complex kinetics into orthogonal multiplicative and additive residual channels, simplifying the structural discovery process.
- **Challenge 2: Model hypothesis confusion due to statistical degeneracy**. When different kinetic hypotheses perform similarly on data (statistical degeneracy), passive observation cannot distinguish the true model. The paper introduces an Active Design of Experiments (DoE) controller that dynamically triggers targeted perturbations upon detecting degeneracy, decorrelating competing model hypotheses and enabling the LLM to make correct judgments based on highly discriminative data.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation Details**: The framework is built on a Two-Level Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) library combined with an LLM agent for reasoning. It uses MHE for state and parameter estimation and logarithmic decomposition for handling kinetic equations. An Active DoE module intervenes with experimental perturbations when uncertainty is high.
- **Evaluation Results**: Evaluated on a Continuous Stirred-Tank Reactor (CSTR) exhibiting unmodeled Haldane inhibition, Luong product toxicity, and secondary metabolite autocatalysis. The framework autonomously expanded the state space and recovered ground-truth parameters with under 6% error, without any a priori kinetic knowledge.
- **Comparison Advantages**: Compared to pure data-driven methods like SINDy, this approach generates models with clear biological meaning and interpretability. Compared to traditional mechanistic modeling, it does not require manual pre-definition of the complete reaction structure and can automatically discover missing kinetic terms.

#### Summary
This paper proposes an innovative LLM-driven framework that addresses the challenges of unknown structure and parameter coupling in bioprocess modeling. By combining the logical reasoning of LLMs, knowledge retrieval via RAG, parameter estimation via MHE, and exploration via Active DoE, it achieves autonomous discovery of complex biological kinetic structures and high-precision parameter identification, providing a new technical path for building high-fidelity digital twins in biomanufacturing.
