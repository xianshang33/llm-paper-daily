## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated capability in proving hard theorems in Lean, and LLM-guided design of optimization heuristics (e.g., AlphaEvolve) has shown progress. However, traditional algorithm design requires producing both the algorithm itself and mathematical proofs of its correctness and efficiency.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current LLM-guided heuristic design is purely empirical, lacking rigorous guarantees on correctness or performance. Meanwhile, automated theorem provers augmented with LLMs only prove given statements and do not engage in algorithm design. There has been little evidence that LLMs can simultaneously design algorithms and guarantee their efficiency via mathematical proof.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed an Agentic Algorithm Design Pipeline**: Modeled after research methodologies in evolutionary algorithms, this pipeline proposes black-box optimization algorithms, formalizes them, and proves their runtime bounds in Lean, completely replacing empirical evaluation.
- **Challenge 1: How to ensure generated algorithm designs are logically valid and defect-free?**
  - **Difficulty**: LLM-generated code may contain logical errors or be unexecutable, and the search space is vast.
  - **Solution**: Introduced "Lean guards" to filter out flawed designs early in the pipeline, ensuring only syntactically and logically feasible algorithms proceed to analysis.
- **Challenge 2: How to automatically prove that the new design is more efficient than the baseline and provide specific runtime bounds?**
  - **Difficulty**: Proving expected runtime requires deep understanding of problem structure and constructing appropriate potential functions and drift bounds, which is more complex than verifying given theorems.
  - **Solution**: The agent not only proposes algorithms but also defines potential functions and proves drift bounds, mathematically guaranteeing improvement in expected runtime over baselines (e.g., (1+1) EA), with all proofs machine-checked in Lean.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: A staged agentic pipeline was built using frontier LLMs as the core engine and Lean as the formal verification backend. No empirical benchmarking was involved.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - Across 8 problem classes, 12 out of 15 runs with frontier models successfully completed the pipeline, generating machine-checked expected runtime bounds.
  - Specific Cases: On the CycleUncover problem, the generated algorithm achieved $O(n^2)$ expected runtime, whereas the classical (1+1) EA requires $e^{\\Omega(n)}$; it matched the optimal $O(n^2)$ order on LeadingOnes; and achieved $O(n^{2+2c})$ for integer-valued submodular minimization.
  - Ablation Study: Removing the staging and asking for zero-shot design with runtime guarantees resulted in copying the baseline (random search) or incomplete proofs.
  - Open-weight models (e.g., Goedel-Prover-V2-32B and Gemma 4) failed to complete the pipeline.
- **Comparison**: Unlike purely empirical LLM heuristic design, this method provides rigorous mathematical guarantees. Compared to traditional ATP, it achieves a fully automated loop from algorithm design to proof, rather than just verifying given propositions.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper presents the first automated pipeline capable of designing efficient black-box optimization algorithms with machine-checked runtime bounds in Lean. The results suggest that machine-checked specifications in Lean can replace empirical evaluation in open-ended algorithm discovery. By combining staged design with formal verification, LLM agents overcome the limitations of purely empirical design, enabling automated algorithm design that is both innovative and theoretically guaranteed, opening new avenues for automated algorithm design in theoretical computer science.
