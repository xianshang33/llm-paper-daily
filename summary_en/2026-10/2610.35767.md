## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Root Cause Analysis (RCA) for microservice failures has seen a surge in LLM-based agents. Current state-of-the-art methods typically rely on "hand-crafted" designs, enforcing strict investigation protocols through standard operating procedures, fixed stages, and role-specific agents.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Existing studies are evaluated on disparate datasets with insufficient baselines, making it unclear whether complex hand-crafted designs outperform allowing capable models to investigate autonomously. The lack of a unified benchmark hinders fair comparison across methods.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed RCAtlas Benchmark and Simple Harness Paradigm**: Introduced RCAtlas, a unified benchmark with 90 validated cases from 8 public datasets. Systematically compared three "simple harnesses" that prescribe no model calls (one-shot evidence package, SQL agent, Codex agent) against five published LLM RCA agents and traditional statistical/causal methods.
- **Challenge 1: Evidence Omission due to Hand-Crafted Orchestration**. The difficulty lies in fixed program logic potentially forcing the model to skip critical evidence (e.g., specific log lines or service call interruptions). The paper demonstrates that simple harnesses, by allowing the model to freely choose what to observe and how to reason, avoid missing decisive evidence caused by preset paths, significantly improving accuracy.
- **Challenge 2: Single-Dimensional Evaluation and Resource Efficiency Trade-offs**. Previous works failed to disentangle the specific impacts of model capability, reasoning effort, and interaction turns. The paper reveals that stronger models mainly improve reliability, multi-turn interactions improve aptitude, and increased reasoning effort yields minimal accuracy gains while multiplying time costs. Simple harnesses dominate the Pareto frontier for cost and time efficiency.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on RCAtlas using 6 models and 3 reasoning effort levels. Compared 3 simple harnesses, 5 published LLM agents, and non-LLM methods (statistical and causal).
- **Results**:
    - **Accuracy**: Simple harnesses achieved 65-77% PR@1 on mid-tier models, significantly outperforming published LLM agents (16-53%) and non-LLM methods (6-39%).
    - **Failure Analysis**: Published agents fixed between 10% to 100% of model calls in their code, leading to skipped decisive evidence as shown in case studies.
    - **Efficiency**: Increasing reasoning effort changed accuracy by only a few points but increased time by up to 7x. Among all runs surpassing the best non-LLM method, every Pareto-optimal run in terms of cost or time utilized a simple harness.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper demonstrates through the unified RCAtlas benchmark that "simple harnesses," which allow models to freely observe and reason, outperform complex hand-crafted agents in microservice RCA. The study indicates that overly prescriptive investigation workflows hinder evidence acquisition. Simple methods not only lead in accuracy but also dominate in cost-time efficiency, suggesting future RCA systems should prioritize enhancing base model capabilities and data access freedom over complex process control.
