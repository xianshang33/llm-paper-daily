## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Interactive environments provide a practical way to evaluate Large Language Model (LLM) agents, with competitive games extending this to decisions shaped by competing goals and other agents' choices. Eurogames, which combine persistent strategic commitments, competition for shared resources, and multiple paths to victory, are ideal for testing complex agent decision-making.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Existing evaluations are often confined to individual games or specific execution frameworks, making it unclear which competitive advantages persist across different settings. There is a lack of unified interfaces and protocols to fairly compare performance across diverse game rules and information structures, and it is difficult to distinguish general competitive strength from game-specific advantages or account for the impact of execution settings and resource usage.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed DECE (Diverse Eurogame Competitive Environments)**: A unified arena spanning ten Eurogames with shared interfaces and evaluation protocols, enabling standardized cross-game assessment.
- **Challenge 1: Interface Consistency and Context Management Across Diverse Rules**. The difficulty lies in adapting agents to a unified observation-action interface while maintaining tool usage and context management capabilities across games with vastly different rules. DECE addresses this by designing shared interfaces and evaluation protocols that allow agents to handle the complexity of different games within a unified framework, ensuring fair cross-game comparisons.
- **Challenge 2: Distinguishing General Competitive Strength from Game-Specific Advantages and Quantifying Execution Variance**. The challenge is that the same model may perform differently across various execution harnesses, and high performance does not necessarily imply high resource efficiency. DECE uses seat-balanced multiplayer tournament protocols and matched studies to isolate the effects of model capability from execution frameworks. It also introduces resource usage (token/cost) as an additional evaluation dimension, revealing the inconsistency between performance advantages and resource efficiency.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation and Deployment**: DECE includes ten Eurogame environments (e.g., Splendor, Azul, Carcassonne, 7 Wonders, Wingspan, Burgundy, Brass, Ark Nova) and integrates an Arena Orchestrator for scheduling, executing, and recording matches. The study evaluated 12 "model-harness" configurations, covering 9 mainstream LLMs (such as OpenAI, DeepSeek, Qwen, Claude, Gemini, Grok) and 4 execution harnesses.
- **Evaluation Results**: The primary study conducted 480 three-player matches, with a total of 1,032 matches completed including supplementary studies. Results showed broad consistency in rankings across games, alongside game-specific strengths. Matched studies revealed significant performance variations for the same model across different execution harnesses. Furthermore, performance advantages did not consistently translate into better token or cost efficiency.
- **Comparison**: Compared to previous works focusing on single games or ignoring the impact of execution frameworks, DECE provides a more comprehensive perspective. It demonstrates that evaluating complete agent configurations requires considering both competitive performance across games and the resource consumption required to achieve it.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper introduces DECE, a unified Eurogame arena for evaluating LLM agents. Through large-scale experiments involving multiple models, frameworks, and games, the study reveals patterns of cross-game performance consistency, the significant impact of execution frameworks on results, and the decoupling of performance from resource efficiency. The research advocates for comprehensive metrics that include both competitive performance and resource efficiency when evaluating LLM agents, providing important references for future agent system development and benchmarking.
