## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: LLM-driven agent scaffold evolution improves agent workflows through iterative candidate generation and evaluation. Evaluation serves two distinct roles: guiding subsequent search and determining the final selected scaffold.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Evaluating every candidate on the full task set incurs substantial execution costs. Existing approaches primarily treat evaluation as a fitness-estimation problem using sparse scoring or cascades, failing to effectively distinguish between the different precision requirements for "search guidance" versus "final selection," leading to inefficient budget allocation.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed EvalAlloc**, an evaluation allocation framework that combines sparse search-time feedback with targeted final confirmation.
- **Challenge 1: How to maintain effective evolution while reducing search costs?**
  - **Difficulty**: Search requires many iterations, making full evaluation prohibitively expensive, yet removing feedback entirely degrades search quality.
  - **Solution**: A short fully evaluated warm-up establishes a shared informative core and calibrates a response model with fixed item parameters during search. This allows sparse feedback to maintain comparability, supporting continuous evolution at a low cost.
- **Challenge 2: How to accurately discriminate among highly competitive final candidates under a limited budget?**
  - **Difficulty**: Final selection requires reliable discrimination among top candidates that often have similar performance, where sparse feedback lacks sufficient discriminative power.
  - **Solution**: After search, the model is refitted, and paired successive halving is applied. This strategy reuses accumulated observations and executes only missing evaluations, providing targeted additional assessments to improve final selection accuracy.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated across four benchmarks and two evolution frameworks.
- **Results**: EvalAlloc achieved higher mean performance than full evaluation in most settings.
- **Efficiency**: The method used approximately half as many evaluation cells as full evaluation, significantly reducing computational costs while maintaining or surpassing performance.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper investigates evaluation budget allocation in LLM-driven agent scaffold evolution, highlighting the distinct needs of search guidance and final selection. The proposed EvalAlloc framework effectively balances cost and performance by employing "sparse search feedback + targeted final confirmation." Experiments demonstrate that this approach significantly reduces evaluation overhead while improving the performance of the final agent scaffold, offering an efficient paradigm for automated Agent optimization.
