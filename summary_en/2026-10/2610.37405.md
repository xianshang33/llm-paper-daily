## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) often answer factual questions correctly but lack traceable evidence. In high-stakes domains like healthcare and law, users require verifiable evidence to trust the output. Knowledge Graphs (KGs) serve dual roles: providing missing facts and supplying supporting triples for auditability.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current KG agents treat KGs merely as answer sources. Their training data lacks evidence triples, and reward mechanisms only verify answer correctness, ignoring retrieved evidence. Consequently, agents often provide correct answers without retrieving supporting triples, leaving the reasoning process opaque and untrustworthy.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed FoG Framework**: A framework designed to train KG agents to retrieve evidence behind their answers, comprising evidence-grounded synthesis, a KG interaction agent, and Evidence-Coverage Process Reward (ECPR).
- **Challenge 1: Lack of training data with complete evidence chains**. It is difficult to automatically align questions with their supporting triples. **Solution**: The paper introduces "evidence-grounded synthesis," which recursively hides explicit entities behind verifiable sub-questions. This ensures that every synthesized question inherently carries its supporting triples by construction, creating high-quality evidence-aligned data.
- **Challenge 2: Traditional rewards fail to incentivize evidence retrieval**. Standard RL rewards only feedback on final answer accuracy, failing to distinguish whether evidence was actually retrieved. **Solution**: The paper proposes ECPR, which credits each supporting triple to the specific turn where it is first retrieved during RL training, explicitly incentivizing the agent to actively seek and record evidence.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on three KGQA benchmarks.
- **Key Results**: FoG enables open-source agents with only 3B-4B parameters to substantially outperform much larger baselines in both answer accuracy and evidence coverage.
- **Comparison**: Compared to existing retrieval-based and agent-based methods, FoG significantly improves both accuracy and evidence completeness. It also demonstrates zero-shot cross-KG transfer capabilities, highlighting its generalization and robustness.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the "correct but unsupported" issue in LLM factual QA by proposing the FoG framework. By synthesizing data with inherent evidence chains and designing process rewards that incentivize evidence retrieval, FoG successfully trains small-parameter KG agents to achieve high accuracy and high evidence coverage. The method effectively clears the "fog" in reasoning, enhancing the credibility and interpretability of KG-augmented LLMs, and surpasses larger models across multiple benchmarks.
