## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents are increasingly tasked with extended analytical work, including writing code, running experiments, and producing full research papers. Organizations rely on these reported results, making the verification of numerical claims a critical data provenance issue.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current benchmarks and integrity checks, often performed by single LLM judges, identify failures without explaining their nature. Existing methods either check for internal consistency or verify if a value exists anywhere in the agent's output, failing to establish whether the value originates from the specific experiment it claims to describe.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a deterministic claim-to-evidence checker**: This tool scores whether the surrounding context identifies the appropriate result and calibrates each claim against numerical decoys to verify provenance.
- **Challenge 1: Distinguishing "Fabrication" from "Misattribution"**
  - **Description & Difficulty**: Most failures are not overt cheating but misattribution (values exist in files but come from wrong experiments/runs). Traditional binary judgments struggle to differentiate unsupported claims from misattributed ones, leading to high error rates.
  - **Solution**: Manual labeling of 256 claims revealed ~80% of unsupported claims correspond to values in agent files from wrong sources. The proposed checker, with rules fixed prior to labeling, certifies claims with >90% precision by verifying contextual alignment, distinguishing "unverifiable" from "false."
- **Challenge 2: Bias and Low Precision of Single LLM Judges**
  - **Description & Difficulty**: Single LLM judges often accept misattributed values and show significant variance in absolute pass rates across vendors, though relative rankings remain stable. Relying solely on LLMs for integrity auditing is unreliable.
  - **Solution**: The study shows the deterministic checker achieves >90% precision (Cohen's kappa = 0.89), while an LLM verifier accepts ~3x more misattributed claims. The paper advocates combining both methods for optimal auditing.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation**: The authors re-analyzed 540 judgments from a public benchmark (9 agents, 15 problems) and manually labeled 256 numerical claims from 18 submissions. The deterministic checker certified 78 out of 85 claims as traceable against blinded human annotators, achieving >90% precision.
- **Comparison Results**:
  - **vs. LLM Judge**: While the LLM verifier recovered more traceable claims, it accepted approximately three times as many misattributed ones compared to the deterministic checker, highlighting their complementary nature.
  - **vs. Existing Benchmarks**: Applied to 99 submissions, the checker revealed significant differences in numerical traceability across agents. A second judge from a different vendor preserved the official agent ranking but doubled the pass rate, demonstrating that absolute integrity outcomes depend heavily on judge choice.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper identifies that numerical errors in AI-generated research papers stem primarily from "misattribution" rather than "fabrication." Existing LLM judges fail to effectively distinguish between these error types. The authors propose a deterministic claim-to-evidence checker that verifies numerical sources with high precision and demonstrate the necessity of combining it with LLM verifiers. The findings underscore the importance of "provenance-first" auditing to ensure data quality and research integrity in AI-produced scientific reports.
