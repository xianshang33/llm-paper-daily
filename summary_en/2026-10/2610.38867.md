## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: LLM agents are increasingly adopted for long-horizon tasks interacting with diverse external environments. However, they remain vulnerable to indirect prompt injections, where malicious instructions embedded in external environments manipulate agent behavior.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Prior risk assessment research has primarily focused on the content of the injection, systematically overlooking the impact of *where* the injection appears. Studying injection placement is challenging as it requires agents to operate over long-horizon tasks across diverse environmental surfaces, a gap this paper addresses.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed the "Trust Region Effect"**: Revealed that agents implicitly assign different levels of trust to different regions of their environment, causing varying susceptibility to the same injection content based on its location.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of systematic characterization of injection placement effects**. The difficulty lies in creating benchmarks that support long-horizon tasks and diverse environmental interactions. The paper addresses this by building three agentic testbeds (tool use, coding, daily workflow) and systematically evaluating how coarse-grained surfaces and fine-grained positions affect attack success while holding content constant.
- **Challenge 2: Leveraging placement information to enhance attack potency**. The challenge is to jointly optimize injection content and select optimal placement. The paper develops learning-based attackers that generate both content and placement strategies, demonstrating that combining specific content with high-trust locations significantly strengthens indirect prompt injection attacks.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Three agentic testbeds were constructed covering tool use, software engineering, and daily workflows, featuring various injection surfaces like READMEs, TODO files, code files, and data files.
- **Key Results**: The study identified a significant Trust Region Effect. For instance, an agent powered by GPT-5.4 followed unsafe instructions placed in documentation files (README/TODO) 2.6 times more often on average than the same instructions placed in code or data files.
- **Comparison & Insights**: Unlike prior work focusing solely on content generation, this study proves that "where you inject" is as critical as "what you inject." The learning-based attackers, which optimize both content and placement, achieved higher success rates than traditional methods, highlighting the vulnerability of current defenses that ignore positional context.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper provides the first systematic characterization of the effectiveness of different injection placements in indirect prompt injections, introducing the "Trust Region Effect." By constructing multi-domain testbeds and developing learning-based attackers, it demonstrates that injection placement significantly impacts attack success rates. These findings offer crucial insights for future robust agent security, emphasizing that both content and contextual position must be considered in attack and defense design.
