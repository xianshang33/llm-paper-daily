## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) excel in single-agent tasks but struggle in Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) due to non-stationarity and incomplete information. Epistemic Game Theory suggests that strategic behavior is driven by an agent's beliefs about others' private information and how these beliefs update during interactions.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current LLM-based agents lack an explicit epistemic layer. They either map observations directly to actions or rely on unstructured prompting for reasoning, which is not treated as a structured epistemic process. Even when opponent modeling is used, it is typically decoupled from policy learning, preventing co-evolution and effective adaptation in complex interactions.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed EpiAgent Framework**: A framework that equips LLM agents with an explicit, learnable epistemic layer to maintain beliefs over others' private information and combine them with available information into an epistemic state for action selection.
- **Challenge 1: How to dynamically update beliefs about others' private information during interaction?**
  - **Difficulty**: Private information is unobservable, and beliefs must be accurately inferred and updated based on history. Traditional methods struggle to tightly integrate belief updates with policy optimization.
  - **Solution**: The policy is decomposed into "Epistemic Update" and "Action Planning." An intrinsic prediction reward is introduced to evaluate how well inferred beliefs predict subsequent observations, driving the explicit learning of the belief layer.
- **Challenge 2: How to jointly optimize belief updates and action planning in a non-stationary environment?**
  - **Difficulty**: Belief quality affects action planning, and actions influence future observations and beliefs. This bidirectional dependency creates a complex optimization landscape where optimizing stages independently leads to suboptimal results.
  - **Solution**: Formulated as a bi-level optimization problem, solved via "Epistemic Self-Play." This algorithm alternately optimizes both stages through self-play, allowing belief inference and policy selection to co-evolve.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated in both mixed and competitive scenarios against prompting-based and RL-based baselines.
- **Main Results**: EpiAgent consistently outperforms all baselines across tested scenarios.
- **Comparative Analysis**:
  - Compared to prompting-based methods, EpiAgent provides more structured reasoning via its explicit epistemic layer, avoiding the instability of free-form generation.
  - Compared to RL-based methods, EpiAgent better handles non-stationarity under incomplete information by explicitly modeling opponent beliefs.
- **Further Analysis**: Ablation studies confirm the benefits of dedicated epistemic training, showing significant improvements in belief inference accuracy and demonstrating the contribution of learned beliefs to action planning.

## 4. Conclusion
EpiAgent successfully addresses the limitations of existing LLM agents in incomplete information multi-agent interactions by introducing an explicit, learnable epistemic layer. Its core innovation lies in decomposing cognitive updates and action planning into a bi-level optimization problem, solved via Epistemic Self-Play to achieve co-evolution. Empirical results validate its effectiveness in improving belief inference accuracy and decision-making performance, offering a new paradigm for building robust multi-agent systems.
