## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Current Large Language Model (LLM) agents for personal health can read wearable data and provide recommendations, but they lack an auditable, falsifiable learning mechanism for the individual. They apply population knowledge to individuals without maintaining a body of explicit, probabilistic claims that are revised by evidence.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Existing systems (e.g., PH-LLM, PHIA) operate as a one-way flow from data to recommendation. They fail to capture the "predict-observe-revise" loop used by experienced coaches. They do not address the unique challenges of N-of-1 science: the subject cannot be reset, observations are costly and noisy, and there is a competing objective between learning about the athlete and improving their performance.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed EpiCoach**: An agentic system that treats a single athlete as the subject of accountable N-of-1 science, utilizing an evidence-gated hypothesis registry and a mechanistic digital twin.
- **Challenge 1: How to ensure auditability and falsifiability of personalized knowledge?**
  - **Difficulty**: LLMs are prone to post-hoc rationalization, creating a "garden of forking paths" where any outcome can be explained after the fact, lacking scientific rigor.
  - **Solution**: Introduced a "prediction ledger" instead of a chat log. Every hypothesis must issue a probabilistic prediction before a session occurs, and beliefs are updated only through logged statistical fits, closing the path for post-hoc explanations.
- **Challenge 2: How to build an accurate physiological model from noisy consumer wearable data while balancing learning and training outcomes?**
  - **Difficulty**: Wrist sensor data is noisy, the subject cannot be reset, and the system must balance maximizing information gain (learning) with safety and performance improvement (coaching).
  - **Solution**: Constructed a mechanistic digital twin including a lagged heart-rate response model, a fitness-fatigue model with learned time constants, and a latent technical-descending skill state. The agent prescribes training weeks as constrained experiments that maximize information gain subject to load and safety constraints.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: Evaluated over 18 months on a single cross-country mountain biker. Included a retrospective benchmark and a live prospective phase where the agent ran weekly cycles with a safety officer veto mechanism.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - **Retrospective Benchmark (Rolling-origin replay)**: The digital twin outperformed standard forecasters on heart rate prediction but not on segment time; the agentic loop matched the performance of a static model.
  - **Live Prospective Phase**: In the first few weeks, the ledger sealed 20 predictions and scored 14. Descent forecasts calibrated after one correction, while climb forecasts were pessimistic due to athlete improvement. The safety officer vetoed the agent twice. All corrections were preserved in the registry.
- **Comparison**: Unlike existing LLM health agents that only recommend without learning, EpiCoach provides an auditable evidence chain. Unlike traditional training platforms that use fixed constants (e.g., 42-day fitness decay), EpiCoach learns individual-specific parameters and environmental responses.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper presents EpiCoach, an agentic Bayesian experimental design system for individualized physiology. By establishing a falsifiable hypothesis registry and a mechanistic digital twin, it addresses the lack of auditable learning processes in current LLM health agents. Experiments demonstrate that the system can learn individual characteristics from noisy wearable data while ensuring safety, providing a template for auditable personalization in digital health. The authors release the registry, benchmark, and protocol.
