## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) show promise in personalized tutoring, content generation, and adaptive learning path planning. However, true capability development requires integrating these elements into a coherent learning process that evolves with the learner.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current LLM-based educational applications often focus on generating isolated instructional materials or conducting single-turn tutoring dialogues. They lack systematic coordination among learning goals, activities, and assessments. Consequently, they fail to effectively use accumulated learning evidence to dynamically adjust subsequent support, leading to situations where improved performance during AI-assisted practice does not necessarily translate into genuine independent capability growth.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a "Human-Participatory Multi-Agent Organization" framework and its instantiation, Agentic University (AU)**: This system treats learners as organizational members who collaborate with specialized agents to maintain a revisable "Capability Program," linking personal goals, multi-modal learning activities, and layered assessments.
- **Challenge 1: How to align goals, activities, and assessments while using learning evidence to determine next steps?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional methods struggle to maintain consistency among these three elements in a dynamic learning process and lack mechanisms for evidence-driven dynamic adjustment.
  - **Solution**: AU uses the "Capability Program" as a central coordinating artifact to explicitly connect personal goals, cross-modal activities, and layered assessments. Learner feedback and performance directly guide subsequent support, ensuring dynamic, evidence-based adjustments.
- **Challenge 2: How to enable sustained learner participation and agency within multi-agent systems?**
  - **Difficulty**: Existing systems often position learners as passive recipients, lacking control over their learning paths and deep engagement, resulting in insufficient cognitive support.
  - **Solution**: By introducing the "Human-Participatory Multi-Agent Organization" paradigm, learners act as organizational members co-maintaining the Capability Program. This approach makes learner participation the continuous basis for multi-agent coordination, enhancing learner agency and cognitive support.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation and Deployment**: Agentic University (AU) is a system designed for capability development in university students, with open-source code available. It employs a multi-agent architecture with specialized agents coordinated via the shared "Capability Program."
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - **Quantitative Metrics**: Users in the AU group achieved a mean assessment gain of 12.19 points on a 50-point scale, significantly higher than the 5.61 points in the control group.
  - **Qualitative Feedback**: Users reported stronger cognitive support and noted advantages in program organization, assessment design, and revision quality.
- **Comparison**: Compared to traditional LLM educational systems that only provide content generation or static path planning, AU achieves significantly better learning outcomes and user experience through dynamic coordination and human participation mechanisms.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper presents Agentic University, a personalized learning system based on Human-Participatory Multi-Agent Organization. By incorporating learners into the multi-agent collaboration loop and maintaining a dynamically revisable Capability Program, AU effectively addresses the challenge of aligning goals, activities, and assessments. Experiments demonstrate that this method outperforms existing approaches in improving learning outcomes and providing cognitive support, offering a new paradigm for adaptive learning centered on learner participation.
