## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents demonstrate capability in multi-turn natural language negotiation. However, existing research predominantly focuses on agreement rates, overlooking whether negotiated commitments create tangible value after execution. In high-stakes domains like international trade, fluent agreements do not guarantee feasibility, binding power, or actual economic value.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current benchmarks (e.g., bilateral or Diplomacy-style) primarily evaluate payoff division, preference inference, or strategic interaction, ending at agreement or match outcomes. They fail to test if natural language commitments can be grounded into executable terms that yield value under downstream constraints (e.g., capacity, network limits). Key failure modes include: unreliable mapping from dialogue to structured policy terms; under-delivery due to capacity or third-party demand constraints; and local deal success potentially harming aggregate Economic Health Index (EHI) via trade balance costs or inflation.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed TradeWar Benchmark and TACTIC Agent**: TradeWar is a multi-country simulation benchmark that translates negotiation dialogues into concrete tariff and trade-share policies, enforces capacity and network constraints during settlement, and evaluates outcomes via an Economic Health Index (EHI). TACTIC (Trade-aware Adaptive Consolidation and Theory-of-mind for International Commerce) is an agent that iteratively refines negotiation strategies across episodes without updating base LLM weights, leveraging prior experience, opponent behavior, and past settlement outcomes.
- **Challenge 1: The Gap Between Natural Language Commitments and Executable Policies**. The difficulty lies in cooperative language often remaining rhetorical rather than actionable, and potentially violating physical or policy constraints. TACTIC addresses this by grounding dialogue into specific tariff and trade-share terms and validating their feasibility during the settlement phase.
- **Challenge 2: Long-term Strategy Adaptation Under Delayed Execution Feedback**. The challenge is that Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and interaction memory alone are insufficient for handling delayed execution feedback, where negotiation impacts on macroeconomics are lagged and complex. TACTIC solves this through cross-episode Adaptive Consolidation and Theory-of-mind, iteratively refining strategies based on past settlement results and opponent behavior to improve long-horizon performance.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Experiments conducted over 20 sequential episodes with three random seeds on the TradeWar benchmark. Baselines include PromptLLM (using zero-shot CoT) and MemoryLLM.
- **Key Results**:
  - PromptLLM achieved a 75% agreement rate but a final EHI of only 57.1 (×100).
  - TACTIC achieved a significantly higher final EHI of 68.8.
  - Over 20 episodes, TACTIC's final EHI improved from 58.2 to 73.2, whereas an ablation without cross-episode updates only reached 65.2, demonstrating the effectiveness of the adaptive mechanism.
- **Robustness**: Under technology control and energy crisis shocks, TACTIC exhibited smaller performance degradation compared to PromptLLM and MemoryLLM.
- **Conclusion**: Results indicate that CoT deliberation and interaction memory alone do not fully address delayed execution feedback, while settlement-grounded adaptation significantly improves long-horizon negotiation performance.

#### Summary
This paper identifies the limitation of existing LLM negotiation benchmarks in ignoring "execution-grounded" value. It introduces the TradeWar benchmark, emphasizing the translation of negotiations into executable policies and evaluating their macroeconomic impact. The proposed TACTIC agent, through cross-episode adaptive strategy optimization without model fine-tuning, significantly improves the Economic Health Index and demonstrates superior robustness under dynamic shocks. This work advances LLM agents from simple dialogue negotiation towards capabilities with actual execution value and long-term strategic planning.
