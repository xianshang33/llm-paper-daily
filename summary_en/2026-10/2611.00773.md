## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large language model (LLM) agents are increasingly used for automated scientific research, linking literature analysis, planning, and experimentation. However, evaluating the "action" selected by an agent does not determine the specific experiment specified by its instruction, as omitted interventions or replication counts leave critical choices to the executor, creating ambiguity.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current research agent benchmarks and evaluation methods focus on task success and step-level correctness but fail to resolve experimental design choices left unspecified in an action. A scientifically relevant instruction may correspond to multiple executable designs with varying information values and costs, and existing methods lack metrics to quantify this "design specification" and "execution ambiguity."

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Reading-Set Framework**: This framework scores the designs admitted by an instruction under a common cost rule with per-design budget gating, making the dependence of the instruction on specific designs visible.
- **Challenge 1: How to quantify execution ambiguity and information value of instructions?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional evaluations only look at action names, ignoring the variety of specific experimental designs possible under the same action name, thus failing to measure actual information acquisition efficiency.
  - **Solution**: Introduced the "reading-set" concept to evaluate all possible designs covered by an instruction, scoring them under a unified cost rule to transform design specifications and execution ambiguities into measurable metrics.
- **Challenge 2: How to validate the consistency of evaluation metrics with human judgment?**
  - **Difficulty**: There is a lack of standardized datasets to verify whether automated evaluation metrics truly reflect the quality of scientific experimental designs.
  - **Solution**: Constructed an author-curated research-action evaluation dataset with 60 scenarios and 240 candidate actions, demonstrating that the scenario-level "information-per-cost" metric has a mean within-scenario correlation of 0.733 with judgments from 18 human participants.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Tested across 30 synthetic states and used the newly constructed dataset of 60 scenarios for complementary evaluation.
- **Key Results**:
  - Across two model families, complete-design requests increased affordable efficiency by 465.98% and 327.91%, respectively, compared to the traditional "action-name" baseline.
  - On the new dataset, the proposed scenario-level "information-per-cost" metric showed a mean within-scenario correlation of 0.733 with human judgments, indicating strong alignment.
- **Comparison**: Unlike traditional methods that only evaluate action selection, this framework distinguishes the information value and cost of different specific designs, addressing the inability of previous methods to measure execution-level ambiguity and providing a more reproducible basis for assessment.

#### Summary
This paper addresses the issue of instruction ambiguity in the experimental design phase of LLM research agents by proposing a reading-set based evaluation framework. By quantifying the design space allowed by an instruction and its information value under a unified cost, the method significantly improves evaluation accuracy. Experiments show that specifying complete design norms greatly enhances experimental efficiency, and the proposed evaluation metrics correlate highly with human expert judgments, providing a reproducible foundation for assessing how research agents translate scientific intent into feasible experiments.
