## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Agentic AI and Network Digital Twins (NDTs) are combined to form Cognitive Digital Twins (CDTs) that translate operational intents into network changes for closed-loop management.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Current LLM-based planners often overlook the side effects of network changes on monitoring and recovery mechanisms. For instance, service rerouting may leave monitoring traffic on the old path, rendering data invalid (a problem termed "self-decoupling") and breaking the assurance loop required for subsequent decisions.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: Closed-Loop Dependency Assurance (CLDA), a mechanism that augments agentic AI-enabled NDTs with dependency-aware validation.
- **Challenge 1: Self-decoupling of monitoring and recovery dependencies**. Network changes can invalidate monitoring data or rollback capabilities while achieving their primary goal, a nuance difficult for LLMs to detect. **Solution**: CLDA introduces a Loop Dependency Graph (LDG) to explicitly link service requirements with monitoring, paths, and recovery prerequisites, using deterministic rules to project actions and validate intermediate states.
- **Challenge 2: Risks in intermediate states of multi-step plans and scalability**. Multi-step reconfigurations may temporarily lose monitoring validity, and deterministic verification is computationally intensive at scale. **Solution**: CLDA supports deterministic "make-before-break" repairs using temporary monitoring mechanisms and demonstrates scalability in synthetic studies with up to 1,024 routers.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation**: CLDA was evaluated through controlled reconfigurations, a 240-trial LLM-based planning campaign, and live network execution. A separate synthetic study assessed scalability.
- **Results & Comparison**: Unlike pure LLM-based approaches, CLDA uses explicit, deterministic rules for assurance. Results show it effectively prevents monitoring invalidation and loss of recoverability caused by network changes, addressing the logical gaps in LLM planners while maintaining scalability in large network topologies.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper highlights the risk of Agentic AI undermining its own assurance loop via "self-decoupling." The proposed CLDA framework addresses this by using a Loop Dependency Graph and deterministic rules to validate dependencies that LLMs often miss. This ensures continuous observability and recoverability during network changes, providing a robust foundation for reliable Cognitive Digital Twins.
