## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: The communication topology of Large Language Model Multi-Agent Systems (LLM-MASs) governs information propagation and encodes critical domain knowledge and expert workflow designs. This topology is valuable intellectual property (IP) and plays a decisive role in system utility and safety.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current defenses focus on detecting or mitigating attacks (e.g., prompt injection, jailbreaking) but do not proactively conceal the topology itself. Attackers can stealthily reconstruct the topology from agent outputs via black-box access, leading to IP leakage and enhanced security risks, which existing methods fail to prevent.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Illusion**: A proactive topology obfuscation defense framework for LLM-MAS that injects fake edges and messages to present a deceptive topology while preserving system behavior.
- **Challenge 1: How to obfuscate topology without degrading task performance?**
  - **Difficulty**: Randomly adding fake connections or messages can disrupt normal agent collaboration, altering output distributions and reducing task accuracy, thereby compromising system utility.
  - **Solution**: Illusion carefully selects "low-impact" candidate fake edges and messages to ensure output-distribution equivalence and task-accuracy non-inferiority, thus confusing attackers without affecting normal business logic.
- **Challenge 2: How to make the fake topology indistinguishable under black-box observation?**
  - **Difficulty**: Attackers may use similarity-based filtering or LLM judges to distinguish real messages from fake ones, or employ adaptive pruning to remove anomalous connections, rendering obfuscation ineffective.
  - **Solution**: Illusion designs fake edges to be indistinguishable from genuine messages under black-box observation. Experiments show these fake edges resist both similarity-based and LLM-judge filtering, remaining effective even against adaptive pruning.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Results**: Extensive experiments demonstrate that Illusion significantly reduces attacker inference capabilities. The edge-level F1 score drops from over 0.87 to 0.62, and graph similarity decreases from over 0.94 to 0.71.
- **Performance Preservation**: The method largely preserves original output distributions and task performance, validating its behavior-preserving nature.
- **Robustness Comparison**: Unlike existing passive defenses that only detect or mitigate attacks, Illusion proactively obfuscates the topology. Its generated fake connections resist advanced filtering techniques (including similarity filtering and LLM judges) and remain robust under adaptive pruning, demonstrating superior resilience compared to baseline approaches.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the IP and security risks associated with topology leakage in LLM-MAS by proposing the Illusion framework. By injecting behavior-preserving fake edges and messages, Illusion successfully obfuscates the true topology in black-box environments, significantly reducing attacker inference accuracy while maintaining system task performance and output stability, offering an effective proactive defense for LLM-MAS topology confidentiality.
