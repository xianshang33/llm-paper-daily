## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: In mission-critical digital engineering and operations such as nuclear energy, aviation, and national security, AI is tasked with high-risk operations (e.g., space domain awareness, computational fluid dynamics simulations, microreactor control). These scenarios are stateful, bound to specialized software, and governed by strict physical and procedural constraints.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Pure learning models (including fine-tuned LLMs) are fast but lack final authority on "what is safe," suffering from hallucinations and uncontrollability. Traditional symbolic systems or physics simulators are reliable but lack flexibility and automation capabilities. Sole reliance on LLMs fails to meet regulatory requirements for hard safety limits and verifiability in high-stakes domains.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed GURU**: A hierarchical neuro-symbolic multi-agent system for verification-aware automation.
- **Challenge 1: How to ensure safety in mission-critical operations while maintaining high efficiency?**
  - **Difficulty**: LLMs are prone to unpredictable errors, which can be catastrophic in high-risk environments. Traditional methods struggle to balance speed with absolute safety.
  - **Solution**: GURU employs a closed-loop workflow with a "Neuro-Symbolic-Physics" three-tier architecture. LLMs act only as proposers, while all consequential actions are checked against hard limits by a symbolic rules engine and confirmed by validated physics models, ensuring safety while leveraging AI for acceleration.
- **Challenge 2: How to achieve generalization and high precision in complex, unseen physical simulations and cross-domain tasks?**
  - **Difficulty**: Single models struggle to converge or predict accurately on unseen aircraft geometries or complex space debris trajectories, and demands vary significantly across domains like CFD and space awareness.
  - **Solution**: Navigation, Action, and Planning agents share a Common Internal Language and training factory. By integrating knowledge graph retrieval, role adapters, and a frozen self-supervised world model with validated propagation for space tasks, GURU achieves high convergence rates and low variance on unseen data.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD)**: The converged-simulation rate on unseen aircraft geometries increased from under 1% to 74% over 100 single-attempt cases. It ran 54 live cases up to Mach 6 across three facilities. Ablation studies attributed 13 points of boundary-layer capture to the symbolic layer.
- **Software Control (ParaView)**: Knowledge-graph retrieval and role adapters raised ParaView control success from 55% to 75%.
- **Space Domain Awareness (SDA)**: In conjunction risk assessment, GURU's frozen self-supervised world model scored highest among five methods, outperforming fully fine-tuned LLMs with several-times-lower seed variance, demonstrating that trustworthy design sacrifices no accuracy.
- **Scalability and Deployment**: Demonstrated a radar-to-avoidance debris pipeline for a 2026 reentry; the hierarchy coordinated simulated teams of 200 aircraft. Training scaled near-linearly past 1,000 Aurora nodes and ran at 8,000 nodes.
- **Other Applications**: The approach transferred to reactor anomaly detection and extends to a control-grade microreactor digital twin where validated physics, not learned models, serves as the safety basis.

#### Summary
The GURU system successfully resolves the conflict between AI safety and efficiency in mission-critical domains through its innovative hierarchical neuro-symbolic architecture. By placing LLMs under strict constraints of symbolic rules and validated physics rather than relying on them for final safety decisions, GURU ensures verifiability. Experimental results show significant improvements over existing methods in CFD convergence, software control success, and space risk assessment accuracy, offering a scalable, trustworthy solution for automation in sensitive sectors like nuclear energy and aviation.
