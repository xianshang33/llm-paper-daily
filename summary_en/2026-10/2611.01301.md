## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in multi-agent settings, exhibiting emergent collective behaviors. However, the role of curiosity as a driver of these dynamics remains understudied. Human psychology suggests curiosity drives learning, exploration, and innovation, and is influenced by cultural backgrounds (curiosity contagion).
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current multi-agent LLM research focuses mainly on planning, tool use, or safety evaluation, often overlooking "curiosity" as an intrinsic driver of collaboration quality. There is also a lack of systematic evaluation of how cultural grounding modulates curiosity and interaction outcomes.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed CuriousTogether Framework**: A framework for evaluating curiosity-driven multi-agent LLM interactions across three complementary dimensions: novelty-seeking, epistemic curiosity, and social curiosity.
- **Challenge 1: How to quantify and stimulate different types of curiosity in multi-agent collaboration to enhance task performance?**
  - **Difficulty**: Curiosity is an abstract concept difficult to translate into actionable LLM instructions, and different tasks (e.g., reasoning vs. creation) require different types of curiosity drivers.
  - **Solution**: The paper designs task-specific interaction protocols. For instance, it stimulates novelty-seeking in creative generation via "guiding questions," epistemic curiosity in reasoning via "clarifying questions" and "requesting evidence," and social curiosity in social tasks via "perspective-taking questions." Experiments show this guidance outperforms non-curious settings in 7 out of 8 tasks.
- **Challenge 2: How does cultural background affect multi-agent interaction outcomes and potential biases?**
  - **Difficulty**: Cultural norms vary widely; blindly introducing cultural grounding may introduce noise or bias in precision-required tasks, while being beneficial in social tasks, making balance challenging.
  - **Solution**: The paper introduces country-conditioned cultural grounding. Findings indicate that cultural grounding benefits story generation and social tasks (reducing stereotype rates by 14.9 percentage points) but may hinder precision-oriented reasoning tasks, revealing the scenario-dependent nature of cultural adaptation.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation Setup**:
  - **Tasks**: Covers 8 tasks including reasoning (CSQA, StrategyQA), creative generation (Story generation, Hypothesis generation, Codenames), and social bias mitigation (Bias Scenarios, ETHICS).
  - **Baselines**: Compared against Zero-shot single-agent and Token-matched single-agent baselines.
  - **Ablations**: Compared All-curious, One-curious, and No-curious settings, as well as settings with and without cultural grounding.
- **Key Results**:
  - **Performance Improvement**: Curiosity-guided multi-agent settings outperformed non-curious ones in 7 out of 8 tasks. Notably, semantic diversity in story generation improved by 20.6%, and stereotype rates in social tasks decreased by 14.9 percentage points.
  - **Cultural Impact**: Cultural grounding performed better in creative and social tasks but worse in precise reasoning tasks.
  - **Efficiency Trade-off**: Compared to single-agent baselines, the inference-time multi-agent curiosity-based approach offered favorable quality-token trade-offs, achieving higher output quality with reasonable token consumption.

## 4. Conclusion
The CuriousTogether framework demonstrates that curiosity-guided interaction is a practical mechanism for improving multi-agent collaboration. By decomposing curiosity into novelty-seeking, epistemic, and social dimensions, and incorporating cultural grounding, the method achieves significant improvements in reasoning, creative, and social tasks. The study not only highlights the potential of curiosity in reducing bias and enhancing creativity but also points out its complex impacts across different task types and cultural contexts, providing important insights for designing more adaptive and socially aware AI systems in the future.
