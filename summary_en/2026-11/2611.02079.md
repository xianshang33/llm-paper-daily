## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: The capabilities of Large Language Model (LLM) agents depend not only on model weights but also on their "Harness" (prompts, tools, middleware, and control logic). Automatic Harness Optimization (AHO) can significantly improve agent performance without modifying model weights.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current AHO methods primarily optimize for response quality, ignoring cost, which is critical for real-world deployment. Extending these methods to joint quality-cost optimization introduces a challenge: applying final deployment preferences during intermediate updates can prematurely discard candidates that are valuable for future improvements, limiting the optimization potential.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ParetoSaddler**: A Pareto-guided optimization framework for agent harnesses that jointly optimizes task response quality and LLM token cost.
- **Challenge 1: Premature Elimination of Intermediate Candidates**. In multi-objective optimization, directly applying deployment preferences discards intermediate candidates that may be suboptimal in one dimension but hold potential for future gains, restricting the search space. **Solution**: ParetoSaddler separates intermediate candidate promotion from final deployment selection. It uses Bayesian bootstrap Pareto support to promote candidates showing credible progress in either quality or cost, preserving diverse search paths for subsequent iterations.
- **Challenge 2: Instability and Noise in Optimization Results**. Due to the stochastic nature of LLMs, single-execution results may not reflect true harness performance, leading to ineffective optimizations based on noise. **Solution**: The method employs repeated executions and Bayesian bootstrap screening to filter out candidates with weakly supported gains, ensuring that only statistically significant improvements drive the optimization process.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on ClawMark and GAIA2 benchmarks using domain-disjoint splits to ensure generalizability.
- **Results**:
    - **ClawMark**: Quality improved from 70.86% to 81.35%, while cost per task decreased from $1.17 to $1.01.
    - **GAIA2**: Quality improved from 56.22% to 62.89%, with cost per task dropping significantly from $0.78 to $0.45.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Compared to quality-only baselines (e.g., AutoSaddler-Quality) and other multi-objective methods (e.g., Meta-Harness-Pareto), ParetoSaddler discovers higher-quality harnesses in lower-cost regions. Analysis confirms that Pareto-aware optimization retains intermediate candidates discarded by strict deployment preferences, leading to superior quality-cost trade-offs.

#### Summary
ParetoSaddler addresses the challenge of joint quality and cost optimization in LLM agent harnesses. By introducing a Bayesian bootstrap Pareto support mechanism, it effectively decouples intermediate exploration from final selection, avoiding local optima caused by early preference application. Experiments demonstrate that the method simultaneously improves answer quality and significantly reduces inference costs across multiple benchmarks, outperforming existing single and multi-objective optimization baselines.
