## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: While LLM coding agents are increasingly automating model post-training, it remains unclear if they can act as Forward-Deployed Engineers (FDEs) who discover customer requirements and make deployment decisions. Major industry players like OpenAI and Anthropic are heavily investing in this role, emphasizing the transition from idea to production systems.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current research primarily focuses on benchmark accuracy or isolated code generation, lacking a systematic evaluation of the full lifecycle in real enterprise environments, including requirement discovery, hardware constraints, data permissions, and delivery. High benchmark accuracy does not guarantee production readiness, and there is a lack of systematic comparison across base models and post-training methods regarding compute budget utilization.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed an FDE Agent Simulation Framework based on a Real Enterprise Text-to-SQL Case**: This framework simulates the entire process from ambiguous requests to final delivery, encompassing both discovery and delivery phases.
- **Challenge 1: Incompleteness and Ambiguity in Requirement Discovery**. The difficulty lies in the customer's initially vague request, requiring the agent to uncover implicit acceptance criteria, data permissions, and hardware constraints through multi-turn dialogue. The paper's method uses a simulated customer grounded in real-case knowledge, but results show that even the strongest models only discover over 80% of hidden requirements (40-50% for open-weight models), revealing that 40% of requirements were missing or misrepresented.
- **Challenge 2: Unreliable Execution and Insufficient Resource Planning in Delivery**. The challenge involves translating discovered requirements into concrete post-training recipes (e.g., base model selection, SFT/GRPO, quantization) while meeting performance/cost constraints. The paper's method allows the agent to plan and execute these recipes, but only 25% of runs passed all acceptance criteria, highlighting issues with unreliable execution, underused compute budgets, and a lack of systematic comparisons.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation and Deployment**: The study is grounded in records from a completed enterprise Text-to-SQL project, annotating customer requirements and operational facts. It constructs an experimental environment with 15 models and 8 coding-agent configurations. The process has two phases: (1) Discovery: Agent interacts with a simulated customer to clarify requirements; (2) Delivery: Agent plans and executes post-training recipes (model selection, fine-tuning, quantization, etc.).
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - **Discovery Phase**: Strongest models discovered >80% of hidden requirements, while open-weight models discovered only 40-50%. 40% of hidden requirements were missed or misrepresented.
    - **Delivery Phase**: Only 25% of runs across all 8 coding-agent configurations passed all acceptance criteria.
- **Comparison and Bottleneck Analysis**: Unlike works focusing solely on benchmark accuracy, this paper demonstrates that benchmark metrics do not equate to production readiness. It identifies four key bottlenecks: (1) Incomplete discovery; (2) Unreliable execution; (3) Underused compute budgets and lack of systematic comparison; (4) Disconnect between benchmark metrics and production capability.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper systematically investigates the capability of LLM agents to act as Forward-Deployed Engineers (FDEs), specifically in enterprise Text-to-SQL scenarios involving requirement discovery and delivery. The study finds that while LLMs are strong in coding tasks, significant bottlenecks remain in handling ambiguous requirements, planning complex engineering decisions, and ensuring production-grade reliability. The paper suggests human review of discovered requirements, monitoring of agent plans during execution, and explicit specification of experimental environments (including hardware, budget, and production checks) to improve delivery reliability and efficiency.
