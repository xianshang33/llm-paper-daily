## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Abnormal conditions in chemical processes often require interventions beyond regulatory control. While Basic Process Control Systems (BPCS) handle normal operations and Safety Instrumented Systems (SIS) provide protection, neither determines the optimal corrective action after a fault. This task typically falls to human operators who must interpret alarms and act to prevent shutdowns.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Traditional Fault Detection and Diagnosis (FDD) and Fault-Tolerant Control (FTC) methods are often limited to specific models or rules, struggling with complex multivariate coupling and unstructured knowledge. They lack the advanced reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) and often miss effective validation mechanisms for generated actions, leading to reliability issues in dynamic environments.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed OperAItor Framework**: An open-source framework for developing and evaluating LLM agents for supervisory fault management in chemical processes, integrating multivariate statistical monitoring, fault diagnosis, knowledge-graph retrieval, action planning, and action generation.
- **Challenge 1: LLM-generated control actions may contain hallucinations or violate physical/safety constraints, posing high risks if executed directly.**
  - **Difficulty**: LLMs lack precise understanding of underlying physical models, potentially generating actions that cause instability or safety violations.
  - **Solution**: Introduced five action-validation configurations, including no validation, LLM-based judge, digital-twin simulation, and deterministic admissibility checks (with/without consequence projection), ensuring action safety and feasibility through multi-layer verification.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of standardized benchmarks to evaluate different LLM architectures and validation methods in complex chemical scenarios.**
  - **Difficulty**: Existing studies often test in simplified environments, lacking complex dynamic benchmarks with real thermodynamics, kinetics, and complete safety systems.
  - **Solution**: Constructed a dynamic reactor-distillation benchmark based on isopropanol dehydrogenation, featuring a 113-variable differential-algebraic model, 8 PI control loops, 22 alarms, and 11 independent SIFs, evaluated across 20 single and multiple fault scenarios.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The framework operates in a simulated plant environment with a nominal feed rate of 100 kmol/h. The agent architecture combines statistical monitoring and knowledge graphs to process real-time data and generate supervisory actions.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - Without agent intervention, 18 out of 20 scenarios resulted in plant stops, causing an economic loss of approximately $399,000.
  - Using validated agent configurations with GPT-5 nano, Qwen3 8B, and Mistral 7B reduced stops to 5-8, recovering $209,000–$265,000 in losses.
  - For the smaller Llama 3.2 3B model, deterministic admissibility checks reduced stops from 13 to 7, whereas LLM-based validation alone resulted in 15 stops, highlighting the importance of deterministic checks for smaller models.
- **Comparison**: Compared to traditional non-intervention or single-validation methods, OperAItor's hybrid validation strategy significantly improved fault recovery rates and economic outcomes, providing a reproducible benchmark for comparing LLMs and validation methods.

## 4. Conclusion
The OperAItor framework successfully demonstrates the potential of LLM agents in supervisory fault management for chemical processes. By introducing rigorous action validation mechanisms, particularly deterministic admissibility checks, it effectively compensates for LLM limitations in physical consistency. The study proves that agents can significantly reduce unplanned shutdowns and economic losses, offering a standardized benchmark with complex dynamic models and safety systems to advance the reliable application of Agentic AI in industrial process control.
