## What the Paper Does

### Background
Legal Judgment Prediction (LJP) requires models to perform multi-step reasoning and retrieve external legal evidence. As LLM-based agents develop, they are increasingly applied in the legal domain to compensate for the limitations of parametric knowledge in covering frequently revised legal provisions.

### Why Existing Work Falls Short
The performance of existing legal search agents is often constrained by the quality of retrieval queries. They typically formulate queries directly from initial user requests, overlooking latent legal issues and legally salient details. Furthermore, traditional "Supervised Fine-Tuning followed by Reinforcement Learning" (SFT+RL) methods suffer from two main drawbacks: exploration is anchored to the model's initial hypotheses, making it difficult to break out of established thinking patterns, and outcome rewards alone cannot distinguish between different types of search failures, hindering effective learning from errors.

## Core Contributions

### Proposed LegalEvolver
A query optimization framework specifically designed for LJP-oriented retrieval, aiming to generate legally informative queries.

### Challenge 1: Inefficient sampling of high-quality queries and search trajectories, limited by initial model capabilities
Relying solely on unguided high-temperature rollouts is inefficient and constrained by the model's initial capabilities, making it difficult to produce diverse, high-quality queries. LegalEvolver addresses this by heuristically optimizing queries as seeds, thereby improving the efficiency of trajectory generation and overcoming initial capability limitations.

### Challenge 2: Lack of fine-grained guidance signals to optimize search behavior
Outcome rewards do not provide sufficient feedback to correct specific search errors. LegalEvolver summarizes rollout experiences into rubrics and uses them to guide multi-round iterative self-evolution. By iteratively performing rubric optimization and rubric-guided reinforcement learning, the model surpasses its initial performance ceiling.

## Implementation, Deployment, and Evaluation Results with Comparisons

End-to-end performance on extensive benchmarks demonstrates the effectiveness of query optimization for LJP. Compared to direct SFT followed by RL, LegalEvolver shows the effectiveness of rubric guidance. Additionally, evolving rubrics further outperform fixed rubrics, confirming the benefits of the self-evolution mechanism. Experimental results indicate that the method significantly improves retrieval quality and final judgment prediction accuracy by generating queries with greater legal insight.

## Summary

LegalEvolver effectively addresses the problems of low query quality and sparse training signals in legal search agents by introducing heuristic query seed optimization and a rubric-based self-evolution mechanism. This approach not only improves retrieval relevance but also breaks through model performance bottlenecks via iterative self-improvement, offering new insights for agent applications in the legal domain.
