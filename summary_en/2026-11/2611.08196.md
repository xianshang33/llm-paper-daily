## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Complex problems with limited context, such as verifying scientific claims, require additional knowledge for reasoning. While Large Language Models (LLMs) possess vast pre-trained knowledge, they struggle to fully leverage it during reasoning, often relying on surface text which leads to biases and hallucinations.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current methods fail to effectively capture the implicit states of entities within the problem context and struggle to introduce latent but crucial entities (e.g., inferring "intestinal epithelium" from "JAM-A"). Furthermore, there is a lack of mechanisms to validate the premises used in reasoning, making the foundation of the solution unreliable.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SMART**: A multi-agent framework that captures the implicit state of participating entities using a structured role-based prompting strategy. It curates state knowledge into diverse, complementary, and solution-salient premises using various LLMs.
- **Challenge 1: Capturing Implicit Entity States and Missing Knowledge**. The difficulty lies in identifying key entities not explicitly mentioned in the claim and understanding complex interactions. SMART addresses this by dynamically identifying relevant entities and assigning "expert" agents to each, generating contrastive premises to uncover deep, connected state knowledge.
- **Challenge 2: Reliability and Verification of Premises**. The challenge is that LLM-generated knowledge can be hallucinated, compromising reasoning accuracy. SMART addresses this by grounding premises in verifiable real-world knowledge, using curated premises to guide solution reasoning, ensuring the reasoning chain is well-founded.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Evaluation**: SMART was evaluated on three datasets containing complex scientific claims. The framework employs multi-agent collaboration combined with structured prompting to acquire and verify state knowledge.
- **Results & Comparison**: Experiments show that SMART's knowledge-guided reasoning improves validation performance by up to 20%. Ablation studies demonstrate that grounding premises in real-world knowledge is necessary, contributing an additional 5% performance gain. Compared to traditional methods relying solely on internal LLM knowledge or direct reasoning, SMART significantly reduces hallucinations and enhances reasoning depth through explicit state knowledge acquisition and verification.

#### Summary
The SMART framework successfully addresses reasoning challenges in complex scientific claim verification caused by limited context and missing implicit knowledge. Its core innovation lies in transforming entity states into verifiable premises and deepening understanding through contrastive reasoning via multi-agent collaboration. Experiments confirm that this approach significantly improves validation accuracy and highlights the importance of grounding premise knowledge for enhancing LLM reasoning reliability.
