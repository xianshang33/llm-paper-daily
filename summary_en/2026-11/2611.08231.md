## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are widely adopted for information access, content generation, and agent-based tasks, yet their outputs remain susceptible to subtle, high-risk hallucinations. Given the scale of AI-generated content, manual verification is infeasible, necessitating robust post-generation hallucination detection.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current benchmarks often focus solely on factual errors in text, lacking systematic evaluation of faithfulness hallucinations in agent trajectories. Furthermore, they typically provide only sample-level labels without fine-grained annotations for localization, correction, or attribution, limiting the ability to diagnose model reliability in complex scenarios.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed HaluCheck**: A large-scale, bilingual benchmark comprising over 42k instances, covering both factual hallucinations in text and faithfulness hallucinations in agent trajectories, with multi-level fine-grained annotations for detection, localization, correction, and attribution.
- **Challenge 1: Difficulty in Fine-Grained Localization and Correction**. The challenge lies in requiring models to not only detect errors but also precisely locate and correct them, demanding high semantic understanding and knowledge boundary awareness. HaluCheck addresses this by providing controlled perturbations and fine-grained labels, exposing significant gaps in current models and offering precise signals for training and evaluation.
- **Challenge 2: Complexity of Hallucination Detection in Agent Scenarios**. This is difficult because agent hallucinations involve consistency across tool calls, planning logic, and context, where errors can lead to unsafe actions. HaluCheck introduces faithfulness hallucination evaluation in agent trajectories, revealing severe deficiencies in context integration and process monitoring, thus filling a critical gap in existing evaluations.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Data Construction**: HaluCheck consists of HaluText (text hallucinations) and Agent Perturbation modules. Data is generated using strategies like numerical deviation, detail invention, and planning errors, covering diverse tasks and perturbation degrees.
- **Evaluation Results**: Experiments on 14 LLMs show that while models possess basic sample-level detection capabilities, they struggle significantly with fine-grained localization, correction, and agent hallucination detection. Stronger models demonstrate better uncertainty expression (self-awareness) but fail to fully resolve the issues.
- **Comparison & Analysis**: Further analysis indicates that Chain-of-Thought (Thinking), web search, and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) offer partial improvements, but none completely solve the problem. Compared to prior works focusing only on factual accuracy, HaluCheck highlights substantial performance bottlenecks in agent interaction and fine-grained error correction.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper presents HaluCheck, a large-scale bilingual benchmark designed to systematically evaluate and advance post-generation hallucination detection in LLMs. By incorporating multi-level annotations for both textual factual hallucinations and agent faithfulness hallucinations, the study reveals major deficits in current LLMs regarding fine-grained localization, correction, and agent scenario reliability. Although techniques like reasoning, search, and fine-tuning provide some benefits, reliable multi-dimensional hallucination detection remains an open challenge, with HaluCheck serving as a solid foundation for future research and diagnosis.
