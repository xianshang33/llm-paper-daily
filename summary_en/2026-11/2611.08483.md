## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Many problems in mathematical discovery and scientific computing can be framed as program search tasks. Recently, LLM-guided evolutionary search frameworks (e.g., FunSearch, AlphaEvolve) have made significant progress by iteratively refining and combining high-scoring solutions.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current LLM-guided frameworks are often slow and expensive. This is primarily because each agent must generate a long textual chain of thought for every iteration, and maintaining population diversity requires repeating this generation process many times, leading to high computational costs and low efficiency.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed SwarmEvolve**: A framework where hundreds of low-cost agents per round operate in swarms, reasoning in latent space without decoding to text, and sharing information by passing KV caches.
- **Challenge 1: Inefficiency and High Latency due to Text Generation**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional text-based evolutionary search requires LLMs to generate verbose chains of thought token-by-token, which is time-consuming and limits the number of solutions explored per unit of time.
  - **Solution**: SwarmEvolve enables agents to reason directly in latent space, bypassing text decoding. Agents exchange information via KV caches, significantly increasing throughput and search speed.
- **Challenge 2: Maintaining Diversity and Effective Crossover in High-Speed Search**
  - **Difficulty**: Rapid iteration risks converging to local optima or producing duplicate solutions, while traditional text-based crossover operations struggle to efficiently merge ideas from multiple agents.
  - **Solution**: A novelty filter retains the most distinct ideas by measuring distances between agents' hidden state vectors. Additionally, a summarizer agent receives combined KV caches from all swarm members, allowing idea crossover to occur naturally in latent space without complex text reconstruction.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on 21 problems in mathematical discovery and scientific computing, using Qwen3 8B on a single GPU.
- **Baselines**: Compared against OpenEvolve, ShinkaEvolve, CodeEvolve, and AlphaEvolve (using frontier LLMs).
- **Key Results**:
  - **Speed & Efficiency**: SwarmEvolve reached the best scores achieved by other state-of-the-art frameworks in 60 minutes within a median of only 3 to 8 minutes.
  - **Performance**: At the 15-minute mark, SwarmEvolve achieved higher mean best scores than all baselines.
  - **Solution Quality**: On several problems, SwarmEvolve with the smaller Qwen3 8B model improved upon solutions initially found by AlphaEvolve using larger, frontier LLMs.
  - **Throughput**: SwarmEvolve generated significantly more programs than other methods in the same timeframe (e.g., 734 vs. 52 in combinatorics).

## 4. Conclusion
SwarmEvolve overcomes the speed and cost limitations of traditional LLM-guided evolutionary search by enabling agent swarms to communicate and reason in latent space. By leveraging KV cache sharing and novelty filtering based on hidden states, it achieves efficient idea crossover and diversity maintenance. The results demonstrate that this approach offers a fast, low-cost, and high-performance solution for program search in scientific domains, outperforming existing methods even with smaller models.
