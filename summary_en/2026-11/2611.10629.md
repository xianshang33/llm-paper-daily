## What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed as autonomous agents for first-principles materials simulations, such as Density Functional Theory (DFT). Existing systems typically wrap models in complex scaffolds (structured APIs, retrieved knowledge, verifier loops) and evaluate performance solely based on final report scores.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current studies often report system performance in isolation, lacking controlled comparisons to quantify the actual gain of "engineering scaffolds" versus "directly prompting frontier models." Furthermore, traditional benchmarks focus only on task outcomes, ignoring "provenance"—a critical aspect in physics—failing to ensure that high scores result from genuine computational execution, leading to a potential divergence between outcome and process.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed DFT-Agent Bench**: A benchmark comprising 200+ Quantum ESPRESSO (QE) tasks spanning relaxation, electronic structure, equation-of-state, and elastic-tensor pipelines, equipped with QE-derived ground truth and public-reference cross-checks.
- **Challenge 1: How to quantify the true performance lift provided by scaffolds?**
  - **Difficulty**: Different systems use varying models and engineering setups, making it hard to isolate model capability from engineering optimization.
  - **Solution**: Conducted a controlled comparison under a unified execution harness, contrasting a near-bare ReAct baseline with a bundled v5.1 scaffold across two models (DeepSeek-v4-pro and Claude-Opus-4-8). This isolated the specific gains from scaffolding, showing significant improvements in bands+DOS (+0.08/+0.11) and d-electron vc-relax (+0.17).
- **Challenge 2: How to ensure the authenticity and traceability of evaluation results?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional scoring rules can be exploited by agents that generate plausible reports without performing actual calculations, meaning high scores do not necessarily reflect correct physical computation processes.
  - **Solution**: Introduced a "provenance-audited scorer" that performs a four-flag execution audit. If the required QE execution contract is not met (e.g., incomplete or unmarked executions), the score is set to zero regardless of the rubric score, ensuring the evaluation reflects genuine computational workflows.

## Implementation, Deployment, Evaluation Results, and Comparison
- **Implementation & Deployment**: Developed DFT-Agent Bench with 200+ QE tasks across multiple material tiers. Implemented a provenance audit mechanism as an execution contract check. Released frozen diagnostic slices, prompts, scorers, and solver revisions.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - **Scaffold Effects**: The complete scaffold improved performance on both models for bands+DOS and d-electron vc-relax. Gains were minimal for high-scoring relaxation and equation-of-state tasks.
  - **Model Dependency**: Elastic tensor results were model-dependent; DeepSeek-v4-pro improved from 0.18 to 0.40, while Claude-Opus-4-8 slightly decreased from 0.62 to 0.59.
  - **Fixed Analysis**: In a fixed 100-task analysis with retrieval disabled, DeepSeek gained +0.17 and Opus gained +0.10 against a historical pool baseline (p < 0.001).
  - **Provenance Audit**: Successfully caught incomplete or unmarked executions that would have received high rubric scores, demonstrating the limitations of outcome-only scoring.
- **Comparison**: Unlike general agent benchmarks, this study emphasizes the importance of provenance in physics. Unlike isolated system reports, it provides a decoupled analysis of scaffold versus model choices.

## Summary
This paper reveals through DFT-Agent Bench that engineering scaffolds significantly help specific low-baseline tasks in autonomous DFT workflows but offer diminishing returns on high-baseline tasks. Crucially, it highlights the necessity of "provenance auditing" in evaluating scientific computing agents, pointing out that relying solely on outcome scores can lead to misjudgment of an agent's true capabilities. The study provides a more rigorous benchmark and methodology for evaluating LLM agents in materials science.
