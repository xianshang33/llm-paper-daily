## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Much of the automation assigned to LLM agents is procedural, requiring strict adherence to a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) defined at the start, despite individual steps requiring low intelligence.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current approaches rely on restating instructions or resuming stopped agents. However, agents inevitably drift from procedures over long runs. Restating instructions merely shifts the drift to another shortcut rather than solving the underlying fidelity issue in long-horizon tasks.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed an Executable Finite-State Machine (FSM)**: This mechanism keeps workflow state outside the model's context and enforces it at three critical points: tool calls, state transitions, and the end of the agent's turn.
- **Challenge 1: Procedure Drift in Long-Horizon Runs**
  - **Difficulty**: As interactions extend, LLMs tend to ignore initial SOPs, taking shortcuts or terminating early due to context limits. This drift is cumulative and resistant to simple prompt engineering.
  - **Solution**: The FSM acts as an external runtime controller, decoupling state management from the model's memory. By hard-constraining state transitions at key checkpoints, it eliminates drift regardless of the model's internal tendencies.
- **Challenge 2: Balancing Capability and Cost for Small Models**
  - **Difficulty**: Complex long-horizon logic is typically assumed to require large, expensive models, as smaller models often fail due to insufficient reasoning or context management.
  - **Solution**: Since the FSM provides long-horizon reliability via runtime enforcement rather than model capability, smaller, cheaper models (e.g., Claude Haiku 4.5) can effectively handle procedural work. The method allows small models to outperform larger ones at a fraction of the cost.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Pre-registered experiments on SOP-Bench involving batches of 200 accounts. Baselines included providing SOP only in the first message, adding SOP reminders per message, and auto-continue mechanisms.
- **Key Results**:
  - Claude Haiku 4.5 with FSM correctly and procedurally decided on an average of 139 accounts, compared to 48 for the strongest baseline (95% CI of difference [46, 130]).
  - The FSM maintained consistent accuracy throughout the run, showing no late-stage degradation.
  - Performance gains correlated with the degree of drift in plain agents, confirming the FSM's effectiveness in suppressing drift.
- **Cross-Model and Cross-Benchmark Validation**:
  - Analyses on SWE-bench Verified, $\tau^2$-bench, and staged/cross-context workflows suggest that reliable long-horizon automation stems from stronger execution control, not just stronger models.
  - Cost-efficiency: Haiku 4.5 + FSM averaged more correct decisions than plain Claude Sonnet 4.6 at only 38% of the cost.

#### Summary
This paper addresses the inherent instruction drift in LLM agents during long-horizon procedural tasks, demonstrating that traditional prompt optimization is insufficient. The authors propose an Executable FSM framework that externalizes workflow state and enforces constraints at critical nodes, achieving high instruction fidelity. Experiments show that this approach not only significantly improves task completion rates but also enables low-cost small models to outperform high-cost large models, highlighting the importance of "strong execution control" in building reliable AI agents and offering a new path for reducing automation costs.
