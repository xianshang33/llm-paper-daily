## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Runtime anomaly detectors for Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS) are typically either black-box models learned from normal data or hand-derived from physical models. The former misses stealthy attacks that keep signals within normal ranges, while the latter requires significant expert effort for each specific plant.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Data-driven methods (e.g., autoencoders) model statistical correlations without physical constraints, failing to detect statistically plausible but physically impossible attacks (e.g., replay, offset) and lacking fault localization. Physics-based methods address these issues but are bottlenecked by the need for manual derivation and tuning of residual equations by engineers.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Physics-Informed Agents (PIA)**: A framework leveraging Large Language Model (LLM) agents to automatically write, evaluate, and refine physical residual detectors, producing compact, interpretable, and efficient runtime anomaly detection systems.
- **Challenge 1: How to automatically generate physically valid detection logic without expert intervention?**
  - **Difficulty**: Generating accurate physical equations directly from natural language descriptions is error-prone and may result in physically invalid or numerically unstable expressions.
  - **Solution**: PIA leverages the LLM's inherent knowledge of hydraulics, mechanics, and control logic to propose physical residuals (e.g., pump characteristics, mass balances). It iteratively tests and refines these candidates against normal operating data via an evaluation workbench to ensure physical validity and numerical stability.
- **Challenge 2: How to ensure the detector captures stealthy attacks while minimizing false positives?**
  - **Difficulty**: Purely physics-based descriptions may ignore operational noise, leading to false alarms, while data-only approaches miss stealthy manipulations.
  - **Solution**: A "hypothesis-testing" mechanism is introduced where the agent observes candidate performance on unseen normal data and reaction to synthetic manipulations. Through iterative refinement, the detector is optimized to remain silent on normal data while covering all sensors/actuators, achieving high sensitivity to stealthy attacks.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The resulting detector consists of compact, human-readable residual equations. Deployment does not require the LLM; it executes mathematical calculations in milliseconds and explicitly identifies the component responsible for each alarm.
- **Evaluation Results**: On the BATADAL water distribution attack benchmark, PIA (using Claude Opus 5.5) achieved an F1 score of 0.878 on test attacks.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: This significantly outperforms the strongest of six statistical and deep learning baselines, which achieved an F1 of 0.504. PIA effectively detects attacks concealed by replayed and offset sensor readings and provides component-level attribution for every detected attack. Ablation studies show that agents relying solely on plant descriptions lose up to 0.28 F1, highlighting the critical role of data-driven validation.

## 4. Conclusion
PIA successfully addresses the limitations of black-box models (uninterpretability, missed stealthy attacks) and high costs of manual physical modeling in CPS anomaly detection. By combining LLM-based physical reasoning with data-driven validation, it achieves automated, interpretable, and efficient runtime anomaly detection, significantly outperforming existing baselines and offering a new paradigm for industrial control system security.
