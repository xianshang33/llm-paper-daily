## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Embodied intelligence systems require fast and reliable inference for real-time perception, reasoning, and control. Advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have significantly enhanced agent capabilities.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Deploying these large models is challenging due to limited computational resources and energy budgets on robotic platforms. While collaborative inference (partitioning models across devices, edge, and cloud) is promising, determining the optimal partitioning point remains an open problem. Existing solutions fail to adequately address the dynamic interplay between wireless network conditions, model structures, and privacy requirements, lacking a systematic framework for 6G environments.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed an Edge-Centric Collaborative Inference Framework**: The framework uses the edge server to coordinate model partitioning and execution, optimizing performance for embodied intelligence in 6G networks.
- **Challenge 1: Determining optimal partition points under dynamic network conditions**. It is difficult because wireless channel fluctuations, intermediate data sizes, and heterogeneous hardware capabilities are tightly coupled, rendering static strategies ineffective. The paper addresses this by conducting simulations and real-world experiments over WiFi and 5G, demonstrating that optimal strategies vary dynamically and identifying latency-minimizing partition points.
- **Challenge 2: Lack of empirical benchmarks for modern embodied AI models**. Most prior work focuses on traditional CNNs, lacking evaluation of recent VLA and LLM models on embedded hardware. The paper performs detailed collaborative inference experiments for ResNet-101, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and OpenVLA-7B on NVIDIA Jetson Nano/AGX Xavier devices and an AGX Thor edge server, establishing best practices for these specific hardware combinations.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: A testbed was built using terminal devices (NVIDIA Jetson Nano, AGX Xavier) and an edge server (AGX Thor) connected via WiFi and 5G. The system implements model split inference with edge-coordinated scheduling.
- **Evaluation Results**: Experiments show that optimal partitioning strategies significantly depend on network bandwidth, latency jitter, and model architecture. Specific partition layers minimizing end-to-end latency were identified for Qwen2.5-VL-7B and OpenVLA-7B. The results demonstrate that collaborative inference meets 3GPP TS 22.261 low-latency requirements (<10ms or 10-100ms) more effectively than pure local or cloud execution.
- **Comparison**: Compared to static splitting or single-device execution, the proposed framework dynamically adjusts to real-time network states, effectively balancing computational load and communication overhead, especially in high-bandwidth, low-latency 5G/6G scenarios.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper reviews the state of collaborative inference for embodied intelligence and proposes an edge-centric framework to address resource constraints and network dynamics in the 6G era. Extensive experiments on real hardware and networks validate the effectiveness of dynamic model partitioning, providing specific deployment guidelines for mainstream models like ResNet, Qwen2.5-VL, and OpenVLA. The article concludes by discussing future directions for collaborative inference in 6G-empowered embodied intelligence, emphasizing the deep integration of networking and AI.
