## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are evolving from passive Q&A systems into autonomous agents capable of planning, tool use, and executing actions within clinical systems. In high-stakes fields like radiology, these agents are proposed for workflows such as protocol optimization and report generation. However, unlike incorrect answers which can be ignored, erroneous actions by agents write false information into medical records, posing significant patient safety risks.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Previous studies primarily focused on information retrieval accuracy or static response evaluation, lacking comprehensive assessments of safety during the "action execution" phase. Furthermore, while indirect prompt injection is a known risk, there is a lack of systematic, multi-layered empirical evaluation on how to quantify and mitigate severe violations when agents perform critical operations like modifying medical records in realistic workflow simulations.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a**: Multi-model evaluation framework for layered safeguarding of agentic AI in radiology workflows, systematically comparing three protection strategies: permissions-only, deterministic rules, and rules combined with a locally run LLM checker.
- **Challenge 1: Agents prone to generating clinically relevant false or unsupported information during high-risk actions, where single-layer protections are insufficient.**
  - **Difficulty**: Agents may execute incorrect writes due to context misunderstanding or logical errors that simple permission controls cannot detect at the content level.
  - **Solution**: The paper introduces a layered safeguard mechanism, specifically adding a local LLM checker on top of deterministic rules. This checker performs a secondary verification of the agent's intent and content before action execution, significantly reducing the rate of high-severity violations.
- **Challenge 2: Agents are susceptible to misleading information (e.g., indirect prompt injection), leading to hallucinations that are difficult to distinguish as "misjudgment" rather than "missed information."**
  - **Difficulty**: Even with safeguards, agents may open tampered documents and cite erroneous data. Traditional blocking mechanisms may either over-block valid actions or fail to catch subtle judgment errors.
  - **Solution**: By constructing synthetic scenarios with misleading information variants and running large-scale simulations (1638 episodes), the study analyzes agent document access behaviors and error attribution. It reveals that errors stem mainly from misjudgment of available information, quantifying the limitations of current safeguards and highlighting areas for improvement.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The study used a simulation design with 48 radiologist-reviewed base scenarios (covering finding communication, tumor board prep, and follow-up), 43 of which had misleading information variants (91 total). Six advanced LLM agents (Claude Sonnet 5.5, GPT-6.1-Sol, GLM-5.3, MiniMax M3, Gemma-4-31B, Qwen3.8-27B) were tested under three conditions: permissions-only, deterministic rules, and rules + local LLM checker, totaling 1638 episodes.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - **Baseline Risk**: With permissions-only, 7.3%-27.1% of episodes had high-severity violations (pooled average 19.4%).
  - **Rule-based Protection**: Adding deterministic rules reduced violations to 16.7% (-2.8 percentage points; statistically weak significance).
  - **Layered Protection (Rules + LLM Checker)**: Violations further dropped to 13.2% (-6.2 points vs baseline, 95% CI: -9.5, -3.0; -3.5 points vs rules-only, 95% CI: -6.3, -0.7), showing statistically significant improvement.
  - **Model Differences**: No significant difference was detected between agents (P = .43), and task success rates were not noticeably decreased.
  - **Misleading Scenario Analysis**: In misleading variants, agents opened the altered document in 83% of cases, and 58% of erroneous statements cited it. Most blocked actions (64%-68%) were false positives (would not have caused a violation), indicating a high over-blocking rate.
- **Comparison**: Unlike prior work focusing on output accuracy, this study emphasizes risks in the "action" phase. It demonstrates that while layered safeguards significantly reduce risk, they do not eliminate errors, particularly those arising from misjudgment rather than information omission.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper evaluates the safety of agentic AI in radiology workflows through large-scale simulations. It shows that layered safeguards, especially those incorporating a local LLM checker, significantly reduce high-severity violations but do not prevent them entirely. Errors in the presence of misleading information are primarily due to misjudgment rather than failure to access information. This suggests future safety mechanisms should focus on enhancing agents' ability to judge source credibility, rather than relying solely on external rule-based blocking, while balancing safety with operational efficiency to minimize false positives.
