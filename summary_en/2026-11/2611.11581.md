## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: In tool-using LLM agents, when a tool call fails, the model must decide whether to retry, correct, or stop. However, identical error feedback text can lead to drastically different recovery behaviors depending on its placement within the conversation context (e.g., retained in tool messages vs. presented as new user input).
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Prior research primarily focuses on the content of error feedback or generic retry policies, overlooking the critical impact of "Delivery" (how the error is presented, including history retention and turn structure). Error text alone is insufficient for guiding correct model responses, and there lacks a systematic study isolating how delivery mechanisms independently affect recovery.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A controlled "delivery design space" for tool call errors and an optional deterministic inference engine recovery layer that optimizes agent error recovery by adjusting error placement, guidance, history retention, and turn structure.
- **Challenge 1**: Inconsistent model behavior caused by the contextual position of identical error texts (e.g., Llama models exhibiting severe stopping failures when failed exchanges are removed).
  - **Difficulty & Solution**: It is difficult to distinguish content issues from structural issues. The paper conducts two pre-registered studies with deterministic reruns across 13 open models, finding that a new user turn retaining the failed exchange effectively avoids stopping failures, proving delivery form is a key factor independent of content.
- **Challenge 2**: Lack of a unified and efficient error recovery mechanism, as generic retry strategies fail to adapt to all error types and model scales.
  - **Difficulty & Solution**: Different models respond differently to history rewriting vs. new user turns. The proposed recovery layer serves as a non-inferior alternative to history rewriting, significantly improving success rates on the ToolMaze benchmark, demonstrating that delivery optimization is a distinct factor rather than a universal fix.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The intervention is implemented as an opt-in recovery layer within a deterministic inference engine, which infers failures from flags and text to transform messages. This can also be executed by clients or gateways.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - **ToolMaze Benchmark**: Success rates on explicit errors increased by 14.2 percentage points for 7-8B models and 10.5 percentage points for 32-70B models.
  - **Loud/Quiet Benchmark**: No measurable gain was observed in 24 multi-turn tasks involving visible failures and silent corruption, scored by final-state agreement.
  - **Comparison**: The method of using a new user turn with retained failed exchanges is non-inferior to history rewriting in pooled synthetic comparisons but is not optimal for every model. Performance is further improved if the client provides exact fault types.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Unlike previous works focusing solely on feedback content or generic retries, this study isolates the "delivery" variable, proving it is an independent factor influencing recovery, outperforming simple repetition or blind stopping.

#### Summary
This paper reveals that "error delivery method" is a key independent factor influencing error recovery behavior in LLM agents. By systematically varying how error information is presented in the dialogue, the authors propose a lightweight inference engine recovery layer. Experiments show that this method significantly improves error handling success rates for both small and large models on specific benchmarks (like ToolMaze), particularly in preventing premature stopping due to missing context. The study emphasizes that error recovery should not rely solely on engine-exclusive solutions or universal retry policies but should be optimized in conjunction with specific delivery forms.
