## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Building an automated portfolio requires a policy that connects market information to positions and orders, considering stock selection, cash reserves, and rebalancing jointly. While Large Language Models (LLMs) can provide company and macroeconomic assessments, integrating these unstructured insights into strict capital allocation policies remains challenging.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Recent LLM trading agents (e.g., TradingAgents, FinAgent) focus on analytical roles, tools, and reflection, or treat language merely as a prediction signal. They often lack explicit rules for combining multiple analyst reports into a shared portfolio budget and execution policy, resulting in opaque connections between analysis and capital allocation.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Hybrid Multi-Agent Trading System**: Combines LLM analysts (company news and macroeconomic) with momentum-based portfolio construction and rule-based execution.
- **Challenge 1: How to translate unstructured LLM analysis into executable investment weights?**
  - **Difficulty**: LLM outputs are ambiguous and risky for direct trading, making integration with traditional quantitative factors difficult.
  - **Solution**: Introduced a rule-based Coordinator that receives structured reports, uses momentum rankings for main allocation patterns, and applies composite scores for minor adjustments, explicitly defining which fields can alter stock weights or total exposure.
- **Challenge 2: How to evaluate the actual contribution of LLMs in a real trading loop versus randomness?**
  - **Difficulty**: Financial markets are noisy, making it hard to distinguish LLM-driven alpha from market beta, and real-time live testing is costly and risky.
  - **Solution**: Employed a broker-recorded paper account for forward testing and retrospective replays to compare configurations (same-session vs. deferred assessment), using deterministic fallbacks to ensure reproducibility and isolated variable analysis.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The system features company news and macroeconomic agents returning structured reports to a Coordinator. The Coordinator sets stock weights and cash targets using numerical factors. The execution layer applies versioned rebalancing rules. The system operated a broker-recorded paper account from March 11 to August 28, 2026.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - The account achieved a 34.5% return, compared to 14.4% for the S&P 500 ETF (SPY) over the same period.
  - This raw return advantage came with a larger maximum drawdown.
  - In retrospective replays, reconstructed policies outperformed matched SPY and QQQ benchmarks under both same-session and next-session assessment rules.
- **Comparisons & Design Analysis**:
  - LLM-enabled configurations showed excess returns compared to momentum-only or random baselines.
  - Design comparisons examined cash authority, selective invocation, and response contracts. Findings indicated that constant-cash control yields return estimates close to stance-based sizing, and shorter report contracts improve parsing observability but may alter recommendations.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper proposes and validates a hybrid trading architecture integrating LLM semantic analysis with traditional momentum strategies and rule-based execution. By explicitly defining mapping rules from analysis reports to capital allocation, the system significantly outperformed major indices in the test window, albeit with higher volatility. The study highlights the critical role of explicit integration policies (such as cash management and report structure constraints) in stabilizing performance and ensuring interpretability in LLM trading systems, providing empirical evidence of LLMs' value in financial decision-making loops.
