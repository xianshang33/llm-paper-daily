## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: LLM agents are increasingly operating in long-horizon, verifier-rich environments, requiring the ability to learn reusable knowledge from experience. Current self-improvement mechanisms often write lessons derived from single episodes directly into memory.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Existing methods lack principled criteria for assessing the transferability, retention value, and commitment risk of updates. Local success is an unreliable signal, leading to overgeneralization (failure in superficially similar but semantically different contexts), conflicts with previously successful behaviors, and the accumulation of stale or harmful persistent memory.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Falsification-Gated Self-Evolution (FGSE)**: A verifier-grounded framework that represents each candidate lesson as a structured hypothesis with explicit preconditions, behavior changes, expected effects, and verifiers.
- **Challenge 1: How to ensure new knowledge is truly transferable rather than a result of accidental success?**
  - **Difficulty**: Success in a single episode may be coincidental; direct memorization leads to failure in new tasks.
  - **Solution**: FGSE applies hypotheses only in a temporary state and validates them via "target-transfer probes" on related but distinct tasks, ensuring generalization before considering commitment.
- **Challenge 2: How to prevent new knowledge from disrupting existing successful behaviors (catastrophic forgetting/regression) and avoid harmful updates?**
  - **Difficulty**: New rules may conflict with old ones or produce harmful outcomes in edge cases, which traditional methods struggle to detect before writing.
  - **Solution**: FGSE introduces "falsification probes" to find counterexamples and "archived-regression probes" to check for negative impacts on historical successes. It decides to commit, refine, or reject the hypothesis based on measured gain and risk.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on web navigation (WebArena), app operation (AppWorld), tool use ($\tau$-Retail, $\tau$-Airline), and long-term memory benchmarks.
- **Key Results**:
  - **Performance**: FGSE achieved strong results, including a 39.8% success rate on WebArena, 78.8% average completion on AppWorld, and Pass$^1$ scores of 0.724 and 0.487 on $\tau$-Retail and $\tau$-Airline, respectively.
  - **Safety & Stability**: Compared to direct memory writing, FGSE significantly reduced risks: falsification failures dropped to 5.0%, regression damage to 1.3%, and harmful committed updates to 3.6%.
- **Comparison**: The results demonstrate that treating memory updates as hypotheses and subjecting them to rigorous testing supports more reliable long-term adaptation than existing direct reflection or self-feedback mechanisms, effectively mitigating overgeneralization and regression.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses the reliability issue in long-term memory updates for LLM agents by proposing the FGSE framework. By converting experiential lessons into structured hypotheses and subjecting them to transfer, falsification, and regression tests before committing to persistent state, FGSE effectively balances learning and safety. Experiments show that this method not only improves task success rates across complex benchmarks but also significantly reduces the risk of harmful updates and performance regression, offering a new paradigm for safe self-evolution in agents.
