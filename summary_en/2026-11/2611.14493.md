## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Deploying LLM-based analytics agents in enterprise settings requires evaluation frameworks capable of reliably detecting failures in complex, multi-tool workflows. Current methods often rely on manual spot-checks or simple heuristics, which struggle with the stochastic nature and complexity of agents.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: There is a lack of systematic comparison among three major evaluation paradigms (trace-based LLM judging, text-only LLM judging with red-teaming, and deterministic heuristics) regarding stability, known risk detection, and unknown risk discovery. Manual checks often miss "silent failures" (syntactically valid but semantically wrong), and the impact of information availability on evaluation quality remains unclear.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a three-phase comparative study**: Systematically evaluated Strands Evals (trace-based), PromptFoo (text-only + red-teaming), and Agenta (deterministic heuristics) on two supply chain analytics agents.
- **Challenge 1: Evaluation Stability**. LLM stochasticity can cause fluctuating evaluation results. **Solution**: Quantified stability through repeated measurements, finding that 97% of configurations met the stability threshold, proving the reliability of the evaluation harnesses independent of the specific agent.
- **Challenge 2: Precision-Recall Balance in Risk Detection**. Text-only outputs lack context, leading to high false alarm rates. **Solution**: Demonstrated that trace-based evaluation leverages execution context to achieve the best F1 score (0.90), while text-only evaluation generated 5x more false alarms despite matching recall, highlighting the critical role of contextual information.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Used frozen execution traces in a controlled setting to evaluate two analytics agents.
- **Stability Results**: 97% of configurations achieved stability thresholds, indicating high consistency.
- **Detection Capability**: On 72 ground-truth test cases, the trace-based framework (Strands Evals) performed best with an F1 of 0.90. The text-only framework (PromptFoo) had 5x more false positives. Deterministic heuristics (Agenta) performed poorly. Notably, the evaluators corrected 9 ground-truth failures initially missed by human reviewers.
- **Unknown Risk Discovery**: Uncovered 24 novel failures with zero overlap between functional testing and security red-teaming, demonstrating their complementarity.
- **Comparison Conclusion**: LLM-based harnesses surface subtle errors missed by humans; information availability dictates evaluation quality; functional and security evaluations are complementary.

#### Summary
The paper establishes the superiority of trace-based LLM judging for functional assessment and the necessity of adversarial red-teaming for security validation through a rigorous three-phase study. It recommends a dual-framework strategy: using trace-based evaluation for functional accuracy and adversarial red-teaming for security, ensuring comprehensive reliability and safety for enterprise-grade analytics agents.
