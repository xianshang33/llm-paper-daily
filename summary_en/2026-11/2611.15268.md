## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Small open-source models (e.g., Gemma 4 12B) are suitable for private customer service deployments due to data privacy constraints but lag significantly behind frontier models in adhering to written policies and maintaining behavioral consistency.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current approaches either evolve the "harness" (instructions/rules) or train weights via Reinforcement Learning (RL). However, most hybrid methods rely on stronger teacher models (e.g., Claude Opus, GPT-5.5) to generate rule edits. Furthermore, gains from harness evolution often fail to generalize to held-out tasks, and small models remain inconsistent even when provided with correct rules.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Teacher-Free Co-Evolution Framework for LLM Agents**: Enables a base model to simultaneously evolve its external knowledge carrier (Harness/Skills) and internal weights (LoRA) without assistance from a stronger model.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of specific policy knowledge leads to unsolvable tasks**. Difficulty: The model does not know what rules it is missing. Solution: The base model acts as an "editor" to diagnose failed conversations, generates new rules, and subjects them to rigorous gating (code checks, counterexample self-review, retesting, and noise-calibrated validation) to ensure only high-quality knowledge is added.
- **Challenge 2: Inconsistent execution of known knowledge reduces reliability**. Difficulty: In-context learning alone is insufficient for stable behavior. Solution: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is used to train a LoRA adapter on multi-turn conversations with tools and rules, internalizing correct behaviors into weights to enhance consistency.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Uses Gemma 4 12B as the base, reading domain policies from on-demand skills. Each round involves serving fresh tasks, diagnosing failures to update rules, and performing GRPO fine-tuning. No training or selection is performed on official test sets.
- **Results on $\tau^2$-bench (Retail & Airline domains)** after two rounds:
    - **Pass@1**: Improved by 12.5 points (Retail) and 19.5 points (Airline).
    - **Pass@4**: Improved by 18.4 points (Retail) and 22.0 points (Airline).
- **Comparison with Related Work**:
    - Outperformed the reference agent (same model with full policy in system prompt) by 11.8/8.0 points (Pass@1) and 22.8/16.0 points (Pass@4).
    - Closed most of the performance gap to three frontier models under the same protocol.
    - Ablation studies showed that rules rescue previously unsolvable tasks (providing new knowledge), while trained weights make solved tasks reliable (improving consistency), demonstrating complementary effects.

#### Summary
This paper demonstrates that small open-source models can effectively leverage their own deployment logs to improve performance through a "Knowledge in the Harness, Reliability in the Weights" co-evolution mechanism, without needing a stronger teacher model. By addressing knowledge gaps via automatic rule generation and inconsistency via reinforcement learning, the method significantly narrows the gap with frontier models while maintaining data privacy, offering a viable path for self-improving private LLM deployments.
