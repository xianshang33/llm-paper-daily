## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Oral exams are effective for assessing students' reasoning and clarifying ambiguities through follow-up questions, and they are resistant to LLM-based cheating. However, traditional oral exams suffer from poor scalability (examiner time costs grow linearly with enrollment) and low consistency (significant grading variability among and within examiners).
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current automated assessments often rely on static text or multiple-choice questions, lacking interactivity. Semi-automated approaches that enforce strict scripting to ensure consistency sacrifice the conversational flexibility that makes oral exams valuable.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a Scalable Conversational Assessment System**: An LLM-based system using a two-agent architecture to conduct interactive text-based conversations for assessing argumentation skills.
- **Challenge 1: Preventing Answer Leakage and Maintaining Independence**. The difficulty lies in single-agent systems potentially leaking answers or grading criteria during interaction. The solution is a two-agent architecture: an 'Interviewer' agent interacts with the student receiving only guidance on the next question, while a 'Hidden Evaluator' agent holds the full rubric and goals, tracking progress and directing the interviewer, thus isolating knowledge from interaction.
- **Challenge 2: Robustness Against Adversarial Strategies**. Students may use prompt injection, answer extraction, or confident fabrication to deceive the system. The paper addresses this by testing with LLM-simulated students using fictional assessment topics (to prevent pre-training knowledge leakage) and various adversarial personas, validating the system's stability under pressure.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The system was deployed in a small elective AI literacy course for both formative and summative assessments.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    1. **Simulation**: Tested against LLM personas with specific ability levels or adversarial tactics (e.g., answer-extractors, prompt-injectors). A novel aspect was using fictional topics to isolate pre-trained knowledge.
    2. **Real-world Review**: Manual and post-hoc AI review of transcripts identified areas for improving follow-up question generation and ensured grading credited only student-contributed ideas.
- **Comparison**: Unlike manual oral exams, this system scales; unlike static auto-grading, it retains interactive depth; and compared to single-agent solutions, the two-agent design significantly reduces answer leakage risks.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper presents a scalable conversational assessment system using a two-agent LLM pipeline, combining the interactive depth of oral exams with the scalability of automation. By separating 'interviewing' and 'evaluating' roles, it effectively mitigates answer leakage. Feasibility was validated through simulated adversarial environments and real classroom deployment. The study highlights the need for further optimization in follow-up question generation and accurate attribution of ideas, offering a new paradigm for large-scale personalized assessment of argumentation skills.
