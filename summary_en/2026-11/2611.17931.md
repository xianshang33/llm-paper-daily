## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: In mineral exploration, verifying geological hypotheses relies on designing various geochemical experiments. While LLM agents increasingly automate downstream geoscientific data analysis, the crucial upstream capability of automated experimental design (deciding which experiments to conduct before costly lab work) remains largely unexplored.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Evaluating this capability is difficult due to two main reasons. First, geochemical experiments exhibit high methodological diversity and subjectivity; different geologists may choose different instruments and strategies for the same hypothesis, meaning no single protocol serves as a unique reference answer. Second, open-ended experimental plans resist representation as structured labels, making objective scoring challenging.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed MineralExp**: A benchmark that reformulates open-ended geochemical experimental design as a hypothesis-based experiment selection problem, specifically a structured multi-label classification task.
- **Challenge 1: Subjectivity and Diversity of Experimental Protocols**. The difficulty lies in the lack of a single ground truth due to expert variability. The paper addresses this by abstracting concrete instrument names and procedural details into a shared taxonomy of 19 experimental types across five mineral-system processes (Source, Transport, Deposition, Modification, Preservation), yielding deterministic multi-label targets while accommodating diverse protocols.
- **Challenge 2: Difficulty in Objective Scoring of Open-Ended Plans**. The difficulty is that unstructured natural language plans are hard to quantify. The method solves this by transforming the task into multi-label classification, allowing the use of standard multi-label metrics and an LLM-as-a-Judge evidence-coverage metric for objective evaluation.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Dataset Construction**: MineralExp comprises 277 cases and 1,572 reference experimental plans, constructed from published ore-deposit literature and verified by domain experts.
- **Evaluation Setup**: The benchmark evaluates four open-source agent harnesses paired with seven frontier LLMs.
- **Results**: Current agents generally achieve high recall but low precision, indicating a tendency to propose more experiments than necessary to test the hypothesis.
- **Comparison Significance**: As the first benchmark for upstream experimental design in this domain, it fills the gap left by existing benchmarks focused on downstream data analysis, highlighting the current deficiency of LLMs in precise decision-making versus broad retrieval.

## 4. Conclusion
MineralExp successfully addresses the challenges of subjectivity and quantification in geochemical experimental design by introducing a shared taxonomy. It provides a standardized evaluation platform and reveals that current LLM agents suffer from low precision (over-designing experiments), pointing the way for future development of more cost-effective and precise scientific AI agents.
