## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are evolving into interactive agents capable of operating in digital and physical environments. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are a promising path toward embodied AGI, but real-world physical agents face high trial-and-error costs and lack of environmental controllability.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Traditional 2D games, web interfaces, or simplified simulators lack the complex visual parsing, 3D spatial navigation, and multi-stage quest progression required by modern large-scale games. Existing methods struggle to handle long-horizon tasks with finite context windows, accumulated rollout errors, and sparse rewards while maintaining real-time performance. There is also a lack of unified training and deployment frameworks supporting both 2D and complex 3D environments.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Gamer**, a generalist Vision-Language-Action foundation model framework designed for long-horizon tasks in complex interactive environments, featuring an end-to-end architecture combining perception, memory management, and multi-step reasoning.
- **Challenge 1: How to learn cross-domain environmental dynamics from massive heterogeneous interaction data while reducing redundant computation?**
  - **Difficulty**: Device-level keyboard and mouse interaction trajectories are massive and contain significant redundancy, making direct training inefficient and difficult for capturing complex decision logic.
  - **Solution**: The model undergoes continuous pre-training on tens of thousands of hours of device-level interaction data spanning simulated games and embodied environments. It then employs "Sparse-thinking instruction tuning" to substantially reduce redundant computation while preserving the reasoning capabilities needed for complex decisions.
- **Challenge 2: How to overcome finite context, accumulated errors, and sparse rewards in long-horizon interactions while achieving real-time low-latency inference?**
  - **Difficulty**: Long-sequence tasks lead to context window overflow, multi-step rollouts cause error accumulation, and sparse rewards make traditional RL unstable. Additionally, real-time gaming demands extremely low inference latency.
  - **Solution**: A stable multi-turn reinforcement learning method is used to train the policy, specifically addressing long-horizon issues. A cloud-edge inference engine is deployed to serve multiple simulated environments concurrently, supporting unbounded-context execution and maintaining low online inference latency.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: Gamer features a shared training and deployment pipeline supporting both conventional 2D GUIs and complex 3D simulated worlds. The inference stage utilizes a cloud-edge engine capable of concurrent processing of multiple game instances, enabling unbounded-context execution with low latency.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - In multiple challenging 3D open-world games (e.g., Genshin Impact, Honkai: Star Rail, Minecraft), Gamer demonstrates strong generalization, with operational efficiency comparable to skilled human players.
  - It also achieves competitive results on a range of conventional 2D game benchmarks.
- **Comparison**: Unlike agents tailored to specific games or simple environments, Gamer acts as a generalist agent, showing cross-game generalization without extensive retraining for each title. The results suggest that scaling data and algorithms on even repetitive keyboard-mouse trajectories yields substantial gains, proving the stability of learning complex skills in environments with mixed digital and physical properties.

## 4. Conclusion
Gamer is a generalist VLA foundation model for long-horizon real-time game tasks. By leveraging continuous pre-training and sparse-thinking instruction tuning, it effectively utilizes large-scale device-level interaction data. Combined with stable multi-turn reinforcement learning and efficient cloud-edge inference, Gamer addresses context limitations, error accumulation, and real-time constraints in long-sequence interactions. Experiments show that Gamer performs at a level comparable to skilled humans across various 2D and 3D games, offering a viable path toward building generalist agents capable of interacting across digital and physical worlds.
