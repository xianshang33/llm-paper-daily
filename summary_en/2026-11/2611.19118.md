## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Voice agents for phone negotiations must respond quickly, accurately understand spoken amounts, and handle user interruptions (backchannels). Current architectures are either cascaded (STT+LLM+TTS orchestrated by frameworks like Pipecat or LiveKit) or native speech models (end-to-end audio-in/audio-out like GPT-Live).
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: There is a lack of external latency measurements comparing these architectures in realistic negotiation scenarios. Cascaded systems suffer from cumulative latency and separate voice activity detection challenges, while native models are opaque. Furthermore, self-reported latency metrics often underestimate actual user-perceived wait times, and cascaded systems struggle with overlapping speech and specific entity recognition.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A rigorous external measurement comparison framework for Pipecat, LiveKit Agents (cascaded), and GPT-Live (native) in Spanish freight rate negotiation scenarios.
- **Challenge 1: Accuracy and Transparency of Latency Measurement**. It is difficult to capture true user-side wait times as internal metrics are often optimistic and opaque. The paper uses an external validated instrument to show that Pipecat and LiveKit underreport latency by 9% and 22% respectively, and quantifies the fixed network round-trip delay in GPT-Live.
- **Challenge 2: Handling Overlapping Speech and Critical Information Recognition**. Cascaded systems often misinterpret backchannels (e.g., "ok") as turn endings, leading to premature interruptions or false agreements, and have high error rates for spoken amounts. The study finds GPT-Live avoids backchannel errors entirely and achieves significantly lower amount recognition error rates (2.86%) compared to cascaded systems (~13%), demonstrating superior robustness.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Setup**: 47 recordings of a single speaker were used across 5 negotiation scenarios, with 77 calls per architecture, excluding telephone network variables.
- **Latency Results**: GPT-Live had the lowest median latency (2,444.8 ms), followed by Pipecat (2,616.0 ms) and LiveKit (3,090.0 ms). However, the advantage of GPT-Live over Pipecat was not statistically significant (confidence interval includes zero). Tool calls added 0.9-1.1s latency across all architectures, overshadowing architectural differences.
- **Recognition and Interaction Results**:
    - **Word Error Rate (WER)**: GPT-Live (1.47%) outperformed Pipecat (6.03%) and LiveKit (4.93%).
    - **Amount Error Rate**: GPT-Live (2.86%) significantly outperformed cascaded systems (12.61%-13.01%).
    - **Turn-Taking**: Default Pipecat and LiveKit frequently stopped at backchannels, incorrectly registering agreements when users said "ok" over the agent (3/15 for Pipecat, 2/15 for LiveKit), whereas GPT-Live had zero such errors.

## 4. Conclusion
The study emphasizes that latency for voice agents must be measured externally on the listener's side due to significant biases in internal metrics. While GPT-Live's absolute latency advantage is marginal, its robustness in handling overlapping speech (especially backchannels) and critical entity recognition is superior to traditional cascaded architectures. For high-reliability phone negotiation applications, native speech models or heavily safeguarded cascaded systems are necessary.
