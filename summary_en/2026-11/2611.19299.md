## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Staged LLM agents enable realistic market and social simulations by reasoning in free-form text. However, these simulations are prohibitively expensive; a single 102-day run with 200 agents requires over 203,000 LLM calls, yielding only one stochastic realization.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Conventional low-compute distillation methods discard the free-form intermediate states (e.g., daily analyses, rationales) that make LLM agents valuable. Standard distillation assumes structured outputs (labels, action distributions), while existing agent-distillation either retains small LLMs (still costly) or uses tabular/MLP surrogates that cannot capture unstructured semantic nuances.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Staged Semantic Distillation**: A low-compute method that preserves non-structured semantics in a non-LLM student model trained from a single recorded trace.
- **Challenge 1: Preserving free-form intermediate semantics in a non-LLM model.**
  - **Difficulty**: Free-form text is unstructured and rich in nuance, making it difficult to capture without expensive text generation or large models.
  - **Solution**: A frozen encoder maps each teacher output to a 1024-d vector. The student receives typed, stage-tagged vectors with stage-specific visibility, preserving semantic channels without generating text.
- **Challenge 2: Maintaining decision coherence and accuracy during autonomous deployment.**
  - **Difficulty**: In deployment, the student relies on its own previous outputs, leading to potential error accumulation, compounded by varying visibility across stages.
  - **Solution**: Joint supervision across all stages and training with "scheduled self-history" that mimics the deployment protocol, ensuring the student handles its own historical semantic vectors effectively.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Efficiency**: A 13.2M-parameter deterministic student was trained on a single 8GB GPU in 1,658 seconds. Inference requires zero LLM/decoder calls and no text generation, taking only 0.14 ms per decision.
- **Performance vs. Baselines**:
  - **Semantic Fidelity**: Cosine similarity of 0.943 with teacher analysis.
  - **Causal Validity**: Swapping semantic inputs changed 64.7% of decisions (vs. 12.8% for matched donor baseline), proving the semantic channel is live.
  - **Accuracy**: Achieved 0.8347 balanced accuracy in teacher-forced replay and 0.7783 in deployment. This represents a +2.2 point gain over no-semantic retraining.
  - **Agreement with Teacher**: Round-one decision agreement (R) with teacher's recorded analysis was 97.1%, comparable to the teacher's own re-sampling consistency.
- **Scalability**: A 200,000-decision sweep took 12 minutes with zero LLM calls, compared to 3 hours and 400k calls for the teacher.
- **Ablation Studies**: Gains in deployment are driven by scheduled self-history, while semantic content is critical at the teacher-forced operating point.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper introduces Staged Semantic Distillation, successfully addressing the challenge of retaining free-form semantic information in low-compute, non-LLM surrogate models. By leveraging frozen encoders for semantic vectorization and employing scheduled self-history training, the method creates a highly efficient and deterministic agent substitute. The surrogate maintains high semantic consistency and decision accuracy while reducing computational costs by orders of magnitude, enabling large-scale multi-agent simulations that were previously impractical. The key design insight is to invest in the deployment schedule, allowing semantic content to prove its value at the teacher-forced operating point.
