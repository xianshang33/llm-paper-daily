## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents can be organized into multi-agent markets serving as "world models," mapping macro information to emergent macro dynamics. Evaluators typically observe only macro inputs and outputs, while micro-level behaviors remain a black box.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current evaluation methods often rely on a single aggregate score, which fails to distinguish between two distinct stages of capability: "information simulation" (macro info to micro behavior) and "macro emergence" (micro behavior to macro outcome). A single score cannot identify whether failures stem from poor agent execution, structural market biases, or ineffective information processing.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a**: Soft Macro-Control method and a Two-Regime Evaluation Framework that separates and measures both capability stages using only macro observations.
- **Challenge 1: How to achieve macro guidance without intervening in individual decisions and quantify structural bias?**
  - **Difficulty**: Directly intervening in individual trades disrupts natural emergence, and it is hard to distinguish between agent execution failure and inherent market structure preferences.
  - **Solution**: Introduced a "reference path" as public data rather than trading instructions. In the "Reference-Only Regime," symmetric true and flipped references are used. The common realization level R measures capability under the reference, while the paired gap B measures structural bias.
- **Challenge 2: How to effectively evaluate information effectiveness when external information dilutes the reference signal?**
  - **Difficulty**: When external information (e.g., news, fundamentals) is injected, the reference signal is diluted, making the standalone realization level R uninterpretable.
  - **Solution**: In the "Information-Injection Regime," only the gap Delta between true and flipped references remains interpretable, serving as a metric for information effectiveness and interference.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Tested on five single-index LLM-agent markets under conditions with and without news, varying stock/cash scan ratios and technical analysis shares.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - **Macro Control**: Announcing a target path shifts the realized path toward the target. Tracking scores improved by 0.306 and 0.415 (no news) and 0.183 and 0.329 (with news) compared to no-reference baselines.
  - **Structural Bias**: Lowest structural bias (B=0.042) was observed at a 70/30 stock/cash scan ratio.
  - **Realization Peak**: Realization capability peaked at a 3:7 technical analysis share (R=0.894), significantly higher than technical-only setups (R=0.358).
  - **Information Effectiveness**: Delta was 0.0341 with fundamental data and 0.0487 with news.
- **Comparison**: Unlike prior work focusing on agent returns or risk, this study decouples capability stages, providing finer-grained diagnostics and demonstrating the limitations of single aggregate scores.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper addresses black-box LLM agent markets by proposing a novel two-regime evaluation framework that decomposes model capability into information simulation and macro emergence. By introducing soft macro-control and symmetric reference paths, the authors effectively quantify structural bias, realization capability, and information effectiveness using only macro observations. The results demonstrate that this method not only guides market dynamics but also reveals performance bottlenecks under different configurations, offering new theoretical tools and empirical insights for understanding and optimizing LLM-driven economic simulations.
