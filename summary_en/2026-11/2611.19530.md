## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) trading agents are increasingly used for stock trading, with benchmarks ranking them by returns. However, single-run returns are highly volatile due to randomness (average 5.2-point difference between seeds of the same model), making it difficult to distinguish skill from luck.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Live trading tests have small, non-repeatable samples. Traditional backtests suffer from data contamination (using historical data the model may have memorized) or short sample sizes (post-cutoff only). Crucially, many backtests match news to prices by calendar date, allowing agents to see news published after market close (look-ahead bias), and fail to decompose returns into market factor exposure (Beta) and true excess return (Alpha).

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed rta-bench-1**, a look-ahead-free benchmark framework for rigorously evaluating LLM trading agents.
- **Challenge 1: Look-ahead bias from news timing**. The difficulty lies in traditional daily matching inflating news value by including post-close headlines in same-day decisions. The paper solves this by replaying news at minute-level publication times and executing trades at the next open, eliminating temporal leakage.
- **Challenge 2: Memory contamination and ambiguous return attribution**. Long backtests risk model memory leakage, and total returns obscure the source of gains. The paper addresses this by combining "blind" and "named" ticker tests, splitting scores at the model's training cutoff to detect memory, and decomposing returns into factor exposure vs. Alpha. It also benchmarks individual decisions against random selections within the same sector and day.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: Backtests were conducted across three US market regimes (rally, slow bear, crash). Six models from four vendors were evaluated over 91 runs. The benchmark strictly checks for look-ahead bias and calculates four-factor Alpha.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - **No Significant Alpha**: None of the 91 runs showed significant four-factor Alpha.
    - **Source of Returns**: Market and style exposures accounted for a median of 78% of returns.
    - **Decision Quality**: Agents' stock picks performed no better than random choices within the same sector and day.
    - **News Utility**: While agents bought stocks with positive news, ablation studies showed similar returns without news inputs, indicating news provided no edge.
    - **Rule Adherence**: Agents reliably followed prompt rules, e.g., a cash rule reduced stock holdings by 30.3 percentage points.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Unlike KTD-Fin and OpenPM, which mask identities or check record existence, rta-bench-1 quantifies the distortion caused by news timing and validates decision quality against random baselines, highlighting the inability of existing benchmarks to separate luck from skill.

## 4. Conclusion
The paper demonstrates through the rigorous rta-bench-1 benchmark that current LLM trading agents primarily earn Beta, not Alpha. High returns are driven by exposure to market factors rather than genuine stock-picking intelligence or news processing capabilities. The authors advise developers to supply their own trading edges and report factor exposures separately from Alpha, while calling for benchmarks to replay news by publication time to ensure fair evaluation.
