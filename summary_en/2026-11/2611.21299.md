## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are advancing mathematical discovery from benchmark problems to frontier research, leading to an era of "proof abundance." The fundamental challenge is ensuring the correctness of this vast volume of AI-generated proofs and transforming them into trustworthy, machine-verified knowledge.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Direct generation methods and general-purpose coding harnesses (e.g., Codex CLI, Claude Code) struggle with research-level mathematics, particularly in Theoretical Computer Science (TCS), due to intricate quantification, complex dependencies, and strict semantic requirements, failing to ensure end-to-end reliable verification.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Turing Agent**: An agent harness designed to orchestrate end-to-end formal verification of research-level mathematics, featuring a two-scale architecture that integrates theorem and proof formalization agents.
- **Challenge 1: Faithfulness and Complexity Management**. Research-level proofs involve complex logical dependencies and subtle semantics, making direct translation prone to errors or loss of meaning. Turing Agent addresses this via structured proof decomposition and selective semantic review, breaking down complex proofs into manageable modules and aligning semantics at critical steps to ensure Lean code faithfully captures natural language claims.
- **Challenge 2: Verification Efficiency and Completeness**. Long proofs often involve numerous auxiliary lemmas and type-checking errors, causing traditional methods to stall or leave gaps (`sorry`). The framework employs parallel verification to accelerate processing and accumulates proof experience to optimize subsequent steps, automatically exposing unnecessary dependencies and filling proof gaps to achieve gap-free kernel-level verification.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Built on GLM-5.3 and DeepSeek-V4-Pro models, focusing on the TCS domain in collaboration with TCS researchers.
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - Achieved approximately 57-62% faithful formalization success on the Formal TCS Bench.
  - Reached an overall pass@1 of 83% on a TCS-focused subset of RLMEval.
  - Successfully completed formal verification for three previously unresolved TCS tasks from OpenAI's October 2026 collection, one of which required about 100 formally verified auxiliary claims.
- **Comparison**: Outperforms direct generation methods and general-purpose coding harnesses (Codex CLI, Claude Code) in handling high-difficulty, high-dependency research problems, demonstrating significantly higher verification success rates and robustness while generating reusable mathematical knowledge as a byproduct.

## 4. Conclusion
Turing Agent effectively addresses core challenges in faithfulness, validity, and completeness of AI-generated mathematical proofs through its two-scale agent architecture and structured verification strategies. It not only achieves high-success-rate formal verification for research-level TCS problems but also demonstrates the ability to discover simpler proofs and optimize existing structures, providing a trustworthy automated verification solution for the era of "proof abundance."
