## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Agent evolution typically follows a "proposer-selector" loop where a proposer generates candidate artifacts (prompts, skills, memories) and a selector accepts those with higher evaluation scores. Despite its promise, practitioners face a puzzle: methods trade wins across papers, and evolution often fails to bootstrap on certain tasks.
- **Why Existing Work Failed**: Previous research focused on designing more complex proposer systems, assuming that evaluation scores could reliably identify improvements. This premise was untested and ultimately failed because noisy evaluations could not distinguish true improvements from noise, causing the evolution process to stall regardless of the proposer's quality.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed the "Resolution Hypothesis"**: The bottleneck in agent evolution is not the failure to propose better artifacts, but the inability of noisy evaluations to reliably identify them.
- **Challenge 1: Selection Failure at the Candidate Level**. The difficulty lies in evaluation noise making the selector no better than random at distinguishing helpful candidates from harmful ones, leading to accumulated errors. The paper demonstrates through large-scale experiments that while helpful candidates exist, high-noise evaluations cause the selector to frequently retain inferior artifacts, hindering evolution.
- **Challenge 2: Unreliable Ranking at the Method Level**. The difficulty is that performance margins between different evolution methods (often just a few percentage points) are smaller than the variance between two independent runs of the same method. This means published rankings often depend on luck rather than substantive technical superiority, causing confusion in the community.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Scale**: The study covered 7 evolution methods and 6 different LLM models, involving over 10 million LLM calls, providing statistically significant empirical support.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - At the candidate level, data showed selector accuracy was near random levels, confirming that helpful candidates were discarded or harmful ones retained due to noise.
    - At the method level, analysis revealed that performance margins were negligible and obscured by evaluation noise, proving the instability of rankings in current benchmarks.
- **Comparison Conclusion**: Unlike prior work focusing on enhancing proposer capabilities, this paper argues that simply increasing the complexity of candidate generation does not solve the stagnation problem. Instead, reducing evaluation noise and improving the detectability of improvements are key to breaking the bottleneck.

#### Summary
This paper presents the "Resolution Hypothesis" through extensive empirical research, revealing that the root cause of agent evolution stagnation is the inability of noisy evaluations to reliably identify improvements, rather than a lack of proposal capability. The findings indicate that current evolution methods are severely hindered by noise in both candidate selection and algorithm ranking. This calls for a shift in the community's focus from designing complex candidate improvement systems to developing low-noise evaluation mechanisms that make true improvements detectable and accumulable, thereby advancing the development of self-improving agents.
