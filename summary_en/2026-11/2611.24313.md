## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Prompt injection is the dominant unsolved security risk for LLM agents. The community agrees that robustness must be measured against adaptive attackers rather than fixed benchmarks.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Most existing adaptive red teamers aim to maximize Attack Success Rate (ASR). However, ASR measures how often a target breaks, not the diversity of methods used. High ASR can be achieved by reusing a few effective techniques, leaving other vulnerabilities untested and creating blind spots in model hardening.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed AutoPI**: An adaptive red teaming framework designed to find many different ways to break a target, not just to break it often. It leverages PIAtlas, a taxonomy of prompt-injection techniques, to guide search and measure coverage.
- **Challenge 1: Ensuring Diversity and Comprehensive Coverage of Attack Techniques.**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional methods tend to get stuck in local optima, repeatedly using the same efficient attack patterns while ignoring other technique categories.
  - **Solution**: AutoPI generates injection attempts for every sub-technique in the taxonomy and iteratively refines failed attempts using feedback from an LLM judge, ensuring systematic exploration.
- **Challenge 2: Adapting to Evolving Threats and Different Target Models Without Training.**
  - **Difficulty**: Training-based methods are costly and slow to update with new attack vectors; black-box settings lack access to internal model gradients.
  - **Solution**: AutoPI employs a black-box, training-free design that plugs into existing benchmarks. It automatically adapts to new attack techniques as the taxonomy evolves, maintaining relevance without retraining.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: AutoPI is a black-box, training-free system that can be seamlessly integrated into existing benchmarks by replacing fixed attack templates. Code and generated attacks are open-sourced.
- **Evaluation Results**: Evaluated on AgentDojo and IPI Arena against both open-weight and proprietary models. Metrics include ASR and technique coverage (the fraction of taxonomy classes/techniques yielding at least one successful injection).
- **Comparison with Related Work**:
  - On AgentDojo, AutoPI found successful injections against Qwen3.6-35B in all six taxonomy classes and covered over 90% of its techniques.
  - Against the most robust target, Claude Haiku 5.5, AutoPI achieved 20%-50% higher ASR and 25%-400% higher class-level coverage compared to recent state-of-the-art adaptive red teamers, all without requiring training.

## 4. Conclusion
AutoPI addresses the lack of attack diversity in existing adaptive red teaming tools by introducing a taxonomy-guided mechanism (PIAtlas). It prioritizes comprehensive coverage of the vulnerability space alongside high success rates. Experiments demonstrate that AutoPI significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods in a black-box, training-free setting, providing a more thorough and coverage-aware benchmark for LLM agent security evaluation.
