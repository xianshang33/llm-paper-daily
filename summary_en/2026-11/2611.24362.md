## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents frequently interact with untrusted external data, making them vulnerable to Indirect Prompt Injection (IPI) attacks. To enhance robustness before deployment, developers employ "purple-teaming," a process combining automated red-teaming (vulnerability discovery) with adversarial training (mitigation).
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: State-of-the-art Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based red-teaming methods (e.g., RL-Hammer, PISmith) suffer from "mode collapse." While they achieve high Attack Success Rates (ASR), they repeatedly generate similar attack strategies. This lack of diversity means that subsequent adversarial training yields minimal robustness improvements, as the model is not exposed to a broad spectrum of attack vectors.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: PIPETTE, a unified prompt injection purple-teaming framework designed to effectively translate red-teaming discoveries into tangible robustness improvements for defenders.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of Diversity in Red-Teaming (Mode Collapse)**
  - **Description & Difficulty**: Existing RL red-teamers converge to a narrow set of effective but similar attack patterns, failing to cover the attack space comprehensively. This limits the generalization capability of defenses trained on these samples.
  - **Solution**: PIPETTE employs a dictionary learning-based reward mechanism. This encourages the red-teamer to explore a broader range of attack strategies during testing, generating diverse and representative adversarial examples to prevent mode collapse.
- **Challenge 2: Balancing Robustness and Utility in Adversarial Training**
  - **Description & Difficulty**: Traditional adversarial training often compromises performance on benign tasks or fails to specifically address IPI vulnerabilities, leaving models susceptible to strong adaptive attacks.
  - **Solution**: PIPETTE integrates an on-policy adversarial training pipeline tailored for IPI defense. This enables agents to resist injections while maintaining high utility on legitimate user tasks, achieving a balance between security and functionality.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Benchmarks**: Extensive evaluations were conducted on agentic benchmarks such as AgentDojo and ASB.
- **Comparison Results**:
  - **Static Attacks**: Defenders produced by PIPETTE achieved near-zero ASR against static attacks.
  - **Adaptive Attacks**: The framework demonstrated strong resilience against powerful adaptive attacks.
  - **Task Utility**: The defended agents maintained benign task utility close to that of the undefended baseline.
  - **Vs. SOTA**: Compared to RL-Hammer and PISmith, PIPETTE not only provided more diverse attack samples during red-teaming but also resulted in significantly more robust models after adversarial training, addressing the disconnect between high red-teaming ASR and actual model robustness.

## 4. Conclusion
PIPETTE addresses the gap between high attack success rates in red-teaming and actual model robustness. By introducing a dictionary learning-based diversity reward and a specialized on-policy adversarial training pipeline, it creates an efficient purple-teaming framework. This approach ensures that LLM agents are protected against indirect prompt injections without sacrificing performance on normal tasks, offering a practical solution for secure LLM agent deployment.
