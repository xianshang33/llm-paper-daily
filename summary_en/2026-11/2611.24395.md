## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: As global AI infrastructure spending surges, improving the efficiency of training and inference infrastructure is crucial for Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI). Current LLM serving engines achieve only 22–38% of their theoretically optimal end-to-end throughput, indicating significant room for optimization.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Existing benchmarks for agent-driven optimization largely focus on individual software layers (e.g., only GPU Kernels or only Engines). They fail to assess whether agents can deliver end-to-end performance gains under production-scale workloads and lack realistic deployment simulations.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed RSIBench-Infra**: A benchmark comprising 20 training and inference tasks across diverse model families and modalities, supporting deployments on up to 32 accelerators (NVIDIA GPUs and Ascend NPUs) to evaluate agent capabilities at production scale.
- **Challenge 1: Complexity and Consistency of Cross-Stack Optimization**. The difficulty lies in requiring agents to modify code across multiple layers (engine, kernel, instructions) while ensuring consistency in distributed environments. The paper addresses this by introducing an OCI image-layer submission protocol, which captures agent modifications as a single OCI layer applied with the same digest across all ranks, ensuring reproducibility and consistency.
- **Challenge 2: Disconnect Between Local Optimizations and End-to-End Gains**. Local improvements (e.g., faster kernels) do not necessarily translate to system-wide performance boosts and must meet quality constraints. The paper employs an independent evaluation environment that runs clean benchmark jobs without loading the candidate layer, using a quality-gated mechanism to measure true end-to-end performance improvements.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: Agents start from runnable baselines derived from official recipes and modify the software stack. Changes are encapsulated via an OCI image-layer protocol for reproducible deployment. Evaluation occurs in an isolated, clean environment to accurately measure end-to-end performance.
- **Results**: Across all tasks, 66.2% achieved a validated speedup of over 1.10x. This demonstrates that current agents can indeed improve production-scale training and inference performance.
- **Comparison**: Unlike KernelBench or FlashInfer-Bench (kernel-level) and VibeServe or InferenceBench (engine-level), RSIBench-Infra fills the gap in evaluating end-to-end production workloads. The results highlight that while agents show promise, identifying effective strategies and consistently translating local wins into end-to-end gains remains challenging.

## 4. Conclusion
RSIBench-Infra systematically evaluates the ability of agents to optimize training and inference infrastructure at production scale. By introducing cross-layer optimization tasks and a rigorous OCI-based evaluation protocol, it demonstrates that agents can achieve significant end-to-end acceleration (66.2% of tasks >1.1x speedup). However, the study identifies that effectively searching for optimization strategies and ensuring consistent end-to-end benefits remain key challenges for future research, providing critical insights for infrastructure efficiency in recursive self-improvement.
