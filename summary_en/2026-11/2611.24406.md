## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: High-quality causal reasoning datasets are crucial for developing models with reliable causal understanding, especially in specialized domains. However, existing datasets often suffer from incorrect annotations and structural imbalances, leading to biased predictions and limited generalization.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Traditional data cleaning methods or LLM-based judges have limitations. LLM judges rely on their own causal reasoning capabilities, potentially introducing their own errors into the cleaning process (circular reasoning) and lacking precise verification mechanisms. Fine-tuning feedback methods are costly, noisy, and unstable. Existing methods struggle to effectively repair sample-level errors and address dataset-level structural imbalances while maintaining high accuracy.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed CaDO (Causal-Engine-Guided Multi-Agent Data Optimization)**: A multi-agent framework guided by formal causal reasoning for automated diagnosis and repair of causal reasoning datasets.
- **Challenge 1: Semantic Gap and Verification Difficulty between Natural Language and Causal Logic**. Natural language descriptions are ambiguous, making direct verification error-prone. **Solution**: Introduces a Formalizer Agent to translate natural language questions into formal causal queries, minimizing semantic deviations. This makes retrieval and verification explicit and tractable, leveraging a causal engine for precise computation rather than relying on LLM probabilistic guessing.
- **Challenge 2: Multi-granularity Error Diagnosis and Data Balance Repair**. Single-dimensional checks cannot simultaneously detect sample annotation errors and dataset-wide distribution biases. **Solution**: Employs a dual-level diagnosis mechanism where independent Critic Agents identify sample-level errors, while query-type coverage and label distributions are analyzed to detect dataset-level structural imbalances. It then uses reusable repair operators grounded in formal causal semantics to correct errors, and collaboratively generates diverse, semantically consistent new samples via Explorer and Expander Agents to balance the data.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The CaDO framework consists of three key stages: formal causal reasoning, multi-level diagnosis, and automated data repair and generation. Code is publicly available.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - CaDO significantly outperforms the strongest of five data cleaning baselines in 16 out of 18 experimental settings.
    - Downstream model accuracy improves by up to 9.1%.
    - At least 95% of samples flagged by CaDO are defective, demonstrating high precision.
    - On the CLadder dataset, CaDO incorrectly modifies correct labels 92.2% less frequently than an LLM-based judge, proving its reliability and safety.
- **Comparison**: Compared to LLM Judges, CaDO avoids misjudgments caused by the model's own limitations. Compared to fine-tuning feedback methods, CaDO provides more stable and precise single-sample verification without requiring expensive training iterations to detect errors.

#### Summary
CaDO effectively addresses annotation errors and structural imbalances in causal datasets by combining the precision of formal causal engines with the flexibility of multi-agent systems. This method not only improves data quality but also significantly enhances the performance of downstream causal reasoning models, providing an automated, verifiable solution for constructing high-reliability causal datasets.
