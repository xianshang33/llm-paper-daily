## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: AI agents are increasingly expected to act as "AI scientists," requiring three progressively open-ended abilities: correctly executing given computational workflows, selecting the next measurements from a design space, and designing their own experiments. Existing benchmarks primarily evaluate the first two abilities, often within terminal environments or closed loops selecting from predefined candidates.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Evaluating the third ability (autonomous experimental design) requires reliably executing agent-designed experiments with readings grounded in real laboratory data. Existing methods either use fixed replay data (yielding no new results), rule-based simulators (lacking real-world complexity), or direct real-lab usage (prohibitively expensive and slow), thus failing to effectively assess agents in open-ended experimental design scenarios.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed TwinLab-Bench**: A benchmark for evaluating AI scientists on self-designed experiments across 52 tasks in physical sciences, life sciences, and engineering. It supports over 100 experimental action types and scores agent reports by comparing them against hidden answers derived from real laboratory records.
- **Challenge 1: How to provide a reliable experimental execution environment grounded in real-world data without the cost and slowness of physical labs?**
  - **Difficulty**: Real experiments are resource-intensive, while traditional simulators fail to capture real-world nuances and noise, leading to distorted evaluations.
  - **Solution**: Introduced the "TwinLab Agent" as the environment proxy for each task. It reads raw laboratory material records and calls calculators built from these records, virtually eliminating deviations from real records and inconsistent responses, thereby simulating a realistic lab environment.
- **Challenge 2: How to prevent models from passing tests by recalling answers from training data rather than genuine reasoning?**
  - **Difficulty**: LLMs may memorize public scientific data or paper results, measuring memory rather than scientific reasoning capabilities.
  - **Solution**: Employed closed-book probes to control for memorized answers and compared agent-generated reports against hidden, unpublished real laboratory records, ensuring the evaluation focuses on the agent's ability to design and adjust experiments based on real-time feedback.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: TwinLab-Bench covers 52 tasks from various disciplines. Each task is driven by a dedicated TwinLab Agent. The tested agent (Actor agent) proposes experimental steps, and the TwinLab Agent executes them, returning calculations or readings based on real records.
- **Evaluation Results**: The study finds that even state-of-the-art frontier models struggle with these tasks. For instance, Claude Fable 5.2 achieved an average score of only 45.0. An empirical analysis of failure modes was also conducted.
- **Comparison**: Compared to language models seeing only task descriptions, the TwinLab Agent significantly enhances the realism and consistency of environmental feedback. Unlike traditional benchmarks limited to fixed workflows or predefined choices, TwinLab-Bench is unique in assessing the high-level capability of "autonomous experimental design."

## 4. Conclusion
TwinLab-Bench addresses the challenge of evaluating AI scientists' autonomous experimental design capabilities by introducing "digital twin" agents (TwinLab Agents) based on real laboratory records. It offers a complex, realistic environment and feedback mechanism while maintaining low cost and high reproducibility. Benchmark results indicate that current leading AI models have significant room for improvement, with average scores below 50%, highlighting the benchmark's difficulty and its value in guiding future research on AI scientific agents.
