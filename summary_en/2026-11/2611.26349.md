## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: First-principles calculations, such as structural relaxation and band structure calculations, are crucial for predicting material properties but demand substantial computational resources and expertise. Typical Density Functional Theory (DFT) workflows involve costly self-consistent field iterations and eigenvalue solutions.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current machine learning methods often accelerate only individual stages of the workflow (e.g., predicting potential energy surfaces or Hamiltonians) and lack automated agents to translate natural language into complete computational workflows. Traditional methods struggle to balance accuracy, symmetry preservation, and efficiency, especially for complex materials like lanthanides, actinides, and magnetic compounds, while maintaining a high barrier to entry for users.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Perfumer**, a Large Language Model (LLM)-based agentic framework that couples Quantum ESPRESSO (QE) with two specialized predictive models (Perfumer-relax and Perfumer-band), enabling an automated "relaxation-to-band" workflow from natural language requests.
- **Challenge 1: High computational cost and local minima issues in structural relaxation.**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional DFT relaxation requires iterative energy and force evaluations, which is computationally expensive; pure ML potentials may fail far from equilibrium.
  - **Solution**: The Perfumer-relax model performs pre-relaxation to quickly provide structures close to the ground state, followed by QE refinement. This "ML pre-relaxation + DFT refinement" strategy achieved a 2.75-fold overall speedup compared to using QE alone.
- **Challenge 2: Electronic band prediction must strictly preserve physical symmetries without the overhead of Hamiltonian construction.**
  - **Difficulty**: Direct band prediction often violates reciprocal lattice periodicity and time-reversal symmetry; Hamiltonian-based methods (like DeepH) incur high costs in matrix construction.
  - **Solution**: The Perfumer-band model combines a periodic Graph Neural Network with a latent Hermitian spectral head. It returns 12 band energies at any requested wavevector without explicitly constructing the Hamiltonian, strictly preserving reciprocal lattice periodicity and time-reversal symmetry.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Dataset and Training**: The Perfumer-band model was trained on 125,153 self-curated band structures. The test set included 2,607 materials spanning 85 elements, including challenging lanthanide, actinide, and magnetic compounds, from held-out composition families.
- **Performance Metrics**:
  - **Accuracy**: Perfumer-band achieved a median per-material Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 0.157 eV on the test set.
  - **Speed**: Prediction takes approximately 97 ms per material on two consumer CPU cores.
  - **Acceleration**: The overall workflow (pre-relaxation + QE refinement) achieved a 2.75x speedup over standalone QE.
- **Comparison**: Compared to Hamiltonian-based methods like DeepH, Perfumer-band is lighter and faster in inference. Unlike general LLM agents (e.g., GENIUS, MatSciAgent) that primarily coordinate tools, Perfumer integrates specialized physics-aware predictive models to substantially accelerate core computational steps while lowering the expertise barrier via natural language interfaces.

## 4. Conclusion
Perfumer is an innovative LLM-based agentic framework that significantly reduces the cost and barrier of first-principles calculations by integrating specialized ML acceleration models for structural relaxation and band prediction with traditional DFT software (Quantum ESPRESSO). It achieves a 2.75x computational speedup and high-precision band prediction (MAE 0.157 eV), while automating the entire process from structure upload to property prediction via natural language, making it particularly suitable for large-scale material screening involving complex elements.
