## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Beznau Unit 1 in Switzerland, operational since 1969, is the world's most experienced nuclear reactor. Five decades of incremental modernization have left its monitoring systems, alarm logs, variable catalogs, technical specifications, and investigation reports in separate silos never designed for joint querying.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Traditional troubleshooting requires engineers to manually gather knowledge scattered across databases, documents, and colleagues, which is slow and inefficient. General-purpose AI assistants lack the specific architecture needed for such highly fragmented, legacy industrial systems that demand strict traceability and adherence to safety cultures.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed**: A hierarchical multi-agent assistant system built on Large Language Models (LLMs) to support anomaly investigations in nuclear reactor monitoring.
- **Challenge 1: Collaborative Retrieval and Integration from Heterogeneous Data Silos**. The difficulty lies in data being distributed across independent systems not designed for joint queries, with varied formats. The paper addresses this by using an orchestrating agent to break down engineer queries and delegate parts to specialized sub-agents, each with read-only access to a single source, enabling safe and precise information aggregation.
- **Challenge 2: Trustworthiness and Domain Knowledge Integration in Industrial Settings**. The challenge is ensuring AI decisions are traceable and compliant with engineering norms, avoiding "black box" hallucinations. The method encodes domain expertise as natural-language procedures that engineers can read, review, and revise; the system remains strictly advisory, with every answer traced back to its sources, aligning with safety culture.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The system uses a hierarchical architecture with an orchestrating agent for task decomposition and multiple sub-agents for specific data sources (e.g., SAP, document repositories). Domain knowledge is embedded as natural language programs for easy maintenance by non-experts. It was deployed in a case study at the Beznau Nuclear Power Plant, emphasizing "read-only" access to ensure production safety.
- **Evaluation Results**: The paper demonstrates through a case study how the system reduces tedious manual searches in SAP systems and successfully integrates dispersed knowledge to assist in core instrumentation anomaly investigations. While lacking large-scale quantitative benchmarks, it validates the system's usability and organizational adaptability in a real-world industrial environment.
- **Comparison**: Compared to traditional keyword search or single RAG systems, this hierarchical multi-agent architecture better handles complex cross-system queries and addresses the lack of professional logical constraints in general LLMs by incorporating domain knowledge as natural language programs.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper presents not only a technical architecture but also organizational lessons on introducing agentic AI into an industrial culture built on safety, traceability, and conservative change. The Beznau case study proves that hierarchical multi-agent systems can effectively resolve knowledge silos in legacy systems, paving the way for the responsible use of AI in industrial monitoring. It emphasizes the importance of evidence traceability and retaining final decision-making authority with plant personnel in human-AI collaboration.
