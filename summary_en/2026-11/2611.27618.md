## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large Language Model (LLM) agents are increasingly critical in scientific discovery and software development, operating through reasoning-action cycles involving state transitions. Understanding why agents settle into specific "idea basins" or exhibit diverse strategic behaviors is becoming difficult using human intuition alone.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: While previous studies have quantified semantic uncertainty and state-transition structures using semantic entropy or Markov processes, they fail to explain how training mechanisms shape these structures. Specifically, there is a missing quantitative link between post-training objectives (e.g., RLHF, DPO) and the organization of agent-level behavior, making it hard to predict changes in output diversity and reasoning patterns.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a statistical physics-based framework for LLM agent state evolution**: Introduces "agent states" as intermediate variables and uses a partition function to model the ideal KL-regularized transition distribution, quantifying how post-training organizes agent behavior.
- **Challenge 1: How to quantitatively describe the shaping mechanism of post-training on the agent's state space structure?**
  - **Difficulty**: Post-training involves complex reward optimizations and policy updates; traditional token-level metrics fail to capture global structural changes in high-dimensional state spaces.
  - **Solution**: The paper introduces the concept of Global Potential from statistical physics, linking the ideal transition distribution via a partition function to potential structure, mean reward, and state entropy, providing a mathematical tool to measure and predict post-training effects.
- **Challenge 2: How to verify the actual evolution laws of potential structure during post-training?**
  - **Difficulty**: It is necessary to prove that the potential strengthening predicted by the theoretical framework aligns with actual agent behavior and generalizes across tasks.
  - **Solution**: Experiments on Qwen3.5-9B for two tasks (safety test selection and physics reasoning auditing) observed that the potential structure strengthened during post-training as predicted, with joint changes in reward and state entropy providing consistency evidence.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: The study utilizes the Qwen3.5-9B model on two tasks: T1 (sequential selection of safety tests) and T2 (physics reasoning auditing). It tracks agent transitions in a finite state space to compute the partition function, potential structure, mean reward, and state entropy.
- **Evaluation Results**: The experiments demonstrate that the potential structure of the agent's state space strengthens during post-training, indicating more organized and concentrated behavior. The consistency between rising mean rewards and changing state entropy validates the theoretical predictions.
- **Comparison**: Unlike prior works that only measured semantic uncertainty (Farquhar et al.) or modeled reasoning as metastable Markov processes without linking to training dynamics (Kim et al.), this work establishes a quantitative link between post-training objectives and state-level behavior, explaining how training reshapes the probability distribution of state transitions and global structure.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper proposes a new paradigm for understanding LLM agent behavior from a statistical physics perspective. By treating agent states as intermediate variables and introducing global potential and partition functions, it successfully quantifies how post-training organizes and shapes agent reasoning. The study not only fills the theoretical gap between training objectives and state-level behavior but also validates the prediction of potential structure strengthening in practical tasks, offering a robust quantitative tool for understanding and predicting agent performance in complex environments.
