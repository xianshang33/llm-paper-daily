## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Automated meta-analysis requires not only accurate numerical extraction but also the consistent preservation of result identity (including study, comparison, population, outcome, time point, and analysis conditions) across all stages of evidence synthesis.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Current LLM-driven workflows (e.g., Manalyzer, meta-pipe) automate multiple stages but fail to maintain explicit result identity and decision boundaries across steps. Local mismatches or omissions in result identity propagate errors into statistical inputs and final reports, lacking end-to-end verifiability.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed MetaRigor**: An LLM workflow framework for verifiable end-to-end evidence synthesis that organizes results as "Study × Target" units and operationalizes scientific decision boundaries via Task Contracts, programmatic rules, and source binding.
- **Challenge 1: Maintaining Consistent Result Identity Across Stages**. It is difficult to preserve the specific attributes of study results (e.g., population, time point) as they pass through different processing stages due to the contextual nature of LLMs. MetaRigor addresses this by structuring results into "Study × Target" units and employing source binding to ensure every data point is traceable to its origin, maintaining identity consistency.
- **Challenge 2: Balancing LLM Flexibility with Scientific Rigor**. Pure LLM generation lacks the strict adherence to statistical and evidentiary rules required for meta-analysis. MetaRigor solves this by using a hybrid architecture where the model handles context-dependent semantic interpretation, while programs execute formalizable rules, computations, and validations, clearly defining responsibilities through "Task Contracts".

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Setup**: Controlled evaluations were conducted across five stages: evidence acquisition, data extraction, risk of bias assessment, certainty of evidence assessment, and manuscript generation. A downstream end-to-end evaluation was performed on seven cases.
- **Key Results**:
    - Achieved an F1 score of 86.29% for complete result delivery.
    - Reached 80.20% agreement for domain-level certainty of evidence judgments.
    - Generated manuscripts scored 86.00 in human evaluation, outperforming all three baselines.
    - The aggregate F1 score in end-to-end evaluation was 77.01%, exceeding the strongest baseline by 9.78 percentage points.
- **Comparison & Analysis**: Cross-system tracing revealed that local identity errors in existing systems alter statistical inputs and propagate to final reports. Ablation studies confirmed that task constraints and programmatic rules play distinct and critical roles in different stages, validating the effectiveness of the hybrid approach.

## 4. Conclusion
MetaRigor addresses the lack of end-to-end verifiability in LLM-driven evidence synthesis by enforcing explicit result identity, scientific decision boundaries, and provenance relationships. By separating semantic understanding (LLM) from formal verification (programs), it significantly improves the accuracy and reliability of automated meta-analysis, offering a more rigorous paradigm for AI-assisted scientific research.
