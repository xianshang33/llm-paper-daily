## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Multi-agent systems are increasingly used to pressure-test scientific hypotheses through role-playing debates for claim verification. However, reliability remains a critical challenge in automated scientific discovery.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**:
    1. **Self-Critique Bias**: When debating agents are instantiated from the same base model, they share identical latent representations, leading to structural self-confirmation bias rather than genuine independent debate.
    2. **Static Iteration Protocols**: Fixed iteration counts either halt prematurely before evidence stabilizes or waste computational resources after convergence, failing to adapt to the pace of evidence change.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed VerifyAgent**, a multi-agent research verification framework addressing both failure modes.
- **Challenge 1: Homogenization and Self-Confirmation Bias from Same-Source Models**. Using identical model weights limits cognitive diversity, making it hard to uncover deep contradictions. VerifyAgent introduces an asymmetric role architecture (Advocate, Skeptic, Arbitrator) with opposing epistemic directives, significantly lowering role-embedding similarity (0.852 vs. 0.918) and surfacing more contested claims (22.0% vs. 15.4%).
- **Challenge 2: Computational Waste or Insufficient Verification due to Fixed Iterations**. Fixed rounds cannot determine true evidence convergence. VerifyAgent proposes the "Belief-Delta" ($\\Delta B$) convergence criterion, an evidence-grounded stopping rule that eliminates over-iteration (0.0% vs. 62.5%) and reduces LLM calls by 2.4x.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Each supported claim is anchored to verbatim literature snippets audited by an independent NLI classifier for auditable evidence grounding.
- **Evaluation Setup**: 200 runs on 40 research hypotheses using DeepSeek-R1-32B.
- **Results vs. Related Work**:
    - **Role Diversity**: Significantly lower role-embedding similarity compared to symmetric Multi-Agent Debate (MAD) (p = 5.82 × 10⁻⁸).
    - **Efficiency**: Eliminated 62.5% of over-iteration cases seen in Fixed-3 protocols, reducing LLM calls by 2.4x.
    - **Hallucination Rate**: Achieved a 21.7% hallucination rate, comparable to Self-Refine critics (25.9%, p = 0.61), while providing auditable evidence grounding.

## 4. Conclusion
VerifyAgent effectively addresses self-critique bias and low computational efficiency in multi-agent scientific verification by introducing asymmetric role architectures and dynamic convergence criteria. It balances rigorous epistemic verification with compute efficiency, offering a more robust framework for automated scientific discovery.
