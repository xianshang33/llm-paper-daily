## 1. What the Paper Does

### Background
Multi-agent systems (MAS) increasingly coordinate heterogeneous models and tools to solve complex tasks, creating a critical need for reliable evaluation. However, current evaluations often focus on static task completion quality, ignoring the impact of the execution environment on system performance.

### Why Existing Work Falls Short
Existing benchmarks (e.g., AgentBench, SWE-Bench) primarily evaluate agent capabilities under ideal or fixed conditions, neglecting the significant impact of the "Serving Substrate" (underlying inference engines, hardware resource states, load conditions) on MAS workflow performance. Research shows that a MAS workflow fastest in one environment may perform poorly in another, making benchmark results highly conditional on execution methods. This lack of consideration for latency, cost, resource pressure, and failure modes in actual deployment leads to incomplete assessments.

## Core Contributions

### Proposed MASSys Benchmark Framework
The paper introduces MASSys, a benchmark designed to measure the dependence of multi-agent LLM systems on serving substrates. It executes identical candidate-task pairs across various serving substrates and load regimes, covering five workload families: software repair, research synthesis, financial analysis, interactive control, and diagram generation. It records task quality alongside delivery rates, latency, cost, resource pressure, and failure evidence.

### Challenge 1: Performance Ranking Reversal Due to Substrate Changes
**Challenge Description**: The performance ranking of MAS workflows is not static; it fluctuates significantly with underlying resource pressures (e.g., KV-cache pressure, CPU/GPU load), making single-environment evaluations non-generalizable.
**Solution**: The paper conducts controlled diagnostic experiments running the same workflows under different resource pressures (e.g., KV-tight, CPU-tight). Results show that the Star workflow drops from 1st to 4th in mean-latency rank under KV-cache pressure, while Chain rises from 4th to 1st. MASSys reveals this ranking reversal through systematic substrate sweeps, proving that evaluation must account for specific operating conditions.

### Challenge 2: Long-Tail Latency and Effective Quality Degradation Under High Load
**Challenge Description**: As load increases, systems face timeout pressure and long-tail latencies. While the quality of surviving outputs may remain competitive, the "Effective Quality" (proportion of successfully delivered and usable results) drops significantly.
**Solution**: MASSys tracks the relationship between completion rates and tail latencies under different load regimes. It finds that timeouts and retries become dominant failure modes under high load. By tracing tool and runtime data, the paper highlights that these outcomes involve both model serving and non-model execution. It emphasizes reporting which tasks are delivered, how well they are solved, and where execution breaks down, providing a more comprehensive view of system usability under real-world pressure.

## Implementation and Evaluation

### Implementation and Deployment
MASSys implements an execution engine across multiple serving substrates (e.g., vLLM) and load mechanisms. It supports five workload families and records fine-grained service-level metrics (latency, throughput, token cost, tool cost, KV-cache reuse rate, queue wait time) and task-level metrics (completion success rate, failure modes).

### Evaluation Results and Comparison
- **Ranking Reversal**: In a clean environment, Star ranks 1st in mean latency, while Chain ranks 4th. Under KV-cache tight conditions, Star drops to 4th, and Chain rises to 1st, demonstrating that ignoring substrate conditions leads to misleading evaluations.
- **Load Impact**: As load increases from low (0.01 JPS) to high (0.10 JPS), completion rates drop for all workflows, with significant long-tail latencies. Timeout pressure is particularly pronounced under mixed resource-tight conditions.
- **Effective Quality vs. Output Quality**: In several task families, the quality of surviving outputs changes little, but effective quality drops significantly due to failures from timeouts or resource exhaustion. This indicates that traditional benchmarks focusing only on successful output quality overestimate system performance under high real-world loads.
- **Comparison with Non-MAS Benchmarks**: Unlike traditional static benchmarks, MASSys reveals the significant role of tool invocation and non-model execution in total latency and failures, aspects previously overlooked by benchmarks focusing solely on LLM inference.

#### Summary

This paper identifies a critical flaw in current MAS evaluation: the neglect of serving substrate effects. It proposes MASSys, a benchmark framework that systematically evaluates MAS across different serving environments and load conditions. The study demonstrates that MAS workflow performance is highly dependent on the execution environment, exhibiting ranking reversals and effective quality degradation. By recording comprehensive runtime metrics and failure evidence, MASSys provides a new perspective and methodology for designing and evaluating more robust multi-agent systems, emphasizing the importance of evaluating MAS designs under explicit operating conditions.
