## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Skill-augmented reinforcement learning enables LLM agents to reuse task-solving knowledge across episodes. However, existing systems typically expose agents to a flat skill pool and update skills only based on coarse, episode-level outcomes.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current methods conflate two distinct uses of experience: stable "strategies" that should frame the entire episode, and "tactics" that should be retrieved only when encountering local execution gaps. This flat disclosure dilutes context, fails to distinguish global structure from local advice, and lacks fine-grained feedback for optimizing retrieval timing and skill evolution.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed STRIDE Framework**: A framework that introduces progressive skill disclosure into agentic reinforcement learning, guided by strategy-to-tactics retrieval.
- **Challenge 1: How to effectively distinguish and manage global strategies vs. local tactics?**
  - **Difficulty**: Displaying all skills at every step consumes prompt budget and confuses global goals with local operations; fixed delivery rules lack flexibility.
  - **Solution**: STRIDE keeps the selected strategy visible as episode context, exposes only a compact tactic menu, and loads full tactic content only after an explicit retrieval action, enabling on-demand disclosure.
- **Challenge 2: How to provide fine-grained credit assignment for skill retrieval and evolution?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional methods rely solely on episode-level outcomes, making it hard to determine the effectiveness of specific retrievals, leading to delayed or inaccurate skill updates.
  - **Solution**: STRIDE turns each retrieval into an event-level credit unit. The same event provides process feedback for learning "when to retrieve" and supplies evidence for skill bank evolution, enabling more precise policy optimization.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated on ALFWorld and WebShop benchmarks using Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B models.
- **Key Results**:
  - On **ALFWorld**, STRIDE achieved an average success rate of 92.7% with Qwen3-4B and 94.9% with Qwen3-8B.
  - On **WebShop**, STRIDE improved over the strongest reproduced skill-augmented baseline by 4.5 score points and 1.9 percentage points in success rate.
- **Comparison Conclusion**: Results demonstrate that skill reuse is most effective when persistent strategies preserve global task structure and tactics are disclosed only at local decision bottlenecks. STRIDE significantly outperforms existing flat skill pool methods.

#### Summary
STRIDE successfully addresses the confusion between strategies and tactics in agentic reinforcement learning by introducing a progressive skill disclosure mechanism. By treating strategies as persistent context and retrieving tactics on demand, it optimizes context window usage and enhances the efficiency of skill retrieval and evolution through event-level credit assignment. Its superior performance on multiple benchmarks proves its effectiveness in improving LLM agents' capability in handling complex tasks.
