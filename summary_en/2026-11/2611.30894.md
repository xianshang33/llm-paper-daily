## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Coding Agents (CAs) powered by Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly integrated into software engineering workflows. To provide transparency and explainability, some CAs expose their Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning as separate messages, allowing software engineers to understand the rationale behind decisions.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: While CoT has been studied in standalone LLM contexts (e.g., interactive reasoning graphs), there is a lack of empirical evidence regarding its effectiveness as an Explainable AI (XAI) mechanism for end-users in complex Integrated Development Environments (IDEs). Existing literature does not adequately address how the verbosity and complexity of CoT messages, driven by advanced CA capabilities like tool invocation, impact software engineers' cognitive load and trust.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed a**: Qualitative study involving surveys and semi-structured interviews with 36 software engineers to uncover their perceptions, challenges, and design needs regarding CoT messages in CAs.
- **Challenge 1: Excessive Cognitive Load and Mismatched Information**. CoT messages are often overly verbose and contain technical details mismatched with the user's expertise, leading to comprehension difficulties. The paper highlights through thematic analysis that this "over-detailing" creates extraneous cognitive load, causing users to selectively ignore or abandon the tool, thus necessitating designs that adapt information granularity to user expertise.
- **Challenge 2: Interaction Detachment and Trust Issues**. Users perceive CoT messages as "detached" from the conversation, feeling like the "agent is talking to itself," which disrupts interaction coherence. The study finds that this detachment undermines the trust benefits of transparency, indicating that CoT design must better integrate into the conversational flow to maintain user trust and sense of control.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: This study does not propose a new algorithmic model or system deployment but conducts an empirical user study. It employed a mixed-method approach, starting with a survey followed by semi-structured interviews with 36 software engineers.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    - **Positive Feedback**: CoT messages are deemed indispensable for enhancing transparency, scrutability, and trust in CAs.
    - **Negative Feedback**: Key issues include verbosity, mismatch between technical content and user expertise, and a sense of detachment due to the "self-talk" style. These issues lead to comprehension barriers and tool abandonment.
- **Comparison with Related Work**: Unlike prior work such as Pather et al., which focused on interactive reasoning graphs for standalone LLMs, this study focuses on CAs within IDEs that possess tool-calling capabilities. It identifies a gap in handling the specific complexities of CA-generated CoT (e.g., logs from multi-step tool calls) and provides XAI design insights specific to the software engineering domain.

## 4. Conclusion
This paper reveals the dual nature of Chain-of-Thought explanations in Coding Agents: they are useful for transparency but unwieldy due to poor design. While CoT enhances trust, its current presentation often burdens users with verbosity, irrelevance, and interaction detachment. The study synthesizes these insights to guide the design of CoT messages that better support software engineers' explainability needs, calling for future CA designs to prioritize information appropriateness and conversational coherence.
