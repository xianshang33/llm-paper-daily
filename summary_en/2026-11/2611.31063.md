## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) built on Large Language Models (LLMs) solve complex tasks through role specialization and structured communication. However, erroneous responses from upstream agents can propagate through intermediate messages, compromising the final system output.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Current evaluations rely heavily on outcome-based metrics (final answer correctness), which obscure how agents amplify, attenuate, or correct errors during the process. Different error handling paths can lead to identical final labels, making it impossible to distinguish information handling within the workflow or predict system-level error propagation from local measurements alone.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed REPA (Response-Aware Error Propagation among Agents)**: A structural model that connects measured local responses to system-level error propagation, validated on the ErrPropBench-10 benchmark.
- **Challenge 1: How to decouple and quantify different response channels of local agents to erroneous information?**
  - **Difficulty**: Agents may reject, revise, or adopt incorrect inputs, and these behaviors are mixed within input assembly and agent processing, making them hard to isolate.
  - **Solution**: REPA separately estimates "input assembly" and "agent processing" across multiple error channels, distinguishing changes in message content from changes in task performance to precisely capture local response features.
- **Challenge 2: How to accurately map local modeling errors to system-level endpoint prediction errors?**
  - **Difficulty**: Local susceptibility does not directly determine the final system response due to downstream processing and aggregation; simple direct regression fails to capture these complex non-linear dependencies.
  - **Solution**: Theoretical analysis establishes the link between local modeling errors and endpoint prediction errors. The structural model composes separately estimated local responses to create an interpretable mapping from local behavior to system susceptibility.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Experiments span 3 Qwen base models, 6 MAS workflows, and 10 knowledge/reasoning settings in ErrPropBench-10.
- **Key Results**:
  - **Prediction Accuracy**: REPA achieves a mean condition-level Spearman correlation of 0.84 across base models, significantly outperforming direct regression (0.47).
  - **Utility**: In controlled experiments, REPA-guided Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) improves accuracy by an average of 19 percentage points over no retrieval, with only a 16% increase in token usage.
- **Comparison**: Unlike post-hoc attribution relying on full traces or methods requiring additional training, REPA predicts system-level propagation from local measurements without fine-tuning underlying LLMs or observing downstream test outputs, offering better interpretability and targeted optimization capabilities.

#### Summary
This paper addresses the opacity of error propagation mechanisms in Multi-Agent Systems by proposing the REPA model. By decoupling local agent input assembly and processing, and establishing a theoretical link to system-level errors, REPA achieves high-precision error propagation prediction (Spearman correlation of 0.84). It provides an interpretable foundation for targeted system optimization (such as efficient RAG), filling the analytical gap between local behavior and system reliability.
