## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: AI agents often exhibit "laziness" in long-horizon tasks by committing to an answer before gathering sufficient evidence. Evaluations show agents frequently commit even when their stated belief indicates multiple possible answers.
- **Why Existing Work Fails**: Traditional outcome rewards only score the final answer at the end of an episode, failing to distinguish between evidence-backed correct answers and lucky guesses. Thus, they cannot teach the agent when to commit. Process rewards using LLM judges are often expensive and subjective.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Beliefs (RLVB)**, which leverages the simulator's ability to compute the exact posterior over answers at each step during training as a "verifiable belief."
- **Challenge 1: How to teach agents when to act based on evidence sufficiency without external judges or reward models?**
  - **Difficulty**: Outcome rewards are sparse and misleading (rewarding lucky guesses), while process rewards are costly.
  - **Solution**: RLVB uses the simulator-known rules to calculate posterior probabilities. It rewards the agent for continuing exploration if the posterior allows multiple answers and for committing once evidence is sufficient. This reward is objective, relying solely on gathered evidence, not on the agent's internal monologue or external models.
- **Challenge 2: How to ensure agents act consistently with their internal beliefs rather than guessing blindly?**
  - **Difficulty**: Base models often commit randomly even when their belief line expresses uncertainty.
  - **Solution**: RLVB aligns the agent's "belief state" with its "action policy." Post-training, the agent commits in only 0.1-2.2% of turns where evidence is insufficient and belief is uncertain, compared to 3-68% for base models, leading to accurate answers based on high levels of evidence.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Benchmark**: Introduced "Pro Worlds," a visual puzzle benchmark where agents view a large world through a small window and must decide when they have enough visual evidence.
- **Setup**: Evaluated three Vision-Language Models (VLMs) on three benchmarks, two of which were unseen during training (zero-shot generalization).
- **Comparison Results**:
  - **vs. Base Models**: RLVB outperformed base models in all 9 settings, with an average improvement of 17.1%.
  - **vs. GRPO/PPO (Outcome Reward Only)**: GRPO and PPO performed worse than base models on average, whereas RLVB showed significant gains.
  - **vs. PPO + LLM Judge Process Reward**: RLVB outperformed this method by 9.3% on average while using 1.6x fewer GPU-hours, demonstrating superior efficiency.
- **Detailed Analysis**: The trained agent answered with sufficient evidence in 51%, 12%, and 74% of episodes across benchmarks, compared to 8%, 0.4%, and 36% for base models. Accuracy gains stem from answering more often at high evidence levels. At equivalent evidence levels, accuracy is comparable to base models, but the agent no longer acts against its own beliefs.

#### Summary
This paper addresses the "laziness" problem in AI agents, where they prematurely commit to answers. The authors propose RLVB, which uses verifiable posterior probabilities from the simulator as reward signals during training. This teaches agents to explore when evidence is insufficient and commit when it is sufficient, without needing expensive LLM judges. RLVB significantly improves accuracy (avg. +17.1%) and reduces training costs. Experiments demonstrate that RLVB effectively aligns agent actions with their internal beliefs, overcoming limitations of traditional RL in temporal decision-making and evidence accumulation.
