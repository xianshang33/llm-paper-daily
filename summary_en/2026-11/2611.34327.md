## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) built on Large Language Models (LLMs) outperform single-agent systems in complex tasks but incur substantial token overhead due to inter-agent communication. Assigning large LLMs to every agent is common but leads to inefficient resource utilization, far from the Pareto frontier.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Most existing routers treat cost only implicitly rather than as an explicit optimization constraint. Furthermore, fixed accuracy-cost preferences cannot express how much a caller is willing to pay for a specific query, lacking dynamic adaptability to per-query monetary budgets.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed MeterRouter**: A budget-aware dynamic router that incrementally synthesizes a collaboration graph by selecting each agent's role, LLM, incoming connections, and termination conditions, conditioned on the query and a per-query monetary budget.
- **Challenge 1: How to explicitly optimize cost while maintaining accuracy under dynamic budgets?**
  - **Difficulty**: Traditional static topologies or fixed preferences cannot balance precision with specific monetary limits per query, and the search space for multi-agent collaboration is vast.
  - **Solution**: MeterRouter treats cost as an explicit constraint. It initializes by imitating successful execution graphs and refines decisions using a variant of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). The reward function favors correct answers while penalizing spending and budget overruns, dynamically adjusting the collaboration structure within the given budget.
- **Challenge 2: How to efficiently explore the huge configuration space of agent roles, models, and topologies?**
  - **Difficulty**: The combinatorial explosion of selecting models from a heterogeneous pool, defining roles, and constructing communication topologies makes direct reinforcement learning training difficult and unstable.
  - **Solution**: Adopts a two-stage strategy: "Imitation Learning Initialization + GRPO Fine-tuning." It first obtains a good initial policy by imitating high-quality historical execution graphs, then uses GRPO for joint refinement, effectively guiding the policy towards high-reward regions (high accuracy, low consumption).

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Experimental Setup**: Evaluated across five benchmarks using a three-tier LLM pool (covering models of varying capabilities and costs).
- **Key Results**:
  1. **Accuracy**: MeterRouter achieves performance comparable to the strongest MAS.
  2. **Economical**: It reaches this accuracy at 82% lower cost than the strongest MAS router.
  3. **Frontier Extension**: Successfully extends the accuracy-cost frontier beyond the ceiling of single-agent systems.
  4. **Controllability**: Adapts spending to the specified budget and rarely exceeds it.
- **Comparison**: Compared to existing routers that handle cost implicitly or use fixed preferences, MeterRouter achieves superior cost-efficiency and flexibility under explicit budget constraints.

## 4. Conclusion
MeterRouter addresses the issues of high communication costs and lack of fine-grained budget control in multi-agent systems. By introducing explicit monetary budget constraints and a GRPO-based dynamic routing mechanism, it significantly reduces inference costs (by 82%) while ensuring high performance. It enables a flexible trade-off between accuracy and cost, providing an efficient and controllable solution for the practical deployment of multi-agent systems.
