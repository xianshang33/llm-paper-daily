## 1. What the Paper Does
- **Background**: Large language model (LLM) agents are evolving from passive chatbots into autonomous systems operating continuously in dynamic environments. They need to solve current tasks and learn from past interactions to improve future performance.
- **Limitations of Existing Work**: Existing streaming benchmarks often rely on short task sequences or largely independent tasks, providing limited evidence on whether agents can effectively reuse experience when later tasks depend on earlier outcomes.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed EvoLink-Bench**: A benchmark for evaluating self-evolving agents in long-horizon, interdependent task streams.
- **Challenge 1: Lack of evaluation for long-range inter-task dependencies**. The difficulty lies in existing benchmarks isolating tasks, ignoring how prior outputs affect subsequent ones. EvoLink-Bench addresses this by constructing scenarios with persistent workspaces and cross-task dependencies (e.g., office work, dialogue, AutoResearch), forcing agents to utilize prior states and outcomes for later tasks.
- **Challenge 2: Difficulty in distinguishing mechanisms between knowledge acquisition and capability evolution**. Traditional evaluations often conflate factual knowledge correction with execution capability improvement. This benchmark designs two complementary levels: L1 for evaluating language-expressible knowledge acquisition/revision, and L2 for assessing task-level capability development through execution and feedback, revealing that these two forms of evolution require different learning mechanisms.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation & Deployment**: The benchmark covers daily office work, multi-turn dialogue, and AutoResearch-style workflows. Evaluation is split into L1 (factual evolution) and L2 (capability evolution). Experiments used three persistence conditions (Off, Frozen, Evolving) and evaluated five agent frameworks across three backbone configurations on 19,635 scored task slots.
- **Evaluation Results**:
    1. No framework consistently performed best across both L1 and L2 levels.
    2. Retries recovered only a limited proportion of failed tasks.
    3. Persistent-state updates did not necessarily improve subsequent performance.
    4. Evolution effects differed between knowledge-level and capability-level tasks, suggesting distinct learning mechanisms are needed.
    5. Performance gains varied across domains and task streams; improvements in some tasks did not necessarily extend to others.
- **Comparison**: Unlike existing short-sequence or independent-task benchmarks, EvoLink-Bench reveals real weaknesses in agents' long-term dependency handling, showing that accumulating experience does not equate to effectively applying it.

#### Summary
EvoLink-Bench fills the gap in evaluating self-evolving agents in long-horizon, interdependent task streams. By distinguishing between knowledge and capability evolution and introducing persistent state evaluation, the study highlights significant limitations in current agents' experience reuse and cross-task generalization, emphasizing the need for specific learning mechanisms for different types of evolution.
