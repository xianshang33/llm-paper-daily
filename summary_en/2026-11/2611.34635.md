## 1. What the Paper Did
- **Background**: Large pretrained language models (LLMs) excel in interactive tasks but are limited by high computational and memory requirements, hindering deployment in local or privacy-sensitive environments. The study investigates the minimum model capacity required for specialized language agents.
- **Why Existing Work Falls Short**: Previous studies on small models (e.g., TinyStories, MAPF-GPT, DART) either focus solely on natural language generation without decision-making or on sequential decision-making without relying on natural language understanding. There is a lack of frameworks that train compact models from scratch to combine natural language capabilities with complex multi-step decision-making.

## Core Contributions
- **Proposed ZeroHero Framework**: A framework for training Small Language Agents (SLAs) with fewer than 50 million parameters from scratch, utilizing a two-stage training process for efficient multi-step interactive task handling.
- **Challenge 1: How can small models master complex language reasoning and decision-making from scratch?**
  - **Difficulty**: Small models lack pretrained knowledge, making it difficult to directly understand environmental observations and generate reasonable action policies.
  - **Solution**: Employed expert distillation by using a Large Language Model (LLM) as an expert to collect successful trajectories. The SLA is then pretrained to predict the expert's responses based on interaction history, injecting foundational language understanding and decision patterns.
- **Challenge 2: How to maintain language generation quality and logical consistency during RL fine-tuning?**
  - **Difficulty**: Relying solely on environment rewards can lead to nonsensical or logically inconsistent language outputs during exploration, harming long-term task completion.
  - **Solution**: Introduced "reasoning alignment" during Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) post-training. This supplements environment rewards with expert supervision on the generated language, ensuring linguistic rationality and consistency with actions.

## Implementation and Evaluation
- **Implementation**: Agents have fewer than 50 million parameters. Evaluated in three environments requiring language understanding and generation: Crafter (textual observation and reasoning), WebShop (translating instructions to search queries), and Alfworld-Dialogue (clarifying Q&A and household actions).
- **Evaluation Results**:
  - In WebShop, ZeroHero agents approached expert-level performance.
  - In Crafter training rollouts, agents surpassed their demonstration experts.
  - In held-out Alfworld-Dialogue tasks, agents also outperformed their respective demonstration experts.
- **Comparison Advantage**: Unlike LLM agents relying on massive pretrained weights, ZeroHero demonstrates that specifically trained, extremely compact models can not only handle complex interactions but even surpass the large model experts providing demonstrations in certain metrics, significantly lowering deployment barriers.

## 4. Conclusion
The ZeroHero framework successfully validates that language models with very few parameters (<50M) can be trained from scratch into efficient specialized language agents. By combining expert trajectory pretraining with PPO post-training featuring reasoning alignment, the method addresses the challenge of coordinating language and decision-making in complex interactive environments for small models. Experiments show these small agents achieve near-expert or superior performance across multiple benchmarks, offering a viable path for deploying efficient language agents in resource-constrained settings.
